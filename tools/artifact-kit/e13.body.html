Economic Goods and Free Goods

The difference between economic goods and free goods, and why only economic goods have an opportunity cost. Syllabus 1.1.3.

The big idea

Economic goods are goods and services that are scarce: they take resources to produce, so they have a price and an opportunity cost. Free goods are goods that are not scarce: there is enough for everyone without using up resources, so they have no price and no opportunity cost. Most goods are economic goods, and that is why economics exists.

Economic goods and free goods

Economic goodsFree goods
ScarcityScarce: limited supplyNot scarce: unlimited supply
Resources neededUse scarce resources to produceNo scarce resources used
PriceHave a priceNo price
Opportunity costYes: resources could make something elseNo opportunity cost
ExamplesCars, food, healthcare, bottled water, clothesSunlight, air in the open, rain, sea water at the beach

Careful

  • Free goods do not mean 'free of charge' goods. A school meal funded by taxes is free to the pupil but costs resources, so it is an economic good.
  • A good can change category: clean air in a polluted city, or water in a drought, can become scarce and gain a price.
  • Free goods exist, but they cannot solve the basic economic problem because they are only a small part of what people want.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Bottled water

Water from a river is close to a free good, but bottled water uses plastic, labour and transport, and is sold for a price.

Exam use: A free good becoming an economic good.

Clean air

In heavily polluted cities, clean air can become scarce, and people pay for air filters and masks.

Exam use: A free good becoming scarce.

Free healthcare

Where treatment is free at the point of use, it still costs resources, paid for by taxes.

Exam use: Free of charge versus free good.

Sunshine and solar panels

Sunlight is free, but collecting its energy with solar panels uses scarce resources and has a cost.

Exam use: Free input, economic output.

Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain how opportunity cost is different for economic goods and free goods. (Feb/Mar 2024, P12 style)
ReminderEconomic goods use scarce resources: opportunity cost. Free goods use none.
Economic goods are scarce and use up resources, so the resources could have been used to produce something else: there is an opportunity cost. Free goods are not scarce and use no scarce resources, so there is no alternative given up and no opportunity cost.
Q5 Explain 4 marks Explain why a free-of-charge school meal is still an economic good.
ReminderResources used; opportunity cost; paid by taxes.
The meal may be free to the pupil but producing it uses scarce resources such as food, labour and kitchens. Those resources could have been used to produce something else, so there is an opportunity cost. The meal is paid for by taxpayers, so it is an economic good.

Top tips

  • Free good = not scarce = no opportunity cost. Say all three.
  • Economic good = scarce = opportunity cost. Link to resources.
  • Free of charge is not a free good. Taxes pay for it.
  • Examples: sunlight, air, rain for free goods; cars, food, healthcare for economic goods.
