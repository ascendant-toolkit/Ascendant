Determinants of PES

The main influences on whether supply is elastic or inelastic. Syllabus 2.7.3.

The big idea

How elastic or inelastic supply is depends mainly on how quickly and easily producers can increase output when the price rises. The key influences are time, spare capacity, stocks, factor mobility and the nature of production.

Determinants of PES

DeterminantElastic supplyInelastic supply
Time periodLong run: firms can build factories, plant crops, train workersShort run: capacity and resources are fixed
Spare capacityFirms have unused machines and workersFirms already at full capacity
Stocks (inventories)Large stocks of finished goods can be sold quicklyNo stocks (fresh or perishable goods)
Mobility of factorsResources can move easily from other usesResources are specialised and slow to move
Ease of productionSimple, quick production; skilled labour readily availableLong production time (crops, houses, mines)
PerishabilityGoods can be storedFresh goods spoil, so supply cannot be stockpiled
Availability of raw materialsPlentifulScarce or limited by nature

Examples

  • Mass-produced clothes: elastic (spare capacity, stocks, simple production).
  • Wheat in a year: inelastic (growing season); over several years more elastic.
  • Oil: inelastic in the short run; wells take years to develop.
  • Haircuts: fairly elastic (easy to add stylists, though not stored).

An exam answer names the determinant, says whether it makes supply more or less elastic and links it to the product in the question.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain two influences on the price elasticity of supply of a good.
ReminderTime, spare capacity, stocks, mobility.
The time period affects PES. In the short run firms cannot easily expand capacity so supply is inelastic, but in the long run they can build more so supply is more elastic. Spare capacity matters. Firms with unused machines and workers can raise output quickly, so supply is elastic.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain why the supply of fresh fruit is likely to be inelastic in the short run.
ReminderGrowing time; perishable; no stocks.
Fruit takes months to grow, so farmers cannot raise output quickly. It is perishable and cannot be stored, so supply cannot be increased by selling stocks: supply is inelastic.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how the PES of a good could affect the change in price when demand rises.
ReminderElastic: price rises less, quantity more; inelastic: price rises more.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
If supply is elastic, firms can raise output easily when demand rises. Quantity rises by a large amount and the price rises only a little. If supply is inelastic, output cannot rise much, so the price rises by more and quantity by less. The effect is larger in the short run.

Top tips

  • Learn the determinants. Time, spare capacity, stocks, mobility, perishability.
  • Say elastic or inelastic. And name the good.
  • Link to the product. Don't just list.
  • Short run vs long run. Long run is more elastic.
