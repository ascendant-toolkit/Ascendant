Significance of PED

How knowledge of PED influences decisions by consumers, workers, firms and governments. Syllabus 2.6.5.

The big idea

Knowing the PED of a good helps consumers, workers, firms and governments make decisions. Firms use it to set prices, governments use it to decide which goods to tax and what the effects will be, workers and unions use it to understand the effect of wage claims on jobs.

Implications for decision-making

Decision-makerHow PED matters
ConsumersUnderstand which price rises they can avoid (elastic goods) and which they cannot (inelastic necessities); a rise in the price of an inelastic good takes a larger share of spending
WorkersIf demand for the product (and so for labour) is inelastic, unions can push for higher wages without large job losses; if elastic, higher wages raise costs and prices and may cost jobs
Producers (firms)Inelastic: raising price increases revenue; elastic: cutting price increases revenue. Also affects pricing strategies, discounts and promotions, and responses to higher costs
GovernmentsTaxes on inelastic goods (cigarettes, alcohol, petrol) raise lots of revenue because quantity falls little, but are less effective at reducing consumption; taxes on elastic goods reduce consumption more but raise less

Examples

  • Tax on cigarettes: demand is inelastic so tax revenue is high, but consumption falls only a little.
  • Price discrimination: airlines charge business travellers (inelastic) more than leisure travellers (elastic).
  • A sale: shops cut prices on goods with elastic demand to raise revenue.
  • Subsidies and minimum prices have different effects depending on PED.

PED is a tool: its value helps predict outcomes. Estimates may be unreliable and PED can change over time, so effects are uncertain.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain why a government might tax goods with inelastic demand.
ReminderHigh revenue; small fall in quantity.
Demand for goods such as cigarettes is inelastic. A tax raises the price, but quantity demanded falls by a smaller percentage, so many units are still sold and the government raises a lot of tax revenue.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain how knowledge of PED could help a firm decide whether to raise its prices.
ReminderInelastic: raise; elastic: don't.
If demand for its product is inelastic, a price rise will cause a smaller percentage fall in quantity, so revenue will rise and the firm could raise prices. If demand is elastic, a price rise will cause a bigger fall in quantity, so revenue will fall and the firm should not raise prices.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how PED may influence the demands of workers for higher pay.
ReminderProduct demand inelastic: safe to ask; elastic: jobs lost.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
If demand for the firm's product is inelastic, a rise in costs from higher pay can be passed on in higher prices. Sales will fall only a little, so job losses are small and workers can ask for more. If demand is elastic, higher prices cause a big fall in sales, so the firm may cut jobs. Workers may therefore ask for smaller rises in elastic markets.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether a government should rely on taxing inelastic goods to raise revenue and reduce consumption.
ReminderRevenue high; consumption falls little; regressive; alternatives.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Taxing inelastic goods such as cigarettes raises a lot of revenue because quantity falls little. It also raises prices, which discourages some consumption, though the fall is small. However, the tax may be regressive: poorer households spend a larger share of income on it. Illegal markets may grow if prices are high. Overall, it is an effective revenue source but a limited way of cutting consumption, so the government may need other measures such as education.

Top tips

  • Learn the four decision-makers. Consumers, workers, firms, government.
  • Inelastic: tax for revenue. Elastic: tax to reduce consumption.
  • Link to the context. Cigarettes, petrol, holidays.
  • Evaluate. Regressive taxes; reliability of PED; time.
