The Price Mechanism

How prices signal, incentivise and ration to answer what, how and for whom to produce. Syllabus 2.4.1.

The big idea

In a market economy the price mechanism allocates scarce resources: prices rise and fall as demand and supply change, and these price changes signal to consumers and producers what to do and give them incentives to respond. It answers the three basic questions: what to produce, how to produce and for whom to produce.

Three functions of prices

FunctionMeaningExample
SignallingPrices tell producers and consumers where resources are wantedA rising price signals shortage: producers should make more
IncentivePrices and profits give a reason to change behaviourHigher profit encourages firms to produce more; lower prices encourage consumers to buy more
RationingWhen resources are scarce, prices allocate goods to those willing and able to payLimited concert tickets go to those who will pay the most

How the price mechanism answers the three questions

QuestionHow the price mechanism answers it
What to produce?Consumers show what they want by spending. A rise in demand raises prices and profits, so firms switch resources into that good; a fall in demand pushes resources away
How to produce?Firms compete, so they choose the cheapest efficient combination of resources (labour, capital, land) to keep costs low and profit high; changes in factor prices change the method
For whom to produce?Goods go to those willing and able to pay the market price; incomes decide who can afford what

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Resources move automatically to where they are most wanted
  • Firms are encouraged to be efficient and to innovate
  • No need for a central planner: low administrative cost
  • Consumer choice

Weaknesses

  • Goods go to those with money, not necessarily those in greatest need
  • Public goods and merit goods may be under-provided
  • Ignores external costs such as pollution
  • Can lead to monopoly power and unfair outcomes

Electric vehicles

Rising demand for electric cars raised prices and profits, and manufacturers shifted investment towards them.

Exam use: What to produce.

Automation

As wages rise, firms in many industries replace workers with machines.

Exam use: How to produce.

Concert tickets

Tickets for popular events go to those willing to pay the highest price, often through resale.

Exam use: Rationing by price.

Surge pricing

Ride apps raise prices in busy periods, attracting more drivers and rationing demand.

Exam use: Signal and incentive.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain the role of the price mechanism in a market economy. (Oct/Nov 2021, P12 style)
ReminderSignals and incentives; allocate resources.
The price mechanism allocates scarce resources through changes in prices. Rising prices signal that more is wanted and give firms an incentive, higher profit, to produce more. Falling prices signal that less is wanted, so resources move elsewhere.
Q2 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how the price mechanism answers the three key resource allocation questions. (Oct/Nov 2022, P22 style)
ReminderWhat: demand and profit; how: cheapest methods; for whom: ability to pay.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
The price mechanism decides what to produce through demand: a rise in demand raises price and profit, so firms produce more of that good. It decides how to produce because competitive firms choose the cheapest methods to keep costs low. It decides for whom because goods go to those willing and able to pay the price. However, it ignores need, so low-income households may go without.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain why prices are described as signals.
ReminderRising/falling prices show where resources are wanted.
Prices carry information about the state of the market. A rising price signals that demand is strong or supply is short, so firms should produce more. A falling price signals the opposite.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether the price mechanism is the best way of allocating resources.
ReminderFor: efficient, incentives, choice. Against: inequality, externalities, public goods. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
The price mechanism allocates resources through prices, signals and incentives. It is efficient because resources move to where demand is strongest and firms keep costs low to maximise profit. It also offers consumer choice and needs no central planning. However, goods go to those who can afford them, not those in most need. It ignores external costs such as pollution and may under-provide public goods like street lighting. Overall, it is effective for many goods, but the government usually needs to correct its weaknesses, so a mixed approach is likely to be best.

Top tips

  • Three functions: signalling, incentive, rationing. Name them.
  • What, how, for whom. Link each to a price mechanism answer.
  • Say 'willing and able to pay'. That is how goods are rationed.
  • Evaluate with weaknesses. Inequality, externalities, public goods.
  • Use a chain. Demand up, price up, profit up, output up.
