Indicators of Living Standards

Real GDP per head and the Human Development Index, with the advantages and disadvantages of each. Syllabus 5.1.1.

The big idea

Living standards describe the quality and quantity of goods and services people can obtain, and how well they live. Two main indicators are real GDP per head (average income) and the Human Development Index (HDI), which adds health and education to income. Each has advantages and disadvantages, so good answers use both and say what they miss.

Real GDP per head

Real GDP per head = real GDP ÷ population
  • Real means adjusted for inflation, so it shows changes in output, not prices.
  • Per head means divided by population, so it is the average income per person. A big economy with a huge population can have a low figure.
  • Higher real GDP per head usually means people can afford more food, housing, healthcare and education.

Advantages

  • Simple and quick to find, and updated regularly
  • Adjusted for inflation and population
  • Easy to compare over time and between countries
  • Linked to spending power

Disadvantages

  • An average: hides inequality
  • Leaves out the informal economy and unpaid work
  • Ignores health, education, leisure, freedom and the environment
  • Counts harmful output (pollution clean-up, weapons)
  • Cost of living differs between countries

Human Development Index (HDI)

The HDI is a composite index between 0 and 1. A higher value means higher human development. It combines three parts:

ComponentMeasured byWhat it shows
HealthLife expectancy at birthHow long people can expect to live
EducationMean years of schooling (adults) and expected years of schooling (children)Access to and level of education
IncomeGross national income (GNI) per head, adjusted for price differences between countriesMaterial standard of living

Each part is turned into an index between 0 and 1 and the three are combined into one HDI. Countries are grouped as very high (0.800 or above), high (0.700 to 0.799), medium (0.550 to 0.699) and low (below 0.550) human development.

Advantages of the HDI

  • Includes health and education, not just income
  • A single number that is easy to compare
  • Can show a country is doing well or badly for its income

Disadvantages of the HDI

  • Still an average: hides inequality
  • Leaves out the environment, freedom, crime, leisure and quality of life
  • The quality of education and healthcare is not measured
  • Data may be inaccurate, and countries with similar HDI can differ widely

Other indicators

Many other measures are used: life expectancy, infant mortality, literacy rate, doctors per 1,000 people, access to clean water and electricity, internet use, housing quality and calorie intake.

GDP per head and HDI are usually positively related: richer countries tend to have longer life expectancy and more schooling. But there are exceptions, so be ready to use data.

Income is not everything

Some countries, for example Equatorial Guinea, have relatively high GDP per head from oil but a lower HDI because health and education lag behind. Others with modest incomes, such as Costa Rica, have a high HDI.

Q1 Identify 2 marks Identify two measures of living standards. (Feb/Mar 2021, P22)
ReminderReal GDP per head and HDI are safest.
Real GDP per head (average income). The Human Development Index (HDI).
Q2 Explain 2 marks Explain how real GDP per head can be an indicator of living standards. (May/June 2024, P23)
ReminderAdjusted for inflation and population; average income; spending on necessities.
Real GDP per head is the average income per person adjusted for inflation, so a higher value suggests people can spend more and afford more goods, services and basic necessities, raising living standards.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain the three components of the Human Development Index.
ReminderHealth, education, income, each with how it is measured.
The HDI includes health, measured by life expectancy at birth. It includes education, measured by mean and expected years of schooling. It includes income, measured by gross national income per head adjusted for price differences. These are combined into one value between 0 and 1, so a higher HDI means higher human development.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain two disadvantages of using real GDP per head as an indicator of living standards.
ReminderAverage hides inequality; informal economy; ignores health, education, environment.
Real GDP per head is an average. A country may have a high average but with income concentrated among a few, so many people could still live in poverty. It also ignores things that affect well-being. It does not measure health, education, leisure or pollution, so a country with high GDP per head might have long working hours and poor air quality, lowering living standards.
Q5 Analyse 5 marks Analyse the relationship between GDP per head and life expectancy. (Feb/Mar 2022, P22)
ReminderUse data: say the overall relationship, give a high and low example, then explain why. Note an exception if there is one.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Generally there is a positive relationship: countries with a higher GDP per head tend to have a longer life expectancy. For example, a country with GDP per head of $45,000 may have life expectancy of 81, whereas one with $2,000 may have life expectancy of about 60. Higher incomes allow better food, housing, sanitation and healthcare, and governments collect more tax to spend on health. However, the relationship is not exact: some countries with similar incomes differ because of how well healthcare is organised and spent.
Q6 Analyse 5 marks Analyse the relationship between Greece's GDP per head and its HDI value. (May/June 2020, P23)
ReminderUse the data trend: GDP per head fell while HDI rose. Explain why using the HDI components.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Greece's GDP per head fell between 2010 and 2015 while its HDI value rose, so over this period the relationship was negative. The HDI includes health and education as well as income. Improvements in education and healthcare could have more than offset the fall in income, so the HDI could rise even while GDP per head fell.
Q7 Explain 4 marks Explain two ways climate change can lead to a fall in the Human Development Index of a country. (May/June 2024, P23)
ReminderOne link per HDI component: floods and schools, disease and life expectancy, farmland and income.
Floods can damage schools, which reduces mean and expected years of schooling, lowering the education part of the HDI. Flooding can also increase disease, which reduces life expectancy, lowering the health part of the HDI.
Q8 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether or not people living in countries with a high GDP enjoy higher living standards than people in countries with a low GDP. (May/June 2024, P21)
ReminderFor: afford housing, nutrition, healthcare, education. Against: GDP not GDP per head; inequality; informal economy; prices; long hours; pollution. Use 'it depends'.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Living standards describe the quality and quantity of goods and services people can obtain. People in countries with high GDP may be able to afford better housing, food and healthcare, which could increase life expectancy, and higher incomes may buy more and better quality products and education. This is more likely if the high GDP is shared across the population. However, high GDP does not necessarily mean high GDP per head, so incomes could still be low. Income may be unevenly distributed, so some people live in poverty, and a large informal sector may mean GDP under-records income. People in a rich country may work long hours with little leisure, face higher prices, and experience pollution and congestion. Overall, people in high-GDP countries are likely to enjoy higher living standards on average, but it depends on GDP per head, how income is distributed and non-income factors such as health, leisure and the environment.

Top tips

  • Real, per head. Real removes inflation; per head divides by population.
  • HDI has three parts. Health (life expectancy), education (years of schooling), income (GNI per head).
  • Both hide inequality. Always give this as a limitation.
  • Use the data in analysis questions. State the pattern, quote figures, then explain why.
  • Include a non-income factor. Leisure, environment, freedom, crime: ideas beyond GDP.
