How Markets Work

What a market is, examples of markets, and the roles of buyers and sellers. Syllabus 2.1.1.

The big idea

A market is where buyers and sellers meet to trade goods, services or resources. It does not have to be a place: it can be online or by phone. Buyers (demand) want goods at low prices; sellers (supply) want high prices. Prices send signals that help answer the three basic economic questions: what, how and who to produce for.

Buyers and sellers

Buyers (consumers)Sellers (producers)
RoleDemand goods and services to satisfy wantsSupply goods and services to earn income and profit
AimGet the most satisfaction for their money; pay a low priceMaximise revenue and profit; charge a high price
Reaction to a higher priceBuy lessSupply more
ExamplesHouseholds, firms buying inputs, governmentsShops, farmers, manufacturers, workers selling labour

Examples of markets

  • Goods markets: the market for cars, coffee or smartphones.
  • Services markets: hairdressing, banking, tourism.
  • Factor (resource) markets: the labour market (firms buy labour, workers sell it), the land market, the capital goods market.
  • Financial markets: shares, loans, foreign exchange.
  • Local and global: a farmers' market, an online shop, the world oil market.

The price mechanism

The price mechanism is how prices adjust to the actions of buyers and sellers, allocating scarce resources. Prices act as signals and incentives:

  • A rise in demand raises the price and profit, so firms produce more of that good (what to produce).
  • Firms use the cheapest methods to keep costs low and profit high (how to produce).
  • Goods go to those willing and able to pay the price (who to produce for).

Demand means willing and able to pay: wanting a good is not enough without the money to buy it.

Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain the roles of buyers and sellers in a market.
ReminderBuyers demand, pay; sellers supply, earn; price is set between.
Buyers demand goods and services and are willing and able to pay for them. They want low prices and the most satisfaction. Sellers supply goods and services to earn revenue and profit. They want high prices, and the price is set by the interaction of the two.
Q4 Explain 4 marks What is the role of the price mechanism in a market economy? Explain. (Oct/Nov 2021, P12 style)
ReminderPrices signal and give incentives; allocate resources.
The price mechanism allocates scarce resources through changes in prices. When demand rises, the price and profit rise, which signals firms to produce more. When demand falls, price falls and resources move elsewhere.
Q5 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how the price mechanism answers the three key resource allocation questions. (Oct/Nov 2022, P22 style)
ReminderWhat: demand and profit; how: lowest cost methods; who: ability to pay.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
The price mechanism decides what to produce through consumer demand: a rise in demand raises price and profit, so firms make more of that good. It decides how to produce because firms use the cheapest methods to keep costs low and profit high. It decides who to produce for because goods go to those willing and able to pay the price. However, those with low incomes may be left out, so the market may not give a fair outcome.

Top tips

  • A market is buyers and sellers meeting. Not necessarily a place.
  • Demand = willing AND able to pay. Both.
  • Prices signal and give incentives. Say both words.
  • Link the price mechanism to what, how, who. A common question.
  • Use named examples. Labour market, car market, foreign exchange.
