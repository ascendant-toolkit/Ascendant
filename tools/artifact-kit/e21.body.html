Factors of Production and Their Rewards

Land, labour, capital and enterprise, and the rewards of rent, wages, interest and profit. Syllabus 1.2.1.

The big idea

Factors of production are the resources used to make goods and services. There are four: land, labour, capital and enterprise. Each earns a reward (a payment for its use): rent, wages, interest and profit. Because factors are scarce, they have to be allocated, and their rewards are the incomes people earn.

The four factors and their rewards

FactorDefinitionExamplesReward
LandAll natural resources: the ground itself and what comes from or on itFarmland, oil, minerals, forests, fish, waterRent
LabourThe physical and mental effort of people in productionTeachers, builders, nurses, engineersWages (and salaries)
CapitalMan-made goods used to make other goods and servicesMachinery, factories, tools, computers, roadsInterest
EnterpriseThe risk-taking and organising of the other factors to produce goods and servicesAn entrepreneur starting a businessProfit
  • Capital here means physical capital, not money. Money used to buy machinery is financial capital; the machines themselves are the factor.
  • An entrepreneur takes the risk of losing money, which is why profit (a reward for risk) can be negative.
  • Factors can be mobile or not: labour can move between jobs and places, land cannot move.

A common exam trap: the reward for capital is interest, not profit. Profit is the reward for enterprise.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

A coffee shop

The building and land earn rent, baristas earn wages, the machines earn a return as capital, and the owner takes the risk and earns profit.

Exam use: All four factors in one business.

Oil-rich countries

Countries such as Saudi Arabia have a large amount of land in the form of oil reserves, which brings in income.

Exam use: Land as a natural resource.

A technology start-up

The founders take large risks and may earn big profits or lose their money.

Exam use: Enterprise and profit.

Farming

A farmer may rent land, hire workers, buy a tractor and take the risk of a poor harvest.

Exam use: Four factors in agriculture.

ReminderWages; rent.

Q2 Identify 2 marks Identify two rewards to factors of production. (May/June 2022, P22 style)
ReminderAny two: rent, wages, interest, profit.
Wages (or rent). Interest (or profit).
Q3 Define 2 marks Define capital as a factor of production.
ReminderMan-made goods used to produce other goods and services.
Capital is man-made goods, such as machinery, used to produce other goods and services.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain the role of enterprise as a factor of production.
ReminderOrganises other factors; takes risks; profit.
Enterprise organises land, labour and capital to produce goods and services. The entrepreneur takes risks because the business could make a loss. The reward for taking the risk is profit.
Q5 Explain 4 marks Explain the difference between land and capital as factors of production.
ReminderNatural vs man-made; rent vs interest.
Land is natural resources such as oil, forests and farmland, whereas capital is man-made, such as machines. Land earns rent while capital earns interest.

Top tips

  • Four factors, four rewards: land-rent, labour-wages, capital-interest, enterprise-profit.
  • Capital means machines, not money. Money is financial capital.
  • Land includes all natural resources. Oil, fish, forests, not only fields.
  • Link examples to a factor. A tractor is capital; a farmer's effort is labour.
