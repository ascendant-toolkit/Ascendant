Causes and Consequences of Changes in Globalisation

What drives globalisation (trade restrictions, transport and communication costs, MNCs) and its effects on trade, competition, the environment, migration, income distribution and development. Syllabus 6.2.2.

The big idea

Globalisation is driven by changes in trade restrictions, changes in transport costs, changes in communication costs and the movement of multinational companies (MNCs). If these changes reverse (higher tariffs, dearer shipping) globalisation can slow. Globalisation affects international trade, competition, the environment, migration, income distribution and economic development, with gains and losses for different groups.

Causes of changes in globalisation

CauseHow it increases (or reduces) globalisation
Changes in trade restrictionsLower tariffs and quotas, free trade agreements and the World Trade Organization make trade cheaper and easier. Higher restrictions (trade wars) slow globalisation
Changes in transport costsContainer ships, bigger freighters, air freight and better ports cut the cost of moving goods, so more is traded and supply chains stretch across the world. Rising fuel costs raise them
Changes in communication costsThe internet, mobile phones and video calls cut the cost of coordinating firms and selling abroad; online trade and services can cross borders instantly
Movement of MNCsMNCs set up production abroad (FDI), spread technology and create supply chains, increasing trade and investment

Effects of globalisation

AreaPossible positive effectsPossible negative effects
International tradeMore trade, specialisation, lower prices, more choiceDependence on other countries; global shocks spread quickly
CompetitionMore competition lowers prices and raises quality and innovation; consumers gainDomestic firms that cannot compete close; jobs lost; some MNCs dominate markets
The environmentSpread of green technology; some countries adopt higher standardsMore transport emissions, pollution and resource depletion; production moves to places with weak regulations
MigrationWorkers fill skill shortages; migrants earn higher wages and send remittances; ideas spreadBrain drain from poorer countries; pressure on housing and services; wage pressure on some local workers
Income distributionJobs and incomes rise in developing countries; poverty may fallSkilled workers and owners gain more than low-skilled workers, so inequality within countries may widen; gains are uneven between countries
Economic developmentFDI, technology and export markets speed up growth and developmentDependence on a few exports or MNCs; exploitation; benefits may not reach the poor

Winners and losers

  • Winners: consumers (choice and lower prices), exporters, skilled workers, MNCs, countries that attract investment.
  • Losers: workers in industries that cannot compete, small local firms, some low-income workers, the environment, countries left out of trade.

Because the effects differ by group and country, answers should usually weigh both sides and end with 'it depends on…'.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Container shipping

The standard shipping container, introduced in the 1950s and 1960s, cut the cost and time of loading goods and made long-distance trade much cheaper.

Exam use: Lower transport costs.

China joins the WTO

China joined the World Trade Organization in 2001. Its exports boomed, lowering prices for consumers worldwide, but some manufacturing jobs in other countries were lost.

Exam use: Trade restrictions falling; winners and losers.

US-China trade war

From 2018 the US and China raised tariffs on each other's goods, raising prices and prompting firms to move some production elsewhere.

Exam use: Higher restrictions slowing globalisation.

The internet and services

Cheap communication allows UK banks to run call centres in India and firms to hire software developers anywhere.

Exam use: Communication costs and globalisation of services.

Q1 Explain 2 marks Explain one cause of globalisation. (Feb/Mar 2021, P22)
ReminderOne mark for the cause, one for the explanation: transport costs, communication costs, trade barriers, MNCs.
A fall in transport costs, which reduces the cost of exporting and importing, so international trade increases.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of globalisation.
ReminderTwo different causes, each developed.
Lower trade restrictions are one cause. Cuts in tariffs and quotas, and trade agreements, make imports cheaper and exports easier, so trade increases. Improvements in communication are another. The internet and mobile phones allow firms to coordinate production and sell to customers in other countries at low cost.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how globalisation could affect income distribution within a country.
ReminderSkilled gain, low-skilled lose, owners gain; but jobs in developing countries; policy.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Globalisation increases demand for skilled workers and for capital. Skilled workers and owners of firms may see higher pay and profits. Low-skilled workers face competition from cheaper labour abroad, so their wages may stagnate or they could lose jobs. The gap between rich and poor within the country may therefore widen. However, globalisation can create jobs and raise incomes in developing countries, reducing poverty there, and governments can use taxes and education to share the gains.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how globalisation could affect the environment.
ReminderNegative: transport, production, resource use, weak regulations. Positive: technology, standards.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Globalisation increases trade and production. More shipping and air freight raises emissions, and greater output uses more resources and creates more pollution. Firms may move production to countries with weaker environmental rules, increasing pollution there. However, MNCs can spread cleaner technology, and consumers and governments may demand higher standards. Overall, the effect depends on regulation and technology.
Q5 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether or not globalisation benefits developing countries.
ReminderFor: FDI, jobs, technology, markets, growth. Against: exploitation, dependence, competition, environment, inequality.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Globalisation is the increasing integration of economies through trade, investment and migration. It could benefit developing countries by bringing foreign investment, jobs and technology and giving access to large export markets, which may raise growth and reduce poverty. Consumers may benefit from cheaper and more varied goods. These gains are larger where governments invest in education and infrastructure. However, local firms may be unable to compete with large MNCs, workers may be paid low wages in poor conditions, and countries may become dependent on a few exports and vulnerable to global shocks. Gains may go mainly to the better off, widening inequality, and production may harm the environment. Overall, globalisation is likely to benefit developing countries that can attract investment and add value, but the benefits are uneven and depend on policy.

Top tips

  • Four causes: trade restrictions, transport costs, communication costs, MNC movement.
  • Six effects: trade, competition, environment, migration, income distribution, development.
  • Weigh both sides. Positive and negative for each area.
  • Say who gains and who loses. Skilled and unskilled, rich and poor, countries.
  • It can reverse. Higher tariffs or shipping costs slow globalisation.
