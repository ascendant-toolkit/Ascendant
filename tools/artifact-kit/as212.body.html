Cloud Computing and Transmission Media

Coursebook: Chapter 2 · Communication · section 2.1 Networks including the internet

Public and private cloud computing, their benefits and drawbacks, copper, fibre, radio, microwave and satellite media, the cell phone network and wired against wireless; animations, labs and the real Paper 1 questions from 2021 to 2025.

This page in five sentences

Cloud computing means accessing services, software or files on a remote server over the internet instead of on your own computer. A public cloud is run by a third party and open to anyone with the right credentials, while a private cloud is a dedicated system available only to one organisation. The benefits are access from anywhere, no hardware to buy, easy sharing and built-in backup; the drawbacks are the need for an internet connection and reliance on someone else for security and backup. Data is carried by transmission media: copper cable (electrical signals), fibre-optic cable (pulses of light), and wireless media such as radio waves (Wi-Fi), microwaves and satellites. Wired links are usually faster, more reliable and more secure; wireless links are more mobile and cheaper to set up.

1 · Cloud computing

Cloud computing is accessing a service, files or software on a remote server rather than on a local computer. The user needs only a device and an internet connection.

Public cloudPrivate cloud
MeaningComputing services offered by a third-party provider over the public internet, open to anyone with the right equipment and credentialsA dedicated, bespoke system, offered over the internet or an internal network, available only to selected users or one organisation
ControlProvider controls security and backupOrganisation has greater control over security, privacy and backup
CostShared infrastructure, cheaperDedicated, more expensive
ExampleOnline file storage, web email, online coursesA company's own storage for its employees' work

Benefits and drawbacks

  • Benefits: data can be accessed from any computer with an internet connection; no need to buy or maintain specific hardware or software; cloud services usually include backup and recovery; documents are easy to share and several people can work on one; storage can be increased quickly; free storage may be offered.
  • Drawbacks: it cannot be accessed without an internet connection; you depend on a third party for security and backup, so you have less control; uploading and downloading large files can take a long time; downtime at the provider stops access; limited free storage may mean paying more in the long term; compatibility problems if you want to move your data.

Compared with a server on the company's own LAN, a public cloud brings a possible loss of control, reliance on an external agency and recurring costs, while a LAN server has a one-off cost and keeps working without the internet.

2 · Transmission media

MediumHow it carries dataCharacteristics
Copper cable (twisted pair, coaxial)Electrical signalsCheap and easy to install; signal weakens over distance (repeaters needed); prone to interference; lower bandwidth than fibre; can be tapped
Fibre-optic cablePulses of light through glass or plastic strandsVery high bandwidth and speed; little interference; works over much longer distances before a repeater; hard to hack into; more expensive and more fragile
Radio waves (including Wi-Fi)Radio signals in all directions from an antennaNo cables, devices can move; shared channel and interference; walls reduce range; less secure unless encrypted
MicrowavesHigh-frequency electromagnetic waves between dishes or towers (cell phone network)High bandwidth over long distances; needs line of sight between towers; affected by weather and obstructions
SatelliteMicrowave signals to and from a satellite in orbitCovers remote and large areas; long delay (latency) because the signal travels far; expensive; affected by weather

The cell phone network

The land is split into cells, designed for maximum line of sight. Each cell has a tower with an antenna that receives and transmits data. Data is sent wirelessly between the phone and the tower using low-power radio signals, and many devices can talk to the same tower at the same time.

3 · Wired and wireless networks compared

WiredWireless
SpeedFaster connection, higher bandwidth, less latencyUsually slower
ReliabilityMore stable, less affected by distance, walls and interferenceSignal degrades, interference possible
SecurityMore secure: needs a physical connectionCan be less secure: anyone in range could try to connect
MobilityFixed to the socketFree movement; no cable; easy to add devices
SetupCabling and expertise neededLess cabling; cheaper initial setup

To justify a choice, tie it to the situation: a gamer who downloads large files and wants low delay suits wired; a person who moves between rooms with a phone suits wireless.

4 · Common ways to lose marks

  • Describing a public cloud as "free". It is offered by a third party to anyone; some storage may be free.
  • Writing "cloud is more secure" or "less secure" with no reason. Say who controls security and backup.
  • Fibre against copper: write what each carries (light against electrical signals) and then a difference such as bandwidth, distance or interference.
  • Saying wireless is "worse". Give the specific drawback: slower, interference, signal degrades, less secure.
  • Forgetting the internet requirement as the main drawback of cloud computing.

6 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Google Drive and OneDrive

Your files are on a provider's servers. You can open them on any device with an internet connection, but not when you are offline unless you saved a copy.

Exam use: public cloud storage.

A bank's own data centre

A bank may run a private cloud so that only its staff can use the system and it controls the security.

Exam use: a private cloud is dedicated to one organisation.

Fibre broadband

Light pulses in glass strands give very fast, stable internet with little interference.

Exam use: fibre has a high bandwidth.

Wi-Fi in a cafe

You can move around with a laptop but the speed drops with distance and walls, and a public hotspot can be less secure.

Exam use: radio waves: mobility against interference.

Satellite internet in a remote village

No cable reaches the village, so a dish links to a satellite. It works anywhere but there is a noticeable delay.

Exam use: satellites cover remote areas but have a long delay.

M/J 2022 · 9618/13 · Q8 Define / State / Give 1 + 2 + 3 marks A company uses cloud computing. (a) Define cloud computing. (b) State what is meant by a public cloud and a private cloud. (c) Give two benefits and one drawback of using cloud computing.
ReminderRemote server; public open to anyone; private only for selected users.

(a) Accessing a service, files or software on a remote server

(b) Public: computing services offered by a third-party provider over the public internet, available to anyone with the appropriate equipment or credentials Private: computing services offered over the internet or a private internal network, only available to select users, a dedicated system accessible only to the organisation

(c) Two benefits: Can be accessed anywhere with internet access No need to install security, or security might be better No need to perform backups No need to buy specific software or hardware Easy to share documents Multiple people can work on the same document One drawback: Cannot access it without internet access Reliant on someone else for backup or security Cannot access it if the server goes down

Common mistakeDefining it as 'storing data on the internet'. It is accessing services or files on a remote server.
O/N 2023 · 9618/13 · Q3(a) Explain / Describe 2 + 4 marks A company sells online Computer Science courses to students in different countries. The courses are stored on a public cloud. (i) Explain why the company uses a public cloud to store these courses. (ii) Describe two disadvantages of storing data on a public cloud compared to storing data on a server in a LAN.
Reminder(i) available to anyone, on the internet, shared cost. (ii) control, reliance on third party, internet needed, recurring costs.

(i) Any two: Courses must be available to anyone who wishes to follow them Courses must be available on the internet The company is willing to share infrastructure with other companies which is more economic for the company

(ii) Two disadvantages, a mark for each plus a mark for its expansion: Possible loss of control because the data is stored on someone else's infrastructure; reliance on an external agency for backups and security Requires a reliable internet connection to access the remote data, more likely with a LAN (or increased recurring costs, as the provider's charges must be paid and a LAN costs once)

M/J 2024 · 9618/13 · Q5(a) Define / Describe 1 + 3 marks A multimedia design company has private cloud storage for its employees to store their work. (i) Define the term private cloud. (ii) Describe the benefits to the company of using private cloud storage instead of public cloud storage.
ReminderDedicated; control over security and backup.

(i) Dedicated or bespoke services and storage on a remote server only available to the company

(ii) Any three: Not reliant on a third party which gives greater control over security and privacy and greater control over backup Storage can be tailored or scaled to the company's requirements, for example the amount of storage or facilitating sharing of files

O/N 2025 · 9618/12 · Q8(a) Give 2 + 2 marks A restaurant accesses software and stores data using cloud computing. (i) Give two benefits of storing data using cloud computing. (ii) Give two drawbacks of using cloud computing.
ReminderAccess anywhere, no devices, backup, sharing; internet needed, third-party, slow transfers.

(i) Any two: Cloud storage can be free of charge for a limited amount No need for personal storage devices The data can be accessed from any computer with an internet connection Cloud services usually include backup and recovery Easier to share data or collaborate Storage capacity can be increased quickly and easily

(ii) Any two: It can only be accessed with an internet connection There may be no backups, or reliance on a third party for backups Uploading or downloading data could take a long time Problems at the cloud company such as downtime Compatibility issues if the data is moved Limited storage without paying extra, more expensive in the long term Possible security issues

O/N 2023 · 9618/11 · Q2(c) Identify 2 marks The students can save their school files on a public cloud. Identify two drawbacks of the students storing their files on the public cloud.
ReminderInternet, space, third-party control.

Any two: The students cannot access their files without a reliable internet connection The amount of space for no payment may be limited, so students will have to purchase more space if needed The students do not have control over backup or security of their work; they depend on a third party

M/J 2022 · 9618/12 · Q9(b) Describe 3 marks Describe three differences between fibre-optic cables and copper cables.
ReminderWhat it carries, speed, interference, distance, security, damage.

Any three: Fibre-optic data is transmitted using light; copper cable uses electrical signals Fibre has higher bandwidth, or higher transmission rates, than copper Fibre has a smaller risk of interference (noise) than copper Fibre can be used over longer distances before repeaters are needed Fibre is much more difficult to hack into than copper Fibre is more prone to damage than copper

Common mistakeWriting 'fibre is faster' alone. Pair each point with what the copper cable does.

2. Review questions

Written for practice and revision, not taken from past papers. Use them to check your understanding before attempting the real questions above.

Explain 3 marks A small firm backs up its accounts to a public cloud. Explain one benefit and one risk.
ReminderThink of backup and control.

Benefit: the data is stored off-site and can be recovered if the office computers fail Risk: the firm depends on the provider for security and backup and cannot reach the data without an internet connection

State 2 marks State two reasons why a company might choose a private cloud rather than a public cloud.
ReminderControl.

Greater control over security and privacy Greater control over backup, and storage can be tailored to the company

Explain 3 marks Explain why fibre-optic cable is used for the main links between cities.
ReminderBandwidth and distance.

It has a high bandwidth so it carries a lot of data It can be used over long distances before repeaters are needed It has a low risk of interference and is hard to hack into

Describe 2 marks Describe one situation where a satellite link is suitable and one disadvantage.
ReminderRemote areas; delay.

Suitable where cables cannot easily reach, for example a remote village or a ship Disadvantage: a long delay (latency) as the signal travels to the satellite and back, and it is expensive

Distinguish 2 marks Distinguish between radio waves and microwaves as transmission media.
ReminderCoverage and path.

Radio waves spread in all directions and are used for Wi-Fi to nearby devices Microwaves are higher-frequency waves sent between dishes or towers on a line-of-sight path over long distances

Top tips

  • Define cloud: remote server. Accessing services or files on a remote server.
  • Public v private. Public: third party, anyone. Private: dedicated, one organisation.
  • Drawback number one: the internet. Then reliance on a third party for security and backup.
  • Fibre: light. Copper: electricity. Then bandwidth, distance, interference, security.
  • Wireless: mobile but weaker. Name the specific drawback.
  • Justify. Marks come from linking the choice to the user's needs.
