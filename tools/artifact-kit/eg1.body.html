Definition of Economic Growth

What economic growth is, actual and potential growth, and the business cycle. Syllabus 4.5.1.

The big idea

Economic growth is an increase in an economy's output over time, measured as a rise in real GDP. It can also be described as an increase in the economy's productive capacity: what it could produce if resources were fully used. A growing economy can produce and consume more goods and services, which usually means higher incomes and more jobs.

Two ways to see growth

Actual growth

A rise in real GDP: output actually produced rises, for example because spending rises and unused resources are put to work. Seen as a movement towards the PPC, or an AD shift right.

Potential (long-run) growth

A rise in productive capacity: the PPC shifts outwards, or AS shifts right. This comes from more or better resources and technology.

Growth is not smooth

Real GDP does not rise in a straight line. Output usually moves in a pattern called the business cycle around a long-run upward trend.

PhaseWhat happens to outputTypical features
BoomGrowing fast, close to capacityLow unemployment, rising prices, high confidence
SlowdownGrowth slowsSpending and investment weaken
RecessionReal GDP fallsRising unemployment, falling profits, falling tax revenue
RecoveryGrowth returnsConfidence returns, firms start to hire

The trend line is long-run (potential) growth. The wiggles around it are short-run changes in actual output.

Why governments care

  • Growth is one of the six macroeconomic aims.
  • Higher output can mean higher incomes, more jobs and more tax revenue for public services.
  • But growth can bring costs too: pollution, inflation and unequal gains (see 4.5.3).

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

China's long run of fast growth

China's real GDP grew at around 10% a year for several decades from the 1980s, helped by investment, new technology, trade and a growing workforce. In 30 years output multiplied many times.

Exam use: Compound growth and potential growth from more and better resources.

Japan's slow decades

After the early 1990s Japan's growth was very low for many years, with weak demand and an ageing workforce.

Exam use: Long periods of low growth and why growth rates matter over time.

India's growth

A past paper describes India's growth of around 7% in 2017, driven by government spending and exports, with side effects such as pollution.

Exam use: A growth-rate calculation and a link to the costs of growth.

Q1 Define 2 marks Define economic growth. (Feb/Mar 2021, P22)
ReminderReal GDP is the key idea, plus 'over time'. A productive capacity definition also scores.
Economic growth is an increase in real GDP (the output of the economy) over time.
Q2 Calculate 1 marks Real GDP was $8,750.0bn in 2016 and $9,362.5bn in 2017. Calculate India's economic growth rate in 2017. (Feb/Mar 2020, P22)
ReminderChange ÷ original × 100. Give the answer as a percentage.
(9,362.5 − 8,750.0) ÷ 8,750.0 × 100 = 7%.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain the difference between actual growth and potential growth.
ReminderUse 'whereas'. Actual: use of spare resources. Potential: more capacity.
Actual growth is a rise in real GDP as unused resources are put to work, shown by a move towards the PPC, whereas potential growth is an increase in the economy's productive capacity, shown by an outward shift of the PPC or AS shifting right. Actual growth could be quick if demand rises, but it is limited once resources are fully used, whereas potential growth usually takes longer, as it needs more or better resources or technology.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain what happens to output, jobs and prices during a boom.
ReminderThree things, each linked. Output high, unemployment low, prices may rise.
In a boom real GDP grows quickly and firms are working close to capacity. Firms need more workers, so unemployment is likely to be low. With high demand and little spare capacity, firms could raise prices, so inflation may increase.

Top tips

  • Growth = rise in real GDP over time. Say 'real' and 'over time'.
  • Growth rate = change ÷ original × 100. Show the working.
  • Know actual and potential growth. Move towards the PPC, or the PPC shifts out.
  • Know the cycle. Boom, slowdown, recession, recovery.
