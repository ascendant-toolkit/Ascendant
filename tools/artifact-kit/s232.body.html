Movements Along a Supply Curve

Extensions and contractions in supply and how they are shown on the diagram. Syllabus 2.3.2.

The big idea

A change in the price of a good causes a movement along its supply curve. A rise in price causes an extension of supply (quantity supplied rises: a move up the curve). A fall in price causes a contraction of supply (quantity supplied falls: a move down the curve). The curve itself does not move.

Extension and contraction

Extension in supplyContraction in supply
CauseA rise in the price of the goodA fall in the price of the good
EffectQuantity supplied risesQuantity supplied falls
On the diagramMovement up along the curveMovement down along the curve
CurveThe same curve: no shift

Reasons for the move

  • Higher price, higher profit per unit: firms produce more.
  • Lower price, lower profit: some firms cut output or stop production; marginal cost may exceed the price.
  • Higher prices may also attract new firms into the market.

Examples of a price change

  • Higher world demand raises the price of coffee: coffee farmers extend supply.
  • A fall in the price of oil leads high-cost oil wells to close: contraction.

Extension/contraction is caused by the good's own price. A change in costs, technology or taxes shifts the curve (see 2.3.3).

Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain the difference between an extension in supply and an increase in supply.
ReminderExtension: price change, movement along. Increase: other factor, shift right.
An extension in supply is caused by a rise in the good's own price and is shown by a movement up along the same supply curve. An increase in supply is caused by a change in another factor such as costs and is shown by a shift of the whole curve to the right.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain why a fall in the price of oil may reduce the quantity supplied.
ReminderLower profit; high-cost wells close; contraction.
A fall in price reduces the profit for each barrel. High-cost wells may no longer cover their costs, so they close or reduce output, which causes a contraction in supply: a movement down the curve.

Top tips

  • Own price changes = movement along. Anything else = shift.
  • Extension = price rises. Contraction = price falls.
  • The curve does not move. Say 'a movement along the same curve'.
  • Draw and label P₁, Q₁, P₂, Q₂. Show the arrows.
  • Know the difference from increase and decrease. A classic question.
