Market Prices, Basic Prices, Gross and Net

4.1.3 to 4.1.4: converting national income between market and basic prices and between gross and net values. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper question.

This part in five sentences

The same national income figure can be stated in different ways. Market prices are the prices buyers actually pay, so they include taxes on products (such as sales tax or VAT) and exclude subsidies. Basic prices are the amounts producers receive: basic price = market price − taxes on products + subsidies on products, so basic prices show the value of production without the effect of government taxes and subsidies on products. A gross figure is before deducting capital consumption (depreciation), and a net figure is after it, so net is always lower than the matching gross figure. Combining the two ideas gives measures such as GDP at market prices, GDP at basic prices, GNI at market prices and NNI, and a careful answer states which one is being used.

1 · Market prices and basic prices

Market pricesBasic prices
MeaningWhat the buyer paysWhat the producer receives
Taxes on products (sales tax, VAT, excise)IncludedExcluded
Subsidies on productsExcluded (the buyer pays a lower price, the subsidy is paid to the producer)Included
ConversionBasic = market − taxes + subsidiesMarket = basic + taxes − subsidies
  • Worked example: a product sells for $100 including $15 sales tax, and the government pays the producer a $5 subsidy. The producer receives $85 from the buyer and $5 from the government, so the basic price is $90 (100 − 15 + 5).
  • Economy-wide: GDP at market prices $120bn; taxes on products $15bn; subsidies $5bn. GDP at basic prices = 120 − 15 + 5 = $110bn.
  • Why basic prices are used: they show the value of production before the distortion caused by taxes and subsidies, so they allow fairer comparisons of the output of different industries or countries with different tax systems.
  • Market prices matter for spending: they are the prices that households and firms actually face.

2 · Gross and net

  • Gross means before deducting capital consumption. Net means after deducting it: net = gross − capital consumption.
  • GDP and GNI are gross measures. NNI is a net measure. There are also net versions of GDP (net domestic product, NDP = GDP − capital consumption).
  • Why net can be more useful: it shows how much output is available for consumption and new investment after replacing capital that has worn out, so it better indicates whether capacity is being maintained.
  • Why gross is used more often: capital consumption is difficult to measure accurately, since the lives of machines and buildings vary.

6 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Sales tax on a phone

A phone costs $230 in the shop, including $30 of sales tax. The producer receives $200. National income at market prices counts $230; at basic prices, it counts what producers receive.

Exam use: market prices versus basic prices.

A farm subsidy

The government pays farmers $10 for each tonne of grain. The market price to the buyer is lower than the amount farmers receive in total, so the basic price includes the subsidy.

Exam use: subsidies and basic prices.

A factory that wears out

A factory spends $8m a year just to replace worn-out machines. Gross output counts the $8m of replacement, but the net figure subtracts it, showing the output available to raise living standards or expand capacity.

Exam use: gross versus net.

Comparing countries with different taxes

Two countries produce the same goods, but one has a high sales tax. GDP at market prices looks larger there, while GDP at basic prices removes the effect of the tax, making comparison fairer.

Exam use: why basic prices are used.

Top tips

  • Market prices include taxes. What the buyer pays.
  • Basic prices are what producers get. Market − taxes + subsidies.
  • Gross is before depreciation. Net is after.
  • Write the steps. One adjustment per line.
  • Check the direction. Basic is below market if taxes exceed subsidies.
  • Net is below gross. By capital consumption.
  • State the basis. Say which measure you are using.
