Definition of Price Elasticity of Supply

What PES measures and the difference between elastic and inelastic supply. Syllabus 2.7.1.

The big idea

Price elasticity of supply (PES) measures how responsive the quantity supplied of a good is to a change in its price. If a price rise lets producers increase output quickly and easily, supply is elastic. If producers cannot increase output much even when the price rises, supply is inelastic.

Elastic and inelastic supply

Elastic supplyInelastic supply
ResponseQuantity supplied changes by a bigger percentage than the priceQuantity supplied changes by a smaller percentage than the price
WhySpare capacity, stocks, flexible production, easy to hireProduction is slow, capacity is full, resources are specialised
ExamplesMass-produced factory goods, T-shirts, services that need little equipmentFresh farm crops during a season, housing, oil in the short run, fine art

Why it matters

  • PES shows how quickly output can respond when demand rises: if supply is elastic, prices rise less and output rises more; if inelastic, prices rise more.
  • It helps firms plan production and governments predict the effects of policy.
PES = % change in quantity supplied ÷ % change in price

PES is normally positive because supply curves slope upwards. The next review shows how to calculate it.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Housing supply

Building takes years, so a rise in prices boosts output only slowly.

Exam use: Inelastic supply.

Factory goods

Firms with spare capacity can increase output of phones or shirts within weeks.

Exam use: Elastic supply.

Seasonal fruit

After a price rise growers cannot grow more until the next season.

Exam use: Inelastic in the short run.

Digital goods

Extra copies of software or music cost almost nothing to produce.

Exam use: Highly elastic supply.

Top tips

  • PES = responsiveness of Qs to price. Say 'quantity supplied'.
  • Elastic = flat; inelastic = steep. For drawing.
  • Time and capacity decide it. Name them.
  • Compare % changes. Bigger or smaller.
