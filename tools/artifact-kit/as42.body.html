The Circular Flow of Income

4.2: the circular flow in closed and open economies, injections and withdrawals, and equilibrium and disequilibrium of national income. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

The circular flow of income shows how income flows from firms to households as payments to factors of production (wages, rent, interest, profit) and how expenditure flows back from households to firms as spending on goods and services. In a closed economy with government, withdrawals (leakages) are saving (S) and taxes (T), and injections are investment (I) and government spending (G); an open economy adds exports (X) as an injection and imports (M) as a withdrawal. National income is in equilibrium when injections equal withdrawals (I + G + X = S + T + M): if injections exceed withdrawals national income rises, and if withdrawals exceed injections it falls until balance is restored. The multiplier and the marginal propensities are not required at AS.

1 · The flows (4.2.1)

  • Households supply factors of production (labour, land, capital, enterprise) and receive income; they spend it on goods and services from firms.
  • Three-sector economy: households, firms and the government, which collects taxes (T) and spends on public services and investment (G).
  • Open economy: adds the international sector: foreign buyers purchase exports (X) and domestic buyers purchase imports (M).
  • Money flows round the system, so what one person spends is another's income. Real resources flow the other way.

2 · Injections, withdrawals and equilibrium (4.2.2 to 4.2.3)

Closed economyOpen economy
Withdrawals (leakages)Saving (S), taxes (T)S, T and imports (M)
InjectionsInvestment (I), government spending (G)I, G and exports (X)
EquilibriumI + G = S + TI + G + X = S + T + M
  • Injections > withdrawals: more is flowing in than out, so national income rises (economic growth).
  • Withdrawals > injections: national income falls.
  • The equilibrium condition can be rearranged: (S − I) + (T − G) + (M − X) = 0. So a budget deficit (G > T) or a trade deficit (M > X) must be matched by saving exceeding investment, or by another sector's surplus.
  • Examples: a rise in exports or in investment is an injection and raises national income; a rise in saving or in taxes is a withdrawal and lowers it unless offset by an injection.
Exam tipDraw the diagram with both sides: income and expenditure between households and firms, then label injections and withdrawals. State the equilibrium condition and say what happens to national income when they differ.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

A rise in exports

A car maker wins a big overseas order. Export spending is an injection, so firms hire more workers and pay more income; households spend more, and national income rises until withdrawals (saving, taxes and imports) have also risen to match.

Exam use: an injection raising national income.

Households save more

In a downturn households save more and spend less. Saving is a withdrawal, so unless investment or government spending rises to offset it, national income falls.

Exam use: a withdrawal lowering national income.

A government infrastructure programme

Spending on roads and rail is an injection (G). Firms and workers receive income and spend part of it, so national income rises, at least until higher taxes or imports leak out of the flow.

Exam use: government spending as an injection.

A trade deficit

Imports are higher than exports, so more is leaking out than flowing in from abroad. Unless savings are low or investment and government spending are high, the economy's income will tend to fall.

Exam use: the equilibrium condition and the open economy.

Based on Feb/March 2023 · 9708/22 · Q4(a) 8 marks Explain the circular flow of income in a closed economy and consider the extent to which the circular flow of income changes as an economy becomes an open economy.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 3): the circular flow in a closed economy involves households, firms and the government (1); injections of I and G and withdrawals of S and T (1); equilibrium income is where I + G = S + T (1).
  • Analysis (up to 3): as the economy opens it takes part in the international economy (1) through the injection of X and the withdrawal of M (1), so equilibrium income becomes where I + G + X = S + T + M (1).
  • Evaluation (up to 2): a judgement on the extent of the change, depending on the importance of net international trade (1), and a conclusion (1).
PlanClosed: households, firms, government; I + G = S + T. Open: add X and M; I + G + X = S + T + M. Judge: depends on how important trade is.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

In a closed economy income flows from firms to households as wages, rent, interest and profit, and returns as spending. The government collects taxes and spends. Injections are investment (I) and government spending (G), and withdrawals are saving (S) and taxes (T), so income is in equilibrium when I + G = S + T.

As an economy becomes open it trades with the rest of the world. Exports (X) are a new injection, and imports (M) a new withdrawal, so equilibrium is now where I + G + X = S + T + M.

The extent of the change depends on how important international trade is. For a small economy with exports and imports equal to a large share of GDP, the flow changes a lot, while for a large, mainly domestic economy the change is small. Net trade is what matters, since exports and imports may offset each other.

Based on May/June 2025 · 9708/21 · Q4(a) 8 marks Explain how the circular flow of income in an economy changes when that economy moves from a closed to an open economy and consider what determines the extent of this change.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 3): the way income flows around an economy from firms to households and back to firms (1); payments to factors of production in the form of income, profit and rent (1); payments from all these sources are spent on goods and services (1).
  • Analysis (up to 3): closed economy injections of I and G and withdrawals of S and T; open economy adds spending on exports as an injection and spending on imports as a withdrawal. Maximum 2 if only a closed or only an open economy is considered, with no evaluation marks.
  • Evaluation (up to 2): consideration of what determines the extent of the change, such as the size of trade relative to GDP, with a conclusion.
PlanFlow of income and spending. Closed: I, G, S, T. Open: add X and M. Extent depends on the size of the trade sector.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

The circular flow shows income paid by firms to households for factors of production, spent again on goods and services, in a continuous process.

In a closed economy, injections are investment and government spending, and withdrawals are saving and taxes. In an open economy, exports are added to injections and imports to withdrawals, so international trade now affects national income.

The size of the change depends on how open the economy is, measured by trade as a share of GDP, and on the balance between exports and imports. Economies with large trade sectors change more than economies that mainly trade internally.

Based on May/June 2024 · 9708/22 · Q4(a) 8 marks With the help of a diagram, explain what is meant by the circular flow of income in an open economy and consider the extent to which it can explain economic growth in such an economy.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 3): a simple circular flow diagram (1) that shows separately that expenditure flows from households to firms (1) and income flows from firms to households (1).
  • Analysis (up to 3): injections greater than withdrawals lead to economic growth and a rise in national income (1) and withdrawals greater than injections lead to a fall (1); reference to exports as an injection and imports as a leakage, along with at least one other injection and leakage (1).
  • Evaluation (up to 2): how the relative sizes of injections and withdrawals lead either to growth or to a fall, with a justified conclusion (1).
PlanDiagram. J > W: growth. W > J: fall. Include X and M. Judge: the model explains short-run changes in demand, not long-run growth in capacity.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

The circular flow shows expenditure flowing from households to firms and income flowing from firms to households, with government and the international sector added in an open economy.

If injections (investment, government spending and exports) are greater than withdrawals (saving, taxes and imports), national income rises, which is economic growth. If withdrawals exceed injections, national income falls.

The model explains short-run changes in national income well, because they follow changes in spending, but it says little about what increases an economy's productive capacity, so it only partly explains long-run growth. A conclusion is that it explains demand-led growth but not supply-side growth.

Based on May/June 2023 · 9708/23 · Q4(a) 8 marks With the help of a circular flow of income diagram, explain how equilibrium is determined in an open economy and consider whether a budget deficit or a trade deficit is more likely to cause lasting disequilibrium.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 3): an accurate diagram showing income flows between firms and households (1); correct inclusion of exports and imports (1); correct inclusion of government spending and taxation (1).
  • Analysis (up to 3): an explanation of injections (1), of leakages (1) and of how equilibrium is determined (1).
  • Evaluation (up to 2): consideration of how a budget deficit or a trade deficit leads to disequilibrium (1) and of which is likely to cause lasting disequilibrium (1), with a justified conclusion.
PlanDiagram with X, M, G, T. Equilibrium: J = W. Budget or trade deficit: which lasts? Judge.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

A diagram shows households and firms exchanging income and expenditure, with the government taking taxes and spending, and the international sector adding exports and removing imports.

Injections are investment, government spending and exports, and withdrawals are saving, taxes and imports. Equilibrium national income is where I + G + X = S + T + M, since if injections are larger income rises and if smaller it falls.

A budget deficit (G above T) adds more injections than withdrawals, which can be offset by private saving, while a trade deficit (M above X) is a net withdrawal that must be financed from abroad. A persistent trade deficit is probably more likely to cause lasting disequilibrium, because a country cannot borrow from abroad indefinitely, but a large budget deficit can also persist if the government can finance it.

Top tips

  • Draw both flows. Income from firms to households; expenditure back.
  • Name injections and withdrawals. I, G, X in; S, T, M out.
  • Equilibrium: J = W. I + G + X = S + T + M.
  • Say what happens when they differ. J greater: income rises. W greater: it falls.
  • Closed to open adds X and M. Show the new injection and the new leakage.
  • Use examples. A rise in exports raises national income.
  • Evaluate with the size of trade. More open economies are affected more.
