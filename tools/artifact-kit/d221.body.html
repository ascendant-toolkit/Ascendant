Individual and Market Demand

What demand is, why the curve slopes downwards, and how individual demands add to market demand. Syllabus 2.2.1.

The big idea

Demand is the quantity of a good or service that consumers are willing and able to buy at a given price in a given period. The law of demand: as the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls (and vice versa), ceteris paribus (other things unchanged). The demand curve therefore slopes downwards from left to right. Market demand is the sum of all individual demands.

Why the demand curve slopes downwards

  • Income effect: a lower price means consumers' money buys more, so they can afford more.
  • Substitution effect: a lower price makes the good cheaper compared with substitutes, so consumers switch to it.
  • Diminishing marginal utility: each extra unit gives less satisfaction, so consumers will only buy more if the price is lower.

The market demand curve is found by adding up the quantities every consumer wants at each price (adding horizontally). More consumers in the market means more demand at every price.

The demand diagram

  • Price on the vertical axis, quantity demanded on the horizontal axis.
  • A downward-sloping line labelled D.
  • Read it: at price P the quantity demanded is Q.

Demand is not the same as want: it must be backed by the ability to pay. A demand curve assumes everything except price is unchanged.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Sales and discounts

Shops cut prices in sales and sell more, an example of the law of demand.

Exam use: Price down, quantity demanded up.

Concert tickets

When ticket prices rise, fewer people buy; at lower prices venues sell out.

Exam use: Demand slopes downwards.

More consumers

A rise in a city's population raises market demand for housing and transport.

Exam use: Market demand.

Fuel prices

Higher petrol prices lead some drivers to switch to public transport or cycling.

Exam use: Substitution effect.

Top tips

  • Demand = willing AND able to pay. At a given price.
  • Price on the vertical axis. Label D, price, quantity.
  • Market demand = sum of individuals. Add horizontally.
  • Explain with substitution and income effects. Name them.
  • Say ceteris paribus. Other things unchanged.
