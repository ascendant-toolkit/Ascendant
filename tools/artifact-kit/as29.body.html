Income Elasticity of Demand

2.2.2: the sign and size of YED, normal and inferior goods, and its uses for businesses. Animated demand shifts, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

Income elasticity of demand (YED) measures how responsive demand is to a change in income: YED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in income. The sign classifies the good: positive for normal goods (demand rises as income rises) and negative for inferior goods (demand falls as income rises). The size shows how strongly: between 0 and 1 the good is a necessity (demand rises by a smaller percentage than income), and above 1 it is a luxury or superior good (demand rises by a bigger percentage). YED helps businesses forecast sales over the business cycle and during economic growth or recession, and plan which products to expand.

1 · Sign and size

YEDType of goodIncome rises 10%Example
NegativeInferior goodDemand fallsBasic own-brand goods, bus travel for some

Above +1Normal luxury (superior)Demand rises by more than 10%Foreign holidays, designer goods

2 · Demand curves and income

  • When income rises, the demand curve for a normal good shifts right, raising equilibrium price and quantity; for an inferior good it shifts left, lowering them.
  • When income falls, the effects are reversed: a normal good's demand shifts left, an inferior good's shifts right.
  • A good can change category: demand for a good may be a luxury at low incomes but a necessity or even inferior at high incomes.

3 · Why it matters

  • Planning: firms selling income-elastic goods (cars, holidays) are hit hard in a recession and gain most in a boom; those selling necessities are more stable.
  • Product mix: as an economy develops, demand shifts towards luxuries and services and away from inferior goods.
  • Government: shows how tax and benefit changes affect spending on different goods.
  • Limits: estimates may be inaccurate, and other factors (tastes, prices) change at the same time.
Exam tipUse the sign to classify the good and the size to say necessity or luxury. Give an example, and say that the classification may change with income, place and time.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Budget supermarkets in a recession

When incomes fall, some households switch from branded to own-brand goods. Demand for those own-brand goods rises as incomes fall, so they behave as inferior goods.

Exam use: an inferior good.

Foreign holidays

As incomes rise, spending on foreign holidays tends to rise by a larger percentage. Travel firms may hire more in a boom and cut back sharply in a recession.

Exam use: a luxury with high YED.

Basic foods

Bread and rice are bought whatever the income level, so demand rises only slightly as incomes grow. YED is positive but low.

Exam use: a necessity.

Cars in an expanding economy

Car makers watch income growth closely. High YED means sales could grow quickly in a boom and fall quickly in a downturn, so they may plan production carefully.

Exam use: using YED for business planning.

Based on Feb/March 2024 · 9708/22 · Q3(a) 8 marks With the help of a formula, explain the meaning of income elasticity of demand and consider the extent to which a rise in income will increase the consumption of all goods and services.
What the mark scheme wants

  • Analysis (up to 3): normal goods have a positive value (1); uses a diagram (an upward-sloping income demand curve) or the formula to show why a rise in income increases consumption of such goods (1), justified with an example (1).
  • Evaluation (up to 2): if the good is inferior, demand falls when income rises (1) as consumers switch to normal or luxury goods; the second mark is for a justified conclusion.
PlanDefine and formula. Normal: positive, demand rises. Inferior: negative, demand falls. Conclude: not all goods rise.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Income elasticity of demand measures how responsive demand is to a change in income. It equals the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in income. Normal goods have a positive YED and inferior goods a negative one.

For a normal good such as foreign holidays, a rise in income raises demand, because consumers can afford more of them, so a positive YED shows that consumption increases as incomes rise.

Not all goods will rise. For an inferior good such as a basic own-brand product, a rise in income may lead consumers to switch to better goods, so consumption falls. Overall, a rise in income increases the consumption of normal goods but not of all goods and services.

Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Income elasticity of demand equals the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in income. If its size is greater than one, demand is income elastic, and if it is between zero and one, it is income inelastic.

Demand rises at the same rate as income only if YED equals one. For a necessity, with a YED between zero and one, demand rises by a smaller percentage. For a luxury, with a YED above one, it rises by a larger percentage, and for an inferior good, with a negative YED, demand falls as income rises.

Demand will not always rise at the same rate as income. It is only likely for goods with a YED close to one, and the YED may change as incomes rise and tastes change.

Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Income elasticity of demand is the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in income. It is income elastic if its size is above one and inelastic if between zero and one.

A positive sign means a normal good. If the size is between zero and one, such as basic food, it is a necessity, and if it is above one, such as foreign holidays, it is a superior good or luxury. A negative sign means an inferior good, such as own-brand budget goods, as demand falls when income rises.

The classification may not be fully reliable. A good could be a luxury at low incomes and a necessity at high incomes, and it can differ between countries and over time as tastes change, so the classification is only as confident as the data and period allow.

Based on Oct/Nov 2022 · 9708/21 · Q2(b) 12 marks Discuss whether knowledge of price elasticity of demand or income elasticity of demand would be of greater use to a business that produces cars.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Analysis (up to 8): the usefulness of PED to a car producer (up to 4), such as predicting the effect of a price change on revenue, and of YED (up to 4), such as the effect of changes in income, planning for the future and shifting production between basic and more expensive cars.
  • Evaluation (up to 4): a judgement on whether PED or YED would be of greater use to a car business, with a conclusion (1 mark reserved).
PlanPED: price changes and revenue. YED: growth and recession, product mix. Judge: PED for pricing, YED for planning; which is greater depends on the aim.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Knowledge of PED lets a car producer predict how a price change may affect demand and revenue: if demand is elastic, a price cut may raise revenue, and if inelastic, a price rise may. Knowledge of YED shows how demand could change when incomes change, so the firm can plan production for a boom or recession and decide whether to shift towards more expensive models as incomes rise.

Both are useful, but since cars are expensive and demand is likely to be income elastic, YED may be of greater use for long-term planning. PED may be more useful for short-term pricing, so the greater use may depend on the firm's aim and on the accuracy of the estimates.

Based on Oct/Nov 2020 · 9708/21 · Q3(b) 12 marks Explain the implications of price elasticity of demand and income elasticity of demand for business decisions. Discuss which measure is likely to be more useful to a business.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Analysis (up to 8): the usefulness of PED to a business (up to 4), for example showing how a price change may affect revenue, with the price of an elastic good reduced and an inelastic good raised, though substitutes, definition of the product, share of income and time must be considered; and of YED (up to 4), showing how a change in income may affect revenue, though whether the good is normal or inferior and the proportion of income must be considered.
  • Evaluation (up to 4): a judgement on the relative extent to which PED and YED are useful, with a conclusion (1 mark reserved).
PlanPED: pricing and revenue. YED: forecast sales as incomes change. Limits for each. Judge which helps more given the business.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

PED helps a business decide whether to raise or cut prices to increase revenue, but it needs to take account of the availability of substitutes and the time period. YED helps a business forecast how sales may change as incomes change, so it can decide whether to expand or reduce production, but it must allow for whether the good is normal or inferior.

PED is likely to be more useful for short-term pricing decisions, while YED may be more useful for long-term planning, particularly in a growing economy. Which is more useful depends on the type of product and the firm's aims.
