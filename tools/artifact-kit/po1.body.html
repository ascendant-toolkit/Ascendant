Absolute and Relative Poverty

What absolute and relative poverty mean, how they are measured and how they differ. Syllabus 5.2.1.

The big idea

Absolute poverty is when people cannot afford the basic necessities of life: enough food, clean water, shelter, clothing and healthcare. It is measured against a fixed poverty line, such as the World Bank's international poverty line of a few dollars a day (adjusted for price differences between countries). Relative poverty is when people's income is too low compared with the rest of society for them to take part in the normal activities of that society. It depends on inequality, so it exists in rich countries too.

Measuring poverty

  • Poverty line: an income below which a person counts as poor. The international line is set by the World Bank.
  • Poverty rate: the percentage of the population below the poverty line.
  • Number in poverty = population × poverty rate.
Poverty rate = number of people in poverty ÷ total population × 100

Why the difference matters

  • Economic growth can lift people out of absolute poverty if the extra income reaches the poor.
  • But if the rich gain more than the poor, relative poverty may not fall, or may rise, even though absolute poverty falls.
  • Policies differ: absolute poverty needs higher incomes and basic services; relative poverty needs redistribution and a smaller gap.
  • Poverty has costs: poor health, less education, lower productivity, social problems and wasted resources.

Extreme poverty falling

The share of the world's people in extreme poverty has fallen sharply since 1990, helped by fast growth in China and India, although many people in sub-Saharan Africa remain in absolute poverty.

Exam use: Growth reducing absolute poverty.

Relative poverty in rich countries

In countries such as the UK and the US, millions of people are below the relative poverty line even though few are in absolute poverty. Hawaii is a past paper example, with about 9% of its population in poverty.

Exam use: Relative poverty in a high-income economy.

Setting the line

The World Bank has raised its international poverty line over the years to reflect higher prices; older papers used $1.90 a day, newer figures are higher.

Exam use: The line is a convention, not a fixed truth.

Homelessness

People sleeping on the streets of rich cities lack shelter, a basic necessity, so they are in absolute poverty even in a high-income country.

Exam use: Absolute poverty can exist in rich countries too.

Q4 Calculate 1 marks Guyana has a population of 0.8 million and a poverty rate of 12%. Calculate the number of people living in poverty. (May/June 2023, P22 style)

Q5 Explain 4 marks Explain why relative poverty may exist even in a high-income country.
ReminderRelative to average; inequality; participation in society.
Relative poverty is measured against the incomes of others in the same country. In a high-income country the average income is high, so people with low incomes may still be able to afford basic necessities but not what most others take for granted, such as housing in good areas or school trips. If the rich gain more than the poor, the gap widens, so relative poverty could remain or rise even when absolute poverty is low.
Q6 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how economic growth could affect absolute and relative poverty.
ReminderGrowth may lift people over the line; relative poverty depends on distribution.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Economic growth is a rise in real GDP and raises incomes. If the extra income reaches poor households, for example through more jobs and higher wages, more people may be able to afford basic necessities, so absolute poverty could fall. However, relative poverty depends on the gap between rich and poor. If higher earners gain more, the median income rises and the relative poverty line rises with it, so relative poverty could stay the same or increase. Governments may need policies such as progressive taxes and benefits to share the gains.

Top tips

  • Absolute = basic necessities, fixed line. Relative = compared with others in the country.
  • Say it can exist in rich countries. Relative poverty does, and so can absolute poverty among the homeless.
  • Growth helps absolute poverty more reliably. Relative poverty depends on distribution.
  • Use the formula. Poverty rate = number in poverty ÷ population × 100.
  • Name the line. International poverty line (World Bank) for absolute; percentage of median income for relative.
