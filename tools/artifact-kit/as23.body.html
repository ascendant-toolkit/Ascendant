Market Equilibrium and the Effects of Shifts

2.4.1 to 2.4.2: equilibrium and disequilibrium, excess supply and excess demand, single and combined shifts of demand and supply. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

Market equilibrium is the price and quantity where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, so the market clears and there is no tendency to change. If the price is above equilibrium there is excess supply (a surplus), so the price tends to fall; if it is below there is excess demand (a shortage), so the price tends to rise, and this is called disequilibrium until the market adjusts. A shift of demand or supply moves the market to a new equilibrium: a right shift of demand raises price and quantity, a left shift lowers both, a right shift of supply lowers price and raises quantity, a left shift raises price and lowers quantity. When both curves shift at once, one of price or quantity is certain and the other depends on the relative size of the shifts.

1 · Equilibrium and disequilibrium (2.4.1)

  • Equilibrium: Qd = Qs at the equilibrium price; no pressure to change while conditions stay the same. All who want to buy at that price can, and all firms can sell what they supply (the market clears).
  • Price above equilibrium: Qs > Qd, excess supply. Firms cut prices, so quantity demanded rises and quantity supplied falls, until the surplus is gone.
  • Price below equilibrium: Qd > Qs, excess demand. Buyers compete, so price rises, quantity supplied rises and quantity demanded falls.
  • Disequilibrium can arise from a shift in demand or supply, or from a government price control such as a maximum or minimum price.
  • Calculation: to find the equilibrium from equations, set Qd = Qs and solve for P, then put P into either equation to find Q.

2 · Effects of shifts (2.4.2)

  • Both shift together: demand and supply both up: quantity rises, price depends on which shift is bigger. Demand up and supply down: price rises, quantity depends. Demand down and supply up: price falls, quantity depends. Both down: quantity falls, price depends.
  • The size of the change in price and quantity depends on the elasticity of the curves: a shift of demand against an inelastic supply curve raises price a lot and quantity a little.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Concert tickets

Tickets priced below what people will pay sell out at once, a shortage. Resale sites show a price closer to equilibrium, because demand exceeds supply at the face price.

Exam use: excess demand and the tendency for price to rise.

Fruit at the end of a market day

Stall holders cut prices near closing time to clear unsold stock. A surplus at the daytime price pushes the price down until the quantity supplied matches what people will buy.

Exam use: excess supply and the fall in price.

Housing

More people moving to a city raises demand for homes. Supply is slow to respond, so prices rise a lot and quantity rises little: a shift of demand against an inelastic supply curve.

Exam use: elasticity and the size of price and quantity changes.

Coffee crop failure and a new trend

A frost cuts supply (left) just as a trend raises demand (right). Price rises for certain, but quantity depends on which shift is bigger.

Exam use: two shifts at once and which change is uncertain.

Based on Feb/March 2021 · 9708/22 · Q4(a) 8 marks Explain what is meant by equilibrium price and quantity in the market for a good and how price and quantity will be affected by both a rise in the wage rate paid to the workers producing the good and a rise in wages paid to all workers in the economy.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 2): equilibrium occurs where supply equals demand (1); there is no tendency to change (1).
  • Application (up to 3): a rise in wages for the producers' workers shifts supply to the left (1) because costs rise (1), so the equilibrium price rises and quantity falls (1).
  • Application (up to 3): a rise in wages across the economy shifts demand to the right, or to the left in the case of an inferior good (1), because incomes rise (1), with the effect on equilibrium price and quantity explained (1).
PlanDefine equilibrium. Wage rise for this industry: supply left, price up, quantity down. Wages across the economy: demand shifts too.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Equilibrium price and quantity occur where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, so there is no tendency for the price to change while conditions stay the same.

A rise in the wage rate paid to the workers producing the good raises costs, so supply shifts to the left, which raises the equilibrium price and lowers the equilibrium quantity. A rise in wages across the economy raises incomes, so demand for a normal good shifts to the right, which raises price and quantity. For an inferior good, demand could shift to the left instead, so price and quantity could fall.

Based on May/June 2023 · 9708/22 · Q3(a) 8 marks With the help of a diagram, explain what is meant by equilibrium in a market and consider the extent to which the equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity are likely to change for a product following an increase in wages for labour across the whole economy.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 3): the equilibrium point is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (1), with no tendency to change (1), shown on an accurately labelled diagram with price and quantity (1).
  • Analysis (up to 3): either a demand-side approach, with demand shifting right for a normal good or left for an inferior good, or a supply-side approach, with supply shifting left because of higher costs; the best response only is marked.
  • Evaluation (up to 2): the extent of the change depends on factors such as elasticity and whether the good is normal or inferior.
PlanDefine and draw. Pick one route: demand (income up) or supply (costs up). Evaluate with elasticity and good type.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Equilibrium is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, giving the equilibrium price and quantity, with no tendency to change. The diagram shows demand and supply crossing at P1 and Q1.

A rise in wages across the economy may raise incomes, so demand for a normal good shifts to the right and the equilibrium price and quantity rise. Higher wages could also raise firms' costs, which would shift supply to the left and raise the price, but lower the quantity.

The extent depends on the good: for an inferior good demand shifts left, and for a labour-intensive product the supply effect could be larger. The size of the price rise also depends on the price elasticity of demand and supply.

Based on Oct/Nov 2020 · 9708/23 · Q2(a) 8 marks Using diagrams, explain what might cause a market to be in disequilibrium and how market forces would lead to equilibrium being restored.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 2): a diagram showing equilibrium (1) with a clear statement of what equilibrium means (1).
  • Application (up to 4): a diagram showing disequilibrium and why it might arise, with a shift in demand or supply (1) and a cause of the shift (1); the excess demand or excess supply (1); and the new equilibrium price and quantity (1).
  • Application (up to 2): an explanation of how market forces restore equilibrium. Maximum or minimum prices are accepted as a cause of disequilibrium.
PlanEquilibrium diagram. Shift one curve (with a cause). Excess demand or supply. Price adjusts to a new equilibrium.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

A market is in equilibrium when, given present conditions, there is no tendency for change: demand equals supply at the equilibrium price and quantity.

Disequilibrium could arise if a drought shifts the supply of wheat to the left. At the old price quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied, so there is excess demand. Buyers bid the price up, which contracts demand and extends supply, until the market clears at a higher price and a lower quantity. The same process works in reverse for a surplus, with price falling.

Top tips

  • Define equilibrium properly. Qd = Qs and no tendency to change. Say the market clears.
  • Say what is certain. For two shifts, one of price and quantity is certain.
  • Name surplus and shortage correctly. Above equilibrium: excess supply. Below: excess demand.
  • Draw before and after. Label old and new curves and the new equilibrium price and quantity.
  • Calculate with equations. Set Qd = Qs, solve for P, then find Q.
  • Bring in elasticity. Steep curves mean bigger price changes.
  • Use the sequence. Shift, disequilibrium, price change, new equilibrium.
