Causes of Current Account Deficits and Surpluses

Why a country may run a current account deficit or surplus: competitiveness, demand, prices, exchange rates and income flows. Syllabus 6.4.2.

The big idea

A current account deficit arises when a country spends more on imports and payments abroad than it earns from exports and receipts. A surplus is the reverse. The main causes are differences in competitiveness (prices, quality, productivity, exchange rate), demand at home and abroad (incomes, growth), the prices of key exports and imports (such as oil), and flows of income and transfers. Always link a cause to exports, imports or income flows.

Causes of a current account deficit

CauseHow it leads to a deficit
Rising incomes and spending at homePeople buy more goods, including imports, so imports rise faster than exports
Low price and non-price competitivenessHigher costs and inflation than rivals, low productivity, poor quality or limited variety make exports less attractive and imports more attractive
A high exchange rateExports dearer abroad, imports cheaper
Weak demand abroadRecession in trading partners cuts demand for exports
Falling prices of main exports or rising import pricesExport revenue falls; import bill (for example oil) rises
Dependence on importsFew domestic alternatives for oil, food or machinery
Primary income deficitsProfits and interest paid abroad (to foreign firms and lenders) exceed income received
Secondary income outflowsAid given and remittances sent abroad exceed receipts

Causes of a current account surplus

CauseHow it leads to a surplus
Strong competitivenessLow costs, high productivity, advanced technology, high quality: exports strong, fewer imports
A low or undervalued exchange rateExports cheap abroad, imports dear
High demand abroad, high export pricesMore export revenue (for example oil exporters when oil prices are high)
Low domestic spending or high savingFewer imports
Primary and secondary income inflowsLarge overseas investments, remittances from workers abroad
ProtectionTariffs and quotas reduce imports

Examples of specific links

  • Oil price and the UAE: an oil exporter's export revenue rises when the oil price rises, so its current account moves towards surplus; when the price falls it moves towards deficit.
  • Inflation and the current account: higher inflation than competitors makes exports dearer, so a higher inflation rate tends to go with a worse current account.
  • Technology: advances that raise productivity and quality make exports more competitive and imports less necessary.
  • Economic growth: fast growth raises incomes and imports, which can worsen the current account (a conflict of aims).

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

China and Germany's surpluses

China and Germany run large current account surpluses, driven by strong, competitive manufactured exports and relatively high saving.

Exam use: Competitiveness and saving as causes of a surplus.

The US deficit

The United States has run a large current account deficit for decades, because Americans spend more on imports than the country earns from exports.

Exam use: High spending causing a deficit.

Oil exporters

Saudi Arabia and the UAE move between surplus and deficit as oil prices change.

Exam use: Export prices and the current account.

Greece before 2010

Greece ran a large current account deficit in the 2000s, with fast-growing imports and high costs that made exports uncompetitive.

Exam use: A deficit from low competitiveness and strong demand.

Remittance-dependent economies

Countries such as Nepal and the Philippines receive large remittances, which offset trade deficits.

Exam use: Secondary income inflows.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of a current account deficit.
ReminderTwo different causes, each linked to exports, imports or income flows.
One cause is rising incomes at home. People spend more, including on imports, so imports could rise faster than exports. Another is a lack of competitiveness. If costs and prices are higher than rivals' or quality is lower, exports are less attractive and imports more attractive, so the deficit widens.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain the relationship between the oil price and the UAE's current account balance. (Oct/Nov 2024, P21)
ReminderOil is a major export: price up, export revenue up, surplus; price down, revenue down, smaller surplus or deficit.
The UAE exports a lot of oil, so oil is a large part of its export revenue. When the oil price rises, export revenue rises, so the current account surplus widens (or deficit narrows). When the oil price falls, export revenue falls, so the surplus shrinks or a deficit may appear. There is therefore a positive relationship between the oil price and the current account balance.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how advances in technology may improve the current account balance of a country. (Feb/Mar 2023, P22)
ReminderProductivity, quality, lower costs, new products; imports substituted.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Advances in technology can raise productivity and lower costs of production. Firms can cut prices or improve quality, so exports become more competitive and demand for them could rise, increasing export revenue. New products and better quality may also make domestic goods more attractive than imports, reducing import spending. Technology exports, such as software, may become a new source of services income. The effect depends on how quickly firms adopt technology and on competitors' progress.
Q4 Analyse 5 marks Analyse the relationship between inflation rates and current account balances. (May/June 2022, P21 style)
ReminderUse data: typically higher inflation goes with a worse balance. Explain via competitiveness.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Generally countries with higher inflation rates tend to have current account deficits, whereas countries with low inflation tend to have surpluses. For example, a country with 10% inflation may have a deficit of 4% of GDP and one with 1% inflation a surplus of 3%. Higher inflation makes a country's exports dearer than rivals', so export demand falls, and imports become cheaper in comparison, so they rise. There are exceptions, for example an oil exporter with high inflation but a surplus because of high oil revenue.
Q5 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how a country's current account deficit might be reduced if its firms become more competitive. (Oct/Nov 2020, P21)
ReminderCompetitiveness: price, quality, productivity; exports rise, imports fall; link to the deficit.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
If firms become more competitive their products are cheaper or better. Foreign buyers are more likely to buy them, so demand for exports and export revenue rise. Domestic consumers may switch from imports to home-produced goods, reducing import spending. Higher exports and lower imports narrow the trade in goods deficit and so the current account deficit. However, it may take time to improve competitiveness and rivals may improve too.

Top tips

  • Causes of a deficit: high home spending, low competitiveness, high exchange rate, weak demand abroad, falling export prices, income outflows.
  • Link each cause to exports, imports or income. Do not just name it.
  • Know examples. Oil price and oil exporters; inflation and competitiveness; technology.
  • Surplus causes are the reverse. Plus remittances and investment income.
  • Evaluate with time lags and elasticity. Competitiveness takes years to build.
