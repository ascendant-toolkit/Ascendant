Consumption, Saving, Investment and the Accelerator

The consumption function, the MPC and APC, what shifts consumption and saving, what determines investment, and the accelerator principle. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

Consumption is the largest part of aggregate demand and depends mainly on disposable income: the consumption function is C = a + cY, where a is autonomous consumption and c is the marginal propensity to consume (MPC). What is not consumed is saved, so S = Y − C and MPC + MPS = 1. Consumption and saving also depend on wealth, interest rates, confidence, credit and taxes, while investment depends on interest rates, expected profit and confidence, and on the accelerator principle: investment depends on the rate of change of national income. Because investment is an injection that is also volatile, it is a main cause of swings in the economy.

1 · The consumption function

C = a + cY  ·  S = Y − C  ·  MPC = ΔC ÷ ΔY  ·  APC = C ÷ Y

With a = 100 and c = 0.75, a household with income $400 consumes 100 + 0.75 × 400 = $400, so it saves nothing (break-even income). At income $200 it consumes $250 (it dissaves, using savings or borrowing); at income $800 it consumes $700 and saves $100.

The average propensity to consume falls as income rises, while the MPC stays constant on a straight-line function. A change in income moves you along the function; a change in anything else (wealth, confidence, interest rates) shifts it.

3 · Investment and the accelerator

Investment (spending on capital goods) depends on the rate of interest (the cost of borrowing), expected profit and business confidence, the amount of spare capacity, technology and government policy. The accelerator principle says that investment depends on the rate of change of national income, not its level: I = v × ΔY, where v is the accelerator (the capital-output ratio).

The key insightInvestment falls in year 4 even though income is still rising, because income is growing more slowly. That makes investment, and so aggregate demand, unstable. A slowdown in the growth of income can cut investment sharply, and a stronger recovery can lift it by more than the rise in income.

Evaluating the accelerator

  • It assumes firms are already at full capacity. With spare capacity, a rise in demand can be met without new investment.
  • It assumes a constant capital-output ratio, and that firms can get the capital goods and finance.
  • Expectations and interest rates also matter: firms may invest because they expect growth, or hold back despite growth if confidence is low.
  • Time lags: orders for machinery follow changes in income after a delay.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

A housing boom

When house prices rise, owners feel wealthier and may spend more, and may borrow against the value of their homes. Consumption rises even with no change in current income: the consumption function shifts up.

Exam use: the wealth effect as a shift of the consumption function.

Car makers during a slowdown

If sales of cars are still rising but more slowly, car makers may stop expanding their factories. Orders for machines and robots fall even though the market has not shrunk, which hits the firms that make capital equipment hardest.

Exam use: the accelerator: investment depends on the rate of change of income.

Interest rate cuts and business confidence

A central bank cuts rates to encourage investment. If firms expect weak demand, they may leave projects on hold, so investment responds little.

Exam use: interest-inelastic investment and the role of confidence.

Oct/Nov 2021 · 9708/41 · Q4(a) 12 marks Explain what economists mean by the marginal propensity to consume and consider the importance of this concept in relation to government macroeconomic policy.
What the mark scheme wants
  • The MPC: the proportion of an extra unit of income that is spent (the change in consumption divided by the change in income), with the link to the MPS.
  • Its importance for the government's macroeconomic aims: the MPC determines the size of the multiplier, so it affects how far fiscal changes (or tax cuts) change income, output, employment and inflation, and how much is needed to close a gap.
  • Developed analysis relates the MPC to each of several policy goals, with comment on its limits (it may change, and varies between groups).
Oct/Nov 2021 · 9708/41 · Q4(b) 13 marks Discuss what determines the level of savings in an economy and assess the impact of an increase in the level of savings upon economic growth and employment.
Plan
  • Determinants: income and its distribution, interest rates, confidence and expectations, wealth, age structure, access to credit, tax treatment of saving.
  • Impact: a rise in saving is a withdrawal, so if investment does not rise to match it, injections are below withdrawals and income, growth and employment may fall (the paradox of thrift). If savings fund investment, capacity and long-run growth can rise.
  • Evaluate: depends on whether savings are lent to firms and on confidence, on the interest rate, on the state of the economy (recession or boom), and on time.

B · Practice question with a model answer

Practice (not a past paper question) 8 marks Explain the accelerator principle and why it makes investment unstable.
PlanState the principle. Give a numerical example showing investment falling while income rises. Link to instability. One evaluative point.
KANE

The accelerator principle states that the level of investment depends on the rate of change of national income, not its level. If the capital-output ratio is 2, investment in new capital equals twice the change in income.

For example, if national income rises by $10m in one year and by $15m the next, investment would be $20m and then $30m. If income then rises by only $10m, investment falls to $20m even though national income is still rising. Because a small change in the growth of income causes a larger change in investment, and investment is an injection, aggregate demand can swing sharply, which may amplify booms and recessions. The effect could be weaker if firms have spare capacity, if the capital-output ratio is not constant, or if confidence and interest rates matter more than current growth.

Top tips

  • Define MPC as a change: extra consumption divided by extra income. Do not confuse it with the average propensity to consume (C ÷ Y).
  • Separate movements from shifts: a change in income moves you along the consumption function; wealth, confidence, interest rates and taxes shift it.
  • Remember MPC + MPS = 1 in a simple model, and use it to find the multiplier.
  • For the accelerator, talk about the rate of change of income. Investment can fall while income is still rising.
  • Use the numbers. A short example with income, change in income and investment gains application marks.
  • Evaluate the accelerator with spare capacity, constant capital-output ratio, confidence and interest rates.
