Start here: market failure first, then the policy

Every policy in section 8.1 answers a named market failure. In the exam, the failure comes first and you choose the tool. Keep this map in mind through all four parts.

Market failureWhat goes wrongPolicies that fitWhere in 8.1
Negative externality (pollution, smoking)Social cost is above private cost, so too much is produced or consumedIndirect tax, tradable permits, regulation, quotas, bans, minimum price, informationParts 1 to 3
Positive externality, merit good (education, vaccines)Social benefit is above private benefit, so too little is produced or consumedSubsidy, direct provision, information and nudges, compulsionParts 2 and 3
Public good (street lighting, defence)Non-rival and non-excludable, so free riders and no private supplyDirect provision paid for by taxationPart 3
Imperfect informationChoices made without knowing the quality or riskLabels and ratings, licences, regulation, nudgesParts 3 and 4
Monopoly powerPrice above cost and output below the efficient levelPrice caps, competition policy, deregulation, privatisation, nationalisationPart 3
Inequity, instability of pricesThe market outcome is seen as unfair or too volatile for producers or buyersMaximum and minimum prices, taxes and subsidiesPart 1
