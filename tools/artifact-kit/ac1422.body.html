Packet Switching and Routers

Coursebook: Chapter 14 · Communication and internet technologies, section 14.2 Circuit switching, packet switching

How a message is divided into packets and routed independently, what a router does, benefits and drawbacks, and packet against circuit switching; animations, a packet network simulator and the real 9618 questions from 2021 to 2025.

This page in five sentences

Packet switching splits a message into small packets, each with a header holding the source and destination IP addresses and a sequence number, and sends every packet independently, so different packets may take different routes and arrive out of order. A router at each step reads the destination IP address in the header, looks it up in its routing table, and sends the packet on its next hop. At the destination the packets are reassembled in the right order using the sequence numbers, and missing or damaged packets are re-sent. Because the bandwidth is shared and packets can be re-routed, packet switching suits data networks such as the internet, though reassembly takes time. You need to be able to describe it, state benefits and drawbacks, describe a router, and compare it with circuit switching.

1 · The packet

HeaderPayload
source IPdestination IPsequence numberhop counta small piece of the message
  • The message is divided into equal-sized packets.
  • The header holds key information: the source and destination IP addresses, the packet (sequence) number and a hop count. The payload is the data.

2 · How packet switching passes a message across a network

  1. The message is divided into packets.
  2. Each packet is given a header with the source and destination IP addresses and a sequence number.
  3. Each packet is sent independently, and may travel by a different route. Routers choose the optimum route at the time, depending on congestion and availability, and re-route if a route is unavailable.
  4. The packets may arrive out of order.
  5. At the destination the packets are reassembled into the original message, in the correct order, using the sequence numbers.
  6. If a packet is missing or damaged a re-send request is made and it is sent again; if a packet's hop count runs out it is discarded and a re-send can be requested.
Complete message receivedTwo ways packet switching makes sure a complete message arrives: packets are checked on arrival and a re-send is requested for any that are damaged or missing; and packets are routed individually, so if a route is blocked a packet is sent another way.

3 · The router

A router connects networks and passes packets on towards their destination. When a packet arrives:

  1. It reads the destination IP address from the packet header.
  2. It looks in its routing table, which holds information such as the available hops, the netmask, the gateway to use, adjacent routers and the status of routes.
  3. It decides the next hop (the best route) and sends the packet on.
  4. It reduces the hop count by 1 each time. When it reaches 0 the packet is discarded.

4 · Benefits and drawbacks

BenefitsDrawbacks
Packets can be re-routed around faulty or busy lines, so they are more likely to arriveDelay: packets must be reordered and reassembled at the destination
A lost or damaged packet can be re-sent on its ownDelay from packets that must be re-sent, and from sharing bandwidth with other traffic

6 · Common ways to lose marks

  • Not saying each packet is sent independently and may take a different route.
  • Forgetting the header and what is in it: the IP addresses and sequence number.
  • Describing a router as "something that connects computers". Say it reads the destination IP address, uses a routing table and sends the packet on its next hop.
  • For benefits and drawbacks, giving "it is faster" or "it is slower" without a reason.
  • In a comparison, describing each method separately without saying what is different.
  • Describing checking for completeness and re-sending when the question says to leave it out.

6 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Loading a web page

The page is sent as many packets. They may reach your device by different routes and out of order, and your computer puts them back in order. If one is lost, only that packet is sent again.

Exam use: packets sent independently, reassembled at the destination, re-sent if missing.

A router in your home

Your home router reads the destination address of every packet from your devices and passes it to your internet provider's router, which passes it on, hop by hop.

Exam use: a router reads the destination IP address and uses a routing table to choose the next hop.

A cable cut under the sea

Internet traffic is re-routed along other cables, and users may only notice a slight slowdown, because there is no fixed circuit.

Exam use: packets can be re-routed around a failed route.

Sending a large photo by email

The photo is split into many packets that share the lines with everyone else's traffic. It arrives complete even though the packets travelled separately.

Exam use: bandwidth is shared; packets are reassembled.

A video call that stutters

Packets arriving late or out of order cause delays and glitches, which is why live calls are harder on a packet-switched network than a dedicated circuit.

Exam use: a drawback: delay while packets are reordered or re-sent.

Benefits, drawbacks and comparing methods

M/J 2021 · 9618/31, 32, 33 · Q6 Give 4 marks Give two benefits and two drawbacks of packet switching.
ReminderTwo benefits and two drawbacks.

Benefits (max 2): Accuracy: delivery of the message is accurate Completeness: missing packets are detected and a re-send is requested Resilience: if the network changes, data can be sent another way The path is also available to other users: the bandwidth is not all used / simultaneous use by many users Better security: packets are sent by different routes Drawbacks (max 2): Time delays to correct errors, or from errors in packets Requires complex protocols for delivery Unsuitable for real-time transmission applications

M/J 2022 · 9618/31, 33 · Q3 Describe 8 marks Data can be sent over networks using either circuit switching or packet switching. Describe both methods of data transmission. Include a different advantage and disadvantage for each method.
ReminderFour marks for each method: two description points, an advantage, a disadvantage.

Circuit switching (max 4): a dedicated circuit the circuit is established before transmission starts and released after data is transferred using the whole bandwidth all data is transferred over the same route Advantage: data / frames arrive in order and do not need to be reassembled Disadvantage: nobody else can use the circuit even if it is idle / less secure as only one route is used

Packet switching (max 4): data is split into packets each packet is given its own route the routing for a packet depends on the congestion packets may not arrive in the order sent Advantage: packets can be rerouted if there are problems / more secure as harder to intercept Disadvantage: time taken to reassemble packets at the destination

2. Review questions

Written for practice and revision, not taken from past papers. Use them to check your understanding before attempting the real questions above.

Describe 3 marks A 1200-byte message is sent by packet switching in packets of 400 bytes. Describe what is added to each packet and what happens at the destination.
ReminderHeader, order, reassembly.

The message is split into 3 packets; each has a header with the source and destination IP addresses and a sequence number. The packets may arrive by different routes and out of order. At the destination they are put in order using the sequence numbers and reassembled into the message.

Explain 2 marks Explain why packet switching is more resilient than circuit switching when a cable is cut.
ReminderAlternative routes.

Each packet is routed independently, so routers can send packets by another route around the fault. In circuit switching all the data follows one route, so the transmission ends.

Describe 3 marks A packet arrives at a router with destination IP address 172.16.4.9. Describe what the router does.
ReminderTable, next hop, hop count.

It reads the destination IP address from the packet header. It looks the address up in its routing table to find the next hop. It reduces the hop count by 1 and sends the packet on to the next router.

State 2 marks State two drawbacks of packet switching for a live video call.
ReminderDelay and order.

Packets may arrive late or out of order and have to be reordered, causing delay. Lost packets have to be re-sent, which causes more delay.

Explain 2 marks Explain why a packet has a hop count.
ReminderWhat if it goes round in circles?

The hop count is reduced each time the packet passes a router. When it reaches zero the packet is discarded, so a packet cannot travel round the network forever.

Top tips

  • Packets, header, independent. Divided into packets; a header with IP addresses and a sequence number; each sent independently.
  • Different routes, any order. Packets may arrive out of order, so they are reassembled.
  • Router: header, table, next hop. Reads the destination IP, checks the routing table, sends it on its next hop, reduces the hop count.
  • Benefits with reasons. Re-routing, resending single packets, shared bandwidth, security by different routes.
  • Drawbacks with reasons. Delay reordering or resending, complex protocols, not for real time.
  • For comparisons, put both in each point. 'Circuit switching ..., whereas packet switching ...'.
