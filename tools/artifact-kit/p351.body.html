Demand for Factors of Production

What influences firms' demand for labour, capital and land: product demand, factor prices, availability and productivity. Syllabus 3.5.1.

The big idea

The demand for a factor of production (labour, capital, land) is a derived demand: firms want factors because of the goods and services they help produce. So the demand for factors depends on the demand for the product, the price of the factor and its substitutes, the availability of the factor and its productivity.

Influences on the demand for factors

InfluenceEffect on demand for the factorExample
Demand for the productHigher product demand raises demand for the factors needed to make itMore demand for electric cars raises demand for battery workers and lithium
Price of the factorA higher price cuts the quantity demanded; a fall in price raises itDearer labour makes firms hire fewer workers
Price of other (substitute) factorsIf one factor becomes dearer, firms may switch to a cheaper substituteHigher wages may lead firms to buy robots
AvailabilityScarce factors are dearer and used less; abundant factors are used moreA shortage of skilled engineers limits hiring
ProductivityMore productive factors give more output per $ spent, so demand is higherFirms demand more skilled, well-trained workers
  • Factors can be substitutes (labour and machines) or complements (workers and the tools they use).
  • A factor's marginal revenue product (the extra revenue an extra unit brings) sets how much a firm will pay.

The word to remember is derived: demand for factors comes from demand for the final product.

Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Q1 Define 2 marks Define derived demand.
ReminderDemand for a factor from demand for the product.
Derived demand is demand for a factor of production that arises from the demand for the product it helps to make.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two influences on the demand for labour.
ReminderProduct demand, wage, productivity, substitutes.
The demand for the firm's product: if it rises, more workers are needed. Labour demand shifts right. The price of labour: a higher wage reduces the quantity of labour demanded as firms switch to machines or cut output.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how an increase in the productivity of workers may affect a firm's demand for labour.
ReminderMore output per worker; higher MRP; may hire more or fewer.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Higher productivity means each worker produces more. The extra revenue from each worker (MRP) rises, so labour is more valuable to the firm. The firm may demand more workers and pay higher wages. If product demand is limited, the firm may need fewer workers to produce the same output.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether a rise in wages will always reduce the demand for labour.
ReminderSubstitutes, product demand, productivity, elasticity. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A higher wage raises costs, so firms may hire fewer workers or replace them with machines. However, if the rise is matched by higher productivity, labour may still be good value. If product demand is strong, firms may keep hiring. Overall, a wage rise is likely to reduce the quantity of labour demanded, but not always, because it depends on productivity, product demand and the availability of substitutes.

Top tips

  • Derived demand: from the product. Say it.
  • Five influences. Product demand, factor price, substitutes, availability, productivity.
  • Link to a firm. Wages, machines, output.
  • Evaluate: productivity. Higher wages can be justified.
