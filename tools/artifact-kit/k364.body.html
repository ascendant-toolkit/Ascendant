Calculation of Revenue

Calculating TR and AR and how sales influence revenue. Syllabus 3.6.4.

The big idea

Revenue is calculated with TR = price × quantity and AR = TR ÷ quantity. The influence of sales on revenue is direct: for a given price, more sales mean more revenue. But to sell more a firm may need to lower its price, so what happens to revenue depends on the elasticity of demand and on costs.

The influence of sales on revenue

  • If the price is constant, a rise in sales raises TR in proportion: 10% more units means 10% more revenue.
  • If a firm cuts its price to sell more, revenue rises only if the quantity rises by a bigger percentage than the price falls (elastic demand).
  • Sales can be increased by advertising, better quality, wider distribution or a shift in demand, which raises revenue at every price.
  • Revenue is not the same as profit: more sales at a lower price may increase revenue but not profit.

Q1 Calculate 3 marks A firm cuts its price from $8 to $6 and sales rise from 40 to 60. Calculate the change in total revenue.
Reminder320 → 360.
Old TR = 8 × 40 = $320. New TR = 6 × 60 = $360. TR rises by $40.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain how an increase in sales may affect a firm's revenue.
ReminderHigher quantity at same price raises TR; price cuts need elasticity.
Total revenue is price multiplied by quantity sold. If price stays the same, more sales raise revenue. If the firm cut its price to sell more, revenue rises only if the percentage rise in quantity exceeds the percentage fall in price.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how advertising may influence a firm's revenue.
ReminderDemand right; sales up; costs.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Advertising aims to change tastes and raise demand for the product. More units are sold at each price, so total revenue rises. It may also allow the firm to raise its price. However, advertising costs money, so profit may not rise as much as revenue.
Q4 Discuss 6 marks Discuss whether a firm should cut its prices to raise revenue.
ReminderPED, costs, rivals. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A price cut raises sales, but revenue only rises if demand is elastic. If demand is inelastic, revenue falls. Rivals may also cut prices, cancelling the gain. Costs also matter: extra sales may raise costs and lower profit. Overall, it depends on elasticity and costs.

Top tips

  • TR = P × Q; AR = TR ÷ Q. Show working.
  • Price constant: sales up, revenue up. Say it.
  • Link price cuts to PED. Elastic or inelastic.
  • Revenue is not profit. Mention costs in evaluation.
