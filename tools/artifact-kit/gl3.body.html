The Role of Multinational Companies

What MNCs are, why they locate abroad, and their advantages and disadvantages for host and home countries. Syllabus 6.2.3.

The big idea

A multinational company (MNC) is a firm that operates (produces or sells) in more than one country. Its head office is in its home country; its factories, shops and offices abroad are in host countries. MNCs bring jobs, investment and technology to host countries but may also send profits abroad and out-compete local firms. Home countries gain profits and tax revenue but may lose jobs when production moves abroad.

Why MNCs locate abroad

  • Lower costs: cheaper labour, land, raw materials; lower taxes (a 'tax haven' may have low or no corporation tax).
  • Access to markets: large or growing consumer markets; to avoid import tariffs by producing inside the country.
  • Skilled workers and good infrastructure (roads, ports, power, internet).
  • Natural resources close to the source.
  • Political stability, government incentives and grants.
  • Spreading risk across several countries.

Few MNCs may choose a region with poor infrastructure, political instability, corruption, a small market, a shortage of skills or high taxes.

Advantages and disadvantages for host countries

Advantages

  • Jobs and higher wages; fewer unemployed
  • Investment in capital and infrastructure (FDI)
  • Technology and skills transferred through training
  • Tax revenue for the government
  • Exports may rise, improving the current account; more choice, competition and lower prices
  • Higher output and productivity, economic growth; may reduce poverty

Disadvantages

  • Profits sent back to the home country
  • Local firms may be driven out by competition
  • Low wages, poor working conditions or exploitation of workers
  • Pollution and depletion of resources
  • Tax avoidance; MNCs may move away when costs rise ('footloose'), leaving unemployment
  • Dependence on the MNC; best-paid jobs may stay at head office

Advantages and disadvantages for home countries

Advantages

  • Profits returned raise income, tax revenue and the current account
  • Lower-cost production abroad can mean lower prices at home
  • Head-office and research jobs
  • Bigger markets and higher revenue for the firm

Disadvantages

  • Jobs lost at home when production moves abroad
  • Investment goes abroad instead of at home
  • Possible tax avoidance by moving profits abroad
  • Less control over standards in other countries; reputation risk

Do MNCs reduce poverty?

They could, by creating jobs and raising wages, training workers and paying taxes that fund services. They may not if wages stay very low, profits leave, or the benefits go to a small group.

Q1 Identify 2 marks State two reasons why a foreign MNC may want to operate in Bermuda. (Feb/Mar 2022, P22)
ReminderLow taxes, high GDP per head (market), tariffs, skilled workers, low inflation.
Low or no corporation tax (a tax haven). A high GDP per head (strong market) or skilled, educated workers.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two benefits that an MNC can bring to its host country. (Feb/Mar 2023, P22)
ReminderJobs, investment, technology, tax revenue, exports; each with a link.
An MNC creates jobs. It employs local workers and pays wages, so unemployment falls and incomes and spending rise. It brings investment and technology. Workers learn new skills and local firms may copy new methods, raising productivity and growth.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain two benefits of MNCs to their home countries. (Oct/Nov 2022, P23)
ReminderProfits returned, tax revenue, lower prices, head-office jobs. Make sure it is the home country.
Profits earned abroad can be returned to the home country, raising income and tax revenue and improving the current account. Producing abroad at lower cost may reduce the price of goods at home, so home consumers could pay less.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse why few MNCs may choose to locate in a particular region. (May/June 2022, P23)
ReminderPoor infrastructure, instability, small market, skills shortage, taxes, corruption.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
MNCs choose locations that give low costs and access to markets. A region with poor infrastructure, such as roads and power, makes production and transport costly. Political instability or corruption increases the risk of losing investment. A small market or low incomes means few customers, and a shortage of skilled workers raises training costs. High taxes or tariffs on imports of inputs reduce profit. Governments could attract MNCs with incentives and by investing in education and infrastructure.
Q5 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how a MNC could reduce poverty in a host country. (May/June 2022, P22)
ReminderJobs and wages, tax revenue to fund services, training, local suppliers, exports. Add a limit.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
An MNC creates jobs and pays wages. Households that were unemployed or in low-paid work earn more, so they can afford basic necessities. The MNC pays tax, giving the government revenue to spend on education and healthcare. It trains workers and buys from local suppliers, creating more jobs and higher productivity. However, if wages are very low or profits leave the country, the effect on poverty may be small.
Q6 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether or not MNCs always benefit their host countries. (May/June 2020, P23)
Reminder'Always' invites 'not always'. Benefits and costs; depends on wages, tax, local firms.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
An MNC operates in several countries; the host country is where it has factories or shops. MNCs could benefit host countries by creating jobs, bringing investment and technology, paying taxes and raising exports. Competition may also lower prices and improve quality for consumers. These benefits are greater when MNCs buy from local suppliers and train workers. However, host countries may lose out if profits are sent abroad, workers receive low wages in poor conditions, local firms cannot compete and close, or MNCs pollute. MNCs can also move away when costs rise, leaving unemployment. Overall, MNCs usually benefit host countries but not always: it depends on how much tax they pay, whether profits are reinvested and how well the government regulates them.
Q7 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether or not MNCs increase production and productivity in their host countries. (May/June 2020, P21)
ReminderFor: investment, technology, skills, competition. Against: local firms close, profits leave, low-skilled jobs, may import inputs.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Production is the amount of output and productivity is output per worker. MNCs can raise production by investing in factories and machinery and employing more workers, and their technology and training could raise workers' skills and productivity. Competition from MNCs may force local firms to become more efficient. This is more likely if skills and technology spread to local firms. However, MNCs could drive local firms out of business, reducing local production, and may use mainly imported inputs and low-skill jobs, so productivity gains may be limited. Overall, MNCs are likely to increase production, but productivity gains depend on training, links with local firms and the type of work.
Q8 Discuss 6 marks Discuss whether or not foreign MNCs reduce poverty in their host countries. (Feb/Mar 2025, P22)
ReminderJobs and wages; tax; training; against: low wages; profits sent abroad; displaced local firms.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Poverty is when people cannot afford basic necessities or take part in society. MNCs may reduce poverty by creating jobs and paying higher wages than local firms, so households can afford more, and by paying taxes used for services. They can train workers and give local suppliers orders. The effect is larger where MNCs employ many local people at fair wages. However, wages may be low and conditions poor, profits may be sent abroad, and MNCs may close local firms, raising unemployment. Overall, MNCs are likely to reduce poverty if they employ locals and the government taxes and regulates them well, but the benefit could be small if most gains leave the country.

Top tips

  • Host or home? Check which country the question asks about: a common error.
  • Benefits: jobs, investment, technology, tax, exports. Link each with a result.
  • Costs: profits abroad, local firms, wages, pollution, footloose. Give at least one.
  • Why MNCs locate: costs, markets, skills, stability. And avoid tariffs by producing inside.
  • 'Always' means 'not always'. It depends on tax, wages and local links.
