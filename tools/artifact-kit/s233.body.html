Shifts of a Supply Curve

Causes of increases and decreases in supply and how they are shown on the diagram. Syllabus 2.3.3.

The big idea

A change in any factor other than the good's own price shifts the whole supply curve. A shift to the right is an increase in supply (more is supplied at every price). A shift to the left is a decrease in supply (less at every price).

Causes of a shift

FactorIncrease in supply (right)Decrease in supply (left)
Costs of production (wages, raw materials, energy, rent)Costs fallCosts rise
TechnologyNew technology raises productivity and cuts costs(Rarely: loss of technology)
Taxes and subsidiesA subsidy or a cut in taxes on producersA tax on producers (such as VAT or an excise tax)
Weather and natural disastersGood harvest weatherDrought, flood, disease (for agriculture)
Number of firmsNew firms enter the marketFirms leave the market
Prices of other goods (producers' alternatives)The price of a good they could produce instead fallsThe price of an alternative rises, so resources switch
Government regulationFewer regulationsNew environmental or safety regulations raise costs
ExpectationsPrices expected to fall in future: sell nowPrices expected to rise: hold back stock

Key terms

  • Subsidy: a payment by the government to producers to lower their costs: increases supply.
  • Indirect tax: a tax on goods and services that raises producers' costs: decreases supply.
  • Productivity: output per worker; technology raises it and lowers cost per unit.

On a diagram: shift right S to S₁ for an increase; left S to S₂ for a decrease. Always label the axes and both curves. A price change of the good itself moves along; everything else shifts.

Exam use: Weather.

Government payments to farmers lower costs and raise supply.

Q1 Analyse 6 marks Analyse the causes of a shift to the right in the supply curve of chocolate. (Feb/Mar 2023, P22 style)
ReminderCosts fall, technology, subsidy, good weather for cocoa, more firms.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A fall in the cost of cocoa or other inputs reduces producers' costs, so chocolate firms can supply more at every price. New technology could raise productivity and lower unit costs, so supply increases. A government subsidy lowers costs. It encourages firms to produce more at each price. The size of the shift depends on how much costs fall.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of a shift to the left in the supply curve of a good.
ReminderA tax, rising costs, a drought, new regulations.
A tax on producers raises costs, so firms supply less at every price. A rise in wages or raw material costs reduces profit, so supply decreases at each price.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain how a subsidy would affect the supply of a good.
ReminderLowers costs; supply shifts right.
A subsidy is a payment from the government to producers. It lowers their costs of production, so they can supply more at every price and the supply curve shifts right.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how advances in technology could affect the supply of a good.
ReminderProductivity, cost, quality; supply right.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
New technology can raise productivity and lower the cost of production. Firms can supply more at each price, so the supply curve shifts to the right. Lower costs may also lead to lower prices. The benefit depends on how quickly firms adopt the technology and the cost of investing.

Top tips

  • Shifts are caused by non-price factors. Name the factor.
  • Right = increase; left = decrease. Say 'more at every price'.
  • Link to costs. Most supply shifts work through costs.
  • Subsidy increases supply; tax decreases it. Know both.
  • Draw S and S₁ and label axes. Marks for the diagram.
