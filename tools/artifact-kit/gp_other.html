__N__ · Quotas, prohibitions, licences, deregulation and property rights

These five appear on the 9708 list of measures but not on every course page. Each is a way of dealing with a specific kind of failure.

MeasureWhat it isUsed forEvaluation
Production quotaA legal limit on the quantity produced or sold, so supply is fixed at the quota (vertical) and the price rises if the quota is below the market quantityOver-production with a negative externality, demerit goods, over-use of resources such as fish stocksCertain result and easy to explain. Higher prices give producers windfall gains, a black market may appear, quotas are hard to share fairly, and there is no reward for cutting output below the limit
Prohibition (ban)Making production, sale or use illegalHarm so serious that no level is acceptable: leaded petrol, certain pesticides, asbestosRemoves the externality at the source. Costs of enforcement, loss of consumer choice, and the risk of black markets and criminal supply
LicencePermission needed to produce or sell, often with conditions or a limit on numbersDemerit goods (alcohol and tobacco sellers), information failure (qualified doctors, electricians), controlling the number of sellersRaises standards and limits harm. A limited number of licences can become valuable, which may protect incumbents, restrict competition and raise prices
DeregulationRemoving rules that restrict entry or competitionMonopoly power and inefficiency created by barriers to entry, such as restrictions on airline routes or taxisMore competition may cut prices and costs. Safety and quality standards may slip, firms may exploit consumers who lack information, and re-regulation may follow. It only works if new firms actually enter
Property rightsClearly assigned legal ownership or use of an asset, so owners can stop others imposing costs, or charge them, and bargainNegative externalities where the affected party can be identified (a polluter and a downstream fishery); positive externalities, through patents that let inventors keep the benefitA market solution with no tax or subsidy. It fails where nobody can own the resource (air, sea, climate), where many people are affected, when bargaining is costly or one side is far weaker, and the starting allocation of rights decides who pays
Quotas and licences work on quantityA quota or a limit on licences fixes the quantity and lets the price rise. A tax or minimum price fixes the price and lets the quantity fall. Say which one you are using when you draw the diagram.
