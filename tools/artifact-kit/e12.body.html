Resource Allocation Decisions

What, how and who: the three basic economic questions every society must answer. Syllabus 1.1.2.

The big idea

Because of scarcity, every society must answer three basic economic questions: what to produce, how to produce and who to produce for. These decisions allocate scarce resources between competing uses. Different economic systems answer them in different ways, with the market, the government, or a mixture deciding.

The three basic questions

QuestionWhat it meansExamples of decisions
What to produce?Which goods and services, and in what quantities, to make with limited resourcesMore healthcare or more military equipment; food or cars; wheat or cocoa
How to produce?Which methods and resource mix: labour-intensive or capital-intensive, which technology, which locationHand-picked or machine-harvested crops; factory with robots or many workers; renewable or fossil energy
Who to produce for?Who receives the output: how income and goods are distributedLuxury goods for the rich or basics for the poor; goods to export or for the home market

Who decides?

  • In a market economy, consumers' spending and firms' search for profit answer the questions through prices.
  • In a planned (command) economy, the government decides.
  • In a mixed economy, both markets and the government take part.

The questions are decisions about resource allocation: they exist because of scarcity. Each answer has an opportunity cost.

Q1 Identify 3 marks State the three basic economic questions.
ReminderWhat, how, who.
What to produce. How to produce. Who to produce for.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain what is meant by the question 'what to produce'.
ReminderChoice of goods and services and quantities; scarce resources; opportunity cost.
'What to produce' is the decision about which goods and services, and how many of each, are made. Resources are scarce, so producing more of one good means fewer resources for another. For example, a government may have to choose between more healthcare and more military equipment.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain what is meant by the question 'how to produce'.
ReminderMethod, labour vs capital, technology.
'How to produce' is the decision about the methods and mix of resources used. For example, firms can use labour-intensive methods with many workers or capital-intensive methods with machines. The choice depends on costs, technology and the resources available.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain how the market decides what to produce.
ReminderConsumer demand, price, profit, resources move.
In a market economy consumers' spending shows what they want. When demand for a good rises, its price and profit rise, so firms switch resources into making it and away from less profitable goods.

Top tips

  • Three questions: what, how, who.
  • Give an example with each. Wheat or rice; robots or workers; rich or poor.
  • Link to scarcity. The questions exist because resources are limited.
  • Who decides? Market, government or both.
