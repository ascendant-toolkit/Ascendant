Definition of Market Failure

What market failure is and the forms it takes: over-provision, under-provision, non-provision and monopoly. Syllabus 2.9.1.

The big idea

Market failure occurs when the price mechanism fails to allocate resources efficiently, so that society gets a misallocation of resources: too much of some goods, too little of others, or none at all. It means the market outcome is not the best for society as a whole.

What goes wrong

Type of failureWhat happensExample
Over-provision / over-consumptionMore is produced or consumed than is best for societyDemerit goods (cigarettes), goods with external costs (pollution)
Under-provision / under-consumptionLess is produced or consumed than is best for societyMerit goods (education), goods with external benefits (vaccination)
Non-provisionThe market does not supply the good at allPublic goods (street lighting, national defence)
Restricted supply, higher pricesA dominant firm limits output and charges moreMonopoly

Why it matters

  • Markets are efficient only if prices reflect all the costs and benefits to society.
  • When they do not, there is a case for government intervention (see 2.10).

Market failure does not mean the market has stopped working: it means the outcome is not the best use of resources. Diagrams of market failure are not required at IGCSE.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Climate change

Greenhouse gas emissions impose costs on the world that polluters do not pay for.

Exam use: External costs.

Street lights

Private firms cannot charge for light on a public road, so councils provide it.

Exam use: Non-provision.

Vaccines

People gain private protection but also protect others.

Exam use: External benefits.

A single local water supplier

With no rivals, prices may be high.

Exam use: Monopoly.

Q1 Define 2 marks Define market failure. (May/June 2020, P22 style)
ReminderMisallocation of resources by the price mechanism.
Market failure is when the price mechanism fails to allocate resources efficiently, leading to a misallocation of resources.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two types of market failure.
ReminderOver-provision, under-provision, non-provision, monopoly.
Over-consumption of demerit goods such as cigarettes is a market failure. Consumers ignore the full harm, so more is consumed than is best for society. Non-provision of public goods such as street lighting is another. Firms cannot charge users, so the market does not supply them.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain why external costs lead to market failure.
ReminderCosts on third parties ignored; over-production.
External costs are costs that fall on third parties, such as pollution. The producer does not pay them, so the price is too low. Too much of the good is produced, so resources are misallocated.

Top tips

  • Define market failure with 'misallocation'. That is the key word.
  • Name the type. Over, under, non-provision, monopoly.
  • Give an example. Cigarettes, education, street lighting.
  • Link to government intervention. Why it is needed.
