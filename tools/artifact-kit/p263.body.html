Determinants of PED

The main influences on whether demand is elastic or inelastic. Syllabus 2.6.3.

The big idea

How elastic or inelastic demand is depends on a set of influences. Goods with close substitutes, goods that are luxuries, goods that take a large share of income and goods with a long time to adjust tend to have elastic demand. The opposite gives inelastic demand.

Determinants of PED

DeterminantElastic demandInelastic demand
Availability of substitutesMany close substitutes: easy to switchFew or no substitutes
Necessity or luxuryLuxury or wantNecessity
Proportion of income spentLarge share of income (a car, a holiday)Tiny share of income (salt, matches)
Time periodLong run: time to find alternatives (e.g. buy an electric car)Short run: habits and contracts limit change
Addiction or habitNot habit-formingAddictive or habitual goods (cigarettes, caffeine)
How broadly the good is definedA specific brand (many rivals)A broad category such as "food" or "fuel"

Examples

  • Petrol: inelastic in the short run (few substitutes, a necessity, habitual), more elastic in the long run.
  • A particular brand of trainers: elastic (many rival brands).
  • Salt: inelastic (cheap, tiny share of income, no substitute).
  • Foreign holidays: elastic (luxury, large share of income, many alternatives).

An exam answer should name the determinant, say whether it makes demand more or less elastic and link it to the product in the question.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain two influences on the price elasticity of demand for a good.
ReminderSubstitutes, necessity, income share, time.
The availability of substitutes affects PED. If a good has many close substitutes, consumers can switch easily when its price rises, so demand is elastic. Whether a good is a necessity matters. Necessities such as salt have inelastic demand because people must keep buying them.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain why demand for petrol is likely to be more elastic in the long run than in the short run.
ReminderTime to adjust: alternatives, cars, moving.
In the short run drivers have few alternatives and are tied to existing cars. In the long run consumers have time to switch to electric cars, move closer to work or use public transport, so quantity demanded responds more to the price: demand is more elastic.
Q3 Identify 2 marks Identify two goods with inelastic demand and say why.
ReminderNecessities, addictive goods.
Cigarettes, because they are addictive. Salt, because it is a cheap necessity with no substitute.

Top tips

  • Learn the determinants. Substitutes, necessity, income share, time, habit.
  • Say elastic or inelastic. And name the good.
  • Link to the product. Don't just list determinants.
  • Short run vs long run. Long run is more elastic.
