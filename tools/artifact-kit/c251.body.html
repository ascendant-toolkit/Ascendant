Causes of Price Changes

How shifts in demand and supply change the equilibrium price and quantity. Syllabus 2.5.1.

The big idea

Prices change when demand or supply shifts, moving the equilibrium. A shift in demand moves price and quantity in the same direction. A shift in supply moves price and quantity in opposite directions. To explain a price change, say which curve shifts, why, which way, and what happens to the new equilibrium.

The four basic cases

ChangeCurveNew equilibrium priceNew equilibrium quantity
Increase in demandD shifts rightRisesRises
Decrease in demandD shifts leftFallsFalls
Increase in supplyS shifts rightFallsRises
Decrease in supplyS shifts leftRisesFalls

Causes of price changes

Price rises because…Price falls because…
Demand increases: higher incomes, fashion, advertising, price of a substitute rises, population risesDemand decreases: lower incomes, bad publicity or health warning, price of a substitute falls, population falls
Supply decreases: higher costs, a tax, a drought, new regulations, fewer firmsSupply increases: lower costs, new technology, a subsidy, good weather, more firms

Both curves shift

  • If demand and supply both increase, quantity rises for sure; the price change depends on which shifts more.
  • If demand increases and supply decreases, price rises for sure; the quantity change depends on which shifts more.

How to answer a diagram question

  • Draw D and S with the original equilibrium (P, Q).
  • Shift the relevant curve and label it (D₁ or S₁).
  • Mark the new equilibrium (P₁, Q₁) and state the change.

A change in the good's own price never shifts its own demand or supply curve: price changes are the result of shifts.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Housing in growing cities

A rising population raises demand for homes and pushes up prices and rents.

Exam use: Demand increase.

Oil prices

A cut in output by producers shifts supply left, raising the world oil price.

Exam use: Supply decrease.

Falling cost of electronics

Technology has lowered the cost of making phones and TVs, so prices fall.

Exam use: Supply increase.

Weather and food prices

A poor harvest cuts supply and raises food prices.

Exam use: Supply decrease.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain how an increase in income may affect the price of a normal good.
ReminderDemand rises; shortage; price rises.
A rise in income increases demand for a normal good, shifting the demand curve to the right. At the original price quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied, so there is a shortage. The price rises until a new equilibrium is reached at a higher price and quantity.
Q2 Analyse 4 marks Analyse why the price of German luxury cars may have increased in 2018. (Oct/Nov 2020, P22 style)
ReminderDemand up (incomes, exports) or supply down (costs, tariffs).
Demand for luxury cars may have increased because incomes in key markets rose, shifting the demand curve right and raising the equilibrium price. Supply may have fallen because of higher costs or trade tariffs, shifting supply left and raising the price.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how a drought could affect the market for wheat. (Oct/Nov 2023, P22 style)
ReminderSupply left; shortage; price up; quantity down.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A drought reduces the harvest, so supply of wheat decreases and S shifts left. At the original price quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied: a shortage. The price rises to a new equilibrium P₁ and quantity falls to Q₁. Because wheat is a necessity the rise in price may be large.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how an increase in population size will affect the market for housing. (Feb/Mar 2024, P22 style)
ReminderDemand right; price up; quantity up.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A larger population increases demand for housing, so D shifts right. At the old price there is a shortage, so the price rises to P₁ and the quantity traded rises to Q₁. The quantity rise depends on how quickly new housing can be supplied.
Q5 Analyse 6 marks Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how a subsidy given to producers could affect the market for solar panels.
ReminderSupply right; price falls; quantity rises.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A subsidy lowers producers' costs, so supply increases and S shifts right. At the old price there is a surplus, so price falls to P₁ and quantity rises to Q₁. Consumers pay a lower price and buy more. The effect depends on how much of the subsidy is passed on in lower prices.

Top tips

  • Say which curve shifts. And which way, and why.
  • Draw D, S, then the shift. Label D₁ or S₁, P₁ and Q₁.
  • Describe the new equilibrium. Price and quantity, both.
  • Demand shifts: P and Q same direction. Supply shifts: opposite.
  • Use a shortage or surplus link. It explains why the price moves.
