Factors Affecting an Individual's Choice of Occupation

Wage and non-wage factors that influence the job people choose. Syllabus 3.3.1.

The big idea

People choose an occupation by weighing wage factors (how much it pays) against non-wage factors (everything else that makes a job attractive). The job that gives the greatest overall benefit, not just the highest pay, is usually chosen.

Wage and non-wage factors

TypeFactorsExample
Wage factorsBasic pay, overtime, bonuses, commission, pension, other financial rewardsA sales job with high commission
Job satisfactionInteresting, worthwhile or creative work; sense of purposeA teacher or nurse accepting lower pay
Working conditions and hoursHours, shift patterns, safety, pleasant workplace, holidayAvoiding night shifts
Job securityLikelihood of keeping the jobA permanent public sector post
Promotion and career prospectsChances to progress and gain skillsA graduate trainee scheme
Location and travelCommuting time and cost, living near familyWorking close to home
Qualifications and trainingThe skills required, and training providedA doctor's long training
Fringe benefitsCompany car, free meals, health insurance, childcareHealth cover

Different people weigh the factors differently depending on age, family, income and personality. Two jobs with the same pay may attract different numbers of workers.

Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain why a worker might choose a lower-paid job.
ReminderNon-wage benefits: satisfaction, security, hours.
Non-wage factors can outweigh a higher wage. A job may offer greater job satisfaction or shorter hours. The worker values these more than the extra pay.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how wage and non-wage factors influence a person's choice of occupation.
ReminderWage: pay; non-wage: satisfaction, security, prospects; overall benefit.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Wage factors such as pay and bonuses reward workers financially. Higher pay attracts more workers to an occupation. Non-wage factors such as job security, hours and satisfaction also matter. Some workers accept lower pay for a more enjoyable or secure job. The weight depends on the worker's age, family and income.

Top tips

  • Two groups: wage and non-wage. Name at least two of each.
  • Link factors to a worker. Age, family, income.
  • Overall benefit. Not just pay.
  • Give examples. Teacher, banker, nurse.
