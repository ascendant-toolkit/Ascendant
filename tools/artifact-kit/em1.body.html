Employment, Unemployment and Full Employment

What employment and unemployment mean, who is in the labour force, and what full employment is. Syllabus 4.6.1.

The big idea

Employment means being in paid work. A person is unemployed if they are willing and able to work, are actively looking, but cannot find a job. Full employment is when everyone who is willing and able to work has a job (in practice, only a small amount of unemployment remains). Employment and unemployment are central to the government's aim of full employment (low unemployment).

Who is who?

TermMeaningExample
EmployedIn paid work (full or part time, employee or self-employed)A teacher, a taxi driver, a farmer selling crops
UnemployedWithout a job, willing and able to work and looking for oneA factory worker laid off who is applying for jobs
Labour forceEmployed + unemployedEveryone who is working or looking for work
Not in the labour forceNot working and not looking, for example full-time students, retired people, full-time carersA university student, a pensioner

The working-age population is everyone old enough to work. Some of them are outside the labour force, so they are not counted as unemployed.

Full employment does not mean zero unemployment

Even when the economy is doing well, some people are always between jobs or changing careers. So full employment means that only frictional unemployment remains (a few per cent), and any other jobseeker could find a job. A government aiming for full employment is aiming for low unemployment, not zero.

Other terms

Participation rate

The labour force as a percentage of the working-age population. A fall may mean people have become discouraged, or are in education.

Underemployment and hidden unemployment

Underemployed: working fewer hours than they would like or in jobs below their skills. Hidden unemployed: would work but have stopped looking, so they are not counted.

Why it matters: a high employment level raises output, incomes and tax revenue; unemployment wastes resources. See 4.6.4 for the consequences.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Low unemployment in Japan

Japan has had a low unemployment rate for years (around 2 to 3%), a level many economists would call full employment, even though some people are always between jobs.

Exam use: Full employment does not mean zero unemployment.

Falling participation

In several rich countries many older people retired early or left the labour force during COVID-19. The unemployment rate fell even though total employment was lower.

Exam use: Why unemployment rate and employment can move in different directions.

Discouraged workers

In countries with long spells of high unemployment, some jobseekers stop looking. They are no longer counted as unemployed, so official unemployment understates the real problem.

Exam use: Hidden unemployment and a limit on the official figure.

Q1 Define 2 marks What is meant by unemployment?
ReminderTwo ideas: without a job, and willing and able to work (and looking).
Unemployment is when people are without a job although they are willing and able to work and are looking for work.
Common mistakeSaying 'people who do not work'. Students and retired people are not unemployed.
Q2 Define 2 marks What is meant by full employment?
ReminderEveryone willing and able has a job; some frictional unemployment remains.
Full employment is when everyone who is willing and able to work has a job, so only a small amount of frictional unemployment remains.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain why a full-time student is not counted as unemployed.
ReminderSay what unemployed means, then apply it.
People are only counted as unemployed if they are willing and able to work and are looking for a job. A full-time student is not looking for paid work, so is outside the labour force. If they started to apply for jobs full time they would be counted as unemployed.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain why full employment does not mean zero unemployment.
ReminderFrictional: people between jobs. Also people changing industry or seasons.
Even in a strong economy some people are between jobs. Workers who leave one job take time to find another, and new entrants to the labour force need time to search, so there is always some frictional unemployment. Full employment therefore means that anyone willing and able to work could find a job in a reasonable time, not that unemployment is zero.

Top tips

  • Unemployed means looking for work. Students, retirees and carers are outside the labour force.
  • Labour force = employed + unemployed. Use it in the denominator of the rate.
  • Full employment is not zero unemployment. Frictional unemployment always exists.
  • Say willing, able and looking. Three words that earn definition marks.
