Mobility of Labour

Causes and consequences of changes in the occupational and geographical mobility of labour. Syllabus 3.3.4.

The big idea

Mobility of labour is how easily workers can move to a different job or occupation (occupational mobility) or to a different place (geographical mobility). High mobility helps the economy match workers to jobs and reduces unemployment; low mobility leaves people unemployed while vacancies go unfilled.

Types of mobility

Occupational mobilityGeographical mobility
MeaningAbility to switch between occupations or industriesAbility to move between regions or countries to work
Increased byEducation and retraining, transferable skills, apprenticeships, careers advice, fewer licensing barriersCheaper housing and transport, better information about jobs, help with moving costs, ability to work remotely, fewer immigration restrictions
Reduced bySpecialised skills, lack of qualifications, age, fear of change, costly retrainingHigh housing costs, family and social ties, language and culture, visa rules, poor transport

Consequences of changes in mobility

Higher mobility

  • Lower structural unemployment
  • Workers fill vacancies faster
  • Higher output and productivity
  • Wage differences narrow
  • Better use of resources
  • But: some regions or industries may lose workers (brain drain)

Lower mobility

  • Higher structural and regional unemployment
  • Labour shortages in some areas or sectors
  • Lower output and growth
  • Wage gaps widen
  • Greater need for government help (retraining, relocation grants)

Q1 Define 2 marks Define geographical mobility of labour.
ReminderAbility to move between places to work.
Geographical mobility of labour is the ability of workers to move between areas or countries to find work.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of a fall in the occupational mobility of labour.
ReminderSpecialised skills, age, lack of training.
Workers with very specialised skills find it hard to switch jobs. Their skills are not useful in other industries. Lack of retraining or qualifications also reduces mobility. Workers cannot afford or access training for new jobs.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse the consequences of an increase in the geographical mobility of labour.
ReminderFewer vacancies; lower unemployment; more output; brain drain.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Workers can move to areas with vacancies. Unemployment falls in the region they leave and labour shortages fall elsewhere. Output and productivity rise because labour is used better. However, regions losing workers may face a brain drain and falling demand.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether governments should spend money to increase the mobility of labour.
ReminderBenefits: employment, growth; costs: expense, uncertain results. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Retraining and relocation grants can raise occupational and geographical mobility. They reduce structural unemployment and fill vacancies, raising output. They also reduce welfare spending. However, programmes cost money and many workers may still not move because of family ties. Overall, the benefits are likely to outweigh the costs if schemes are well targeted.

Top tips

  • Two types. Occupational and geographical.
  • Say what raises or lowers mobility. Retraining, housing, ties.
  • Link to structural unemployment. Mismatch.
  • Evaluate costs of policy. Retraining is expensive.
