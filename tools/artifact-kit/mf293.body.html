Causes of Market Failure

Why the market fails: public goods, merit and demerit goods, externalities and abuse of monopoly power. Syllabus 2.9.3.

The big idea

Market failure has several causes. Public goods are not provided because firms cannot charge. Merit goods and goods with external benefits are under-consumed because consumers undervalue the benefits. Demerit goods and goods with external costs are over-consumed because consumers ignore the harm. Monopoly power lets firms restrict output and raise prices.

Causes of market failure

CauseWhy the market fails
Public goodsNon-excludable and non-rival: free riders mean firms cannot earn profit, so they do not provide them
Merit goodsConsumers lack information or undervalue the benefits, and may be unable to afford them, so they buy too little
Demerit goodsConsumers underestimate the harm (addiction, imperfect information), so they buy too much
External costsProducers or consumers do not pay the cost imposed on third parties (pollution, congestion), so prices are too low and output too high
External benefitsThird parties gain without paying (vaccination, education), so prices are too high for the social benefit and output is too low
Abuse of monopoly powerA dominant firm faces little competition, so it can raise prices and restrict output and may be less efficient

Common underlying problems

  • Imperfect information: buyers do not know the true costs or benefits.
  • Ignoring third parties: the price mechanism only reflects private costs and benefits.
  • Lack of competition: a monopolist can set price above cost.
  • Inability to charge: for non-excludable goods.

In an exam, link the cause to the market outcome: say who ignores the cost or benefit, and what is over- or under-produced.

Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain why the market may not provide public goods.
ReminderNon-excludable, free riders, no profit.
Public goods are non-excludable, so firms cannot stop people using them without paying. People free-ride, so firms cannot earn a profit, and therefore the market does not supply the good.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain why demerit goods may be over-consumed.
ReminderUnderestimate harm; addiction; external costs.
Consumers underestimate the harm that demerit goods cause, or are addicted. Demand is therefore higher than the real benefit justifies, so more is consumed than is best for society.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse why an external cost may cause market failure.
ReminderThird parties pay; price too low; over-production.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
An external cost is a cost to third parties, such as pollution. The producer does not pay it, so the price of the good only reflects private cost and is too low. Consumers buy too much and firms produce too much. Resources are over-allocated to the good, so there is market failure. The size of the failure depends on how large the external cost is.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how abuse of monopoly power may lead to market failure.
ReminderRestrict output; higher prices; less choice; inefficiency.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A monopoly is the only seller, so it faces no competition. It can restrict output to raise the price. Consumers pay more and buy less than in a competitive market. With no rivals, the firm may be less efficient and innovative. The harm depends on whether there are substitutes or new entrants.

Top tips

  • Cause, then outcome. Say what is over or under produced.
  • Name who ignores the cost or benefit. Third parties.
  • Public goods: free-rider problem. No profit.
  • Monopoly: restricts output, raises prices. Explain why.
  • Use examples. Pollution, cigarettes, street lighting.
