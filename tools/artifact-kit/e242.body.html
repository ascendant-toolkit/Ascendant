Market Equilibrium

Equilibrium price and quantity from schedules and from demand and supply diagrams. Syllabus 2.4.2.

The big idea

Market equilibrium is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied: the market clears with no shortage or surplus. The equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity are found where the demand and supply curves intersect, or from a schedule where the two quantities match.

Using a schedule

Price ($)Quantity demandedQuantity suppliedResult
102080Surplus of 60
84060Surplus of 20
65050Equilibrium
47030Shortage of 40
29010Shortage of 80

The equilibrium price is $6 and the equilibrium quantity is 50.

Using a diagram

  • Draw a downward-sloping D and an upward-sloping S on the same axes.
  • They cross at the equilibrium point. Read across for the equilibrium price (P) and down for the equilibrium quantity (Q).
  • At this price, buyers and sellers are both satisfied: the market is cleared.

Why equilibrium is stable

  • If the price is above equilibrium there is a surplus, so sellers cut prices.
  • If it is below equilibrium there is a shortage, so the price rises.
  • These forces push the market back to equilibrium.

Equilibrium is not necessarily fair or desirable: it only means demand equals supply. Equilibrium changes when demand or supply shifts (see 2.5).

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Used-car market

Prices settle where the number of cars buyers want matches the number sellers offer.

Exam use: Market clearing.

Online auctions

Auctions let prices rise until buyers and the quantity available match.

Exam use: Finding equilibrium.

Fresh fish at the end of the day

Stallholders lower prices until all the fish is sold.

Exam use: Surplus pushes the price down.

Housing in popular cities

Prices rise where demand exceeds supply until fewer buyers can afford to buy.

Exam use: Shortage pushes the price up.

Top tips

  • Equilibrium: Qd = Qs. Say it in full.
  • Draw D and S and mark the intersection. Label P and Q.
  • Use the schedule. Find where the two columns match.
  • No shortage or surplus. The market clears.
  • Equilibrium is not necessarily fair. Useful for evaluation.
