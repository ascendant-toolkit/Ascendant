Labour-Intensive and Capital-Intensive Production

Why firms choose labour-intensive or capital-intensive methods, with advantages and disadvantages. Syllabus 3.5.2.

The big idea

Labour-intensive production uses a lot of labour relative to capital. Capital-intensive production uses a lot of capital (machinery) relative to labour. Firms choose the method that gives the lowest cost for the output, quality and flexibility they need.

Comparing the two

Labour-intensiveCapital-intensive
ExamplesHairdressing, teaching, farming by hand, restaurantsCar assembly lines, oil refining, automated warehouses
Why adoptedCheap labour; small scale; personal service; products need skill or creativity; machines are costly or unsuitableHigh wages; large scale; need speed, precision and consistency; cheap capital; technology available
AdvantagesFlexible; personal touch; lower start-up cost; easier to adjust to small orders; creates jobsHigh output; lower unit cost at scale; consistent quality; works around the clock; fewer wage costs
DisadvantagesHigher labour costs when wages rise; lower output per worker; variable quality; limited scaleHigh start-up and maintenance costs; breakdowns; less flexible; unemployment; skilled workers needed

Choosing between them

  • Compare relative factor prices: dear labour and cheap capital favour capital-intensive methods; cheap labour and dear capital favour labour-intensive methods.
  • Think about output level, quality needed and the nature of the product.
  • Countries with large populations and low wages (for example India) often use more labour-intensive methods; rich countries use more capital-intensive ones.

Q1 Define 2 marks Define capital-intensive production.
ReminderUses mainly capital.
Capital-intensive production is production that uses a high proportion of capital compared with labour.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two reasons why a firm may choose labour-intensive production.
ReminderCheap labour, personal service, low start-up costs.
Labour may be cheap, so it is the lowest-cost method. The product may need personal service or skill, which machines cannot easily provide.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain two advantages of capital-intensive production.
ReminderHigh output, consistent quality, lower unit cost.
Machines can produce large quantities quickly, so output per worker is high. Machines give consistent quality and work all day, so unit costs are lower.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether a firm should switch from labour-intensive to capital-intensive production.
ReminderCosts, output, jobs, flexibility. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Capital-intensive production can raise output per worker and lower unit costs. It gives consistent quality and can work around the clock. However, machines are costly to buy and maintain and may make some workers redundant. It is less flexible for small or varied orders. Overall, the switch is worthwhile for large-scale, high-volume production when wages are high, but not for small, personalised services.

Top tips

  • Define by proportion. More labour or more capital.
  • Link to relative factor prices. Dear labour, cheap capital.
  • Examples. Hair salon vs car factory.
  • Evaluate with scale. High volume favours capital.
