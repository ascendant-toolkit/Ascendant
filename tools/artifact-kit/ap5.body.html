A Level Economics Revision Plan and Exam Day

A revision plan for A Level Economics: three phases, a planner that weights your time by syllabus size and confidence, a topic tracker, a past paper routine, how to revise, and a last-week and exam-day plan. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and practice.

This part in four sentences

A good plan has three phases: rebuild your knowledge, practise questions by topic, then sit timed papers and fix the gaps. Spend your time where the syllabus is biggest and where you are weakest, and use active methods (recall, practice questions, diagrams from memory) that are far better than re-reading. Space your reviews out, because reviewing after a day, a week and a month beats cramming the night before. This page gives you the planner, a topic tracker, a past paper routine, a last-week checklist and an exam-day plan.

A past paper routine that works

We counted the Paper 3 papers (24 papers, 1,155 questions) and the Paper 4 papers (40) from 2020 to 2025. The mark schemes for the questions are free to download from the exam board, and every lesson page has an exam questions tab with reworded versions.

StageWhenHow
1 · By topic, untimedDuring phase 1 and 2After revising a topic, do the exam questions tab and then 8 to 10 real past questions on that topic. Use the mark scheme straight away.
2 · By section, timedMiddle of phase 2Paper 3 in blocks of 10 questions in 25 minutes. Paper 4 Section A in 36 minutes, an essay in 36 minutes.
3 · Full papersPhase 3Two or three complete papers under exam conditions: quiet room, no notes, exact timing. Do them about a week apart, so you have time to fix the errors.
4 · Learn from the last onesFinal weekRe-do the questions you got wrong and re-read your error log. Do not start a new paper in the last 24 hours.

Mark honestly and keep an error log

  • Mark in a different colour with the mark scheme beside you. Check points, levels and what the examiner asks for.
  • For every lost mark, write one line in the log: the question, the type of mistake and the fix.
  • Review the log weekly. The mistakes that keep repeating are your priorities.
Type of mistakeTypical fix
Knowledge gapGo back to the lesson page, then write a flashcard and re-test in 3 days
Misread the question or the qualifierUnderline command words and "not/least/most likely" every time
Calculation slipWrite the formula first and check units; practise the same type twice
Diagram errorRedraw it from memory three times and add labels
No evaluation or no conclusionAdd a judgement sentence to every plan: see the Paper 4 essay page
Ran out of timePractise timed sections and set a hard stop for each part

What works and what does not

MethodWhy it works (or does not)How to use it in economics
Retrieval practiceTrying to recall strengthens memory far more than re-readingClose the book, write everything you know on a topic, then check
Spaced reviewRevisiting just as you start to forget flattens the forgetting curveReview a topic after 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks and a month
InterleavingMixing topics trains you to choose the right idea for the questionDo mixed sets of questions: one micro, one macro, one international
Practice questionsIt is the closest thing to the examPast questions, marked with the mark scheme
Diagram drillsDiagrams carry marks and show understandingDraw, label and explain 3 diagrams from memory each day
Teach itExplaining shows the gaps in your understandingExplain a chain of reasoning aloud, or write a 60-second summary
Essay planningPlanning is faster than writing and builds structurePlan a 20-mark essay in 5 minutes: three points, one evaluation each, conclusion
Re-reading and highlightingFeels productive, but gives a false sense of knowingUse only as a first pass, never as the main method

Paper 4: a 120 minute plan

  • Each section is worth 20 marks, so give each about 36 minutes. Keep to it: a 20-mark essay worth the same as the data response should not get 60 minutes.
  • For the essay, plan for 4 to 5 minutes (three points with application, analysis and evaluation, and a conclusion), then write for about 30 minutes.
  • Check Section B and C choices in the first 6 minutes: pick the question where you can draw the diagram and name an example without hesitating.

Paper 3: a 75 minute plan

  • First pass about 50 minutes (100 seconds each), second pass about 16 minutes, check about 9 minutes. There is no negative marking: answer every question.

Exam day

WhenWhat to do
The night beforeLight review of your diagram and formula sheet only. Pack ID, pens, a calculator that is allowed, water. Sleep.
The morningEat, arrive early, avoid last-minute panic conversations. Look at your formula sheet once.
First 5 minutesRead the whole paper. Underline command words and marks. Choose the essays. Note times for each section.
DuringStick to your time per section. If stuck, move on and return. Draw diagrams neatly with labels.
Last 5 to 10 minutesCheck every part has an answer, diagram labels, units and conclusions.
Between papersDo not post-mortem. Rest, eat and move on to the next paper.

Top tips

  • Start with a recap of AS. A Level questions assume AS topics, so patch gaps first.
  • Weight your time. More time for bigger topics (7, 11, 9) and for the ones you rate red.
  • Close the book. Recall first, then check. If you can't write it from memory, you don't know it yet.
  • Space it out. Review after 1 day, 3 days, a week, two weeks and a month.
  • Draw every diagram. Draw, label and explain each one without looking.
  • Practise like the exam. Timed, quiet, no notes, then mark with the mark scheme.
  • Rest before the exam. Sleep, food and a calm morning improve marks more than another late night.
