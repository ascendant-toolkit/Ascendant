Banking

The role and importance of central banks and commercial banks. Syllabus 3.1.2.

The big idea

The banking system has two key parts. Commercial banks take deposits and make loans to households and firms, and provide payment services. The central bank is the government's bank and the banking system's regulator: it controls interest rates and the money supply, issues notes and coins, and acts as lender of last resort.

Commercial banks

  • Accept deposits and provide safe places to save.
  • Provide loans and overdrafts to households and firms, charging interest.
  • Operate payment systems: accounts, cards, transfers.
  • Offer advice and other services: mortgages, insurance, currency exchange.
  • Earn profit from the gap between interest charged on loans and paid on deposits.

Importance: banks channel savings to investment, help firms grow and households smooth spending, and make payments easy.

Q1 Identify 2 marks Identify two functions of a central bank.
ReminderSet interest rates, issue notes, lender of last resort, government's bank.
Setting interest rates. Acting as lender of last resort (or issuing notes).
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two functions of commercial banks.
ReminderDeposits, loans, payments.
Commercial banks accept deposits from savers. This provides a safe place to save and funds for lending. They lend to households and firms. Loans let firms invest and households buy homes, and the bank earns interest.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain the importance of the central bank to an economy.
ReminderStability, interest rates, lender of last resort.
The central bank sets interest rates and controls the money supply, which helps keep inflation low and the economy stable. It acts as lender of last resort, which prevents the collapse of banks and keeps confidence in the banking system.
Q4 Discuss 6 marks Discuss whether commercial banks or the central bank are more important to an economy.
ReminderBanks: loans, payments; central bank: stability, rates. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Commercial banks provide savings, loans and payments. They channel savings to investment, helping firms grow. The central bank controls interest rates and prevents banking crises. Without commercial banks little lending would happen, but without a stable central bank the system could collapse. Overall, they depend on each other; the central bank is arguably more important in a crisis.

Top tips

  • Central bank: rates, notes, last resort. Government's bank.
  • Commercial banks: deposits, loans, payments. They earn the interest gap.
  • Give examples of each. Bank of England; high street banks.
  • Explain importance. Stability and investment.
