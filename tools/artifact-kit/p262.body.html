Calculation and Interpretation of PED

Calculating PED, interpreting its value and drawing demand curves for different elasticities. Syllabus 2.6.2.

The big idea

PED is calculated with the formula PED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price. Because demand slopes downwards, PED is negative and we usually ignore the sign and look at its size. The size tells us whether demand is perfectly inelastic, inelastic, unitary, elastic or perfectly elastic.

Calculating PED

% change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100
PED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price

Example: price rises from $10 to $12 (+20%) and quantity demanded falls from 100 to 70 (−30%). PED = −30 ÷ +20 = −1.5. Ignoring the sign, 1.5: elastic.

Interpreting the value

PED (ignoring sign)NameMeaningCurve
0Perfectly inelasticQuantity demanded does not change at allVertical
Between 0 and 1InelasticQuantity changes by a smaller % than priceSteep
1UnitaryQuantity changes by the same % as priceRectangular hyperbola
Greater than 1ElasticQuantity changes by a bigger % than priceFlat
InfinityPerfectly elasticAny rise in price reduces quantity demanded to zeroHorizontal

Use the % change, not the absolute change. For a fall in price the signs are reversed but the result is the same. Always show your working and state whether demand is elastic or inelastic.

Q1 Calculate 3 marks The price of a good rises from $10 to $12. Quantity demanded falls from 100 to 70. Calculate PED. (May/June 2023, P22 style)
Reminder%ΔQ ÷ %ΔP.
%ΔP = 2 ÷ 10 × 100 = 20%; %ΔQ = −30 ÷ 100 × 100 = −30%. PED = −30 ÷ 20 = −1.5.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain what a PED value of 0.4 means.
ReminderInelastic; quantity responds less than price.
A PED of 0.4 means demand is inelastic. A 10% rise in price would cause quantity demanded to fall by only 4%. Consumers are not very responsive to price changes.
Q3 Identify 2 marks Identify the PED value for a perfectly inelastic demand curve.
ReminderZero.
Zero (0). Quantity demanded does not change.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain how a demand curve would look if demand was perfectly elastic.
ReminderHorizontal; any rise loses all sales.
It would be a horizontal line. At that price consumers will buy any quantity, but if the price rises even slightly quantity demanded falls to zero.

Top tips

  • PED = %ΔQ ÷ %ΔP. Always use percentages.
  • Show every step. Percentage changes first.
  • Ignore the sign when interpreting. Compare the size with 1.
  • Learn the five values. 0, <1, 1, >1, ∞.
  • State elastic or inelastic. Finish the answer.
