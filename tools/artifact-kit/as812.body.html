E-R Diagrams and Normalisation

Coursebook: Chapter 8 · Databases · section 8.1 Database concepts

Drawing entity-relationship diagrams from foreign keys, and normalising to first, second and third normal form with a worked example, with animations, labs and the real Paper 1 questions from 2021 to 2026.

This page in five sentences

An entity-relationship (E-R) diagram shows each table (entity) as a box and each relationship as a line, with a bar at the one end and a crow's foot at the many end. To draw one from a list of tables, find each foreign key: the table that holds it is on the many side and the table where it is the primary key is on the one side; a table that holds two foreign keys is usually a linking table. Normalisation organises data to reduce redundancy: 1NF has no repeating groups and every field is atomic; 2NF is in 1NF and has no partial dependencies (every non-key field depends on the whole of the composite key); 3NF is in 2NF and has no non-key (transitive) dependencies (every non-key field depends on the primary key and nothing else). Exam questions ask you to complete an E-R diagram, describe the normal forms, say what normal form a database is in, and normalise a given table.

1 · E-R diagrams

The tables PLANT, CUSTOMER, PURCHASE and PURCHASE_ITEM give this diagram. PURCHASE_ITEM(PurchaseID, PlantName, Quantity) holds the foreign key PurchaseID (to PURCHASE) and PlantName (to PLANT): a linking table, so each of those relationships is one-to-many from the other table to PURCHASE_ITEM.

  1. List every table and its foreign keys.
  2. For each foreign key, draw a line to the table where it is the primary key: that table is the "one" side, the table holding the foreign key is the "many" side.
  3. A many-to-many relationship (a linking table holding the keys of two tables) appears as two one-to-many lines meeting at the linking table.
  4. Draw no extra lines: the mark scheme takes marks off for extra relationships.

2 · Normal forms

Normal formRuleFixing a table
1NFNo repeating groups or repeating attributes; every field holds one value (atomic); a primary key is identifiedSplit repeated fields into their own table, with the key of the first table as a foreign key; split a field such as a full name into FirstName and LastName
2NFIn 1NF and no partial dependencies: every non-key attribute depends on the whole of the (composite) primary keyMove attributes that depend on only part of the key into a new table
3NFIn 2NF and no non-key (transitive) dependencies: every attribute depends on the primary key and no other attributeMove attributes that depend on another non-key attribute into a new table

A database in 3NF has no repeating groups (data is atomic), no partial key dependencies and no non-key dependencies; it also has no many-to-many relationships (they are broken up with linking tables).

Common ways to lose marks

  • Drawing an E-R relationship the wrong way round: the crow's foot (many) end is at the table holding the foreign key.
  • Adding extra lines to an E-R diagram, or leaving a linking table disconnected from one of its tables.
  • Describing 2NF without saying "partial dependency" and what it depends on (part of a composite key).
  • Giving only the name of the normal form when the question asks for the justification.
  • Normalising without a primary key in each new table, or without the foreign keys that link them.

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

A school's form list

A student's subjects are in one cell. Splitting them into their own table, one row per subject, is first normal form.

Exam use: 1NF.

A subject name repeated in every enrolment

If every row of an enrolment table repeats 'Computer Science' with its code, the name depends only on the code: move it to a SUBJECT table.

Exam use: 2NF.

A tutor's name stored with every student

The name depends on the tutor group, not on the student; move it to a TUTOR_GROUP table so a name change is made once.

Exam use: 3NF.

A diagram before building

Database designers sketch an E-R diagram so everyone agrees how tables relate before a line of SQL is written.

Exam use: E-R diagram.

A cinema booking database

Many shows, many customers and many seats: linking tables turn those many-to-many relationships into one-to-many ones.

Exam use: linking tables.

Top tips

  • Crow's foot at the foreign key end. The 'many' side holds the foreign key.
  • No extra lines. Only draw the relationships in the table list.
  • 1NF: repeats and atomic. 2NF: partial. 3NF: non-key.
  • Name the dependency. Partial: part of the composite key.
  • A linking table has two foreign keys. Two one-to-many lines.
  • Give each new table a primary key. And mark the foreign keys.
