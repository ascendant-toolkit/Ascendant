The Keynesian 45° Diagram Explained

A step-by-step explainer of the Keynesian 45° diagram: what the axes show, the 45° line, building the AE line, equilibrium, shifts and the multiplier, saving and injections, and the gaps. With animations, a build-your-own lab, drills and exam questions.

This part in four sentences

The Keynesian 45° diagram (also called the Keynesian cross) plots aggregate expenditure (AE) on the vertical axis against national income (Y) on the horizontal axis. The 45° line is the set of points where expenditure equals income, so it shows every possible equilibrium. The AE line shows how much people, firms, the government and foreigners would plan to spend at each level of income, and equilibrium is where the two lines cross. Everything else in 9.1, the multiplier, the gaps, saving and injections, can be read off this one diagram.

1 · What the axes measure

  • Horizontal axis: national income, Y. In a closed circular flow, income, output and expenditure are three ways of measuring the same thing, so Y is also the value of output. It is real income, because the price level is held constant in this model.
  • Vertical axis: aggregate expenditure, AE. The total planned spending in the economy: AE = C + I + G + (X − M).
  • Both axes use the same scale. This is what makes the line at 45° meaningful.
AE is not ADThe aggregate demand curve has the price level on the vertical axis and shows total spending at different price levels. The AE line has expenditure on the vertical axis and shows total spending at different levels of income, with the price level fixed. They look different on the page and answer different questions.

2 · The 45° line

Because both axes have the same scale, any point on a line from the origin at 45° has a horizontal value equal to its vertical value. So at every point on the 45° line, expenditure equals income: 200 on one axis is 200 on the other. It is not a behavioural line. It is a reference line that answers one question: at what levels of income would spending exactly match the output produced?

That matters because output only stays at a steady level when buyers purchase all of it. If spending is greater than output, stocks (inventories) run down and firms produce more. If spending is less than output, unsold goods pile up and firms cut production. National income can therefore only settle at a point on the 45° line.

3 · The consumption line

Consumption depends on income. Using the consumption function C = a + cY with autonomous consumption a = 100 and an MPC of c = 0.6, households spend 100 even at zero income and 60 cents of each extra dollar of income.

  • The intercept (100) is autonomous consumption. It sets where the line starts on the vertical axis.
  • The slope (0.6) is the MPC. A steeper line means that more of each extra dollar is spent.
  • Where C meets the 45° line (Y = 250 here, because 100 + 0.6Y = Y) is the break-even level of income: households spend exactly what they earn, so saving is zero. Below it they dissave. Above it they save.
  • Saving at any income is the vertical gap between the 45° line and the C line, because S = Y − C. This is why the C line is always flatter than the 45° line: the MPC is less than 1.
Same line as on the consumption pageThe dotted C = Y line on the consumption function chart in the consumption, saving and investment page is this 45° line, and the break-even point is the same idea.

4 · Adding investment, government spending and net exports

Aggregate expenditure adds the other components on top of C.

Add thisTreated asWhat happens to the line
Investment, IAutonomous (set by interest rates, confidence and expected profit, not by current income)The C line shifts up in parallel by the amount of I. The line is now C + I.
Government spending, GAutonomous (set by policy)Shifts up again in parallel: C + I + G.
Net exports, X − MExports are autonomous (set by foreign income and competitiveness). Imports rise with income.Exports shift the line up. Imports flatten the slope, because part of each extra dollar of income is spent abroad. If net exports are negative the line shifts down.
TaxesPart of income is taxed, so disposable income rises by less than incomeFlattens the slope. The slope becomes c(1 − t) − m.

The top line is AE = C + I + G + (X − M). Its intercept is total autonomous spending, and its slope is the share of each extra dollar of income that is spent on domestic output: MPC × (1 − MRT) − MPM. Because every leakage reduces the slope, the AE line is always flatter than the 45° line, so the two lines must cross somewhere.

5 · Equilibrium, and why it is stable

Equilibrium national income is where the AE line crosses the 45° line. With C = 100 + 0.6Y, I = 50 and G = 50 the AE line is 200 + 0.6Y, so equilibrium is where Y = 200 + 0.6Y, which gives Y = 500 (that is Y = autonomous spending × multiplier = 200 × 2.5).

  • Below equilibrium (for example Y = 300): AE = 200 + 0.6 × 300 = 380, which is more than output. Stocks fall unexpectedly, so firms raise production and hire more. Income rises towards 500.
  • Above equilibrium (for example Y = 700): AE = 620, which is less than output. Unsold stocks build up, so firms cut production. Income falls towards 500.

The economy moves towards equilibrium from either side, so the equilibrium is stable. The AE line is planned spending. Differences between planned and actual spending show up as unplanned changes in stocks, which is the signal firms respond to.

6 · A shift versus a movement along the line

Movement along the AE lineShift of the AE line
Caused byA change in incomeA change in autonomous spending (confidence, interest rates, G, taxes, exports, wealth)
ExampleIncome rises, so induced consumption risesA fall in the interest rate raises investment
Effect on equilibriumNone by itself. It is how the economy moves to equilibrium.A new equilibrium at a different income

A rise in autonomous spending shifts AE up by, say, 20. Equilibrium income does not rise by 20. It rises by 20 × the multiplier, which is 50 when the multiplier is 2.5. On the diagram the vertical shift is small and the horizontal change in equilibrium is larger. The reason is the chain of spending: the extra 20 becomes income, 60% of it is spent, that becomes income, and so on. In the third animation you can see the staircase between the AE line and the 45° line that closes in on the new equilibrium.

7 · Saving, leakages and injections on the same diagram

At equilibrium AE = Y, so C + I + G = Y. Subtract C from both sides: I + G = Y − C = S. In an economy with no tax and no trade, saving equals investment plus government spending. With taxes and trade the condition becomes the familiar one:

Equilibrium: S + T + M = I + G + X (leakages = injections)

The two ways of finding equilibrium always give the same answer. The 45° approach looks at expenditure against output. The injections and withdrawals approach looks at what is put into the circular flow against what leaks out. In the fourth animation, the vertical gap between the C line and the 45° line at Y = 500 (saving) is exactly the gap between C and the AE line (I + G).

8 · The full employment level and the gaps

Mark the full employment level of national income (YF) on the horizontal axis. If the AE line cuts the 45° line to the left of YF, AE at YF is below the 45° line and there is a deflationary gap, measured as the vertical distance at YF. If it cuts to the right of YF, there is an inflationary gap. These are covered in detail in the page on equilibrium and the gaps, and you can experiment with them there.

9 · What the model assumes

  • The price level is fixed and firms respond to a rise in spending by producing more, not by raising prices. This is realistic when there is spare capacity (a deflationary gap), and not near full employment.
  • Interest rates, expectations and the money supply are held constant, so investment is treated as autonomous.
  • Planned spending is what matters, and the model says nothing about how long the adjustment takes.
  • The multiplier is the same for every injection, which ignores differences between spending on imports and on home goods.

It is a Keynesian model. Classical and monetarist economists argue that the economy self-corrects to full employment, so for them the cross does not describe the long run.

10 · Common mistakes

MistakeFix
Drawing an AD curve with the price level on the axisLabel the vertical axis aggregate expenditure and the horizontal axis national income
An AE line steeper than the 45° lineIt must be flatter, because the MPC is below 1 and leakages reduce the slope
Starting the AE line at the originIt starts on the vertical axis at autonomous spending
Saying a rise in investment raises income by the same amountIncome rises by the change in investment times the multiplier
Measuring the gap horizontallyThe gap is the vertical distance at full employment. The horizontal distance is the change in income needed.
Confusing the shift with the movementAutonomous spending shifts the line. A change in income moves along it.

11 · How to draw it in an exam

  1. Label the axes: national income (Y) and aggregate expenditure (AE).
  2. Draw the 45° line from the origin and label it.
  3. Draw the AE line starting above the origin, sloping up more gently than the 45° line.
  4. Mark equilibrium where they cross and drop a dashed line to the axis (Y).
  5. If the question is about the gap, mark YF and show the vertical distance between the lines at YF.
  6. For a change, draw the new AE line parallel to the old and mark the new equilibrium, showing that income rises by more than the shift.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

A recession and the shape of the diagram

When confidence collapses, firms cut investment and households cut spending, so autonomous spending falls. The AE line shifts down, equilibrium income falls by a multiple of the shift, and a deflationary gap may open if income falls below the full employment level.

Exam use: using the diagram to show how a fall in confidence causes a downturn. Mention the multiplier.

A fiscal stimulus

A government raises its spending to lift a weak economy. In the diagram this shifts AE up, and income rises by the injection times the multiplier. If households save a large share of the extra income, the multiplier is smaller and the effect weaker.

Exam use: evaluating fiscal policy. Say that the size of the effect depends on the slope of the AE line.

A boom in a trading partner

If a country's main customers grow quickly, their demand for its exports rises. Exports are autonomous, so the AE line shifts up and equilibrium income rises by a multiple of the extra exports.

Exam use: showing how foreign income feeds into national income through net exports.

A rise in interest rates

Higher interest rates make borrowing dearer and raise the reward for saving, so autonomous investment and consumption may fall. The AE line shifts down and equilibrium income falls.

Exam use: showing how monetary policy may work through AE. Mention lags and uncertainty.

Practice (not a past paper question) 8 marks Using a 45° diagram, explain how an increase in investment could raise national income by more than the increase in investment.
What the mark scheme wants
  • A labelled 45° diagram with an AE line shifting upwards, the old and new equilibrium marked.
  • The rise in investment is an increase in autonomous spending, which shifts the AE line up.
  • National income rises by the multiplier times the increase, because each round of extra income leads to further spending, with leakages reducing each round.
  • Evaluation: the effect is smaller if the multiplier is small, and may be mostly price rises near full employment.

Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Aggregate expenditure is the total planned spending in an economy at each level of income, and equilibrium national income is where the AE line cuts the 45° line, because only there does expenditure equal output. Investment is autonomous spending, so a rise in investment shifts the AE line upwards in parallel. At the old level of income, expenditure is now greater than output, stocks fall and firms produce more.

The extra output creates extra income for workers and owners, who spend a proportion of it, and this becomes income for others, so national income rises through several rounds until the new AE line cuts the 45° line at a higher level of income. The final rise is the change in investment multiplied by the multiplier, which is greater than the original rise. However, the rounds get smaller because part of each extra dollar leaks out in saving, taxes and imports, and if the economy is near full employment, much of the extra spending may raise prices rather than output.

Top tips

  • Say what each axis measures. National income on the horizontal and aggregate expenditure on the vertical.
  • Keep the AE line flatter than the 45° line. A line steeper than the 45° line would imply an MPC above 1.
  • Start AE above the origin. The intercept is autonomous spending.
  • Show the new equilibrium, not only the shift. Draw the new AE line parallel to the old, then mark where it crosses the 45° line.
  • Say income rises by a multiple. The horizontal change in income is larger than the vertical shift.
  • Measure the gap vertically. At full employment, between the 45° line and AE.
  • Link to leakages and injections. At equilibrium S + T + M = I + G + X. It is the same equilibrium from another angle.
