Influences on Households' Spending, Saving and Borrowing

How income, interest rates, confidence, age and culture shape what households do with their money. Syllabus 3.2.1.

The big idea

Households choose how to divide their income between spending, saving and borrowing. Their decisions are influenced by income, the rate of interest, confidence, age and culture. These choices matter for firms (sales), banks (deposits and loans) and the whole economy (aggregate demand).

Influences on households

InfluenceEffect on spendingEffect on savingEffect on borrowing
IncomeHigher income: more spendingHigher income: more saving (a larger share at high incomes)Lower income: more borrowing to cover needs; higher income can support more borrowing
Rate of interestHigher rates: less spending on creditHigher rates: more saving (higher reward)Higher rates: less borrowing (dearer)
ConfidenceConfident households spend morePessimistic households save more (precaution)Confident households borrow more
AgeYoung families spend on housing and children; the old spend on healthcareMiddle-aged save for retirement; the old spend savingsYoung adults borrow for homes and education
CultureCultures valuing status may spend moreSome cultures stress thrift and family savingAttitudes to debt vary (religion, tradition)

Consumption and saving

Income = consumption (spending) + saving (+ tax)
Saving ratio = saving ÷ income × 100
  • If households spend more, aggregate demand rises, helping growth and jobs.
  • If households save more, banks have more to lend, but spending falls in the short run.
  • Too much borrowing can lead to debt problems when interest rates rise.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain how a rise in interest rates may affect households' spending and saving.
ReminderSaving up; borrowing and spending down.
A higher interest rate raises the reward for saving, so households may save more and spend less. It also makes borrowing dearer, so households borrow less and spending falls.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain how confidence may influence households' spending.
ReminderOptimism raises spending; fear lowers.
If households are confident about jobs and incomes they spend and borrow more. If they are worried they save more as a precaution and spend less, so firms' sales fall.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how age may influence households' spending, saving and borrowing.
ReminderYoung borrow; middle save; old spend savings.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Young adults often borrow to buy homes or study and have low savings. Middle-aged households have higher incomes and save for retirement. Older households spend savings and on healthcare. Differences depend on income and culture.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether income is the most important influence on household spending.
ReminderIncome vs interest rate, confidence, age, culture. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Income decides how much households can spend. Higher income raises spending and saving. However, interest rates change the cost of borrowing and reward for saving. Confidence and age also matter. Overall, income is the main influence but others can outweigh it in the short run, for example a fall in confidence during a recession.

Top tips

  • Five influences. Income, interest, confidence, age, culture.
  • State the direction. Up or down, and why.
  • Link to firms and banks. Sales and deposits.
  • Evaluate with 'depends on'. Income level, country.
