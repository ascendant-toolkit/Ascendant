This page in five sentences

Quantum cryptography uses the laws of quantum physics, the properties of photons (particles of light), instead of mathematical formulae to create and exchange encryption keys, aiming to give a virtually unbreakable system. Photons are sent down a fibre-optic cable with a polarisation that carries the bits of the key. Measuring a photon changes its state, so if someone eavesdrops the errors this causes can be detected and the key thrown away and another requested. Quantum states cannot be copied (the no-cloning theorem), so the key cannot be duplicated and kept for later. The drawbacks are the cost, the need for dedicated fibre and specialist hardware, a limited range, and that polarisation can be altered on the way, giving errors.

1 · Purpose

Ordinary encryption is protected by hard mathematics: the keys are safe only as long as nobody can work out the answer fast enough. Quantum cryptography is used to:

  • provide a virtually unbreakable method of encryption, or send virtually un-hackable secure messages;
  • do this using the laws and principles of quantum mechanics (properties of photons) rather than mathematical algorithms;
  • detect eavesdropping, because the properties of photons change when they are observed;
  • protect the security of data sent over fibre-optic cables, and allow longer keys to be exchanged.

Its job on this syllabus is the secure exchange of keys: when the keys have been exchanged securely, the message itself is sent.

2 · How it works (the idea)

  1. The sender sends a stream of photons, each given one of several polarisations that stand for a 0 or a 1.
  2. The receiver measures each photon and records the bit.
  3. If an eavesdropper measures a photon on its way, they disturb its state, and the receiver gets some bits wrong.
  4. The two sides compare a sample of their bits over an ordinary channel. If more errors appear than the equipment would cause alone, an eavesdropper is detected, the key is discarded and a new one is requested.
  5. If the sample matches, the rest of the bits form a secret key, which is then used to encrypt the message.

4 · Common ways to lose marks

  • Saying quantum cryptography is "uncrackable" with no reason. Say it uses the laws of physics and detects eavesdropping.
  • Giving the drawback "it is expensive" only. Say what costs: dedicated fibre and specialist hardware.
  • Writing that it protects data on wireless networks. It relies on fibre-optic cables.
  • Confusing it with asymmetric encryption: quantum cryptography is a different, third type used to exchange keys safely.
  • Giving two benefits that mean the same thing, such as "secure" and "hard to hack". Pick two different ideas.

6 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Banks and governments

Organisations with very valuable secrets can use quantum key exchange between two sites linked by fibre. If anyone taps the cable, the errors reveal it.

Exam use: eavesdropping can be detected.

Satellite experiments

Because fibre only works over short distances, researchers have sent quantum keys by satellite to test longer links.

Exam use: range is a limit for fibre-based systems.

Future-proofing

Some computers of the future could break today's mathematical encryption. Quantum cryptography does not rely on that mathematics.

Exam use: security from physics, not algorithms.

Opening a sealed envelope

A paper letter in a wax-sealed envelope shows if someone has opened it. Measuring a photon changes it in the same way, so tampering shows.

Exam use: observing a quantum state disturbs it.

Dedicated lines

Quantum links need their own fibre and special hardware, which is why they are used by a few large organisations and not by ordinary home users.

Exam use: high cost and specialist hardware.

Top tips

  • Purpose: unbreakable by physics. Uses the laws of quantum mechanics, not mathematical algorithms.
  • Eavesdropping detected. Photons change when measured.
  • Two different benefits. Detects eavesdropping, longer keys, no-cloning, protects fibre data.
  • Drawbacks need a reason. Dedicated fibre, specialist hardware, short range, polarisation altered.
  • It exchanges keys. The message is sent after the secure key exchange.
  • Fibre-optic cables. Photons travel along them.
