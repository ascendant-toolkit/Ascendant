Wage Determination

How labour demand and supply, trade unions, government policy and minimum wages determine pay. Syllabus 3.3.2.

The big idea

The wage in a labour market is set by the demand for labour (from firms) and the supply of labour (from workers). The demand curve slopes down (firms hire more at lower wages); supply slopes up (more people want to work at higher wages). The equilibrium wage and employment are where they meet. Trade unions, government policy and a national minimum wage can change the outcome.

Trade unions

  • Unions bargain collectively for higher wages and better conditions.
  • Their bargaining power is greater if many workers are members, the workers have rare skills, demand for the product is inelastic or the firm is profitable; it is weaker if unemployment is high or workers are easily replaced.
  • Higher union wages may reduce employment if demand for labour is elastic.

Government policy and the national minimum wage (NMW)

  • A national minimum wage is a legal minimum rate of pay. It is a minimum price in the labour market.
  • If it is set above the equilibrium wage, a surplus of labour (unemployment) can appear; workers in jobs gain.
  • If it is set below equilibrium it has no effect.
  • Benefits: reduces poverty and exploitation, raises incentives to work. Costs: firms' costs rise, possible job losses, prices may rise.
  • Other policies: taxes, benefits, education and training, and immigration rules affect the supply and demand for labour.

Q1 Define 2 marks Define a national minimum wage.
ReminderLegal minimum pay rate.
A national minimum wage is a legal minimum rate of pay that employers must pay workers.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two influences on the demand for labour.
ReminderProduct demand, productivity, cost of capital.
Demand for the firm's product: if it rises, firms need more workers. Labour demand shifts right. Productivity: more productive workers are worth more to firms, so firms hire more at each wage.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse, using a labour market diagram, how an increase in the supply of labour could affect wages and employment.
ReminderS right; wage down; employment up.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
An increase in supply, for example from immigration, shifts the supply curve to the right. At the old wage there is now a surplus of labour, so the equilibrium wage falls and employment rises. The effect depends on how elastic labour demand is.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether a national minimum wage will benefit workers.
ReminderHigher pay, reduced poverty vs unemployment, higher costs. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A minimum wage sets a legal minimum for pay. It raises incomes for low-paid workers, reduces poverty and may raise motivation. However, if it is above equilibrium, firms may hire fewer workers, causing unemployment. Firms' costs rise and may be passed on as higher prices. Overall, a modest minimum wage is likely to benefit most workers, but a very high one could cost jobs, particularly for young or unskilled workers.
