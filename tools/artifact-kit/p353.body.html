Production and Productivity

The difference between production and productivity, what influences them and how investment affects productivity. Syllabus 3.5.3.

The big idea

Production is the total output of goods and services. Productivity is output per unit of input, for example output per worker per hour. A firm can raise production by using more inputs, but only raising productivity increases output per unit of input and lowers unit costs.

Production and productivity

Production = total output
Labour productivity = total output ÷ number of workers (or hours)
ProductionProductivity
MeaningHow much is produced in totalHow efficiently inputs are used: output per worker or per machine

Influences on production and productivity

  • Quantity and quality of labour: more or better-trained workers; health and motivation.
  • Capital and technology: machinery, equipment and automation.
  • Management and organisation: division of labour, good planning.
  • Working conditions and incentives: pay, bonuses, job satisfaction.
  • Infrastructure and energy supplies.
  • Natural resources and raw materials.

Effects of investment on productivity

  • Investment in new machinery and technology lets each worker produce more.
  • Investment in training and education (human capital) raises workers' skills.
  • Higher productivity lowers unit costs, raising profit, lowering prices and increasing competitiveness and economic growth.
  • Investment costs money now and may take time to pay off.

Q1 Define 2 marks Define productivity.
ReminderOutput per unit of input.
Productivity is output per unit of input, such as output per worker per hour.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain the difference between production and productivity.
ReminderTotal vs per input.
Production is the total output of a firm or economy. For example, a factory makes 1,000 shirts a day. Productivity is output per unit of input. For example, each worker makes 20 shirts a day.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain two influences on the productivity of workers.
ReminderTraining, technology, management, motivation.
Training raises skills, so each worker produces more per hour. Better machinery and technology help workers produce more with the same effort.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how an increase in investment could affect a firm's productivity and costs.
ReminderMachinery and training; output per worker; unit cost; competitiveness.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Investment in machinery and training raises output per worker. Higher productivity lowers unit costs. The firm can cut prices or increase profit and become more competitive. Investment is costly and takes time to pay off, so benefits depend on demand and how well the new equipment is used.

Top tips

  • Production is total; productivity is per input. Use a number example.
  • Calculate output ÷ workers. Show working.
  • Investment raises productivity and growth. Machinery and training.
  • Evaluate: costs and time. Investment is not free.
