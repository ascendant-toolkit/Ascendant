Definitions of Inflation and Deflation

What inflation, disinflation and deflation mean and how inflation changes the value of money. Syllabus 4.7.1.

The big idea

Inflation is a sustained (persistent) rise in the general price level over time. Deflation is a sustained fall in the general price level. Inflation means each unit of money buys less, so its purchasing power falls. The inflation rate is the percentage rise in the price level over a year. Stable, low inflation is one of the government's macroeconomic aims.

Three different situations

TermWhat happensExample
InflationPrice level rises over timePrices rise 4% a year
DisinflationThe rate of inflation falls, but prices are still risingInflation falls from 8% to 2%: prices still rise, more slowly
DeflationThe price level falls (negative inflation rate)Inflation of −2%: prices are 2% lower than a year ago

Common mistake: a fall in the inflation rate is not deflation. Deflation needs the price level itself to fall.

What inflation does to the value of money

If prices rise 5% a year, $100 buys about 5% less each year. The money is worth less: the purchasing power of money falls. This is why inflation matters for savers, workers on fixed pay and anyone on a fixed income.

Inflation rate = (price level this year − price level last year) ÷ price level last year × 100

Levels of inflation

  • Low, stable inflation (around 2% in many countries) is the usual target of central banks. A little inflation is normal in a growing economy.
  • High inflation (for example 10% or more) causes problems for savers, workers, firms and exporters.
  • Hyperinflation is extremely rapid inflation (often defined as over 50% a month), when money loses its value so fast that people stop using it.
  • Deflation may sound good (cheaper goods) but is often harmful: see 4.7.4.

A rise in one price is not inflation. Inflation is about the general price level, usually measured by the Consumer Prices Index (4.7.2).

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Inflation above 10% in 2022

After energy and food prices surged, inflation passed 10% in several European countries in 2022, and central banks raised interest rates sharply.

Exam use: High inflation, its causes and policy response.

Japan's deflation

Japan had falling or flat prices for much of the 1990s and 2000s. Consumers expected prices to fall, so delayed spending, and growth was weak.

Exam use: Deflation and why it can harm an economy.

Hyperinflation in Zimbabwe

In the late 2000s Zimbabwe's prices rose so fast that people carried bundles of notes, and the currency was abandoned.

Exam use: Hyperinflation and money losing its value.

The 2% target

Central banks in the UK, the euro area and the US aim for around 2% inflation, believing it gives stability while allowing prices to adjust.

Exam use: Price stability does not mean zero inflation.

Q2 Define 2 marks Define deflation.
ReminderSustained fall in the general price level.
Deflation is a sustained fall in the general price level (a negative inflation rate).
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain the difference between disinflation and deflation.
ReminderUse 'whereas'. Disinflation: rate falls, prices still rise. Deflation: prices fall.
Disinflation is a fall in the rate of inflation, so prices are still rising but more slowly, for example inflation falling from 8% to 3%, whereas deflation is a fall in the price level itself, so the inflation rate is negative and goods get cheaper.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain what is meant by an inflation rate of 4%, and how it affects the purchasing power of money.
ReminderState the meaning, then the effect: money buys less.
An inflation rate of 4% means the general price level is 4% higher than a year earlier. Each unit of money buys about 4% fewer goods and services, so $100 that bought a basket last year would buy less than the full basket now. The purchasing power of money has fallen.

Top tips

  • Inflation = sustained rise in the general price level. Both 'general' and 'sustained' earn marks.
  • Disinflation is not deflation. A falling inflation rate still means prices rise.
  • Link to purchasing power. Inflation means each unit of money buys less.
  • Use the formula. (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
