Shifts of Demand and Supply and Movements Along Curves

2.1.5 to 2.1.7: causes of shifts of the demand and supply curves and the distinction between a shift and a movement along a curve. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

A movement along a demand or supply curve is caused only by a change in the price of the good itself, and is called an extension or contraction of demand, or an expansion or contraction of supply. A shift of the curve is caused by a change in any other determinant: income, the prices of related goods, tastes, population or expectations for demand; costs, technology, taxes and subsidies, the number of firms and weather for supply. A shift to the right is an increase in demand or supply (more at every price); a shift to the left is a decrease. Always draw the new curve, label the new equilibrium and say which way price and quantity move.

1 · Movement along a curve (2.1.7)

  • Price falls: extension of demand (quantity demanded rises) and contraction of supply (quantity supplied falls).
  • Price rises: contraction of demand and extension (expansion) of supply.
  • The curve itself does not move. A fall in price caused by a rise in supply moves you along the demand curve.
  • Terminology: say quantity demanded or quantity supplied changes for a movement; say demand or supply changes for a shift.

  • A shift of one curve causes a movement along the other (a rise in demand moves you up the supply curve).

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Petrol and public transport

A rise in the price of petrol moves drivers up the supply curve for petrol (contraction of demand) but shifts the demand curve for bus journeys to the right, because buses are a substitute for driving.

Exam use: separating the price of the good (movement) from the price of a related good (shift).

Health scare for eggs

A health scare lowers demand for eggs at every price, so the demand curve shifts left. The price falls, which moves farmers down their supply curve: a contraction in supply.

Exam use: a shift of demand leading to a movement along supply.

Cheaper smartphones

Cheaper components and better production methods shift the supply curve for smartphones to the right. The price falls and the quantity sold rises, an extension of demand along an unchanged demand curve.

Exam use: a shift of supply causing a movement along demand.

Cinema ticket discount

A cinema cuts ticket prices by 20%. More people go (extension along the demand curve). If it launched an advertising campaign instead, the whole curve would move right.

Exam use: the difference between a price change and an advertising campaign.

Based on Oct/Nov 2024 · 9708/22 · Q2(a) 8 marks With the help of a diagram, briefly explain how the cause of a movement along the demand curve differs from the cause of a shift in the demand curve and consider the impact on the demand for one good of price changes in other goods.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 3): an accurate demand diagram, or two diagrams, showing a movement and a shift (1); a movement occurs because of a change in price (1); a shift occurs because of a change in a non-price factor (1).
  • Analysis (up to 3): the impact on demand for one good depending on whether other goods are substitutes or complements: a rise in the price of a complement lowers demand because of joint demand (up to 2); a rise in the price of a substitute raises demand because consumers switch (up to 2). Maximum 2 if only one relationship is analysed. Changes in income, fashion or tastes are not rewarded.
  • Evaluation (up to 2): the overall impact depends on the size of the cross elasticity values (1) and a valid conclusion about the size of the impact (1).
PlanDiagram: movement along (price), shift (non-price). Substitute up: demand up. Complement up: demand down. Judge using XED size.

A movement along the demand curve happens when the price of the good itself changes, so quantity demanded changes, for example from a fall in price causing an extension. A shift happens when a non-price factor changes, such as income or the price of a related good, so demand changes at every price and the curve moves left or right.

If the price of a substitute such as tea rises, some consumers switch to coffee, so demand for coffee shifts to the right. If the price of a complement such as milk rises, coffee drinking becomes more expensive, so demand for coffee shifts to the left because of joint demand.

The overall impact depends on the cross elasticity of demand: if the XED coefficients are small, the substitutes or complements are weakly related and the shift will be small, while large values mean a large shift. So price changes in other goods could matter a great deal for some products and hardly at all for others.

Based on May/June 2022 · 9708/22 · Q3(a) 8 marks With the help of diagrams, use the concept of income elasticity of demand to explain the impact of a fall in incomes on the equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity of a normal good and an inferior good.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 2): the meaning of income elasticity of demand (1) with an accurate formula (1).
  • Application (up to 6): for a normal good, its meaning (1), a diagram showing the demand curve shifting left (1) and a fall in equilibrium price and quantity (1); for an inferior good, its meaning (1), a separate diagram showing the demand curve shifting right (1) and a rise in equilibrium price and quantity (1). One diagram for both goods is not rewarded.
PlanFormula and meaning. Normal good: left shift, price and quantity fall. Inferior good: right shift, price and quantity rise. Two diagrams.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Income elasticity of demand measures how demand responds to a change in income: percentage change in quantity demanded divided by percentage change in income.

For a normal good, income elasticity is positive, because demand rises as income rises. A fall in incomes therefore shifts demand to the left and, with supply unchanged, the equilibrium price and quantity both fall. For an inferior good, income elasticity is negative, so a fall in incomes shifts demand to the right, and the equilibrium price and quantity both rise. Each effect should be shown on a separate diagram.

Top tips

  • Own price: movement. Anything else: shift. This is the most tested rule in the topic.
  • Use the right terms. Extension and contraction for movements; increase and decrease for shifts.
  • Name the direction of both price and quantity. Right shift of demand: price and quantity rise.
  • Draw a separate diagram for each change. Two goods or two changes mean two diagrams unless told otherwise.
  • Normal and inferior goods differ. A fall in income shifts demand left for a normal good, right for an inferior good.
  • Costs shift supply, not demand. A cost for the producer never shifts the demand curve.
  • One shift causes a movement on the other curve. A demand rise moves you up the supply curve.
