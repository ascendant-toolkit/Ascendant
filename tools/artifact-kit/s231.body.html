Individual and Market Supply

What supply is, why the curve slopes upwards, and how individual supplies add to market supply. Syllabus 2.3.1.

The big idea

Supply is the quantity of a good or service that producers are willing and able to sell at a given price in a given period. The law of supply: as the price of a good rises, the quantity supplied rises (and vice versa), ceteris paribus. The supply curve therefore slopes upwards from left to right. Market supply is the sum of all individual firms' supplies.

Why the supply curve slopes upwards

  • Profit motive: a higher price means a higher profit per unit, so firms want to produce more.
  • Rising marginal costs: producing more usually costs more per extra unit (overtime, less suitable resources), so firms need a higher price to cover them.
  • New entrants: higher prices attract more firms into the market.

The market supply curve is found by adding up the quantities all firms will supply at each price. More firms in the market means more supply at every price.

The supply diagram

  • Price on the vertical axis, quantity supplied on the horizontal axis.
  • An upward-sloping line labelled S.
  • At price P producers supply quantity Q.

Supply means willing and able to sell: producers need the resources and capacity as well as the wish to sell. A supply curve assumes everything except price is unchanged.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Farmers and crop prices

When the price of wheat rises, farmers plant more and supply more.

Exam use: Price up, quantity supplied up.

Surge pricing

Taxis and ride apps pay drivers more at busy times, and more drivers come online.

Exam use: Higher price, more supplied.

More firms

As a new industry grows more firms enter, raising market supply at every price.

Exam use: Market supply.

Oil production

Higher oil prices make expensive wells profitable, so output rises.

Exam use: Rising marginal cost.

Top tips

  • Supply = willing AND able to sell. At a given price.
  • Price on the vertical axis. Label S, price, quantity.
  • Market supply = sum of individuals. Add horizontally.
  • Explain with profit and rising marginal cost. Name them.
  • Say ceteris paribus. Other things unchanged.
