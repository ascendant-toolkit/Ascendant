Reasons for Government Spending

Why governments spend, the main areas of spending and their effects, and how the multiplier magnifies an injection. Syllabus 4.2.2.

The big idea

Governments spend for four main reasons: to influence economic activity, to reduce market failure, to promote equity and to pay interest on the national debt. Spending is a powerful tool because of the multiplier effect: each dollar spent is passed on as income to others, so total demand rises by more than the first injection.

The four reasons

Influence economic activity

More spending raises G, a part of AD, which could increase output, growth and employment. Less spending can cool an overheating economy.

Reduce market failure

Public goods (defence, street lighting) will not be provided by private firms. Merit goods (education, healthcare) would be under-provided. Spending can also pay for regulators and environmental protection.

Promote equity

Pensions, unemployment benefit, housing support and free schooling help people on low incomes and could reduce inequality.

Pay interest on debt

Earlier borrowing has to be paid for each year. This spending buys no new services, which is why a large debt has an opportunity cost.

The multiplier effect

Spending is a powerful tool because of the multiplier effect: the final rise in AD is bigger than the first injection. If $20m of new spending is passed on in rounds (people spend $16m, then $12.8m and so on), total income and output could rise by $100m, five times the first change. The multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − share of extra income spent).

Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − share of extra income spent)  ·  Total change in income = initial injection × multiplier

The more people save, pay in tax or spend on imports out of extra income, the smaller each round and the smaller the multiplier. These are the leakages.

Evaluating spending

  • Opportunity cost: money spent on one area cannot be spent on another.
  • Time lags: building a hospital or training teachers takes years.
  • Borrowing: if spending is not funded by tax, the deficit and debt interest rise.
  • Spare capacity: if the economy is already close to full capacity, extra spending could mostly raise prices.

Stimulus after a crisis

After the 2008 financial crisis and during the COVID-19 pandemic, many governments raised spending (support payments, wage subsidies, infrastructure) and some cut taxes temporarily such as lowering VAT rates. Deficits and national debt rose sharply as a result.

Exam use: expansionary fiscal policy in action. Cover AD, growth and jobs, then the cost: bigger deficit, higher debt and interest payments.

Spending on health in a pandemic

During the COVID-19 pandemic many governments sharply raised healthcare spending on hospitals, testing and vaccines, and paid wage support to keep people in work.

Exam use: Reason for spending: reduce market failure (a merit good) and influence economic activity. Evaluate with the size of the resulting deficit.

Infrastructure for growth

Many governments fund roads, rail, ports and broadband. Large projects such as new high-speed rail lines have often cost more and taken longer than first planned.

Exam use: Short-run AD effect, long-run AS effect, then evaluate with opportunity cost and time lags.

Large pension bills

In countries with ageing populations, pensions and healthcare take a growing share of government spending, leaving less for other areas.

Exam use: Opportunity cost of spending and why governments may need to raise taxes or borrow more.

Q1 Define 2 marks What is meant by the multiplier effect?
ReminderTwo ideas: an initial change in spending, and a final change in income bigger than that.
The multiplier effect is when an initial injection of spending, such as government spending, leads to a larger final increase in aggregate demand and national income, as the money is spent and re-spent.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two reasons why a government spends on healthcare.
ReminderTwo separate reasons, each developed. Merit good, equity, productivity, market failure.
Healthcare is a merit good that people may under-consume if they have to pay, so governments provide it to make sure people with low incomes can still receive treatment, which could improve equity. A second reason is to improve productivity. Healthier people take fewer sick days and may work more effectively, which could raise output and long-run growth.
Q3 Calculate 4 marks The government increases spending on a new railway by $40m. People spend 75% of any extra income. Calculate the total rise in income.
ReminderMultiplier first, then multiply by the injection. Units.
Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − 0.75) = 1 ÷ 0.25 = 4. Total rise in income = 4 × $40m = $160m.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how an increase in government spending on infrastructure could affect an economy.
ReminderShort run (AD, multiplier) then long run (AS). Hedged words. Add the cost for evaluation if you have time.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Infrastructure spending is part of G. Firms and workers building a road or railway receive income and spend part of it, so aggregate demand could shift right through a multiplier effect. Real GDP could rise and unemployment fall. In the longer run, better transport and broadband may lower firms' costs and raise productivity, which could shift aggregate supply right and give growth with less inflationary pressure. However, it may take years to build and the money has an opportunity cost.
Q5 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether or not increasing government spending is the best way to reduce unemployment.
ReminderBoth sides. For: raises AD, jobs in the projects, multiplier. Against: time lags, deficit, inflation near full capacity, other policies (supply-side) may work better for structural unemployment.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Government spending is a part of AD and can be used to create demand for workers. If spending on construction or public services rises, firms and public bodies are likely to hire more workers, and the multiplier effect could create further jobs as those workers spend. It could be particularly effective in a recession with spare capacity. However, projects can take time to plan, so the effect may arrive late. The spending may raise the budget deficit and national debt, and if the economy is close to full capacity it could cause inflation. It may also not help workers whose skills do not match the jobs available, so supply-side measures such as training could do more for those people. Overall, spending is more likely to help when unemployment is caused by low demand. The best solution depends on the cause of the unemployment and on the state of the economy and public finances.

Top tips

  • Four reasons to spend: influence activity, reduce market failure, promote equity, pay debt interest.
  • Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − share spent). Then multiply by the injection.
  • Name the area of spending. Healthcare, education, pensions, infrastructure give you something specific to write about.
  • Split short run and long run. Spending changes AD at once; education and infrastructure may change AS later.
  • Evaluate with opportunity cost, time lags and borrowing. And spare capacity: near full capacity, extra spending mostly raises prices.
