Quantity and Quality of Factors of Production

What increases or decreases the quantity and quality of land, labour, capital and enterprise. Syllabus 1.2.2.

The big idea

The quantity of a factor is how much of it is available; the quality is how productive each unit is. Both can increase over time (or fall), which changes how much an economy can produce. Changes in the quantity and quality of factors are the main causes of economic growth and shifts in the PPC (production possibility curve).

What changes quantity and quality

FactorCauses of a change in quantityCauses of a change in quality
LandDiscovery of new resources (oil, minerals); reclaiming land from the sea; depletion or exhaustion of resources; loss of land to flooding or buildingFertilisers and irrigation improve soil; pollution and soil erosion reduce it
LabourPopulation growth, immigration or emigration; ageing; changes in the retirement age; more people working (women, older workers)Education, training, better health and nutrition; learning by experience; brain drain reduces skills
CapitalInvestment in new machinery, factories, infrastructure; depreciation and wear and tearTechnological advances: more efficient, faster machines and software
EnterpriseMore people starting businesses; government support, access to finance, lower taxes on start-upsBetter business skills, training and access to knowledge and technology

Consequences

  • More factors or better-quality factors increase an economy's productive capacity: it can make more goods and services (economic growth).
  • Higher quality raises productivity (output per worker or per unit of capital), lowering costs.
  • Falls in quantity or quality, such as a natural disaster, depletion or brain drain, reduce potential output.
  • Mobility matters: factors that can move to where they are needed (labour moving to new jobs) are used more efficiently.

Quantity and quality are often linked: investing in capital raises quantity and, with new technology, quality too.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Immigration and labour supply

Many countries have grown their labour force through immigration, which adds workers and can fill skill gaps.

Exam use: Quantity of labour.

Education in South Korea

Large investment in education helped raise the skills of its workforce and supported fast growth.

Exam use: Quality of labour.

Oil discoveries

New oil and gas finds add to a country's land resources and can raise exports and incomes.

Exam use: Quantity of land.

Fishing and depletion

Over-fishing has reduced fish stocks in many regions, cutting future output and jobs.

Exam use: Depletion of land resources.

Automation

Robots and software raise the quality of capital and productivity in factories.

Exam use: Quality of capital.

Q1 Identify 2 marks Identify two causes of the increase in the quantity of a country's factors of production. (Feb/Mar 2022, P22 style)
ReminderAny two: immigration, discovery of resources, investment in capital, more enterprise.
Immigration (more labour). Investment in machinery (more capital) or discovery of new resources.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two ways the quality of labour can be improved.
ReminderEducation, training, healthcare; productivity.
Education and training can increase workers' skills, so they produce more per hour. Better healthcare and nutrition keep workers healthy, so they work harder and take fewer days off.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain why a country's capital stock may increase.
ReminderInvestment; technology; depreciation offset.
Firms and the government may invest in new machinery, factories and infrastructure. If investment is greater than depreciation the stock of capital rises. This increases productive capacity.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how an increase in the quantity and quality of its factors of production could affect an economy.
ReminderCapacity, productivity, growth, PPC shift, costs.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
An increase in the quantity of land, labour or capital means the economy can produce more. Better quality, through training and technology, raises productivity so output per worker rises. Costs of production fall and the economy's productive capacity rises, which is economic growth and an outward shift of the PPC. The effect depends on whether the extra factors are used and on whether demand rises to match.
Q5 Analyse 6 marks Analyse the influences on the mobility of two factors of production. (Oct/Nov 2022, P22 style)
ReminderLabour: skills, housing, family ties. Capital: specificity, transport costs.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Labour mobility is influenced by skills and training: workers need the right qualifications to move to a new job. Housing costs, family ties and language also make it harder to move. Capital mobility depends on how specific the machinery is, a specialised machine cannot easily be used in another industry, and moving large machines costs money. Mobility is usually higher for general-purpose factors and in the long run.

Top tips

  • Quantity = more units; quality = better units. State which one.
  • Link to growth. More or better factors shift the PPC outwards.
  • Name specific causes. Immigration, investment, training, technology, discovery of resources.
  • Productivity is output per unit of input. Use it for quality.
  • Evaluate with time lags. Education and investment take years to pay off.
