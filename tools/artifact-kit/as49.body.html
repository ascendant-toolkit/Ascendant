Consequences of Unemployment

4.5.4: the economic and social costs of unemployment for individuals, firms, the government and the economy, and the possible benefits. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

Unemployment has costs for the unemployed themselves (lost income, falling skills, poorer health and confidence), for firms (weaker demand for their products), for the government (lower tax revenue and higher spending on benefits, which have an opportunity cost) and for the economy, which produces less than it could: output is inside the production possibility curve and below potential output. Long-term unemployment is the most damaging because skills decay and people may become permanently detached from work, and it can worsen inequality, poverty and social problems such as crime. There can be some benefits: firms find it easier and cheaper to hire, and wage and inflation pressures are lower, but these are small compared with the costs. A falling unemployment rate has benefits (more income and output, tax revenue) and risks (inflation, skill shortages and a worse current account).

1 · Costs of unemployment (4.5.4)

WhoConsequences
Unemployed individualsLower income and living standards; skills decay (more so if long-term); loss of confidence; poorer physical and mental health
FirmsLower demand for goods and services as incomes fall; but easier recruitment and lower wage pressure
GovernmentLess tax revenue (income tax, VAT) and more spending on benefits, so the budget deficit rises; opportunity cost of that money
The economyWasted resources: output below potential (inside the PPC); lower AD; lower living standards; possibly lower long-run growth (loss of skills)
SocietyGreater inequality and poverty, crime, family stress and regional decline where unemployment is concentrated
  • Long-term unemployment (over a year) is most damaging: skills fall out of date and employers may avoid hiring long-term jobless people.
  • Youth and regional unemployment cause particular problems: young people lose early work experience, and a whole area can decline.
  • Benefits of low unemployment but risks: more output, income and tax revenue, but at very low unemployment wages and inflation rise, there may be skill shortages, and higher incomes can raise imports.

2 · The government budget (4.5.4)

  • Each unemployed person pays less tax and receives benefits, so rising unemployment worsens the budget twice over (lower revenue, higher spending). These are called automatic stabilisers because they cushion falling demand.
  • The opportunity cost of paying for unemployment is the education, health or infrastructure that could have been funded instead.

3 · Worked example: the cost to the budget and the economy

Suppose unemployment rises by 1 million people. Each had been earning $40,000 and paying $5,000 a year in income tax and VAT, and each now receives $8,000 a year in benefits.

ItemWorkingAnnual effect
Lost output1m × $40,000$40bn less GDP
Lost tax revenue1m × $5,000$5bn
Benefit payments1m × $8,000$8bn
Worsening of the budget$5bn + $8bn$13bn
Fall in household income$40,000 − $8,000 per person$32bn, reducing spending and so AD further
  • The budget cost is $13bn, but the lost output is much greater ($40bn). Unemployment also reduces spending, so firms sell less and may cut more jobs: a downward spiral.
  • Benefits and lower taxes act as automatic stabilisers: they cushion the fall in incomes, but add to the deficit.

4 · On the PPC and AD/AS diagrams

  • PPC: with unemployment the economy is inside the curve: actual output is below the maximum possible. Reducing unemployment moves output towards the PPC.
  • AD/AS: cyclical unemployment shows as equilibrium output below potential (a negative output gap). Raising AD moves the economy towards full employment.
  • Long-run effects: if people are unemployed for long periods their skills decay and some become unemployable, so potential output itself may fall (LRAS shifts left): this effect is sometimes called hysteresis.

6 · The consequences of falling unemployment, and of automation

  • Falling unemployment raises output, income and tax revenue and lowers benefit spending. But as the economy approaches full employment, wages and prices may rise, firms may face skill shortages, and higher incomes can raise imports.
  • Automation and AI can displace workers in some jobs, causing technological and structural unemployment in the short run, but may raise productivity, lower costs and create jobs in new industries over time. The net effect depends on how quickly workers can retrain.
  • Who is affected most? People with low skills, young and older workers, and regions that depend on one industry.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

After a factory closes

A town loses its main employer. Households spend less in local shops, which cut staff in turn. The council collects less tax and pays more in benefits, and young people leave the area.

Exam use: multiplied effects on firms and the government.

Long-term unemployment

Someone out of work for several years finds their skills out of date, and employers become reluctant to hire them. This lowers their lifetime income and the economy's potential output.

Exam use: skill decay and long-term damage.

Youth unemployment

A person who cannot find a first job misses early experience and training. The effect can reduce their earnings for decades and, across many people, limits future growth.

Exam use: long-run costs of unemployment.

Very low unemployment

When almost everyone has a job, employers struggle to find workers and bid up wages. Costs and prices rise, and rising incomes may suck in imports. Low unemployment is good, but not without risk.

Exam use: the possible costs of falling unemployment.

Redundancies in a recession

When firms cut jobs, households lose income and cut spending, so other firms sell less and also cut jobs. The government pays more in benefits and collects less tax, so the budget deficit rises.

Exam use: the consequences of unemployment for firms, the government and the economy.

Long-term youth unemployment

Young people who cannot find work after leaving education miss out on training and early career growth. Years later they may still earn less than those who found work sooner.

Exam use: why long-term unemployment is the most damaging.

Based on May/June 2025 · 9708/22 · Q1(b) 2 marks Identify one possible impact on firms and one possible impact on the government of a very high rate of unemployment.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Firms may find it easier or cheaper to recruit labour, may pay lower wages, or may suffer weaker demand for their products (1).
  • The government may have reduced tax revenue or higher welfare payments (1). Maximum 1 mark for each of firms and government.
PlanFirms: weaker demand or cheaper labour. Government: less tax, more benefits.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

High unemployment lowers incomes and spending, so firms may suffer weaker demand for their products. The government collects less income tax and VAT and pays out more in unemployment benefits.

Based on May/June 2025 · 9708/24 · Q1(e) 6 marks Assess the possible consequences of a falling rate of unemployment for an economy such as Malaysia's.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Analysis (up to 3): possible positive consequences, such as higher overall income levels and living standards, more government tax revenue and less spending on welfare, which could be spent on infrastructure, education and health, more output and export opportunities, and more investment by firms.
  • Analysis (up to 3): possible negative consequences, such as demand-pull inflation if AS cannot keep up, cost-push inflation as wages rise and resources become scarce, a decline in labour productivity, and higher demand for imports leading to a current account deficit.
  • Maximum 4 for analysis; the remaining marks are for a judgement. Generic suggestions are accepted.
PlanPositives: incomes, tax revenue, less welfare, output. Negatives: demand-pull and cost-push inflation, imports. Judge.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

A falling unemployment rate raises incomes and living standards, and means more people paying tax and fewer claiming benefits, so the government has more to spend on infrastructure, education and health. More employment also means more output. But if AD rises faster than supply, the extra demand may cause demand-pull inflation, shortages of workers can push up wages and costs, and higher incomes could raise imports and widen the current account deficit.

Overall the consequences are mostly positive, because lower unemployment raises output and incomes, but if unemployment falls to very low levels the costs of inflation and a worse trade balance become more significant, so the benefits are not unlimited.

Based on Feb/March 2024 · 9708/22 · Q1(d) 6 marks A country increases investment in artificial intelligence (AI) in its industries. Assess the possible impact on unemployment.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Analysis (up to 3): possible increases in unemployment, as AI replaces workers in some jobs (technological and structural unemployment), especially for low-skilled workers who lack the skills to move.
  • Analysis (up to 3): possible reductions in unemployment, as AI raises productivity and growth, creates new industries and jobs, and lowers costs, which raises demand and output.
  • A judgement on the overall impact, depending on retraining, time period and the pace of change.
PlanJobs lost: technological and structural unemployment. Jobs created: new industries, higher growth. Judge: depends on retraining and timing.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

AI can replace workers in routine tasks, so some people lose their jobs, which is technological unemployment. If their skills do not match new jobs, the unemployment is structural and may last. On the other hand, AI raises productivity and lowers costs, which can raise output and demand, and creates jobs in new industries such as software and maintenance.

In the short run AI is likely to raise unemployment for workers in affected industries. In the long run the effect depends on how quickly workers retrain and whether economic growth creates enough new jobs, so the overall impact may be small or even positive.

Top tips

  • Organise by group affected. Individual, firm, government, economy, society.
  • Pair each effect with a reason. Lower income, so less spending.
  • Use the PPC. Unemployment means output inside the curve.
  • Mention government finances twice. Less tax revenue and more benefits.
  • Long-term is worst. Skills decay.
  • Give the costs of low unemployment too. Inflation, shortages, imports.
  • Judge short run versus long run. Retraining matters.
