Consequences of Unemployment

How unemployment affects individuals, firms, the government and the economy. Syllabus 4.6.4.

The big idea

Unemployment has costs for the individual, firms, the government and the whole economy. It wastes resources, so the economy produces less than it could. It lowers incomes and tax revenue and raises government spending on benefits. Good answers say who is affected, how, and end with the link to aims such as growth and living standards.

Consequences for each group

WhoPossible consequences
The individualLower income and living standards; poverty and debt; loss of skills the longer they are unemployed; stress and poorer health; lower confidence; may affect family
FirmsLower consumer spending, so lower sales and profits; falling demand for products; but easier to hire workers and lower wage pressure; wasted potential
The governmentLower tax revenue (income tax, spending taxes, corporation tax) and higher spending on benefits and other social costs, so a larger budget deficit; opportunity cost of that money
The economyWasted resources: output below capacity (inside the PPC); lower real GDP and growth; lower living standards; greater inequality and poverty; social costs such as crime; may lower inflation

Unemployment and the PPC

Unemployed workers are an unused factor of production, so the economy produces inside its PPC. Putting them to work would increase output without using more scarce resources.

Unemployment and the budget

Each person who loses a job pays less tax (income tax, and less sales tax as spending falls) and may claim benefits. So a rise in unemployment moves the budget towards deficit. These effects work automatically, as automatic stabilisers, and cushion the fall in demand.

Is unemployment ever helpful?

  • Firms may find it easier to hire and wage pressure may be lower.
  • Falling demand may reduce inflation.
  • But these small gains are outweighed by lost output, lost incomes and social costs.

Youth unemployment in Spain

After 2008, more than half of young Spaniards were without a job at one point. Many emigrated, and long spells without work lowered their later earnings.

Exam use: Consequences for individuals and the economy: lost skills and output.

Unemployment benefit in a recession

During COVID-19 many governments extended benefits and wage support. Their deficits rose sharply as tax revenue fell.

Exam use: Government consequences.

Regions left behind

Areas that lost major industries have often had high unemployment, lower incomes and social problems for decades.

Exam use: Wider social and economic costs.

A past paper links rising unemployment in Nigeria to the government's budget, with lower tax revenue and higher spending.

Q1 Identify 2 marks Identify two costs of unemployment. (Feb/Mar 2025, P22)
ReminderShort answers: lost output, lower incomes, higher benefit spending, lower tax revenue, social problems.
Lower income (poverty) for the unemployed. Lost output, because resources are unused (or higher government spending on benefits).
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two consequences of unemployment. (Feb/Mar 2022, P22)
ReminderTwo different consequences, each linked: output and growth, incomes and poverty, tax revenue and benefits.
Unemployment means lower output because resources are not fully used. The economy produces inside its PPC, so real GDP and economic growth are lower. Unemployed people receive lower incomes. They can buy fewer goods and services, so poverty may rise and living standards fall.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain two consequences to firms of unemployment. (May/June 2020, P21)
ReminderLower consumer demand, lower profits; but possibly easier recruitment and lower wage pressure.
Unemployed people have lower incomes, so total spending falls. Firms sell less and profits could fall, so some may cut output or close. On the other hand, a larger pool of jobseekers makes it easier to recruit. Firms may find that wage pressure is lower and they can hire workers more cheaply.
Q4 Analyse 4 marks Analyse how an increase in unemployment may affect a government's budget. (Oct/Nov 2022, P22)
ReminderBoth sides of the budget: revenue falls, spending rises, deficit widens.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A rise in unemployment is likely to increase a budget deficit. Fewer workers means less income tax, and lower spending means less sales tax; falling profits cut corporation tax. At the same time the government pays more unemployment benefit and may spend more on other social costs. With lower revenue and higher spending the deficit widens.
Q5 Analyse 6 marks Analyse why a government may have lower unemployment as its main aim. (May/June 2022, P22)
ReminderWasted resources, growth, incomes, tax revenue and benefit savings, living standards, social costs.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Unemployment is a waste of resources, as the economy produces below its capacity. Lower unemployment would raise output and economic growth, and incomes would rise, so living standards could improve and poverty could fall. More people in work would spend more, raising total demand and firms' profits. The government would collect more tax and spend less on benefits, so the budget could improve. It might also reduce social problems such as crime.
Q6 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether or not a fall in a country's unemployment rate will reduce poverty. (May/June 2021, P21; Oct/Nov 2022, P23)
ReminderFor: more households with earnings. Against: low-paid or part-time jobs, other causes of poverty, people outside the labour force, inflation.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
The unemployment rate is the percentage of the labour force without a job, and poverty means having too little income to meet basic needs. A fall could reduce poverty because more people have earnings, so more households could afford food, housing and healthcare, and spending would rise. The government may also spend less on benefits and more on services that help the poor. This is more likely if the new jobs are well paid and full time. However, the new jobs might be low paid, part time or temporary, so households could stay in poverty. Poverty also has other causes such as ill health, old age, low skills or living in a poor region, and a fall in the rate might be because people stopped looking. Overall, a fall in unemployment is likely to reduce poverty, but the amount depends on the quality of jobs and the other causes of poverty.
Q7 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether or not a reduction in the unemployment rate benefits an economy. (Oct/Nov 2022, P21)
ReminderFor: output, incomes, tax revenue, lower benefits. Against: inflation, wage pressure, imports, shortage of workers, if the fall is due to discouraged workers leaving.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A reduction in the unemployment rate means a smaller percentage of the labour force is without a job. It could benefit the economy by raising output and economic growth, as more resources are used. Higher employment raises incomes and spending, and the government collects more tax and pays less in benefits. These gains are larger if the economy has spare capacity. On the other hand, if the economy is near full capacity, more demand could raise prices, and firms may compete for scarce workers, pushing up wages and costs. Higher incomes may also raise imports, and the rate may fall because people stop looking for work. Overall, a fall in unemployment is likely to benefit the economy, particularly when it is caused by higher output rather than people leaving the labour force.

Top tips

  • Four groups: the individual, firms, the government, the economy.
  • Link tax and benefits to the deficit. Revenue falls, spending rises.
  • Unemployment is a waste of resources. The economy is inside its PPC.
  • Give a benefit and a cost where asked for two sides. A little less inflation against lost output and income.
  • Use 'could' and 'may'. Cambridge rewards hedged reasoning.
