Objectives of Firms

Survival, social welfare, profit maximisation and growth. Syllabus 3.6.5.

The big idea

Firms have different objectives. The main ones are survival, profit maximisation, growth and social welfare. The objective chosen depends on the type of firm, its size, its age and who owns it.

Objectives of firms

ObjectiveMeaningTypical firmWhy chosen
SurvivalStaying in business (covering costs and avoiding bankruptcy)New firms, firms in a recession or fierce competitionCannot achieve other aims if it fails
Profit maximisationMaking the largest possible profit (TR − TC)Most private firms, especially with shareholdersRewards owners and funds investment
GrowthIncreasing the size of the firm (sales, market share, output)Firms seeking economies of scale and market powerLower costs, more power, higher long-run profit, more security
Social welfareProviding benefits for society, not only profitPublic sector firms, charities, social enterprises, some ethical firmsAims include jobs, the environment, community service

Do objectives conflict?

  • Growth may reduce short-run profit (spending on expansion), but raise long-run profit.
  • Social welfare aims (fair wages, green production) may raise costs and cut profit.
  • Survival may require cutting prices or profit in the short run.
  • Firms may combine objectives, for example satisficing (making enough profit while pursuing other aims).

Public sector firms usually aim for social welfare; private firms usually aim for profit and growth; new firms aim to survive.

Q1 Identify 2 marks Identify two objectives of firms.
ReminderSurvival, profit maximisation, growth, social welfare.
Profit maximisation. Growth (or survival or social welfare).
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain why a new firm may have survival as its main objective.
ReminderLow sales, high costs, risk of failure.
A new firm has few customers and high start-up costs. It risks running out of money and going bankrupt, so it aims to cover costs and stay in business before pursuing other objectives.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain why a public sector firm may aim for social welfare rather than profit.
ReminderProvide services; government ownership.
Public sector firms are owned by the government to provide services. They may serve unprofitable routes or areas because the community needs them, so they sacrifice profit for social welfare.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether profit maximisation is the most important objective of a firm.
ReminderProfit vs growth, survival, welfare. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Profit maximisation rewards owners and funds investment. Most private firms aim for profit because shareholders demand returns. However, new firms may prioritise survival, some prioritise growth for the long run, and public firms prioritise social welfare. Overall, profit is the main objective for most private firms in the long run, but it is not always the most important in the short run or for firms with other owners.

Top tips

  • Four objectives. Survival, profit, growth, social welfare.
  • Link objective to type of firm. New, private, public.
  • Objectives can conflict. Growth vs short-run profit.
  • Use examples. Start-up, listed company, state hospital.
