AO2 Analysis: Chains of Reasoning and Context

Explain and Analyse: building developed chains of reasoning and applying them to data, context and diagrams. Assessment Objective 2.

The big idea

AO2 is analysis: selecting and using information and data, applying economics to the context (written, numerical, diagrammatic and graphical) and developing links and relationships, that is, showing why and how one thing leads to another. It is 47% of the whole IGCSE, 50% of Paper 1 and 45% of Paper 2, the largest AO. Command words: Explain and Analyse.

Explain and analyse

Command wordWhat it meansWhat earns marks
ExplainSet out purposes or reasons; make relationships clear; say why or how, supported with evidenceA point plus a developed reason: "because… so…" (about 2 marks per developed point)
AnalyseExamine in detail to show meaning, identify elements and the relationships between themA chain of reasoning of at least three links, applied to the context; for 6 marks usually two chains

The chain of reasoning

A good analysis follows a chain: Point → Because → Therefore → So.

Question: Analyse how a rise in the price of oil may affect a country's inflation rate.
Chain: Oil is a cost of production for many firms → higher oil prices raise costs → firms cut supply or raise prices → the general price level rises → inflation increases (cost-push).

  • Start with the cause from the question, not with a definition.
  • Each link should follow logically from the one before.
  • End with the effect asked for (inflation, unemployment, price, quantity).

Using the context, data and diagrams

  • Select the relevant figures from the extract and quote them.
  • Interpret trends (rising, falling, compared with another country) and calculate if asked.
  • Apply theory to the named country, industry or firm.
  • In diagram questions: draw the curves, label axes and curves, show the shift, mark the new equilibrium and explain it in words.

An answer that could be about any country scores less than one that mentions the named product, place or figures in the question.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain why a rise in the price of a good may reduce the quantity demanded.
ReminderSubstitution; income; reasoning.
A higher price makes the good dearer relative to substitutes. Consumers switch to cheaper alternatives, so they buy less. Their income buys fewer units at a higher price. So quantity demanded falls, a movement up along the demand curve.
Q2 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how a rise in the price of oil may affect a country's inflation rate.
ReminderCost-push chain.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Oil is a cost for many firms, such as transport and manufacturing. A higher oil price raises their costs of production. Firms raise prices or cut supply to protect profit. The general price level rises, so inflation increases (cost-push inflation). The effect depends on how much firms rely on oil and on whether costs are passed on.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how a subsidy for solar panels could affect the market.
ReminderS right; lower price; higher quantity.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A subsidy lowers producers' costs, so supply increases and S shifts right to S₁. At the old price there is a surplus, so the price falls to P₁. The quantity bought and sold rises to Q₁. Consumers pay less and buy more solar panels. The cost to the government is an opportunity cost.
Q4 Analyse 4 marks The extract says unemployment rose from 5% to 8% in 2020. Analyse how higher unemployment may affect a country's economy.
ReminderUse the data; link to income and demand.
Unemployment rose by 3 percentage points to 8%, so more people have no earned income. Their spending falls, so firms lose sales. Firms cut output and may lay off more workers, so aggregate demand and growth fall.

Top tips

  • Use a chain: cause, because, so, therefore. At least three links.
  • Use the context. Quote figures and names.
  • Label diagrams and explain them in words. Both earn marks.
  • Explain and analyse: stay on the question. No long definitions.
  • Check the final link answers the question. Inflation, price, jobs.
