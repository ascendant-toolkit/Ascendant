Opportunity Cost

Defining opportunity cost and finding it in examples for consumers, workers, firms, governments and countries. Syllabus 1.3.1.

The big idea

Opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up when a choice is made. It is not the cost of everything you give up, only the single best alternative. Because resources are scarce, every choice has an opportunity cost, except for free goods, which use no scarce resources.

Working it out

List your options, rank them, choose one. The opportunity cost is the one ranked next.

You have $50 and choose to buy…Your ranking of optionsOpportunity cost
Concert tickets1. Concert tickets   2. New trainers   3. Meal outThe trainers (not trainers and the meal)

Examples in different contexts

ContextChoiceOpportunity cost

Leisure timeCycle for an afternoonThe next best use of the time: studying, earning or relaxing
FirmInvest in new technologySpending on advertising or on paying higher wages
FarmerGrow apples on a fieldThe next best crop that could have been grown
GovernmentSpend on roadsThe hospitals or schools that could have been funded
CountryProduce more capital goodsThe consumer goods given up (shown on a PPC)

Free goods such as sunshine have no opportunity cost: no scarce resources are used, so nothing is given up. Opportunity cost is not always measured in money: time and benefits count too.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

University or a job

A school leaver who goes to university gives up the wages they could have earned, as well as paying fees.

Exam use: A worker's opportunity cost.

Government budgets

Money spent on one public project cannot be spent on another, for example a new road or a hospital.

Exam use: Government opportunity cost.

Fields for farming

Land used for biofuel crops cannot be used for food, so some farmers weigh the price of each.

Exam use: A farmer's choice.

Your time

An hour spent on social media is an hour not spent on revision, work or sleep.

Exam use: Time as a scarce resource.

Q1 Define 2 marks Define opportunity cost. (May/June 2021, P22 style)
ReminderNext best alternative given up.
Opportunity cost is the next best alternative that is given up when a choice is made.
Q2 Identify 2 marks Identify two possible opportunity costs of a Danish person using their leisure time to cycle. (Oct/Nov 2020, P22 style)
ReminderAny two other uses of the time: studying, working, relaxing, spending time with family.
Time that could have been used to work and earn income. Time that could have been used to study (or relax).
Q3 State 2 marks State why sunshine has no opportunity cost. (Oct/Nov 2022, P22 style)
ReminderNot scarce; no resources used.
Sunshine is a free good: it is not scarce and uses no scarce resources, so nothing is given up.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain opportunity cost and how it can influence a farmer's decision to grow apples. (May/June 2023, P22 style)
ReminderNext best crop; compare incomes; choose.
Opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up. If the farmer grows apples on the land, the opportunity cost is the next best crop, such as barley. The farmer is more likely to grow apples if the expected income is greater than the income from the next best crop.
Q5 Explain 4 marks Explain the opportunity cost to India of exporting more textiles. (Feb/Mar 2023, P22 style)
ReminderResources used for textiles cannot be used elsewhere; the next best use.
India has scarce resources such as labour, land and capital. Using more of them to make textiles for export means they cannot be used for something else, such as producing other goods or food for the home market, which is the opportunity cost.

Top tips

  • Opportunity cost is only the next best alternative. Not everything given up.
  • Say what is given up. Name the actual alternative in the question.
  • Not just money. Time, wages, benefits and other projects count.
  • Free goods have none. Not scarce, no resources used.
  • Link to scarcity. Resources limited, so choice, so opportunity cost.
