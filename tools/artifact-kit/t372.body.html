Monopoly Markets

Characteristics, advantages and disadvantages of monopoly and the effect of only one firm on price, quality, choice and profit. Syllabus 3.7.2.

The big idea

A monopoly market has only one firm (or one that dominates), with high barriers to entry that stop rivals joining. Because it faces little or no competition, the monopolist can set the price, restrict output and earn high profits. (Diagrams are not required.)

Characteristics of a monopoly

  • A single seller (or a very dominant one, for example over 25% of the market in some countries).
  • High barriers to entry: patents and licences, huge start-up costs, control of resources, economies of scale, brand loyalty, legal protection.
  • No close substitutes for the product.
  • The firm is a price maker: it chooses price and output.

Effect of having only one firm

VariableEffect of monopoly
PriceHigher: no rivals to undercut
QualityMay be lower (no pressure) but can be higher if profits fund research
ChoiceLimited: one supplier, fewer variants
ProfitHigher, because barriers stop new firms competing it away (supernormal profit)
OutputOften restricted

Advantages

  • Economies of scale can lower costs (and prices)
  • High profits can fund research and innovation
  • A natural monopoly (railways, water) avoids duplicated networks
  • Patent protection rewards inventors
  • Easier to regulate than many firms

Disadvantages

  • Higher prices and restricted output
  • Less choice and lower pressure to improve quality
  • Inefficiency: no incentive to cut costs
  • Consumers' real incomes fall
  • Abuse of power: unfair practices, blocking rivals

Governments may regulate, break up, tax or nationalise monopolies, or encourage competition and set price caps.

Q1 Define 2 marks Define a monopoly.
ReminderSingle dominant seller.
A monopoly is a market in which there is only one firm, or a firm that dominates the market.
Q2 Identify 2 marks Identify two characteristics of a monopoly market.
ReminderOne firm, barriers to entry, price maker.
A single firm dominates the market. High barriers to entry (or the firm is a price maker).
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain the effect of having only one firm in a market on price and choice.
ReminderHigher price; less choice.
With no competition, a monopoly can raise its price. Consumers pay more. There is only one supplier, so consumers have less choice of products.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain why a monopoly may earn high profits.
ReminderBarriers; price power.
Barriers to entry stop new firms joining the market. The monopoly can therefore keep its price high and does not lose profit to competitors.
Q5 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether a monopoly is always bad for consumers.
ReminderHigher price, less choice vs economies of scale, innovation. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A monopoly can raise prices, restrict output and reduce choice. With no competition it may also be inefficient. However, economies of scale may lower its costs and prices, and high profits can fund research. A natural monopoly may avoid wasteful duplication. Overall, monopolies usually harm consumers, but benefits are possible if regulated to prevent abuse of power.

Top tips

  • One firm, barriers, price maker. Define it.
  • Effects: price, quality, choice, profit. Learn the list.
  • Barriers: patents, costs, licences. Explain how they work.
  • Evaluate: economies of scale. Monopoly is not always bad.
