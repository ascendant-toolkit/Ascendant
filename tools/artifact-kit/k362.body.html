Calculation of Costs of Production

Calculating TC, ATC, FC, AFC, VC and AVC and drawing the cost curves. Syllabus 3.6.2.

The big idea

Costs are calculated from the definitions (TC = FC + VC; ATC = TC ÷ output) and shown on diagrams. As output rises, AFC falls, AVC falls then rises and ATC is U-shaped, because ATC = AFC + AVC. The U shape comes from economies then diseconomies of scale (and diminishing returns in the short run).

ATC is lowest (about $37.7) around 6 to 7 units: this is the most efficient output.

Diagrams of costs

  • AFC slopes downwards all the way (a rectangular hyperbola).
  • AVC is U-shaped: falls with specialisation, then rises with diminishing returns.
  • ATC is U-shaped and lies above AVC; the gap between them is AFC, which shrinks as output rises.
  • The lowest ATC is at the minimum efficient scale.

When reading a table, compute each average by dividing by output, and check that ATC = AFC + AVC.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Airline seats

Selling more seats spreads huge fixed costs over more passengers.

Exam use: AFC falls.

Car production

Unit costs fall as a plant reaches full capacity, then rise if overcrowded.

Exam use: U-shaped ATC.

Bakery ovens

More loaves make each loaf cheaper until the kitchen is crowded.

Exam use: Falling then rising ATC.

Software

The first copy is costly; later copies cost almost nothing.

Exam use: Falling AFC.

Q1 Calculate 3 marks A firm has fixed costs of $100 and variable costs of $126 when it makes 6 units. Calculate average total cost.
ReminderTC ÷ output.
TC = 100 + 126 = $226. ATC = 226 ÷ 6 = $37.67.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain why average fixed cost falls as output rises.
ReminderFixed cost spread over more units.
Fixed costs do not change with output. As output rises the same fixed cost is shared across more units, so fixed cost per unit falls.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse, using a diagram, why average total cost is U-shaped.
ReminderAFC falls; AVC U-shaped; economies then diseconomies.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
ATC is the sum of AFC and AVC. At low output both AFC and AVC fall, so ATC falls (economies of scale). At higher output diminishing returns raise AVC faster than AFC falls, so ATC rises (diseconomies of scale). The lowest ATC shows the most efficient output.
Q4 Calculate 3 marks Complete the table: output 8, FC $100, VC $204. Calculate TC, AVC and ATC.
ReminderShow each step.
TC = 100 + 204 = $304. AVC = 204 ÷ 8 = $25.5. ATC = 304 ÷ 8 = $38.

Top tips

  • Show the formula. Then the working.
  • ATC = AFC + AVC. Use it to check.
  • Draw three curves. AFC down, AVC and ATC U-shaped.
  • ATC above AVC. The gap is AFC.
