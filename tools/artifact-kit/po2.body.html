Causes of Poverty

Unemployment, low wages, illness, age and environmental factors, and how they combine into a poverty trap. Syllabus 5.2.2.

The big idea

The syllabus lists five causes of poverty: unemployment, low wages, illness, age and environmental factors. They often overlap and reinforce each other, which is why poverty can last for generations. To answer well, name the cause, explain how it cuts a household's income or raises its costs, and say who is most at risk.

The five causes

CauseHow it leads to povertyWho is at risk
UnemploymentNo wage. Benefits may be small or absent. Long spells reduce skills and make it harder to find workWorkers in declining industries, young people, people in recessions
Low wagesEarnings too low to afford necessities, especially with a large family or high living costsUnskilled workers, farmers with low yields, informal workers, part-time and casual workers
IllnessCannot work; medical bills; carers lose earnings; poor health reduces productivity and educationPeople without health insurance or free healthcare, those in places with disease and poor sanitation
AgeChildren cannot earn; the old may have no pension, savings or ability to workLarge families, pensioners without state pension, orphans
Environmental factorsDrought, floods, storms, soil erosion and climate change destroy crops, fish stocks and homes, and cut incomesFarmers, fishermen, people in disaster-prone or low-lying areas

Other causes that link to these

  • Low education and skills: lead to low wages and unemployment.
  • Discrimination: unequal access to jobs, pay and education.
  • War, conflict and refugees: destroy livelihoods and force people to leave.
  • Lack of state benefits and services such as pensions and free healthcare.
  • Lack of access to credit, markets or land.

The poverty trap

Poverty tends to reinforce itself: poor families cannot afford to educate their children, so the children have low skills and low wages and stay poor. Illness, lost income and low savings leave households with no cushion against shocks such as a job loss or a drought.

Poverty over the life cycle

Households often face a higher risk of poverty at the start and end of life: when children cannot earn and cost money, and in old age when working income falls and pensions may be small or missing.

Drought in the Sahel

Repeated droughts in the Sahel region of Africa have destroyed crops and herds, pushing millions of farming families into hunger.

Job losses after factory closures

When major employers close, as in former coal and steel towns, long-term unemployment and low-paid work can leave whole areas with high poverty.

Exam use: Unemployment as a cause.

Out-of-pocket healthcare costs

In countries with little public healthcare, medical bills are a leading reason families fall into poverty, because they sell assets or borrow to pay.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of poverty.
ReminderTwo different causes, each developed: unemployment, low wages, illness, age, environmental factors.
One cause is unemployment. People without a job have no wage to buy food, water and housing, and benefits may be small or absent, so they could fall into absolute poverty. Another cause is illness. Sick people may be unable to work and have medical bills to pay, so their income falls while their costs rise.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of poverty amongst fishermen. (May/June 2021, P23)
ReminderEnvironmental and economic: falling catches, climate change, overfishing, low prices, lack of capital.
One cause is falling fish stocks, for example from overfishing or climate change. If catches fall, fishermen have less to sell, so their incomes fall and they may be unable to afford basic necessities. Another cause is low prices and lack of capital. Small fishermen with old boats cannot catch much and may have to accept low prices from buyers, which keeps their income low.
Q3 Analyse 5 marks Analyse why the children of poor families tend to receive less education. (May/June 2020, P22)
ReminderCost of schooling, need to work, poor health, poor schools; then link to poverty cycle.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Poor families have low incomes and may not be able to afford school fees, books or uniforms. Children may need to work or care for family members instead of going to school. Poor nutrition and illness can reduce attendance and ability to learn, and schools in poor areas may be of low quality. With less education the children have fewer skills, so they are likely to earn low wages and remain poor.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how unemployment can lead to poverty.
ReminderChain: no wage, benefits small, skills fade, debts; then who is most affected.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Unemployment means people have no earnings. Without a wage a household may be unable to afford food, housing and healthcare. Unemployment benefit may be low or limited in time, or not available in some countries, so household income could fall below the poverty line. The longer someone is unemployed, the more their skills and confidence fade, making it harder to find a job again. Families may fall into debt or sell assets, making poverty last longer.
Q5 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether or not unemployment is the main cause of poverty.
ReminderFor: no income. Against: low wages, illness, age, environment, and employed people can be poor. Judge: depends on country.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Poverty means having too little income to afford the basics. Unemployment is a major cause because without a wage a household cannot buy necessities, and in countries without strong benefits unemployed households can quickly fall into absolute poverty. This is more important in recessions and in areas where an industry has closed. However, many people in work are poor because wages are too low, especially unskilled and informal workers and small farmers. Illness, old age without a pension, large families and environmental shocks such as drought are other important causes. Overall, unemployment is one of several causes. Its importance depends on the country: in rich countries unemployment and low wages may dominate, whereas in poorer countries low productivity in farming and lack of healthcare may be more important.

Top tips

  • Five causes: unemployment, low wages, illness, age, environmental factors.
  • Explain how each cuts income or raises costs. No wage, medical bills, lost crops.
  • Name who is at risk. Fishermen, farmers, pensioners, large families.
  • Link causes together. Illness reduces work; low education leads to low wages: the poverty trap.
  • Use the context in the question. Fishermen, farmers, a specific country.
