The PPC: Shifts and Position

1.5.3 to 1.5.4: the causes and consequences of shifts of the PPC, and the significance of being inside, on or outside it. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

A movement along a PPC is a reallocation of existing resources between two goods and involves an opportunity cost, whereas a shift of the PPC is caused by a change in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology. An outward shift raises potential output and is potential economic growth, while an inward shift (a fall in resources) lowers it. The position of the economy matters too: a point on the curve means resources are fully and efficiently used, a point inside means resources are unused or inefficiently used (unemployment), and a point outside cannot be reached with current resources.

1 · Shifts of the PPC (1.5.3)

ChangeCausesEffect on the PPC
Outward shift (growth in potential output)More resources or better quality: investment in capital, education and training, immigration, discovery of natural resources, a larger labour force, improvements in technologyCurve moves out on both axes: more of both goods can be produced
Biased (one-sided) shiftTechnology or resources affecting only one good, such as a new crop variety for wheat or a flood that ruins barley landCurve moves out (or in) on one axis only
Inward shiftFall in the quantity or quality of resources: war, natural disaster, emigration, resource depletion, a rise in the school leaving age (fewer workers)Curve moves in: lower potential output
  • Quantity and quality: a fall in the quantity of labour but a rise in its quality has opposing effects; the net effect depends on which is larger.
  • Consequences of an outward shift: more goods and services can be produced, which can raise living standards, give governments more tax revenue and give more choice. A shift does not involve opportunity cost, because with extra resources more of both goods can be made.
  • Opportunity cost of achieving a shift: to raise future output a country usually has to produce more capital goods now and fewer consumer goods, which is a movement along the current PPC. Growth is not free.
  • Consequences of an inward shift: lower potential output, lower living standards and fewer choices.
  • A shift of the PPC is potential growth. A movement from inside the curve towards it is actual growth without any change in capacity.

2 · The significance of a position within a PPC (1.5.4)

PositionWhat it meansExample
On the curveResources are fully and efficiently used; more of one good means less of the other (opportunity cost)Full employment of labour and capital
Inside the curveResources are unused or used inefficiently; output of both goods is lower than possible. More of one or both goods can be produced at no opportunity costA recession with high unemployment and idle factories
Outside the curveUnattainable with current resources and technology; possible only after growth (or by temporary means such as borrowing or trade)A target that needs more resources than the economy has
  • Why inside is inefficient: with the same resources more of one good could be produced without giving up any of the other. The economy is producing less than its potential: this is a negative output gap.
  • Moving from inside to the curve (for example by cutting unemployment) is actual growth: output rises with no change in capacity and no opportunity cost.
  • Is an inside position permanent? Often not: in a recession, a market economy may recover as demand returns, but it can persist if there is structural unemployment, low confidence or slow adjustment.
  • Knowing the point where an economy operates tells you the level of output of the two goods, but not its trade performance or its growth rate.
Exam tipBe sure to separate the two ideas. Shift = more resources or better technology, potential growth, no opportunity cost. Movement = reallocation (along) or using idle resources (from inside towards the curve).

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

The 2020 recession and idle resources

When economies were locked down in 2020, many factories, shops and workers were idle. The economy moved to a point inside its PPC. When restrictions ended, output recovered towards the curve, which was actual growth without any new resources.

Exam use: a position inside the PPC and the move back towards the curve.

Technology and a shift outwards

Computers, the internet and automation raised the amount of goods and services that the same labour could produce, shifting the PPC outwards over time. People could consume more of both goods and services without giving anything up, in contrast to a simple reallocation.

Exam use: an outward shift caused by better technology, with no opportunity cost.

A flood destroys farmland

A flood that ruins land used for barley lowers the maximum output of barley. If the farmer switches to a new variety of wheat that gives higher yields on the rest of the land, wheat capacity rises. The PPC pivots: barley falls, wheat rises.

Exam use: a biased shift: one axis moves out and the other moves in.

A higher school leaving age

Raising the age at which students can leave school lowers the size of the working population in the short run, shifting the PPC inwards. In the long run better-educated workers raise the quality of labour and may shift the PPC outwards.

Exam use: quantity versus quality of labour and the time period.

Based on Feb/March 2022 · 9708/22 · Q2(a) 8 marks Explain, with the help of a PPC diagram, how a decision to re-allocate resources in an economy to produce more capital goods and fewer consumer goods would affect consumers in both the short run and the long run.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 2): an accurate diagram labelled consumer goods and capital goods, with the PPC touching both axes.
  • Application, short run (3): a movement along the curve (1), meaning fewer consumer goods and services available (1), leading to less choice, higher prices or lower living standards (1).
  • Application, long run (3): an outward shift of the curve (1), perhaps leading to more consumer goods due to a larger capacity (1) and a rise in living standards (1).
PlanPPC with consumer and capital goods. Short run: movement along. Long run: outward shift.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

A PPC with consumer goods on one axis and capital goods on the other shows the combinations the economy can produce. Re-allocating resources to capital goods is a movement along the curve from a point with many consumer goods to a point with more capital goods.

In the short run consumers are worse off: fewer consumer goods and services are available, so there is less choice and prices may be higher, and living standards may fall.

In the long run the larger stock of capital expands the economy's capacity, so the PPC shifts outwards. The economy could then produce more of both goods, including more consumer goods than before, so living standards may rise above their original level.

What the mark scheme wants

  • Application, growth (up to 3): an outward shift of the curve with a valid example (1) and an explanation of what might cause it (1) and the diagram (1).

PlanOne diagram: shift outward (e.g. new technology) and movement from inside the curve (e.g. higher demand cuts unemployment).

On a PPC for consumer goods and capital goods, growth of the economy's potential output is shown by an outward shift of the whole curve. A reduction in unused resources is shown by a movement from a point inside the curve to a point on or nearer the curve.

Growth might be caused by an improvement in technology, which raises the amount that can be produced with the same resources, so both outputs can rise.

A reduction in unused resources might be caused by higher aggregate demand, which leads firms to hire the unemployed, so output of both goods rises with no new resources and no opportunity cost.

Based on May/June 2023 · 9708/21 · Q2(a) 8 marks With the help of a diagram, explain the difference between the causes of a movement along, and a shift of, a PPC and consider which is likely to have the most immediate impact on an economy.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (3): a PPC with labelled axes (1); a movement along it showing a trade-off between the two goods (1); a shift showing more of both goods with no trade-off (1).
  • Analysis (3): a movement along is caused by a reallocation of resources between goods and involves an opportunity cost (2); a shift is caused by an increase in the quantity or quality of resources and allows more of both goods (1).
  • Evaluation (2): a valid judgement on which has the most immediate impact, and a conclusion.
PlanMovement: reallocation, opportunity cost. Shift: more or better resources, technology. Judge which is quicker.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

A movement along a PPC, for example from more consumer goods to more capital goods, is caused by a reallocation of resources, and it involves a trade-off: more of one good means less of the other. A shift of the PPC outwards allows more of both goods with no trade-off.

A movement is caused by a decision to use existing resources differently, whereas a shift is caused by an increase in the quantity or quality of resources, such as investment in machinery, training or new technology.

A reallocation can be made almost immediately by a change in spending or policy, so it is likely to have the most immediate impact. A shift takes years of investment, education or innovation to build capacity, so its effect is slower, though more lasting.

Based on Oct/Nov 2022 · 9708/21 · Q3(a) 8 marks Explain, with the use of a diagram, how the PPC of an economy could be affected by a decrease in the quantity of labour available but an increase in its quality.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 2): a PPC for an economy with two goods that shows the maximum combinations with resources fully used.
  • Application, fall in quantity (up to 3): the PPC shifts to the left (1), with a diagram (1) and an explanation that the productive potential of the economy falls (1).
  • Application, rise in quality (up to 3): the PPC shifts to the right (1), with a diagram and an explanation that productive potential increases (2).
PlanFewer workers: shift in. Better skills: shift out. Net effect depends on the size of each.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

A PPC shows the maximum combinations of two goods that an economy can produce with its resources fully used.

A decrease in the quantity of labour reduces the economy's productive potential, so the PPC shifts to the left, with lower possible output of both goods. An increase in the quality of labour, through better training or education, raises productivity, so the PPC shifts to the right, with higher possible output of both goods.

The overall effect depends on which change is larger. If the improvement in quality outweighs the fall in numbers, the PPC will shift outwards overall, but if not it will shift inwards.

On a PPC for manufactured goods and agricultural goods, a point inside the curve shows that the economy is not using all of its resources, or is using them inefficiently.

Output of both goods is lower than possible. For example, workers are unemployed or factories are idle, so the economy could produce more of one or both goods without giving anything up.

Such a position may not be permanent. In a market economy, demand may recover and firms hire again, moving the economy towards the curve. However it could persist if unemployment is structural, if confidence is low or if markets adjust slowly, so the length of time depends on the cause.

Top tips

  • Separate shift from movement. Shift: more or better resources or technology. Movement along: reallocation. From inside to the curve: using idle resources.
  • Say a shift has no opportunity cost. More of both goods can be made.
  • Use consumer and capital goods. Capital goods now can shift the PPC out later.
  • State what inside means. Unused or inefficiently used resources; output of both goods could rise.
  • Outside is unattainable. Not enough resources now.
  • Mention actual and potential growth. Actual growth: movement towards the curve. Potential growth: an outward shift.
  • Judge permanence. A position inside may be temporary, but can persist with structural problems.
