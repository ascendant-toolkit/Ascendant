Movements Along a PPC

What a movement along the PPC shows about choice and opportunity cost, and why the cost rises. Syllabus 1.4.3.

The big idea

A movement along the PPC shows the economy reallocating resources from one good to the other. More of one good means less of the other: the amount given up is the opportunity cost. With a bowed-out PPC, the opportunity cost rises as more of a good is produced.

Calculating opportunity cost from a PPC

Opportunity cost of one more unit of A = units of B given up ÷ extra units of A gained
PointConsumer goodsCapital goodsOpportunity cost of the move
A090
B20864 capital goods for 20 consumer goods (0.2 per unit)
C407610 for 20 (0.5 per unit)
D606016 for 20 (0.8 per unit)
E80060 for 20 (3 per unit)

The opportunity cost of each extra consumer good rises from 0.2 to 3 capital goods: the more consumer goods are made, the more capital goods must be given up for each extra one. This is why the curve is bowed outwards.

Why opportunity cost rises

  • Resources are not perfectly mobile: those best suited to capital goods are used first for them.
  • To make more consumer goods, resources less suited to them must be switched, so output per resource falls and more capital goods are given up per extra unit.
  • With a straight-line PPC the opportunity cost is constant.

Significance

  • Moving along the PPC shows the choice between goods, for example consumption now versus capital goods for the future.
  • Producing more capital goods today can shift the PPC outwards in future (economic growth), but means fewer consumer goods now.

A government's choice

A country spending more on defence in wartime has less to spend on consumer goods and services.

Exam use: Moving along the PPC.

Investing for growth

Countries such as China have devoted a large share of output to capital goods, giving up some consumption now.

Exam use: Capital goods versus consumer goods.

A farm's land

A farmer who plants more wheat has less land for barley.

Exam use: A firm-level trade-off.

Specialisation

Resources suited to one use are moved to another and become less productive.

Exam use: Why costs rise.

Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain the opportunity cost to Pakistan of producing more consumer goods. (Oct/Nov 2023, P22 style)
ReminderCapital goods given up; resources switched; slower growth.
To produce more consumer goods Pakistan has to move resources away from capital goods. The opportunity cost is the capital goods given up, such as machinery and factories. Fewer capital goods may reduce future growth.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain why the opportunity cost rises along a bowed-out PPC.
ReminderResources not equally suited; less productive switching.
Resources are not equally suited to producing both goods. As more of one good is made, resources less suited to it must be used, so more of the other good is given up for each extra unit.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse, using a PPC, the opportunity cost to an economy of producing more capital goods. (May/June 2022, P22 style)
ReminderDraw PPC; move along; fewer consumer goods; future growth benefit.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A PPC shows the combinations of consumer and capital goods the economy can produce. If the economy moves along the PPC to produce more capital goods, it gives up consumer goods. The opportunity cost is the consumer goods lost, so living standards now may be lower. However, more capital goods may raise productive capacity and shift the PPC outwards in future. The benefit depends on how productive the new capital is and how long it takes.

Top tips

  • Opportunity cost = what is given up. Calculate it from the axis values.
  • Rising opportunity cost = bowed-out PPC. Straight line = constant.
  • Use a diagram. Mark two points and the move between them.
  • Link to the future. More capital goods can shift the PPC outwards.
  • Show working in calculations. Subtract the amounts.
