Definitions of Revenue

Total revenue and average revenue, and how revenue differs from profit. Syllabus 3.6.3.

The big idea

Revenue is the money a firm receives from selling its output. Total revenue (TR) is the total received; average revenue (AR) is the revenue per unit sold. For a firm selling at one price, AR equals the price.

Definitions

TermDefinitionFormulaExample
Total revenue (TR)The total money a firm receives from selling its outputTR = price × quantity sold$5 × 200 = $1,000
Average revenue (AR)Revenue per unit soldAR = TR ÷ quantity sold = price$1,000 ÷ 200 = $5

Revenue is not profit

  • Revenue is the money coming in; costs are the money going out.
  • Profit = total revenue − total cost.
  • A firm can have high revenue but low (or negative) profit if its costs are high.

What revenue depends on

  • The price the firm charges.
  • The quantity sold, which depends on demand, marketing and the price of rivals.
  • For a firm with a demand curve, price and quantity move in opposite directions, so the effect of a price change on revenue depends on PED (see 2.6.4).

Q1 Define 2 marks Define total revenue.
ReminderPrice × quantity.
Total revenue is the money a firm receives from selling its output, calculated as price multiplied by quantity sold.
Q2 Define 2 marks Define average revenue.
ReminderRevenue per unit.
Average revenue is total revenue divided by the quantity sold, that is, revenue per unit.
Q3 Calculate 2 marks A firm sells 200 units at $5 each. Calculate total revenue and average revenue.
ReminderTR = 1,000; AR = 5.
TR = 5 × 200 = $1,000. AR = 1,000 ÷ 200 = $5.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain the difference between revenue and profit.
ReminderMoney in vs revenue minus cost.
Revenue is the money a firm receives from selling its goods. It does not take account of costs. Profit is total revenue minus total costs. A firm with high revenue may still make a loss if costs are high.

Top tips

  • TR = P × Q; AR = TR ÷ Q. AR equals price.
  • Revenue is not profit. Subtract cost.
  • Show working. Units and $.
  • Define with formula. Both words and symbols.
