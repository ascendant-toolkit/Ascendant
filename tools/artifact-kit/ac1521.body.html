The six logic gates, how to write a truth table for a circuit, and the half adder and full adder; animations, adder labs and the real Paper 3 circuit questions from 2021 to 2025.

This page in five sentences

A logic gate turns one or more binary inputs into one output; the Cambridge syllabus uses NOT, AND, OR, NAND, NOR and XOR. A logic circuit joins gates together and you work out its truth table one gate at a time, left to right. A half adder adds two bits using an XOR gate for the sum and an AND gate for the carry. A full adder adds two bits and a carry in, and is built from two half adders and an OR gate. Full adders chained together add whole binary numbers, carrying from one column to the next.

NOT turns 0 into 1 and 1 into 0. AND is 1 only when both inputs are 1. OR is 1 when at least one input is 1. NAND and NOR are AND and OR followed by NOT. XOR is 1 when the inputs are different. A gate may have more than two inputs: a three-input AND is 1 only when all three are 1, and a three-input NOR is 1 only when all three are 0.

2 · Writing a truth table for a circuit

  1. List every combination of the inputs in binary order: 2 inputs give 4 rows, 3 give 8, 4 give 16.
  2. Give every gate output a name (P, Q, R ...) and add a working column for each.
  3. Fill the columns in order from left to right, so a gate is only worked out after the gates that feed it.
  4. The last columns are the outputs. In the exam, one mark is usually for the working columns and the rest for the output column(s).

A circuit with a NOT gate on an input behaves as if that input had been inverted first. A dot where wires meet means the signal is shared by both.

3 · The half adder

A half adder adds two single bits A and B and gives a sum S and a carry C:

ABSC
0000
0110
1010
1101

Sum: S = A XOR B. Carry: C = A AND B. In sum-of-products form, S = ~A.B + A.~B and C = A.B. A half adder cannot accept a carry from a previous column, so it can only be used for the right-hand (least significant) column.

4 · The full adder

A full adder has three inputs: A, B and Cin (the carry from the previous column). It gives a sum and a carry out (Cout). It is two half adders and an OR gate: the first half adder adds A and B (P and Q), the second adds P to Cin (the sum and R), and the OR gate combines the two carries.

Sum = A XOR B XOR Cin, so Sum is 1 when an odd number of the three inputs are 1. Cout is 1 when two or more inputs are 1. As sum-of-products:

  • Sum = A.B.C + A.B.C + A.B.C + A.B.C
  • Cout = A.B.C + A.B.C + A.B.C + A.B.C

Here C stands for the carry in (Cin).

5 · Adding binary numbers

To add two binary numbers, use a half adder for the rightmost column and a full adder for every other column, passing each carry out to the next column's carry in. This is a ripple-carry adder. Adding 0110 (6) and 0111 (7):

1101 is 13. The carry out of the top bit signals overflow when the answer does not fit in the number of bits available.

6 · Common ways to lose marks

  • Working out an output before the gates that feed it. Fill the working columns from left to right.
  • Confusing XOR with OR: XOR is 0 when both inputs are 1.
  • Forgetting that a NOT gate on a wire inverts it for every gate that wire feeds after the NOT.
  • Calling the full adder a "half adder" or the other way round. A half adder has two inputs and no carry in.
  • Not saying what each output means when asked for its purpose: the XOR output is the sum, the AND output is the carry.
  • Writing sum-of-products terms for the 0 rows instead of the 1 rows.

6 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

The calculator in your phone

Inside the processor, an arithmetic logic unit adds numbers using adder circuits built from gates. Every addition you do on a screen is a chain of full adders.

Exam use: full adders chained make a ripple-carry adder.

A light with two switches

A landing light switched from the top and bottom of the stairs is on when exactly one switch is up. That is an XOR gate.

Exam use: XOR is 1 when the inputs are different.

A car that will not start

The engine starts only if the car is in park AND the brake is pressed. An AND gate makes a safe condition out of two inputs.

Exam use: AND is 1 only when every input is 1.

A smoke alarm with a battery check

The alarm sounds if smoke is detected OR the battery is low. OR gates combine conditions where either one is enough.

Exam use: OR is 1 when at least one input is 1.

Checking data for errors

Parity bits use XOR to count whether the number of 1s in a byte is odd or even, the same idea as the sum output of an adder.

Exam use: XOR of several inputs is 1 for an odd number of 1s.

Top tips

  • Name the wires. Give every gate output a letter and add a working column for it.
  • Work left to right. Never fill an output column before the gates that feed it.
  • Look for bubbles. A circle on a gate means NOT after it: NAND, NOR and XNOR.
  • Sum and carry. XOR gives the sum, AND gives the carry. A full adder adds a carry in.
  • Sum-of-products uses the 1 rows. One AND term per row where the output is 1, joined by OR.
  • Count the rows. 2 inputs 4 rows, 3 inputs 8 rows, 4 inputs 16 rows.
