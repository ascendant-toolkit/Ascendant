Reasons for Differences in Wages

Why wages differ between workers: demand and supply, bargaining, discrimination and government policy. Syllabus 3.3.3.

The big idea

Workers in different jobs are paid very different wages. The main reasons are differences in the demand for and supply of labour, bargaining strength, discrimination and government policy. How strongly these act depends on the skill level, the sector (primary, secondary, tertiary) and whether the worker is in the private or public sector.

Reasons for differences in wages

ReasonEffect on wages
Demand for labourHigh demand for a worker's skills (strong product demand, high productivity) raises their wage
Supply of labourFew people with the skills (long training, scarce talent) raises the wage; many people able to do the job lowers it
Bargaining strengthStrong unions or valuable individuals negotiate higher pay
DiscriminationUnequal pay for equal work (for example men and women) can lower some groups' wages
Government policyMinimum wages raise the lowest pay; public sector pay scales; equal pay laws

Applying the reasons

Worker typeWages tend to be…Why
Skilled (surgeon, engineer)HigherScarce skills and long training; high productivity; strong demand
Unskilled (cleaner, labourer)LowerMany workers can do the job; easy to replace; low bargaining power
Primary sector (farming, mining)Often lower (low-skill) but mining can pay wellDepends on skill level, risk and commodity prices
Secondary sector (manufacturing)Middle; unions often strongSkills vary; union bargaining
Tertiary sector (finance, services)Wide range: high for finance, lower for retailSkills and demand differ greatly
Private sectorLinked to profit; wider gapsPay reflects firms' profits
Public sectorSet by government; smaller gaps; more securityPay scales and budgets
Male / femaleGaps can existDiscrimination, occupations, career breaks

Wage gaps are often explained by the demand and supply for each type of labour, but discrimination and government policy can add differences that are not about skills.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain two reasons why wages differ between occupations.
ReminderDemand and supply of labour, bargaining strength, discrimination, government policy.
Differences in demand and supply of labour affect wages. Doctors are scarce and in high demand, so their wages are high. Bargaining strength differs. Workers in strong unions can win higher pay than non-union workers.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain how discrimination may affect wages.
ReminderUnequal pay for equal work.
Discrimination means some workers are treated unfairly, for example by gender. Women may be paid less than men for the same work, so their wages are lower than their skills justify.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse why wages in the private sector may differ from those in the public sector.
ReminderProfit vs pay scales; job security; budgets.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Private sector pay depends on firms' profit and demand for labour, so wages can vary widely. Public sector pay is set by government scales and budgets, so differences are smaller. Public sector jobs often offer more security and pensions, which may make up for lower pay. The size of the gap depends on skills and the budget.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether skill level is the most important reason for differences in wages.
ReminderSkills vs demand, bargaining, discrimination, policy. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Skilled workers earn more because skills are scarce and highly productive. Training is costly, so supply is low. However, wages also depend on demand: a rare skill nobody wants is not well paid. Bargaining strength, discrimination and government policy also matter. Overall, skill is a key reason but demand and bargaining can outweigh it, so it is not always the most important.

Top tips

  • Reasons: demand/supply, bargaining, discrimination, policy. Learn them.
  • Link to skills, sector, public/private. Use the syllabus list.
  • Explain through supply and demand. Scarcity and value.
  • Use examples. Surgeon, cleaner, footballer.
