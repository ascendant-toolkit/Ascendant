Consequences of Price Changes

How price changes affect sales, revenue, consumers and producers, and how to show changes in market conditions on diagrams. Syllabus 2.5.2.

The big idea

A change in price affects sales (the quantity sold) and the revenue and profit of producers, as well as the quantity and choices of consumers. Demand and supply diagrams show the impact of a change in market conditions, such as a rise in income, a tax, a subsidy or a change in costs.

Effects on sales and revenue

Total revenue = price × quantity sold
  • When the price rises (movement along demand), quantity demanded falls. Revenue changes depend on how responsive demand is (see PED in 2.6).
  • When demand shifts right, both price and quantity rise, so revenue rises.
  • When supply shifts right (increase), price falls but quantity rises: sales rise, and revenue may rise or fall.
  • When supply shifts left (decrease), price rises and sales fall.

Effects on consumers and producers

Market changeConsumersProducers
Price rises (demand up)Pay more; some buy less or switch to substitutesHigher revenue and profit per unit; more sales; incentive to produce more
Price falls (supply up)Pay less; can buy more; living standards may riseLower price per unit but more sales; profit depends on costs
Price rises (supply down)Pay more and buy lessHigher price per unit but lower quantity; costs have risen

An effect on revenue needs both price and quantity: multiply the new price by the new quantity and compare with the old.

Solar panels

Falling costs and subsidies have lowered prices and sharply increased sales.

Exam use: A supply increase.

Housing booms

Rising demand raises prices and builders' revenue, but makes homes less affordable.

Exam use: Winners and losers.

Fuel taxes

Taxes on producers raise costs and reduce supply, so prices rise and sales fall.

Exam use: A supply decrease.

Health campaigns

Warnings on sugary drinks have reduced demand and sales.

Exam use: A demand decrease.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain how an increase in the price of milk may affect the revenue earned by milk producers.
ReminderQuantity falls; revenue depends on PED.
Revenue is price multiplied by quantity sold. A higher price means each litre earns more, but quantity demanded falls. If demand is inelastic, revenue rises; if elastic, revenue falls.
Q2 Analyse 6 marks Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how a greater awareness of the health benefits of a product will affect its market. (Oct/Nov 2022, P22 style)
ReminderTastes: D right; price and quantity up; revenue up.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Greater awareness changes tastes in favour of the product, so demand increases and D shifts right. At the old price there is a shortage, so the price rises to P₁ and quantity to Q₁. Producers' sales and revenue rise. Producers may increase supply in time, limiting the rise in price.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how a subsidy given to producers could affect consumers and producers. (Feb/Mar 2022, P22 style)
ReminderS right; price falls, quantity up; consumers gain; producers' costs fall.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A subsidy lowers production costs, so supply increases and S shifts right. The price falls and the quantity rises, so consumers pay less and can buy more. Producers' costs are lower and sales rise, though the price per unit is lower. The cost to the government is an opportunity cost.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, the effects of higher quality products on the market. (Oct/Nov 2024, P22 style)
ReminderBetter quality: D right; price and quantity up.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Higher quality makes the product more attractive, so demand increases and D shifts right. The equilibrium price and quantity rise. Producers' sales and revenue rise. If quality needs costly inputs, supply may fall, reducing the increase in quantity.

Top tips

  • Revenue = price × quantity. Compare old and new.
  • Name the change in market conditions. Then which curve it shifts.
  • Discuss consumers and producers. Both gain or lose differently.
  • Use a diagram. Label D₁, S₁, P₁, Q₁.
  • Evaluate with elasticity or time. Effects vary.
