Causes and Types of Unemployment

Frictional, structural, cyclical and seasonal unemployment: causes, examples and how to tell them apart. Syllabus 4.6.3.

The big idea

The syllabus names four types of unemployment: frictional, structural, cyclical and seasonal. They have different causes, so they need different solutions. When you answer, name the type, say what causes it, and give an example.

The four types

TypeWhat it isCausesExample
FrictionalTemporary unemployment while changing jobs; short termTime to search and apply; lack of information; new entrants to the labour forceA graduate looking for a first job; someone who has just left a job
StructuralWorkers' skills or locations do not match the jobs available; long termDecline of an industry; new technology; globalisation; immobility of labourCoal miners after pit closures when vacancies are in solar installation or in another city
CyclicalNot enough demand in the economy; also called demand-deficientA recession: firms cut output and jobsCar factory workers laid off in a downturn
SeasonalJobs depend on the time of yearWeather, holidays, harvestsSki instructors in summer; fruit pickers after the harvest

Telling them apart

Ask: are there vacancies?

Yes, but the unemployed cannot fill them (wrong skills or place): structural. Yes, and they will soon fill them: frictional. No, because demand is low: cyclical. Yes, but only at certain times of year: seasonal.

Ask: how long and why?

Short: frictional or seasonal. Long: structural. Tied to the business cycle: cyclical.

What causes structural change?

  • Decline of an industry: falling demand for coal, steel or textiles.
  • Technology: machines or software replace some jobs (technological unemployment).
  • Globalisation: firms move production to countries with lower costs.
  • Immobility of labour: occupational (skills) and geographical (location) barriers stop workers moving to the jobs.

Policies depend on the type: demand-side policy for cyclical, supply-side policy for structural and frictional, and diversification for seasonal. See 4.6.5.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Coal and steel closures

In countries such as the UK in the 1980s the closure of coal mines and steelworks left many workers unemployed for years, as new jobs were in other industries and regions.

Exam use: Structural unemployment from industry decline and immobility.

The 2008 recession

After 2008, unemployment rose sharply in Spain and the US because demand fell across the economy. Spain's rate passed 25%.

Exam use: Cyclical unemployment and the link to recession.

Tourism in Greece and Spain

Resort areas have high unemployment in winter and low unemployment in summer.

Exam use: Seasonal unemployment.

Jordan in 2020

A past paper asks which type of unemployment Jordan mainly experienced in 2020, linking the type to economic conditions.

Exam use: Naming and justifying a type from context.

Q1 Define 2 marks Define frictional unemployment. (Feb/Mar 2022, P22)
ReminderBetween jobs, temporary.
Frictional unemployment is unemployment of workers who are between jobs while they search for a new one; it is temporary.
Q2 Define 2 marks Define cyclical unemployment. (May/June 2021, P21)
ReminderLack of total demand, in a recession. Nothing for a fall in demand for one product.
Cyclical unemployment is unemployment caused by a lack of total (aggregate) demand in the economy, for example during a recession.
Q3 Define 2 marks Define structural unemployment. (May/June 2021, P23)
ReminderMismatch of skills or location; change in the structure of the economy.
Structural unemployment is unemployment caused by a change in the structure of the economy, such as the decline of an industry, where workers' skills or location do not match the jobs available.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain how frictional unemployment differs from cyclical unemployment. (May/June 2021, P22)
ReminderUse 'whereas'. Cause, length, and whether vacancies exist.
Frictional unemployment is temporary unemployment while workers move between jobs, whereas cyclical unemployment is caused by a fall in total demand in a recession. Frictional unemployment exists even when the economy is doing well and there are vacancies, whereas in cyclical unemployment there are not enough jobs for all who want one, so it can last as long as the downturn.
Q5 Explain 4 marks Explain two types of unemployment. (Oct/Nov 2023, P22)
ReminderPick two and give cause and example for each.
Structural unemployment occurs when workers' skills do not match the jobs available. If an industry such as coal mining declines, former miners may lack the skills or be unable to move to where vacancies are, so they stay unemployed for a long time. Seasonal unemployment depends on the time of year. Hotel and ski-resort staff may lose work in the off-season, so unemployment rises and falls through the year.
Q6 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how new technology could cause unemployment.
ReminderReplace workers, structural mismatch. Then evaluate: new jobs, retraining.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
New technology such as robots or software can do tasks that workers did before. Firms may need fewer workers, so some lose their jobs. The unemployed may lack the skills for the new jobs created, or live far from them, so this is likely to be structural unemployment which could last a long time. However, technology can also create new jobs and raise incomes, so unemployment may only be temporary if workers can retrain.

Top tips

  • Four types: frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal.
  • Name the cause, not just the type. Search, mismatch, low demand, time of year.
  • Apply to the context. Closing mine: structural. Recession: cyclical. Tourism: seasonal.
  • Ask about vacancies. None because demand is low: cyclical. Vacancies unfilled: structural.
  • Link the type to the policy. That is how you prepare for 4.6.5.
