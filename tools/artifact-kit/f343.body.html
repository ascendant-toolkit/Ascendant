Economies and Diseconomies of Scale

How internal and external economies and diseconomies of scale affect costs, and the U-shaped ATC diagram. Syllabus 3.4.3.

The big idea

Economies of scale are the advantages of producing on a larger scale: average total cost (ATC) falls as output rises. Diseconomies of scale are the disadvantages of becoming too large: ATC rises as output rises. Together they give the U-shaped ATC curve.

Internal economies of scale (within the firm)

TypeHow unit costs fall
Purchasing (bulk-buying)Large orders earn discounts from suppliers
TechnicalLarge firms use bigger, more efficient machines and mass production
ManagerialSpecialist managers raise efficiency
FinancialLarge firms borrow at lower interest and raise finance more easily
MarketingAdvertising costs are spread over more units
Risk-bearingDiversifying products and markets reduces risk

External economies of scale (from the industry)

  • Industry growth brings skilled labour pools, specialist suppliers, better transport and shared research: all firms in the industry benefit (for example the tech cluster in Silicon Valley).
  • They shift the whole ATC curve downwards.

Diseconomies of scale

  • Internal: poor communication, slow decisions, management difficulties, low morale and motivation, weak control and coordination.
  • External: congestion, rising wages and land prices, shortage of resources as an industry grows too large.

The ATC diagram

  • ATC falls as output rises (economies of scale), reaches a minimum (the lowest cost per unit, the minimum efficient scale), then rises (diseconomies of scale).
  • External economies shift the whole ATC curve down; external diseconomies shift it up.

Q1 Define 2 marks Define economies of scale.
ReminderAverage costs fall as output rises.
Economies of scale are the advantages of large-scale production that cause average total costs to fall as output rises.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two internal economies of scale.
ReminderPurchasing, technical, managerial, financial.
Purchasing economies: large firms buy in bulk. They get discounts, so cost per unit falls. Technical economies: large firms use efficient machines. Output per worker rises and unit costs fall.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of diseconomies of scale.
ReminderCommunication, management, morale.
Communication problems in a large firm slow decisions, so costs per unit rise. Workers may feel remote and less motivated, so productivity falls and unit costs rise.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse, using an ATC diagram, how a firm may experience economies and diseconomies of scale.
ReminderU-shaped ATC; falling then rising.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
ATC falls as output rises because of economies of scale such as bulk-buying and specialisation. ATC reaches a minimum at the minimum efficient scale. Beyond this, communication and coordination problems raise ATC: diseconomies of scale. The firm should aim to operate near the minimum.

Top tips

  • Draw a U-shaped ATC and label axes. Mark minimum efficient scale.
  • Internal vs external. Within the firm or industry.
  • Give causes for both economies and diseconomies. Two of each.
  • External economies shift the curve down. Internal ones are movements along it.
