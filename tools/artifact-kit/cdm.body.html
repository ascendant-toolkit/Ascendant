Check Digits: Modulo-10 (ISBN-13) and Modulo-11

Two weighted methods for building and testing a check digit. Both multiply each digit by a weighting, add the results and test the total. They differ in the weightings, the divisor, and which typing mistakes they catch.

The big idea

A check digit is the last digit of a code, chosen so that a weighted total of all the digits passes a test. To make one, total the data digits and work out what must be added to pass. To test one, total every digit including the check digit and see if it passes. The two methods below use the same steps with different numbers.

2 · ISBN-13 worked example

The first 12 digits are 978030640615.

digits     9  7  8  0  3  0  6  4  0  6  1  5
weights    1  3  1  3  1  3  1  3  1  3  1  3
products   9 21  8  0  3  0  6 12  0 18  1 15
total = 9+21+8+0+3+0+6+12+0+18+1+15 = 93
93 ÷ 10 leaves 3, so check digit = 10 − 3 = 7
ISBN-13: 9780306406157

To test 9780306406157, add 7 × 1 to the 93. The total is 100, a multiple of 10, so it is valid.

3 · Modulo-11 worked example

The first 9 digits are 030640615. The weightings count down from 10 to 2 and the check digit has weighting 1.

digits     0  3  0  6  4  0  6  1  5
weights   10  9  8  7  6  5  4  3  2
products   0 27  0 42 24  0 24  3 10
total = 130
130 ÷ 11 = 11 remainder 9, so check digit = 11 − 9 = 2
ISBN-10: 0306406152

To test 0306406152, add 2 × 1 to the 130. The total is 132 = 12 × 11, so it is valid.

Why X?11 − remainder can be 10, and 10 needs two characters. In an ISBN-10 it is written as the letter X. If the remainder is 0 the check digit is 0, not 11.

4 · Which mistakes get caught?

Modulo-10 (weights 1, 3)

Catches any single wrong digit. Catches most swapped neighbours, but misses a swap of two digits that differ by 5 (such as 2 and 7). It also misses any swap of two digits an even distance apart, because they share a weighting.

Modulo-11 (weights counting down)

Catches any single wrong digit and every swapped pair, because every position has a different weighting and 11 is prime, so no swap can leave the total a multiple of 11. The price is the occasional X.

Check digits are for data entryThey find typing and scanning mistakes. Parity, checksums and CRCs find mistakes in transit. See the data entry v data transmission resource for the contrast.

Descriptions are kept general.

The ISBN on a book

New books carry a 13-digit ISBN. A bookshop system recalculates the last digit when you type or scan it, so a slip is flagged straight away. Books printed before 2007 have a 10-digit ISBN that uses modulo-11, which is why some end in X.

Exam use: the standard scenario for both methods. Name the method, show the weights and total.

Supermarket barcodes

The 13-digit barcodes on products use the same 1, 3, 1, 3 weighting and divide by 10, so a misread digit makes the till beep.

Exam use: a scanner is a source of data entry errors; the check digit is the answer.

NHS numbers

The ten-digit NHS number in England ends in a modulo-11 check digit, with weightings counting down from 10 to 2. Numbers that would need a check digit of 10 are not issued.

Exam use: shows modulo-11 protecting a number that people read out and type in.

Exam-style questions

Open each question for reminders, a model answer and common mistakes. Each highlighted phrase earns one mark.

Q1 Calculate 3 marks The first 12 digits of an ISBN-13 are 978013110362. The digits are multiplied by 1 and 3 in turn, the products are added and the check digit makes the total a multiple of 10. Work out the check digit.
ReminderShow every product so method marks are safe.
9 + 21 + 8 + 0 + 1 + 9 + 1 + 3 + 0 + 9 + 6 + 6 = 73
73 ÷ 10 leaves 3, so check digit = 10 − 3 = 7
Digits multiplied by alternating 1 and 3. Total 73. Check digit 7 (10 − 3).
Common mistakeGiving the remainder, 3, as the check digit.
Q2 Calculate 3 marks A modulo-11 check digit is added to the code 48215. The digits are multiplied by 6, 5, 4, 3 and 2, the products are added, and the total is divided by 11. Work out the check digit.
ReminderCheck digit = 11 − remainder.
4×6 + 8×5 + 2×4 + 1×3 + 5×2 = 24 + 40 + 8 + 3 + 10 = 85
85 ÷ 11 = 7 remainder 8, so check digit = 11 − 8 = 3
Total 85. Remainder 8 when divided by 11. Check digit 3 (so the code is 482153).
Common mistakeDividing by 10 out of habit.
Q3 Show 3 marks The code 351460 uses the modulo-11 method, with weightings 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. Show that it is not valid.
ReminderThe check digit (here 0) is part of the total, with weighting 1.
3×6 + 5×5 + 1×4 + 4×3 + 6×2 + 0×1 = 18 + 25 + 4 + 12 + 12 + 0 = 71
71 ÷ 11 = 6 remainder 5
Each digit multiplied by its weighting. Total 71. 71 is not a multiple of 11 (remainder 5), so the code is invalid.
Common mistakeLeaving out the last digit from the total.
Q4 Describe 4 marks Describe how a modulo-11 check digit is worked out.
ReminderWeights, multiply, divide, subtract.
Each digit is given a weighting, counting down (for example 10 to 2). Each digit is multiplied by its weighting and the products are added. The total is divided by 11 and the remainder found. The check digit is 11 minus the remainder (0 if the remainder is 0, and X if the result is 10).
Common mistakeForgetting to say the remainder is taken away from 11.
Q5 Explain 2 marks Explain why some ISBN-10 numbers end in the letter X.
ReminderWhat value does X stand for?
The modulo-11 calculation can give a check digit of 10. 10 is two characters, so it is written as X to keep the check digit one character long.
Common mistakeSaying X means the code is invalid.
Q6 Show 3 marks The ISBN-13 9780131103628 has been typed. Show that it is not valid.
ReminderThe first 12 digits total 73 (see Q1). The check digit has weighting 1.
73 + 8×1 = 81, and 81 is not a multiple of 10
The first 12 digits are weighted 1 and 3 in turn and added, giving 73. The check digit 8 is added: 81. 81 is not a multiple of 10, so the ISBN is invalid.
Common mistakeComparing 8 with the correct check digit but showing no total.
Q7 Explain 3 marks Two neighbouring digits, 2 and 7, are swapped in an ISBN-13. The check digit still passes. Explain how this can happen.
ReminderThink about what the swap does to the total.
Neighbouring digits have weightings 1 and 3, so each digit is multiplied by a different weighting after the swap. The total changes by 2 × (7 − 2) = 10. A change of 10 leaves the total a multiple of 10, so the check passes and the error is missed.
Common mistakeSaying a check digit can never miss a swap.
Q8 Compare 4 marks Compare the ISBN-13 method with the modulo-11 method.
ReminderBoth methods in every point.
Both multiply each digit by a weighting, add the products and use the remainder after division to find the check digit. ISBN-13 uses weightings 1 and 3 in turn, whereas modulo-11 uses weightings that count down. ISBN-13 divides by 10 and gives a digit 0 to 9, whereas modulo-11 divides by 11 and can give 10, written X. Modulo-11 catches every swap of two digits, whereas ISBN-13 misses some, such as digits differing by 5.
Common mistakeDescribing each method separately.

Top tips

  • Making and testing differ. Making: total the data digits only. Testing: include the check digit (weighting 1).
  • Check digit = divisor − remainder. Not the remainder itself.
  • Remainder 0 means check digit 0. Never 10 or 11.
  • Modulo-11 weights count down to 1. ISBN-13 weights alternate 1, 3.
  • Show every product. Method marks survive an arithmetic slip.
  • Say which mistakes are caught. Both catch a wrong digit; only modulo-11 catches every swap.
  • Check digits are for data entry. Do not use them to answer a transmission question.
