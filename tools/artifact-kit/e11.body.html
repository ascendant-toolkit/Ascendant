Finite Resources and Infinite Wants

The basic economic problem, scarcity, and how it affects consumers, workers, firms and governments. Syllabus 1.1.1.

The big idea

The basic economic problem is that people have infinite (unlimited) wants but the resources available to satisfy them are finite (limited). This gap is called scarcity. Because of scarcity, everyone has to make choices, and every choice has an opportunity cost. Every consumer, worker, firm and government faces the problem; no country is free of it, however rich.

Key definitions

TermMeaning
NeedsGoods and services essential for survival, such as food, water, shelter
WantsGoods and services people would like to have; they are unlimited
Finite resourcesLand, labour, capital and enterprise are limited in supply
ScarcityLimited resources compared with unlimited wants
ChoiceHaving to select some things and give up others

Examples of the problem for each decision-maker

Decision-makerUnlimited wantsLimited resourcesChoice
ConsumersMany goods and servicesLimited income and timeSpend on a phone or on holidays
WorkersHigher pay, leisure, job satisfactionLimited time (24 hours a day), skills, energyWork overtime or have more leisure
Producers (firms)More output and profitLimited finance, workers, raw materials, machineryWhich product to make; whether to invest in new machinery or marketing
GovernmentsBetter healthcare, schools, defence, roadsLimited tax revenue and ability to borrowSpend on hospitals or on schools

Wealthy countries still face scarcity: wants always grow with income. Saying a country is rich does not remove the problem. Answering an exam question, link the example to limited resources and a choice.

A household budget

A family must decide between paying for a larger home, a car, holidays and savings with a limited income each month.

Exam use: Consumers face scarcity.

Government budgets

Every year governments choose how to divide limited tax revenue between health, education, defence and transport.

Exam use: Government choices.

A firm's investment choice

A small business with a limited loan must choose between new equipment and advertising.

Exam use: Producers face scarcity.

A student's time

A student has a limited number of hours a day to divide between study, work, friends and sleep.

Exam use: Workers and time.

Q1 Define 2 marks What is the basic economic problem? (Oct/Nov 2020, P11 style)
ReminderInfinite wants but finite resources.
The basic economic problem is that people have infinite (unlimited) wants but resources are finite (limited), so choices have to be made.
Q2 Define 2 marks Define scarcity.
ReminderLimited resources compared with unlimited wants.
Scarcity is the shortage of resources in relation to unlimited wants.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain how scarcity affects consumers.
ReminderLimited income, unlimited wants, choose, opportunity cost.
Consumers have limited income but unlimited wants. They cannot afford everything, so they have to choose which goods and services to buy. Buying one thing means giving up another, so every choice has an opportunity cost.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain why a government faces the basic economic problem.
ReminderLimited revenue; wants for health, education, defence; choice.
A government has limited tax revenue and borrowing, but there are unlimited demands for spending on health, education, defence and roads. It has to choose between them, so spending more on one means less on another: an opportunity cost.
Q5 Explain 4 marks Explain why even a wealthy country such as Switzerland still faces the basic economic problem. (Oct/Nov 2022, P11 style)
ReminderFinite resources, wants rise with income.
Even a wealthy country has finite resources such as land, labour and capital. People's wants are unlimited and tend to increase as incomes rise, so there are still not enough resources to produce everything, and choices must be made.

Top tips

  • Always say 'infinite wants' and 'finite resources'. Both halves score the mark.
  • Scarcity is the cause; choice is the result. Opportunity cost follows.
  • Name the decision-maker's limited resource. Consumers: income. Workers: time. Firms: finance. Governments: revenue.
  • Rich countries face scarcity too. Wants grow with income.
  • Use a specific example. 'Spend on hospitals or schools.'
