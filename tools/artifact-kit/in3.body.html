Causes of Inflation

Demand-pull and cost-push inflation, the wage-price spiral, and imported inflation. Syllabus 4.7.3.

The big idea

The syllabus gives two causes of inflation: demand-pull and cost-push. Demand-pull inflation happens when total demand grows faster than the economy can supply. Cost-push inflation happens when firms' costs of production rise and they pass them on in higher prices. On an AD/AS diagram, demand-pull is AD shifting right; cost-push is AS shifting left.

Demand-pull inflation

  • What happens: AD rises faster than AS, so firms raise prices. It is strongest when the economy is close to full capacity.
  • Causes of a rise in AD: more consumer spending (lower interest rates, tax cuts, higher confidence, a rise in the money supply, higher wages), more investment, more government spending, more exports.
  • Typical signs: low unemployment, strong growth, rising wages.

Cost-push inflation

  • What happens: firms' costs rise, so they raise prices to protect profits. AS shifts left: output may fall while prices rise.
  • Causes: wages rising faster than productivity; higher prices of raw materials and energy; higher indirect taxes; a fall in the exchange rate, which raises the price of imported inputs (imported inflation); higher profit margins; tariffs.

Wage-price spiral

Higher prices lead workers to demand higher wages, which raise firms' costs, so prices rise again. Wage rises can be both demand-pull (more spending) and cost-push (higher costs).

Inflation can fall even when demand rises

If supply grows by more (productivity gains, lower costs, a stronger exchange rate), the price level can fall or rise by less even when AD increases.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

The 1970s oil shocks

When oil prices quadrupled in 1973, firms' costs rose sharply and many economies had high inflation and high unemployment together.

Exam use: Cost-push inflation from higher raw material prices.

Energy and food prices in 2022

Energy and food prices surged after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, pushing up firms' costs and household bills in many countries.

Exam use: Cost-push inflation. Some countries also had demand-pull from earlier stimulus.

Pakistan's demand-pull in 2017

A past paper asks for causes of demand-pull inflation in Pakistan, where government spending and consumer spending were growing.

Exam use: Identify two causes.

A weaker currency in Turkey

When the Turkish lira fell sharply, imports cost much more in lira and inflation rose well above 50% at one stage.

Exam use: Imported inflation after a fall in the exchange rate.

Q1 Identify 2 marks Identify two possible causes of demand-pull inflation. (May/June 2020, P22)
ReminderName two things that raise total demand.
A cut in interest rates (or income tax) raising consumer spending. An increase in government spending (or exports).
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of inflation. (Oct/Nov 2021, P21)
ReminderOne demand-pull and one cost-push is the simplest pair, each developed.
One cause is demand-pull inflation. If consumers spend more, for example after a cut in income tax or interest rates, total demand could rise faster than supply, so firms could raise prices. Another cause is cost-push inflation. If wages or raw material prices rise, firms' costs rise and they may pass this on as higher prices.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain two ways an increase in wages can cause inflation. (Oct/Nov 2021, P23)
ReminderOne way is demand-pull (spending), one is cost-push (costs).
Higher wages raise workers' incomes and so consumption, which increases total demand and could cause demand-pull inflation if supply cannot keep up. Higher wages also raise firms' costs of production, so firms may raise prices, causing cost-push inflation.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain why inflation may fall even if there is an increase in total demand. (Oct/Nov 2020, P21)
ReminderSupply must rise by more: productivity, technology, lower costs, stronger exchange rate.
An increase in total supply may be greater than the increase in total demand, for example if productivity or technology improves so firms can produce more at lower cost. Costs of production may also fall, for example because of lower raw material prices or a stronger exchange rate, which lowers import costs, so firms may not need to raise prices and the price level may rise by less or fall.
Q5 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how a rise in tourism can increase inflation in an economy. (Oct/Nov 2024, P21)
ReminderTourism raises demand; may raise costs (wages, rents). Both types.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A rise in tourism increases spending in the economy, by foreign visitors, so total demand could rise. Hotels, restaurants and shops are likely to sell more and may raise prices if they are close to capacity, causing demand-pull inflation. Tourism could also raise demand for workers and land, pushing up wages and rents, which raise firms' costs and could cause cost-push inflation. A rise in the exchange rate from higher foreign currency inflows may offset some of this.
Q6 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how a fall in a country's foreign exchange rate could increase its inflation rate. (Oct/Nov 2022, P22)
ReminderImports dearer; raw materials; cost-push; also exports cheaper raise demand.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A fall in the exchange rate makes imports dearer in the home currency. Imported raw materials, fuel and components cost firms more, so costs of production rise and AS could shift left, causing cost-push inflation. Imported consumer goods also cost more, which directly raises the price level. At the same time exports are cheaper, so demand for them could rise, raising AD and causing demand-pull inflation. The size of the effect depends on how much the country imports.

Top tips

  • Two causes: demand-pull and cost-push.
  • Demand-pull: AD shifts right. Cost-push: AS shifts left. Draw the diagram.
  • Wages can be both. More spending (demand) and higher costs (cost-push).
  • Exchange rate fall = imported inflation. Cost-push, plus cheaper exports raising demand.
  • Mention capacity. Demand-pull is stronger near full capacity.
