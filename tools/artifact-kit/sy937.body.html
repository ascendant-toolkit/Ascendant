Policies to Reduce Unemployment and Their Effectiveness

9.3.7: demand-side and supply-side policies for each type of unemployment, and how to judge how effective they are. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

The right policy depends on the type of unemployment. Demand-side policies (expansionary fiscal and monetary policy, a lower exchange rate) raise aggregate demand and work on cyclical (demand-deficient) unemployment. Supply-side policies improve how the labour market works and work on frictional, structural and real wage unemployment, so they lower the natural rate. Each policy has limits: time lags, cost, crowding out, inflation, balance of payments effects and fairness. A strong answer matches policy to cause and judges effectiveness.

2 · Demand-side policies

Fiscal policy

Raise government spending or cut taxes (a bigger budget deficit). AD shifts right, the multiplier raises income and output, and firms hire more.

  • Strengths: direct, can be targeted (public works), works well with spare capacity.
  • Weaknesses: time lags, crowding out, debt and interest costs, may raise inflation near full capacity, leakages (saving, imports), no effect on structural unemployment.

Monetary policy

Lower interest rates, quantitative easing or a lower exchange rate. Cheaper borrowing raises consumption and investment, and a lower exchange rate raises exports.

  • Strengths: quick to change, independent of politics.
  • Weaknesses: weak when confidence is low or interest rates are already near zero (liquidity trap), lags, inflation and asset price risks, a lower exchange rate raises import costs.
Keynesian and monetarist viewsKeynesians support demand management when there is a negative output gap and wages are sticky. Monetarists argue demand policy cannot cut unemployment below the natural rate for long without inflation, so the answer is the supply side (see 9.3.4).

3 · Supply-side policies

Interventionist

  • Education and training, apprenticeships: better skills, occupational mobility.
  • Job centres and information: shorter search.
  • Subsidies for hiring the long-term unemployed or for new industries; regional policy and enterprise zones.
  • Relocation grants and housing support: geographical mobility.
  • Infrastructure: jobs now and capacity later.

Market-based

  • Reduce unemployment benefits or tighten conditions: stronger incentive to work.
  • Cut income tax and employers' payroll taxes: more incentive to work and to hire.
  • Weaken union power; reform or restrain the minimum wage: lower real wage unemployment.
  • Deregulate and ease hiring and firing: firms hire more readily.
  • Competition and free trade: efficiency, new jobs in competitive industries.
Weaknesses of supply-side policySlow (training takes years, mobility improves slowly), costly, may be unfair (lower benefits, weaker unions may increase hardship and inequality), cannot create jobs if demand is weak, and uncertain: retraining may not match the jobs that exist.

4 · Judging effectiveness

  • Cause: does the policy fit the type of unemployment?
  • Speed: demand policy acts within months to a few years; supply-side policy takes years.
  • Cost and funding: deficits, debt and crowding out, or the cost of training.
  • Side effects: inflation, current account deficit, inequality, environment.
  • Conflicts between aims: cutting unemployment may raise inflation (Phillips curve trade-off) or worsen the balance of payments.
  • Scale of the problem: a small negative gap needs a small stimulus; hysteresis or deep structural change needs targeted help.
  • Conclusion: usually a mix of demand and supply-side policy matched to the causes.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Germany's short-time work scheme

In the 2008 to 2009 recession, German firms cut hours instead of jobs, with the state paying part of the lost wages. Unemployment barely rose even though output fell sharply, and firms kept skilled workers for the recovery.

Exam use: a targeted policy for cyclical unemployment that also prevented hysteresis. Evaluate on cost to the government and whether it delays needed structural change.

Fiscal stimulus after the financial crisis

Many governments cut taxes and raised spending in 2009, and central banks cut rates to near zero and began quantitative easing. Output recovered but budget deficits and debt rose sharply and the recovery in jobs was slow in some countries.

Exam use: demand-side policy with deficit and debt costs. Say effectiveness depended on the size of the gap, confidence and the multiplier.

Apprenticeships and youth training

Countries such as Germany and Switzerland run apprenticeships that combine work and study, and have low youth unemployment. Other countries have tried to copy them with mixed results.

Exam use: a supply-side policy for structural and youth unemployment. Evaluate on time lags, employer participation and how well training matches the jobs on offer.

Benefit reform

Several countries have shortened the period for which unemployment benefits are paid or linked benefits to job-search requirements. Unemployment fell in some cases, but critics point to lower pay, hardship and more low-quality jobs.

Exam use: market-based supply-side policy. Give both sides: stronger incentives against greater hardship and inequality.

Based on Oct/Nov 2022 · 9708/43 · Q5(b) 13 marks 'The introduction of new technology will increase efficiency but will also cause an increase in unemployment.' Discuss the extent to which you agree.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Technology raises productivity (output per worker), which makes production more efficient and can lower costs and prices.
  • Substituting machines for labour can cause structural unemployment, especially for routine jobs.
  • Counter: new industries and jobs are created, lower prices raise demand, growth creates jobs, and retraining can reduce unemployment. Other types of unemployment (cyclical, frictional) are unrelated to technology.
  • Conclude by weighing the short-run job losses against the long-run gains in output and jobs, and the role of policy.
PlanEfficiency. Job loss. Job creation. Role of retraining. Judgement.

Based on May/June 2023 · 9708/41 · Q4 20 marks A government increases its budget surplus. Evaluate the statement that this will increase unemployment in the short run but make it easier to control a balance of payments deficit in the long run.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge and analysis (14): a budget surplus (revenue above spending); lower government spending or higher taxes reduce AD and, through the multiplier, output, so demand-deficient unemployment may rise; links to interest rates and the exchange rate; the balance of payments accounts.
  • Evaluation (6): the short-run rise in unemployment depends on spare capacity and how the surplus is achieved; lower AD reduces imports and inflation, which improves competitiveness and the current account only over time; other causes of a current account deficit (structural competitiveness, exchange rate); a lower deficit may also lower debt and interest rates.

Practice (not a past paper question) 8 marks Explain why a fiscal stimulus is more effective at reducing cyclical unemployment than structural unemployment.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Cyclical unemployment is caused by a lack of demand, so raising AD with government spending or tax cuts creates demand for labour (about 3 marks).
  • Structural unemployment is caused by a mismatch of skills or location, so extra demand does not match the unemployed to vacancies; training or mobility policies are needed (about 3 marks).
  • Evaluation: a stimulus may help some structural unemployment if it creates jobs in the right region, and if it is too large it may only raise inflation (about 2 marks).
PlanCause of cyclical, so the stimulus fits. Cause of structural, so it does not. One evaluative point.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Cyclical unemployment arises because aggregate demand is too low, so firms cut output and jobs. A fiscal stimulus, such as higher government spending or lower taxes, raises aggregate demand, and with the multiplier effect national income, output and employment all rise, so firms need more workers and cyclical unemployment falls.

Structural unemployment is different because it is caused by a mismatch between the skills or location of workers and the jobs available. If a coal miner has no electrical qualifications, extra demand for installers will not employ him, because he is not able to do the work. The vacancies and the unemployed exist side by side, so raising demand has little effect and may only raise wages and prices. Retraining or help to move would be more effective.

However, a stimulus aimed at jobs in the declining region, such as public works there, could still reduce some structural unemployment, and a deep recession can turn into structural unemployment through hysteresis, so early demand support may prevent that.

Top tips

  • Match policy to cause first. Say which type of unemployment you are tackling before you name the policy.
  • Draw the diagram. AD shifting right towards LRAS for demand-side policy. LRAS or labour market diagrams for supply-side policy.
  • Give the chain. Policy, then AD or labour market change, then output or jobs. Do not just list the policy.
  • Name two or three limits. Time lags, crowding out, inflation, balance of payments, debt, cost, fairness.
  • Split supply-side into two groups. Interventionist (training, subsidies) and market-based (benefits, taxes, unions). Examiners reward the distinction.
  • Say that a mix usually works best. A conclusion that matches different policies to different causes shows judgement.
  • Use 'could' and 'depends on'. The effect depends on spare capacity, the size of the multiplier and the time horizon.
