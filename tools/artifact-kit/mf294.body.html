Consequences of Market Failure

The effects of market failure: over-consumption, under-consumption, non-provision and restricted supply under monopoly. Syllabus 2.9.4.

The big idea

The consequences of market failure are the misallocation of resources: too much of some goods (demerit goods and goods with external costs), too little of others (merit goods and goods with external benefits), none of public goods, and higher prices and restricted supply under monopoly. These harm economic welfare and living standards.

Consequences by cause

CauseConsequenceEffects on society
Demerit goods and goods with external costsOver-consumption and over-productionHealth damage, pollution, congestion, higher health costs for the government, wasted resources
Merit goods and goods with external benefitsUnder-consumption and under-productionLower skills and health, lower productivity, lower growth and living standards
Public goodsNon-provisionSociety goes without defence, street lighting and clean air unless the government steps in
Monopoly powerRestricted supply and higher pricesConsumers pay more and get less choice; reduced real incomes; possible inefficiency

Wider implications

  • Misallocation of resources: resources are not in the most beneficial use for society.
  • Reduced welfare: society's well-being is lower than it could be.
  • Inequality: poorer households suffer more from under-consumed merit goods and pollution.
  • Cost to the government: it may have to pay for healthcare, clean-up and regulation.
  • A case for intervention: governments use taxes, subsidies, regulation and provision (see 2.10).

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain two consequences of market failure.
ReminderOver-consumption, under-consumption, non-provision, higher prices.
Demerit goods such as cigarettes are over-consumed. This harms health and raises costs for the government. Merit goods such as education are under-consumed. This leaves workers less skilled and productivity lower.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain the consequence of non-provision of public goods.
ReminderSociety goes without; government provides.
If public goods are not provided, society goes without goods such as defence and street lighting. Everyone is worse off, so the government has to provide them and fund them through taxes.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse the consequences of a monopoly restricting supply. (Oct/Nov 2023, P22 style)
ReminderHigher prices, less choice, lost consumer welfare, possible inefficiency.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A monopoly restricts output to push up the price. Consumers pay more and buy less, so their real incomes and welfare fall. They also have less choice. With no competition the firm may become inefficient. The harm depends on whether substitutes exist.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether market failure is a more serious problem for developing countries than for developed countries.
ReminderUnder-provision of merit/public goods; externalities; capacity to fix; judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Market failure means resources are misallocated, such as under-consumption of merit goods. Developing countries often have poor health and education provision, so under-consumption is severe. They have less tax revenue to correct it. Developed countries face pollution and monopoly problems but have more resources to intervene. Overall, the impact is likely to be greater in developing countries because needs are larger and government capacity is smaller, though the type of failure differs.

Top tips

  • Use the syllabus words. Over-consumption, under-consumption, non-provision.
  • Say who loses. Society, consumers, government.
  • Link to living standards. Productivity, health, welfare.
  • Mention government costs. Healthcare, clean-up.
  • Evaluate. Size of the failure, capacity to fix.
