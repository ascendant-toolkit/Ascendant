Division of Labour

What division of labour is, and its advantages and disadvantages. Syllabus 3.3.5.

The big idea

Division of labour (worker specialisation) is when the production of a good is split into separate tasks, each done by a different worker (or group), so each worker specialises in one task. Adam Smith's pin factory is the classic example. It raises output but can make work boring and leaves production dependent on every stage.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Higher productivity: workers become skilled and quick at one task (practice makes perfect)
  • Time saved switching between tasks
  • Lower costs and prices through economies of scale
  • Workers can be matched to the task they do best
  • Easier to train new workers for a single task
  • Suits mass production and machines
  • Higher output raises wages and profit

Disadvantages

  • Boredom and low motivation from repetitive work
  • Dependence: one stage stopping halts all production
  • Loss of wider skills and difficulty finding other jobs (low occupational mobility)
  • Lower quality if workers lose pride in the finished product
  • Higher labour turnover and absence
  • Workers can be replaced by machines more easily

Example: making pins

One worker doing every step might make 20 pins a day. If ten workers each do one or two steps, they may make thousands between them.

Division of labour is specialisation by workers within a firm. Specialisation by countries or firms is a different idea.

Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether division of labour benefits firms and workers.
ReminderProductivity and wages vs boredom and dependence. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Division of labour lets workers specialise, raising productivity and lowering unit costs. Firms gain lower prices and more profit; workers may earn higher wages. However, repetitive work may reduce motivation and skills, and workers can be replaced by machines. Overall, firms usually gain, but workers' gains depend on training and job variety, so firms may use job rotation to reduce the downsides.

Top tips

  • Define: tasks split between workers. Specialisation.
  • Advantages: productivity, cost, training. Disadvantages: boredom, dependence, skills.
  • Use the pin factory example. Easy to remember.
  • Evaluate with job rotation. A way to reduce the downsides.
