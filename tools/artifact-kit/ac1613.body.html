Scheduling Routines

Coursebook: Chapter 16 · System software, section 16.1 Purposes of an operating system

Why scheduling is needed and the four routines (first come first served, shortest job first, shortest remaining time, round robin) with Gantt charts and waiting times; animations, a scheduling simulator and the real Paper 3 questions from 2022 and 2025.

This page in five sentences

With many processes ready to run and one CPU, the OS needs scheduling to choose which process runs next, so that the CPU is never idle, all processes get fair access and high-priority jobs finish first. The syllabus routines are first come first served (run in order of arrival, to completion), shortest job first (run the process with the shortest burst time, to completion), shortest remaining time (the pre-emptive form of shortest job first) and round robin (each process gets a fixed time slice in turn). Each has a benefit and a drawback: shortest job first and shortest remaining time give the lowest waiting times but can starve long processes, round robin is fair but swaps processes often, first come first served is simple but short jobs wait behind long ones. The average waiting time is the usual way to compare them.

1 · Why scheduling is needed

  • It makes multi-tasking possible: several processes appear to run at the same time.
  • It keeps the CPU busy all the time, which reduces idle time.
  • It gives all processes fair access to the CPU, and prevents starvation (a process never getting a turn).
  • It allows high-priority jobs to be completed first.
  • It keeps waiting times short and makes processes execute efficiently.

2 · Words to know

TermMeaning
Burst timeThe CPU time a process needs to finish.
Waiting timeTime a process spends in the ready queue: turnaround time minus burst time.
Turnaround timeTime from arrival to finishing.
ThroughputNumber of processes completed per unit of time.
Pre-emptiveThe scheduler can stop a running process before it has finished (time slice ended, or a better process arrived).
Non-pre-emptiveA process keeps the CPU until it finishes or blocks.
Time quantum (slice)The fixed length of time each process may run in round robin.
StarvationA process is held back for ever because others are always chosen first.

3 · The four routines

RoutineFunctionBenefitDrawback
First come first served (FCFS)Processes are queued as they arrive and run one at a time, in that order. Non-pre-emptive.Simple: no complex logic, little processor overhead; no starvation because every process eventually runs.Short jobs wait behind long ones, so the average waiting time can be long.
Shortest job first (SJF)The process with the shortest burst time is chosen next and runs to completion. Non-pre-emptive.Short jobs do not wait for long ones; the average waiting time is low and throughput is high.Long jobs may starve if shorter ones keep arriving.
Shortest remaining time (SRT)The pre-emptive version of SJF: the process with the least time left runs. When a process with a shorter burst time arrives, the running process is replaced.Short processes finish very quickly; waiting time is minimised.Long processes may starve; many switches.
Round robin (RR)Each process runs for a fixed time quantum in turn; when its slice ends, its state is saved and it goes to the back of the queue. Pre-emptive.Fair: no starvation, and a short response time for every process.Many switches between processes; long jobs take longer overall; poor if the quantum is badly chosen.

5 · Common ways to lose marks

  • Writing only "it is fair" or "it is fast". Name the property: no starvation, shortest waiting time, a fixed time slice.
  • Describing shortest job first as pre-emptive. SJF runs to completion; the pre-emptive routine is shortest remaining time.
  • Giving no function for round robin: a fixed time quantum, state saved when the slice ends, the next process runs.
  • Forgetting that a drawback of SJF and SRT is starvation of long processes.
  • Mixing waiting time (in the queue) with burst time (CPU needed).

6 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

The queue at a canteen

First come first served: the first person in the queue is served first. It is fair, but someone who only wants a drink waits behind a large order.

Exam use: FCFS has no starvation but short jobs may wait.

The express till

Shortest job first: customers with few items go first. Waiting is shorter overall, but a customer with a full trolley may be pushed back if express customers keep arriving.

Exam use: SJF risks starving long jobs.

A teacher helping a class

Round robin: the teacher gives each student two minutes, then moves to the next, and comes back round. Nobody is ignored, but no student gets a long uninterrupted time.

Exam use: fixed time slices, state saved between turns.

A hospital emergency department

Priority scheduling: the most urgent case is seen first and may interrupt a less urgent one (pre-emption).

Exam use: higher-priority jobs are completed first.

Your computer running many apps

Operating systems mix these ideas: time slices for fairness, priorities for important tasks, and short jobs quickly so the system feels responsive.

Exam use: real schedulers combine routines.

O/N 2022 · 9618/32 · Q9 Explain / Describe 3 + 6 marks (a) Explain the need for scheduling in process management. (b) Describe these scheduling routines and identify a benefit for each one: shortest job first; round robin; first come first served.
Reminder(b) two marks each: function and benefit.

(a) Any three: Scheduling allows more than one program or task to appear to be executed at the same time (multi-tasking, multiprogramming) To allow high-priority jobs to be completed first To keep the CPU busy all the time to ensure that all processes execute efficiently and have reduced waiting times, fair access to the CPU and no starvation of some processes

(b) Shortest job first: processes are executed in ascending order of CPU time required, so short processes run first and then longer ones Benefit: increased throughput, because more processes can be executed in a smaller amount of time
Round robin: each process is served by the CPU for a fixed time slice, so all processes get the same priority Benefit: starvation does not occur, because in each cycle every process gets a time slice
First come first served: no complex logic, each process request is queued as it is received and executed one by one Benefit: starvation does not occur because every process will eventually run, or there is less processor overhead

Common mistakeDescribing shortest job first as 'the quickest process' without saying the processes run in ascending order of CPU time needed, and giving no benefit.
M/J 2025 · 9618/32 · Q5(b) Describe 4 marks Describe the function of the shortest job first scheduling routine and give a benefit of this routine.
ReminderFunction (up to three marks) and one benefit.

Function, up to three: The processes are queued as they arrive The process with the shortest time to complete (burst time) is selected first and executed The process continues until complete or put in a waiting state; it is non-pre-emptive The scheduler continues to choose shorter processes over longer ones if they keep being added, which can cause starvation for longer jobs

Benefit, one: Shorter jobs do not have to wait for longer jobs; significantly reduces average waiting time; or higher throughput

Common mistakeCalling it pre-emptive. A shortest job first process keeps the CPU until it is complete or waiting.
M/J 2025 · 9618/31 · Q8(b) Explain 4 marks Explain the function of the shortest remaining time scheduling routine and give a benefit of this routine.
ReminderShortest burst first, but pre-emptive.

Function, up to three: The processes are queued as they arrive Processes with the shortest burst time are executed first It is a pre-emptive scheduling routine: when a process with a shorter burst time arrives, the existing process is replaced by the shorter process The scheduler continues to choose shorter processes over longer ones, which can cause starvation for longer jobs

Benefit, one: Processes with a short burst time are processed very quickly; or waiting time is minimised

Common mistakeLeaving out that a newly arrived shorter process pre-empts the running one. That is what separates it from shortest job first.
M/J 2025 · 9618/33 · Q6 Describe / Explain 2 + 4 marks (a) Describe one reason why scheduling is necessary in process management. (b) Explain the function of the round robin scheduling routine and give a benefit of this routine.
Reminder(b) fixed time quantum, state saved, next process, plus a benefit.

(a) Any two marks: Scheduling is required so all processes are executed in a timely manner and enables multi-tasking or multiprogramming to minimise CPU idle time to ensure no process is starved of resources, or fair access to resources ensures jobs are completed in order of priority

(b) Function, up to three: Processes are queued as they arrive It is a pre-emptive routine A fixed time quantum (equal time slice) is given to each process When a time slice ends the status of the process is saved and queued so it can continue next time The next process is executed for its time slice; its previous state is restored if applicable If a process completes within its time slice, the next process is executed

Benefit, one: Reduces average response time by limiting each process to a fixed amount of time; or no starvation of resources

2. Review questions

Written for practice and revision, not taken from past papers. Use them to check your understanding before attempting the real questions above.

Distinguish 2 marks Distinguish between shortest job first and shortest remaining time.
ReminderPre-emption.

Shortest job first runs the chosen process to completion (non-pre-emptive) Shortest remaining time replaces the running process when a process with a shorter remaining time arrives (pre-emptive)

Calculate 3 marks Three processes arrive at time 0 with burst times P1 = 5, P2 = 2 and P3 = 3. Using first come first served, state the waiting time of each process and the average.
ReminderP1 first.

P1 waits 0, P2 waits 5, P3 waits 7 Total waiting = 12 Average = 12 / 3 = 4

Calculate 3 marks For the same processes use shortest job first. State the order, each waiting time and the average.
ReminderShortest first: P2, P3, P1.

Order: P2, P3, P1 Waiting: P2 = 0, P3 = 2, P1 = 5 Average = 7 / 3 = 2.33 (shorter than FCFS)

Explain 3 marks Explain one drawback of round robin when the time quantum is very small.
ReminderWhat happens at each switch?

The OS must switch between processes very often Each switch needs the state of the process to be saved and restored So much of the CPU time is spent switching instead of running the processes

Explain 2 marks Explain why a long process may starve under shortest remaining time.
ReminderShorter processes keep arriving.

The scheduler always chooses the process with the least time left If shorter processes keep arriving, the long one is never chosen

Top tips

  • Four routines. Round robin, shortest job first, first come first served, shortest remaining time.
  • Function plus benefit. Most questions want both. Learn one of each for every routine.
  • SJF runs to completion. SRT is the pre-emptive one: a shorter arrival replaces the running process.
  • Round robin: fixed time slice. State saved when the slice ends; the next process runs.
  • Starvation. SJF and SRT can starve long jobs; FCFS and round robin do not.
  • Waiting time is time in the queue. Turnaround minus burst.
