Movements Along a Demand Curve

Extensions and contractions in demand and how they are shown on the diagram. Syllabus 2.2.2.

The big idea

A change in the price of a good causes a movement along its demand curve. A fall in price causes an extension of demand (quantity demanded rises: a move down the curve). A rise in price causes a contraction of demand (quantity demanded falls: a move up the curve). The curve itself does not move.

Extension and contraction

Extension in demandContraction in demand
CauseA fall in the price of the goodA rise in the price of the good
EffectQuantity demanded risesQuantity demanded falls
On the diagramMovement down along the curveMovement up along the curve
CurveThe same curve: no shift

Reasons for the move

  • Substitution effect: consumers switch towards or away from the good as its relative price changes.
  • Income effect: a lower price raises real income, so consumers can buy more.
  • Diminishing marginal utility: consumers buy more only if the price falls.

Examples of a price change

  • A shop's sale reduces the price: extension.
  • A rise in tax on cigarettes raises the price: contraction.
  • A fall in the cost of production lowers the price: extension.

The exam trap: extension/contraction (price of the good itself changes, movement along the curve) versus increase/decrease in demand (other factors change, the curve shifts).

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Black Friday

Big discounts push prices down, and shops sell far larger quantities.

Exam use: Extension in demand.

Tobacco taxes

Higher taxes raise cigarette prices and cut the quantity demanded, though not by much.

Exam use: Contraction in demand.

Cheaper flights

Budget airlines cut fares, and more people choose to fly.

Exam use: A price fall.

Fuel price spikes

When petrol prices jump, some people drive less.

Exam use: Contraction.

Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain the difference between an extension in demand and an increase in demand. (Feb/Mar 2021, P22 style)
ReminderExtension: price change, movement along. Increase: other factor, shift right.
An extension in demand is caused by a fall in the good's own price and is shown by a movement down along the same demand curve. An increase in demand is caused by a change in another factor such as income and is shown by a shift of the whole curve to the right.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain how a rise in the price of cigarettes will affect the quantity demanded.
ReminderContraction; substitution; income; addiction may limit.
A rise in price causes a contraction in demand: a movement up along the demand curve. Cigarettes are dearer, so some smokers cut down or quit and affordability falls. The fall may be small because of addiction.

Top tips

  • Own price changes = movement along. Anything else = shift.
  • Extension = price falls. Contraction = price rises.
  • The curve does not move. Say 'a movement along the same curve'.
  • Draw and label P₁, Q₁, P₂, Q₂. Show the arrows.
  • Know the difference from increase and decrease. A classic question.
