Paper 1 Multiple Choice: Technique and Practice

How Paper 1 works, a 60 minute plan, the four-step method, how wrong options are built, nine question types with a way to attack each, and sixteen real past questions.

This part in five sentences

Paper 1 is a multiple choice paper of 30 questions in 1 hour, each with four options and worth 1 mark, so it carries 30 of the 90 marks at AS Level; you answer on a separate sheet in soft pencil, a calculator is allowed, and the instructions say each correct answer scores one mark and mention no deduction for a wrong one, so every question should be answered. The questions cover the whole syllabus, broadly in syllabus order, and test knowledge and understanding, application and analysis through definitions, calculations, tables, short scenarios and diagrams. Each wrong option is built to catch a particular mistake (a reversed relationship, the wrong curve, a true statement that does not answer the question), so the best method is to read the stem carefully, predict the answer, then test every option. Time is not usually the problem: you have 2 minutes per question, so the marks are lost to misreading, calculation slips and unfinished elimination. This page sets out the facts, the method, the question types with real examples, and a time plan.

2 · Using the 60 minutes

StageTimeWhat to do
First pass35 to 40 minutesAnswer every question you can do with confidence at once (about 1 to 1.5 minutes each). Mark with a tick the ones you are unsure of and leave long diagram or calculation questions for later.
Second pass15 to 20 minutesReturn to the marked questions. Do the calculations, sketch the diagrams, eliminate options.
Final check5 minutesMake sure every question has an answer on the sheet (a guess costs nothing). Check that answers are in the right row.
  • Do not spend more than 3 minutes on any one question the first time.
  • Fill in the answer sheet as you go, not at the end, and check the question number after skipping.
  • If two options remain, choose one and move on: you can return if time allows.

3 · The four steps

StepWhat to doWhy
1 · ReadRead the stem twice. Underline not, least likely, most likely, always, only, units, and the exact thing asked (price, quantity, revenue).Misreading a "not" question turns a right answer into a wrong one.
2 · PredictCover the options and decide what the answer should be, or draw a quick diagram.It stops you being pulled towards a plausible-looking option.
3 · TestRead all four options. Cross out the clearly wrong ones and say why each is wrong. Check the remaining ones against the stem.The right answer is often only the best of two partly true options.
4 · CheckFor calculations, check units, signs and percentage versus percentage point. For policies, check the direction and the time period.Many wrong options are the answer with a sign or unit error.

4 · How wrong options are built

DistractorWhat it doesExample
Reversed relationshipRight variables, wrong direction"A depreciation lowers the price of imports"
Wrong curve or wrong conceptShifts demand when supply should shift, or uses a movement for a shiftA tax shown as a shift of demand
True but irrelevantA correct statement that does not answer the question askedA correct fact about GNI in a GDP question
Partly trueCorrect first half, wrong second half"A merit good is non-rival and under-consumed"
Calculation slipThe answer you get if you forget a stepUsing the new quantity but forgetting to deflate nominal GDP
Absolute words"Always" or "only" make a statement false"Tariffs always raise welfare"
Similar termsSwaps related termsBudget deficit and current account deficit; real and nominal

5 · Question types and how to attack them

TypeTypical stemHow to attack it
1 · Recall and definition"Which type of good is healthcare?"Answer from memory before reading the options; check each option's definition.
2 · Calculation"What is the real GDP in year 2?"Write the formula, substitute, check units; use the options to check your answer.
3 · "Not", "least likely""What is not an example of protectionism?"Decide whether each option is true, and pick the odd one out. Underline "not".
4 · Statement correct or incorrect"What does not happen when price allocates resources?"Test each statement separately against a definition.
5 · Two-part combination"How is this likely to affect the exchange rate and cost-push inflation?"Work out each part separately, then find the option with both right.
6 · Table or data"What is the current account balance?"Work out what is asked from the table; sum with signs.
7 · Diagram"Which area represents total revenue?"Redraw or mark it; label the curves; identify what shifted and why.
8 · Cause and effect"Which policy would reduce both unemployment and the deficit?"Trace the chain step by step; ask what makes it less effective.
9 · Positive and normative"Which statement is normative?"Positive statements can be tested with evidence; normative ones contain "should" or a value judgement.

7 · Words in the stem that change the answer

  • Not, least likely, except: find the false option; three options will be true.
  • Most likely, best explains: several options may be partly true; choose the strongest.
  • Always, only, never: usually make a statement false.
  • Effective, ineffective: consider whether a policy is above or below equilibrium (price controls), or the elasticity.
  • Short run or long run: the answer may differ.
  • Percentage or percentage point: from 4% to 6% is a 2 point rise and a 50% rise.
  • Nominal or real, gross or net, deficit or debt: check the exact term.

8 · Common ways to lose marks

  • Misreading "not", or answering the question you expected.
  • Not reading all four options after seeing one that looks right.
  • Calculating with the wrong figures (nominal instead of real; price instead of percentage change).
  • Spending too long on one question, then guessing the last ones.
  • Leaving blanks: there is no penalty for a wrong answer.
  • Shifting the wrong curve in a diagram question.
  • Changing a right answer without a clear reason.
Exam tipFor every question you get wrong in practice, write down which distractor caught you: a reversed relationship, the wrong curve, a true-but-irrelevant option or a calculation slip. You will see the same traps again.

6 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

A 'not' question

'What is not an example of protectionism?' Options included export subsidies, import subsidies, quotas and tariffs. Three are protectionist; subsidising imports would encourage imports, so it is the odd one out.

Exam use: reading 'not' and finding the false option.

A calculation with a table

A table gives exports, imports, services and income. The current account balance is the sum of the four balances with signs: (150 − 200) + (70 − 50) + (120 − 100) + (15 − 20) = −15.

Exam use: summing with signs and checking the units.

A two-part combination

'A worsening current account will affect the exchange rate and cost-push inflation how?' A fall in the exchange rate raises import prices, so cost-push inflation rises: depreciate and increase.

Exam use: working each part separately.

A true statement that does not answer

In a question on why a government would devalue, an option about reducing demand-pull inflation is true of other policies but is the opposite of the aim of a devaluation.

Exam use: a true-but-irrelevant distractor.

Normative or positive

'The rate of interest should be reduced' is normative because it contains a value judgement; 'if interest rates fall, consumption is likely to increase' can be tested with evidence.

Exam use: spotting 'should'.

Top tips

  • Underline 'not', 'always', 'only', 'most likely'. Misreading costs the mark.
  • Predict, then look. Cover the options first.
  • Test all four options. The best of two partly true ones.
  • Calculate with the formula. Percentages, real values, signs.
  • Draw a quick diagram. Shift or movement, which curve.
  • Never leave a blank. A wrong answer is not penalised in the instructions.
  • Two minutes a question. Mark and return after 3 minutes.
