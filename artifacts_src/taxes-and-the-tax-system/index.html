Taxes and the Tax System

Progressive, proportional and regressive taxes, direct and indirect taxes, average and marginal rates and the effect of a tax-free allowance. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

Taxes are classified by how the average tax rate changes with income. A progressive tax takes a larger share of income as income rises (income tax with higher bands and a tax-free allowance), a proportional tax takes the same share, and a regressive tax takes a smaller share (many sales taxes, because the poor spend a larger share of income). Direct taxes (income, wealth, inheritance) are paid on income or wealth, indirect taxes (sales, fuel duty) on spending. A government can reduce inequality by making the tax system more progressive and by taxing wealth, but very high rates may affect incentives and encourage avoidance.

2 · Average and marginal rates

The average rate is total tax divided by income. The marginal rate is the tax on the next dollar earned. With a tax-free allowance of $10,000 and a 20% rate above it, a person on $30,000 pays $4,000 (an average rate of 13.3%) and the marginal rate is 20%. As income rises, the average rate climbs towards 20% but never reaches it, which makes the tax progressive.

Disposable incomeDisposable income is income minus direct taxes plus cash benefits. Sales taxes are paid out of disposable income, so they are not subtracted when it is calculated. If a household earns $100 and pays $45 income tax and receives $5 in benefits, its disposable income is $100 − $45 + $5 = $60.

Income tax bands and allowances

Most countries tax income in bands with higher rates on higher slices, and exempt a first slice of income. A worker moving into a higher band pays the higher rate only on the part above it, so the average rate rises more slowly than the marginal rate.

Exam use: a worked example of a progressive tax. Distinguish marginal from average rates.

Sales taxes and fuel duty

A 10% sales tax takes the same rate from every purchase, but a poorer household spends nearly all its income while a richer one saves more. So the tax takes a larger share of the poorer household's income: it is regressive in effect.

Exam use: indirect taxes are likely to be regressive. A Paper 3 question asked which tax changes increase inequality.

A tax-free allowance

Most income taxes let people earn a first slice free of tax. A person on a low income may pay no tax at all, while a person on a high income pays tax on most of what they earn, so the average rate rises with income.

Exam use: why an allowance makes an income tax progressive.

Practice · 12 marks · Explain what is meant by equity and consider whether a progressive income tax is likely to promote it.
PlanDefine equity (fairness, a value judgement, different from equality). Define progressive tax with a numerical example. Link to ability to pay and the Lorenz curve. Evaluate: incentives, avoidance, cost, whether people agree it is fair. Conclude.
KANE

Equity means fairness, which is a value judgement, so it is different from equality, which means that everyone receives the same income or wealth. A progressive income tax is one in which the average tax rate rises as income rises, so that higher income earners pay a larger share of their income in tax.

A progressive income tax is likely to promote equity if fairness is judged by ability to pay. For example, with a tax-free allowance of $10,000, a rate of 20% on income from $10,000 to $40,000 and 40% above that, a worker earning $30,000 pays $4,000, an average rate of about 13%, and one earning $100,000 pays $30,000, an average rate of 30%. The richer worker therefore contributes a larger share, and the revenue could fund benefits and public services that help low-income households. This is likely to reduce the Gini coefficient and move the Lorenz curve towards the line of equality.

However, people disagree about what is fair. Some would argue it is fairer for everyone to pay the same share of their income, or that people should keep what they earn. High marginal rates could also reduce the incentive to work, save and take risks, and encourage tax avoidance, which could lower revenue and growth. Overall, a progressive tax is likely to promote equity as many people understand it, but how far depends on the rates, on avoidance, and on how the revenue is spent.

Top tips

  • Describe tax types by the average rate. Progressive rises, proportional constant, regressive falls with income. Use a tiny numerical example.
  • Use average rates to classify a tax. Progressive means the average rate rises with income, proportional means it is constant and regressive means it falls.
