Production Possibility Curves

Defining a PPC, drawing it and interpreting what it shows about scarcity and opportunity cost. Syllabus 1.4.1.

The big idea

A production possibility curve (PPC) shows the maximum combinations of two goods or services an economy can produce with its existing resources and technology, when all resources are fully and efficiently used. It is a model of scarcity, choice and opportunity cost: to produce more of one good, the economy must produce less of the other.

Drawing a PPC

  • Put one type of good on the horizontal axis (for example consumer goods) and the other on the vertical axis (for example capital goods).
  • The curve runs from one axis to the other. Where it meets the vertical axis, all resources make capital goods; where it meets the horizontal axis, all make consumer goods.
  • It is usually drawn bowed outwards (concave to the origin), because resources are not equally good at making both goods.
  • Label both axes and the curve (PPC).

Reading a PPC

FeatureWhat it shows
A point on the curveA combination of the two goods that can be produced with all resources used fully and efficiently
Axis interceptsThe maximum of one good if none of the other is made
SlopeThe trade-off: how much of one good must be given up for more of the other (opportunity cost)
ShapeBowed out: opportunity cost rises as more of a good is made. Straight line: constant opportunity cost

A PPC is drawn for a given time, resource base and technology. It can show goods in an economy, in a firm, or in a household's use of time, though the syllabus concentrates on whole economies.

Q1 Define 2 marks Define a production possibility curve (PPC). (May/June 2021, P22 style)
ReminderMaximum combinations of two goods with existing resources.
A PPC shows the maximum combinations of two goods or services that an economy can produce with its existing resources.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain why a PPC is usually bowed outwards.
ReminderResources not equally suited; opportunity cost rises.
Resources are not equally suited to producing both goods. When an economy makes more of one good it has to use resources less suited to it, so more of the other good is given up for each extra unit: the opportunity cost rises, giving a bowed-out curve.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain how a PPC illustrates scarcity and opportunity cost.
ReminderLimited resources; cannot have more of both; move along the curve.
A PPC shows the limit of what an economy can produce with its scarce resources, so scarcity is shown by the curve itself. To produce more of one good the economy has to move along the curve, which means producing less of the other. The amount of the other good given up is the opportunity cost.
Q4 Identify 2 marks Identify two things a point on a PPC tells us.
ReminderFull employment; efficient; combination possible.
It is a combination of two goods that the economy can produce. All resources are fully and efficiently used.

Top tips

  • Label both axes and the curve. Always.
  • A PPC shows what is possible. Not what consumers want.
  • Bowed out means rising opportunity cost. Straight line means constant.
  • A point on the curve is efficient. All resources fully employed.
  • Draw it with a ruler for the axes and freehand for the curve. Neat diagrams earn marks.
