Robotics

What robotics is, the characteristics of a robot, and the advantages and disadvantages of robots in industry, transport, agriculture, medicine, domestic settings and entertainment.

The big idea

Robotics is about designing, building and operating robots: machines with a mechanical structure, electrical components and a program that does what they are told, exactly and repeatedly.

6.2 Robotics

Robotics is a branch of computer science that covers the design, construction and operation of robots. Examples include factory equipment such as welding arms, domestic robots such as robot vacuum cleaners, and drones.

Characteristics of a robot

A mechanical structure

A framework or body: arms, joints, wheels, legs or propellers, made of metal or plastic.

Electrical components

Sensors to detect the surroundings, a microprocessor to process the data and make decisions, and actuators (motors) to move the parts.

Programmable

Its behaviour is controlled by a program that can be written, changed or replaced, so the same robot can do different jobs.

Sense, think, act

The robot senses with sensors, thinks with the microprocessor and its program, then acts with actuators, repeatedly.

A robot is more than an automated system: it has a physical body that moves or manipulates things. A street light is an automated system but not a robot. The line is not always sharp, so use the three characteristics above.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Can work continuously, without rest, 24 hours a day
  • Very accurate and can repeat a task exactly, so fewer mistakes
  • Can work in dangerous places (heat, radiation, explosives)
  • Can lift heavy loads or do very precise work
  • Lower running costs in the long term

Disadvantages

  • High cost to buy, program and maintain
  • Jobs may be lost to robots
  • Cannot think creatively or cope with unexpected problems
  • Breakdowns and faults stop the work
  • Can be unsafe for people nearby if something goes wrong
Exam tipAlways link advantages and disadvantages to the role in the question (for example surgery: precision, but expensive and cannot react to the unexpected).

Descriptions are kept general. Check current details before quoting specifics.

A car factory

Robot arms weld and paint car bodies all day and repeat each movement exactly.

Exam use: industry: accuracy, 24-hour work, against cost and jobs.

Robot vacuum cleaner

It senses walls and furniture, a microprocessor plans a route, and motors move the wheels and brushes. You program the schedule.

Exam use: a domestic robot with all three characteristics: structure, electrical components, programmable.

Surgical robots

A surgeon controls robot arms that make very small, steady movements through tiny cuts.

Exam use: medicine: precision, but costly and needing a skilled surgeon.

Farm drones

Drones fly over fields, photograph crops and spray only where needed.

Exam use: agriculture: saves time and chemicals, but needs batteries and trained operators.

Bomb disposal robots

A robot is sent to examine and disarm a dangerous device, so no person is at risk.

Exam use: working in dangerous places is a key advantage.

Theme park animatronics

Moving characters follow a programmed show again and again.

Exam use: entertainment: the same program repeats exactly.

Exam-style questions

Open each question for reminders, a model answer and common mistakes. Each underlined phrase earns one mark; the number shows which mark point it is.

Q1 State 2 marks State what is meant by robotics.
ReminderBranch of CS, and what it covers.
Robotics is a branch of computer science that deals with the design, construction and operation of robots.
Common mistakeGiving only an example of a robot.
Q2 Describe 3 marks Describe three characteristics of a robot.
ReminderStructure, electrical, programmable.
A mechanical structure or framework (body, arms, wheels) Electrical components such as sensors, a microprocessor and actuators It is programmable (its instructions can be written or changed)
Common mistakeListing "it moves" and "it is smart". Use the textbook characteristics.
Q3 Explain 4 marks A robot vacuum cleaner cleans a room. Explain how it uses sensors, a microprocessor and actuators.
ReminderSense, think, act, in the scenario.
Sensors (for example proximity or bump sensors) detect walls and furniture. The data is sent to the microprocessor. The microprocessor uses its program to decide the route or turn. Actuators (motors) move the wheels and brushes.
Common mistakeGiving the sensors without saying what they detect in the vacuum scenario.
Q4 Describe 4 marks Robots are used in surgery. Describe two advantages and two disadvantages.
ReminderTwo of each, linked to surgery.
Advantage: can make very precise, steady movements. Advantage: smaller cuts, so patients may recover faster (or the surgeon can work from a distance). Disadvantage: very expensive to buy and maintain. Disadvantage: cannot react creatively to unexpected problems; the surgeon must still supervise.
Common mistakeWriting the same point twice. Give two different points on each side.
Q5 Explain 2 marks Explain why it is an advantage to use a robot to work in a very hot, dangerous factory area.
ReminderSafety.
Robots can work in dangerous conditions where people would be at risk. So the people are kept safe (and work can continue).
Common mistakeSaying "robots are better than humans". Link to danger.
Q6 State 3 marks State three areas in which robots are used and give an example of a robot in each.
ReminderArea plus example.
Industry: for example a welding arm Agriculture: for example a fruit-picking robot or a drone Medicine / domestic / transport / entertainment: a suitable example
Common mistakeNaming the area without an example.
Q7 Explain 2 marks Explain why being programmable is an important characteristic of a robot.
ReminderSame robot, different tasks.
The instructions can be changed or replaced, so the same robot can be made to perform different tasks.
Common mistakeSaying a robot "thinks for itself".

Top tips

  • Three characteristics: mechanical structure, electrical components (sensors, microprocessor, actuators), programmable.
  • Sense, think, act. Use the same words for any robot scenario.
  • A robot has a body that moves things. A street light controller is automated, not a robot.
  • Link each advantage or disadvantage to the role: industry, transport, agriculture, medicine, domestic, entertainment.
  • Do not write "it is smart". Say it follows a program.
