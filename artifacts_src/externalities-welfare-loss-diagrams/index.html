Externalities and Welfare Loss: the Diagrams

Negative and positive externalities on MPB, MPC, MSB and MSC diagrams: market output against the social optimum, overproduction, underproduction and the welfare loss triangle. Animated diagrams built from numbers, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

The firm's supply curve is its private marginal cost (MPC) and demand is the private marginal benefit (MPB). With a negative externality the social cost curve (MSC) lies above MPC, so the market produces more than the social optimum, where MSB = MSC. With a positive externality the social benefit curve (MSB) lies above MPB, so the market produces less than the optimum. In both cases the triangle between the two outputs is a welfare loss, the net social benefit that is thrown away.

1 · The diagram: a negative production externality

The firm's supply curve is the private marginal cost (MPC). The social cost curve lies above it by the external cost per unit. The free market produces where demand meets MPC, which is more than the socially best output.

  • Market output Qm is where MPB = MPC. The price is too low because it leaves out the external cost.
  • Social optimum Qs is where MSB = MSC, which is lower. Between Qs and Qm each unit costs society more than it is worth: overproduction.
  • The welfare loss is the triangle between MSC and MSB from Qs to Qm.

Positive externality (consumption)

MSB lies above MPB. The market produces too little (Qm < Qs), because buyers ignore the external benefit. The welfare loss is the triangle of benefits not realised.

Internalising the externality

Make the decision-maker face the full social cost or benefit. A tax equal to the external cost shifts MPC up to MSC. A subsidy equal to the external benefit shifts MPC down to the social level. Policies are covered in syllabus 8.1.

2 · The positive externality diagram in numbers

Demand (MPB) is P = 100 − Q, marginal cost is 20 + 0.5Q and the external benefit is $15 a unit, so MSB = 115 − Q. The market output is where MPB = MC: Q = 53. The social optimum is where MSB = MC: Q = 63. On each unit from 53 to 63 the social benefit is more than the cost, but the unit is not produced. The triangle has base 10 and height 15, so the welfare loss is 75.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. The numbers are simple on purpose, to show the idea.

Air travel

Flights produce emissions and noise that the airline and passenger do not pay for. The cost to society is higher than the ticket price covers, so more flying is likely than is socially best. A tax on flights could raise the price towards the social cost.

Exam use: the Feb/March 2023 20-mark question. Draw MPC, MSC and demand, mark the welfare loss, then explain a tax, negative advertising or regulation.

Education

An educated person earns more, but others also gain from a more skilled workforce, lower crime and more tax revenue. Because individuals consider only their own return, too little education may be bought without help.

Exam use: a positive consumption externality, MSB above MPB, with under-consumption and a welfare loss.

An orchard and a beekeeper

An orchard owner's trees give bees nectar, and bees pollinate the trees of neighbouring growers. Each side benefits without paying, so both may produce too little.

Exam use: a standard positive production externality, MSC below MPC.

Pollution from a chemical plant

A plant that discharges waste into a river pays for chemicals and labour, but the town downstream pays for water treatment. Output is higher than is best for society.

Exam use: a negative production externality: draw MPC and MSC, show Qm above Qs.

B · Practice question with a model answer

Practice (not a past paper question) 12 marks Using a diagram, explain why a positive consumption externality such as education may lead to market failure.
PlanDefine a positive consumption externality. Diagram: MPB, MSB above it, MC, Qm, Qs and the welfare loss triangle. Explain under-consumption, then one evaluative comment on measurement.
KANE

A positive consumption externality is a benefit to a third party that arises when someone consumes a good and that is not reflected in the price, so the marginal social benefit (MSB) is greater than the marginal private benefit (MPB).

Education is an example: an educated person earns more, but the community may also gain from a more skilled workforce and lower crime. On a diagram, MSB lies above MPB by the external benefit per unit. Buyers decide using MPB, so the market output Qm is where MPB meets marginal cost. The social optimum Qs is where MSB meets marginal cost and is greater than Qm.

Between Qm and Qs each unit gives society a benefit greater than its cost, but is not consumed, so there is a welfare loss shown by the triangle between MSB and marginal cost. The market under-allocates resources to education, which is market failure. The size of the welfare loss may depend on how large the external benefit is, which could be hard to measure, so the under-consumption could be smaller or larger than the diagram suggests.

Top tips

  • Draw the diagram, and label it. Qm, Qs, MPC, MSC, MPB, MSB and the welfare loss triangle. Several 20-mark answers are capped at Level 2 without one.
  • Put the extra curve on the right side. MSC is above MPC for an external cost. MSB is above MPB for an external benefit.
  • Say which way the market goes wrong: negative externality, overproduction and price too low; positive externality, underproduction.
  • Explain the welfare loss as units that cost more than they are worth (negative) or are worth more than they cost but are not made (positive).
  • Refer to the diagram in your writing, not only draw it. Mark schemes ask for diagrams referred to in the text.
