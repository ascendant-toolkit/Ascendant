The Natural Rate of Unemployment

9.3.4: the natural rate and NAIRU, what determines it, and what it means for demand-side and supply-side policy. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

The natural rate of unemployment is the rate of unemployment when the labour market is in equilibrium: it consists of frictional, structural and seasonal unemployment, and it is also the rate consistent with stable inflation (the NAIRU). It is determined by how well the labour market works: labour mobility, information, skills, benefits, union power, the minimum wage and the pace of structural change. The policy implication for monetarists and new classical economists is that demand-side policy cannot cut unemployment below the natural rate for long without accelerating inflation, so lowering the rate needs supply-side policy. Keynesians reply that the natural rate is not fixed and that hysteresis means demand matters too.

1 · Definition

  • Natural rate of unemployment (NRU): the rate of unemployment that exists when the labour market is in equilibrium and the economy is at its long-run potential output. It includes frictional, structural and seasonal unemployment and excludes cyclical and real wage (disequilibrium) unemployment.
  • NAIRU (non-accelerating inflation rate of unemployment): the rate of unemployment at which inflation neither speeds up nor slows down. In practice it is used as another name for the natural rate.
  • It is not zero and not fixed: it differs between countries and changes over time.
natural rate = frictional + structural + seasonal unemployment (as a % of the labour force)

3 · Policy implications

Monetarist and new classical view

  • The natural rate is set by supply-side features of the labour market.
  • A demand stimulus that pushes unemployment below the natural rate can only work while people mistake higher prices for higher real wages. Once expectations adjust, unemployment returns and inflation is higher (the long-run Phillips curve is vertical at the natural rate).
  • To cut the natural rate, use supply-side policy: better information, retraining, weaker union power, lower benefits, labour market reform. This shifts LRAS right.

Keynesian view

  • The natural rate is not fixed: it can be raised by long recessions (hysteresis), so demand management matters.
  • Cyclical unemployment can be cut by demand policy without inflation when there is spare capacity.
  • Supply-side policy takes years, costs money and may be unfair.
Evaluating the monetarist argumentIt depends on how fast expectations adjust, on whether the economy is really at the natural rate (there may be spare capacity, with a negative output gap), on how accurately the natural rate can be estimated, and on whether supply-side reforms work and are acceptable. Estimates of the NAIRU are uncertain and often revised.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

The 1970s stagflation

Governments in several countries used demand policy to push unemployment down, but high inflation followed. By the end of the decade both inflation and unemployment were high, which led many economists to accept that the natural rate sets a limit on what demand policy can achieve.

Exam use: the main evidence behind the natural rate idea. Say that supply shocks (oil prices) also contributed, which weakens a simple monetarist reading.

European labour market reforms

Germany's reforms in the early 2000s cut the length of unemployment benefits, reorganised job centres and made part-time work easier. Unemployment fell from about 11% to under 5% over the next decade.

Exam use: supply-side policy that may have lowered the natural rate. Evaluate: other factors such as wage restraint and global demand also mattered, and low-paid jobs grew.

The US after 2009 and hysteresis

Unemployment peaked at 10% in 2009. For years many economists argued that a lot of it was becoming structural, but a long recovery brought unemployment below 4% by 2019 without a surge in inflation.

Exam use: an example showing the natural rate is not fixed and is hard to estimate. Use it to challenge a confident claim about where the NAIRU is.

Job platforms and information

Online job boards and matching services cut the time it takes to find a vacancy. Faster matching reduces the average length of a frictional spell.

Exam use: improving information as a way to cut the natural rate. Say it works on frictional unemployment, not on a skills mismatch.

Based on Feb/March 2022 · 9708/42 · Q6 25 marks 'Government policies that rely on increasing budget deficits to solve unemployment will not succeed because they do not affect the natural rate of unemployment.' Discuss the extent to which you agree.
What the mark scheme wants
  • A budget deficit as Keynesian demand management that targets cyclical unemployment, with a multiplier effect on AD, output and jobs.
  • The natural rate is unemployment that remains when the labour market is in equilibrium: frictional and structural. Demand policy does not address these, so the statement has some force.
  • Supply-side policies (union reform, lower taxes, retraining, subsidies to growth industries) are more suitable for the natural rate.
  • Evaluation: budget deficits may still fail even for cyclical unemployment (higher interest rates and crowding out, inflation if money is printed, leakages); hysteresis means cyclical unemployment can become structural, so demand policy may affect the natural rate after all.
PlanExplain the Keynesian route. Define the natural rate. Show deficits do not reach it. Evaluate with crowding out, hysteresis and the case for supply-side policy.
Based on May/June 2022 · 9708/41 and 43 · Q7 25 marks Keynesians say bigger budget deficits will cure unemployment. Monetarists say supply-side policies are more effective. Consider which view is more likely to be correct.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Explain a budget deficit and why unemployment is a problem (waste of scarce resources).
  • Keynesian case: a deficit raises AD, and with the multiplier output, income and jobs rise, especially with a negative output gap.
  • Monetarist case: borrowing raises interest rates and crowds out investment, printed money causes inflation, and demand policy cannot help structural or frictional unemployment. Supply-side examples: deregulation, privatisation, lower corporation tax, labour market reform.
  • Evaluation: which view holds depends on the type of unemployment, the state of the economy and time horizon. A good answer concludes that the best policy matches the cause.
PlanDefine deficit and the problem. Keynesian case. Monetarist case. Match policy to cause of unemployment and give a reasoned verdict.

The natural rate of unemployment is the rate when the labour market is in equilibrium, made up of frictional and structural unemployment. One determinant is the level of unemployment benefit. If benefits are high and available for a long time, the unemployed may be less willing to accept the first job on offer, so the average search time is longer and frictional unemployment is higher.

A government could shorten the period for which benefits are paid or link them to evidence of job search, which would give more incentive to take work and lower the natural rate. A second determinant is the skills and mobility of workers. When an industry declines, workers with outdated skills or who live far from vacancies stay unemployed, which raises structural unemployment. Training and housing or transport support would improve matching and shift the LRAS to the right.

However, these supply-side policies take time and cost money, and cutting benefits could increase hardship and inequality, so their effect on the natural rate may be slow and politically difficult.

Top tips

  • Define the natural rate with its parts. Frictional, structural and seasonal unemployment when the labour market is in equilibrium.
  • Say NAIRU means stable inflation. At the natural rate inflation is neither accelerating nor decelerating.
  • Link determinants to a mechanism. Benefits change the incentive to search, union power raises wages, mobility and training change matching, information shortens search.
  • Use the vertical long-run Phillips curve. Demand policy can push unemployment below the natural rate only temporarily, and inflation ends up higher.
  • Match policy to type. Demand policy for cyclical unemployment. Supply-side policy for the natural rate.
  • Evaluate the monetarist view. Expectations may adjust slowly, the economy may have spare capacity, the natural rate is hard to estimate, and hysteresis blurs the line.
  • Be careful with the word 'cause'. Say 'could raise' or 'may lower'. Estimates of the natural rate are uncertain.
