The Business Cycle and Automatic Stabilisers

9.2.3: the phases of the business cycle, its demand-side and supply-side causes and how automatic stabilisers cushion it. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

The business (trade) cycle is the regular but uneven fluctuation of actual output around its long-run trend, with four phases: boom, downturn or recession, trough or slump and recovery. Cycles are caused by swings in aggregate demand (confidence, credit, investment and stock cycles, policy) and by supply shocks and technology. Automatic stabilisers such as progressive income tax and unemployment benefits smooth the cycle without any new decision. They reduce the size of swings but do not remove them.

Know the definitionsA recession is usually two or more consecutive quarters of falling real GDP. A depression is a deep and long recession. Trend growth is the long-run average rate of growth of potential output. The cycle is the movement of actual output around that trend, which is why it links to the output gap (9.2.2).

2 · Causes of the cycle

Demand-side causes

  • Confidence and expectations: optimism and pessimism can spread and feed on themselves (Keynes called these animal spirits).
  • Multiplier and accelerator: a change in spending changes income by a multiple, and a change in income changes investment by more (part of 41.3).
  • Credit and interest rates: easy credit fuels booms, a credit crunch or rate rises deepen downturns.
  • Stock (inventory) cycle: firms cut orders sharply in a downturn to run down stocks, then rebuild them.
  • Policy: late or wrong fiscal and monetary changes can add to swings.

Supply-side and external causes

  • Oil and commodity price shocks raise costs and cut output.
  • Technology waves: bursts of innovation raise potential output and investment, then fade (the real business cycle view).
  • Global shocks: a recession abroad cuts exports; a financial crisis spreads across borders.
  • Natural disasters and pandemics hit supply and demand together.

3 · Automatic stabilisers

Automatic stabilisers are features of the tax and benefit system that move the budget in the opposite direction to the cycle without any government decision.

  • Progressive income tax: in a boom more income is taxed at higher rates, so tax revenue rises faster than income, which dampens spending. In a downturn it falls faster, which cushions disposable income.
  • Unemployment and means-tested benefits: they rise in a downturn as more people qualify and fall in a boom.
  • Corporation tax and VAT also move with profits and spending.
What they do and do not doThey lower the multiplier (higher tax rates and benefits mean less of each extra dollar is spent), so swings in income are smaller. The budget goes into deficit in recessions and towards surplus in booms automatically. They cannot stop a recession or give the economy a push to recover; that needs discretionary policy (a deliberate decision to change spending or taxes).

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

The global financial crisis, 2008 to 2009

A boom in housing credit ended when borrowers began to default. Banks cut lending, firms and households cut spending, world trade fell and many economies had their deepest recession since the 1930s. Unemployment rose for years afterwards, even once output began to recover.

Exam use: a credit-driven cycle with a severe downturn. Use it for causes (credit, confidence), the multiplier, hysteresis and the case for stimulus.

The pandemic recession, 2020

Lockdowns cut output sharply within weeks, then a fast recovery followed in many economies as restrictions eased and governments supported incomes. The fall was far deeper than normal but also shorter.

Exam use: a supply-and-demand shock rather than a normal credit cycle. Evaluate the idea that all recessions look the same.

Tax receipts and benefits in a downturn

In a recession income tax and VAT receipts fall and spending on unemployment benefits rises, so the budget deficit widens without any new law. Governments sometimes add to this with stimulus, but part of the widening is automatic.

Exam use: an example of automatic stabilisers. Say that a rising deficit in a recession is partly a result of the cycle, not only of policy choices.

A stock cycle in manufacturing

When car makers expect weak sales they cut orders for parts and run down stocks. Suppliers see a much larger fall in orders than the fall in final sales, so output drops sharply before it recovers.

Exam use: the stock cycle as a cause of swings. Link to the accelerator.

Based on Feb/March 2023 · 9708/42 · Q1(b) 5 marks Analyse why governments borrow, and why some economists say a government should increase spending during a recession.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Governments borrow to finance spending that tax revenue does not cover, that is a budget deficit (about 2 marks).
  • In a recession higher government spending raises aggregate demand, which raises income, output and employment, magnified by the multiplier, so actual growth could rise (about 3 marks).
  • Credit that there is spare capacity in a recession, so extra demand is unlikely to raise prices much.
PlanWhy borrow. Then a chain: G up, AD up, income, output and jobs up, multiplier. One sentence on spare capacity.
Based on Feb/March 2023 · 9708/42 · Q1(c) 6 marks Consider what is meant by the phrase 'government borrowing could crowd out the private sector'.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Crowding out means higher government spending uses up resources or finance that would have gone to private firms and households (2 marks).
  • Mechanism: to fund a deficit the government sells bonds; to attract buyers it may have to offer higher interest rates (1), and this makes private borrowing dearer, so private investment and consumption fall (1 to 2).
  • Credit evaluation: little crowding out in a deep recession, when there is plenty of spare capacity and low private demand for loans, but more near full employment.
PlanDefine. Interest rate mechanism. Then when it is likely or unlikely to happen.
Based on Feb/March 2021 · 9708/42 · Q6 25 marks Explain why an economy might be in equilibrium at less than the full employment level of output, and critically evaluate alternative policies that might be used to move the economy to full employment output.
What the mark scheme wants

What the mark scheme wants

Automatic stabilisers are features of the tax and benefit system that move government revenue and spending in the opposite direction to the business cycle without any new government decision. For example, a progressive income tax takes a larger share of income in a boom and a smaller share in a downturn, and unemployment benefits rise when more people lose their jobs.

In a recession tax receipts fall faster than income and benefit payments rise, so households have more disposable income than they would otherwise, which limits the fall in consumption. Because part of each extra dollar of income is taxed, the multiplier is smaller, so any shock to spending changes national income by less. The budget moves automatically into deficit in a downturn and towards surplus in a boom. However, stabilisers only reduce the size of swings. They cannot bring about a recovery, so a deep recession may still need discretionary stimulus, and the larger deficit could build up debt if the downturn lasts.

Top tips

  • Name the phase and give the evidence. Say boom, recession, trough or recovery, then link it to output, unemployment and inflation.
  • Recession means falling real GDP for two or more quarters. Do not use it for any slowdown in growth.
  • Unemployment lags. It keeps rising after output starts to recover, and keeps falling in a boom. This is why a recovery can feel like a recession.
  • Give causes in two groups. Demand-side (confidence, credit, the multiplier and accelerator, policy) and supply-side or external (oil prices, technology, global shocks).
  • For stabilisers, say 'automatic'. No new decision is needed. Give progressive taxes and benefits, and the direction of change in both phases.
  • Say what they cannot do. Stabilisers reduce swings. They do not end a recession.
  • Use 'could' about crowding out. In a deep recession there is spare capacity, so crowding out may be small.
