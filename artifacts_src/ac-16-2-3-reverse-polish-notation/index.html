Reverse Polish Notation

Coursebook: Chapter 16 · System software, section 16.2 Translation software

Reverse Polish Notation: why it is used, evaluating with a stack, and converting between infix and RPN; animations, an RPN evaluator and converters, and the real Paper 3 questions from 2021 to 2026.

This page in five sentences

Reverse Polish Notation (RPN), also called postfix, writes the operator after its operands, so 3 + 4 becomes 3 4 +. It is an unambiguous way of writing an expression that can be read from left to right with no brackets and no rules of precedence, which makes it easy for a computer to evaluate. To evaluate RPN you read it left to right with a stack: push each operand; for each operator, pop the last two values, apply the operator (the older value is on the left) and push the result. The final value left on the stack is the answer. You also need to convert between infix (the usual form, with brackets) and RPN in both directions.

1 · Why use RPN?

  • It is an unambiguous way to represent an expression.
  • It is evaluated left to right.
  • It needs no brackets, and no rules of precedence (BODMAS) are needed, because the order of the operators is the order in which they are applied.
  • A stack evaluates it easily: operands are popped in the reverse order to that in which they were pushed. A binary tree can also be used, since both infix and postfix come from tree traversal.

2 · Evaluating RPN with a stack

  1. Read the next item of the expression from the left.
  2. If it is a number or variable, push its value on the stack.
  3. If it is an operator, pop the last two values from the stack: the first popped is the right-hand operand and the second popped is the left-hand operand. Apply the operator and push the result.
  4. Repeat until the whole expression has been read. The one value left on the stack is the result.

3 · Converting infix to RPN

Work out which operation is done first in the infix expression (brackets, then * and / before + and −, and left to right for equal precedence), and write each operation as operand operand operator.

(2 – 6) * (13 + 7) / 5

  1. First bracket: 2 6 –
  2. Second bracket: 13 7 +
  3. Multiply the two results: 2 6 – 13 7 + *
  4. Divide by 5: 2 6 – 13 7 + * 5 /

A reliable method is to draw the expression tree (the operator at the top, its operands below) and read it with a post-order traversal: left branch, right branch, then the operator. Check your answer by evaluating it with a stack.

4 · Converting RPN to infix

Read the RPN left to right and use a stack of sub-expressions: push each operand; for an operator pop two, join them as left operator right and push the result. Add brackets where an operator with lower precedence is inside one with higher precedence. Working through a b – c d + * a /:

  1. a b – gives (a – b)
  2. c d + gives (c + d)
  3. * joins them: (a – b) * (c + d)
  4. a / : the answer is (a – b) * (c + d) / a

5 · Common ways to lose marks

  • Reversing the operands of – or /. The second value popped is the left operand.
  • Leaving out the brackets needed in the infix answer, for example (a – b) * (c + d).
  • Putting all the operators at the end. Each operator goes straight after its operands.
  • In a stack table, showing only the final total. Show the stack after every item, and include the interim values.
  • Explaining RPN with "it is faster". Give the properties: unambiguous, left to right, no brackets or precedence rules.

6 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Calculators that use RPN

Some scientific calculators let you type 3 ENTER 4 + instead of 3 + 4 =. They push numbers on a stack and apply operators to the top values, with no brackets keys needed.

Exam use: RPN is evaluated with a stack.

How a compiler evaluates your sum

When you write (a + b) * c, the compiler converts it into an order of operations that works like RPN: a b + c *. The processor can then do each step with registers or a stack.

Exam use: RPN removes the need for brackets and precedence.

Stack-based virtual machines

Java bytecode and similar systems push operands on a stack and have operators that pop them, which is just the RPN method.

Exam use: operators act on the top of the stack.

A sandwich counter

Order of operations in a queue: put the bread, put the filling, then press 'make sandwich'. The action (operator) comes after what it acts on (operands).

Exam use: operator after operands.

The PostScript language

Printers read PostScript, a language written in RPN: 100 200 moveto draws from the operands that come first.

Exam use: postfix notation is used in real languages.

Top tips

  • Operator after operands. 3 + 4 is 3 4 +.
  • Left to right, stack. Push values; an operator pops two, applies, pushes.
  • Second popped is on the left. Matters for – and /.
  • Show every step. The stack after each item, including interim totals.
  • Brackets in infix answers. A lower-precedence sub-expression inside * or / needs brackets.
  • RPN is unambiguous. No brackets, no precedence rules, left to right.
