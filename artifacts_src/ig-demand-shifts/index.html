Shifts of a Demand Curve

Causes of increases and decreases in demand and how they are shown on the diagram. Syllabus 2.2.3.

The big idea

A change in any factor other than the good's own price shifts the whole demand curve. A shift to the right is an increase in demand (more is demanded at every price). A shift to the left is a decrease in demand (less at every price).

Causes of a shift

FactorIncrease in demand (right)Decrease in demand (left)
IncomeIncome rises (normal goods)Income falls (normal goods); income rises for an inferior good
Tastes and fashionThe good becomes fashionable; a successful advertising campaignThe good goes out of fashion; bad publicity or health warnings
Price of substitutesA rise in the price of a substitute (e.g. tea up, coffee demand up)A fall in the price of a substitute
Price of complementsA fall in the price of a complement (e.g. cheaper printers, more ink demanded)A rise in the price of a complement
PopulationA rising population or more people in the target groupA falling population
ExpectationsPrices expected to rise in futurePrices expected to fall in future
Season and weatherHot weather: ice cream demand upCold weather: ice cream demand down
Credit, taxes and interest ratesLower income tax, cheaper creditHigher income tax, dearer credit

Key terms

  • Substitutes: goods used in place of each other (butter and margarine). Their demands move in the same direction as the other's price.
  • Complements: goods used together (cars and petrol). Their demands move in the opposite direction to the other's price.
  • Normal good: demand rises as income rises. Inferior good: demand falls as income rises (e.g. cheap own-brand goods).

On a diagram: shift right D to D₁ for an increase; left D to D₂ for a decrease. Always label the axes and both curves. Remember: a price change of the good itself moves along; everything else shifts.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of a shift to the right in the demand curve for fish. (Feb/Mar 2022, P22 style)
ReminderIncome, tastes/health campaign, price of substitute, population.
A rise in consumers' incomes could increase demand for fish, because they can afford more at every price. A health campaign promoting fish could change tastes, so more is demanded at every price.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of a shift of a product's demand curve to the left. (Oct/Nov 2023, P22 style)
ReminderA fall in income, a rise in the price of a complement, bad publicity, a fall in the price of a substitute.
A fall in consumers' incomes could reduce demand for a normal good, so less is demanded at every price. A fall in the price of a substitute could make consumers switch, so demand for the product falls at every price.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain why a rise in the price of petrol may reduce the demand for cars.
ReminderComplements; running costs; demand shifts left.
Petrol and cars are complements, used together. A rise in the price of petrol makes running a car dearer, so fewer people want to buy cars and the demand curve shifts left.
Q4 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how a rise in incomes may affect the demand for different types of good.
ReminderNormal vs inferior; luxury; shifts.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A rise in incomes increases demand for normal goods such as holidays and restaurant meals, so their demand curves shift right. It reduces demand for inferior goods, such as cheap own-brand products, as consumers switch to better alternatives, so their demand curves shift left. The size of the shift depends on how sensitive demand is to income.

Top tips

  • Shifts are caused by factors other than own price. Name the factor.
  • Right = increase; left = decrease. Say 'more at every price'.
  • Substitutes: same direction; complements: opposite. Be careful.
  • Normal vs inferior goods. Income effects differ.
  • Draw D and D₁ and label axes. Marks for the diagram.
