Mergers

Horizontal, vertical and conglomerate mergers, with examples, advantages and disadvantages. Syllabus 3.4.2.

The big idea

A merger is when two or more firms join to form one. There are three types. A horizontal merger joins firms at the same stage of production in the same industry. A vertical merger joins firms at different stages of the same production chain. A conglomerate merger joins firms in different industries.

Types of merger

TypeDefinitionExampleAdvantagesDisadvantages
HorizontalFirms at the same stage in the same industryTwo supermarkets; two car makersEconomies of scale; more market share; less competition; cost savings from removing duplicationPossible monopoly power and higher prices; job losses; diseconomies of scale; culture clashes
VerticalFirms at different stages of the same chain: backward (towards suppliers) or forward (towards customers)A bakery buying a flour mill (backward); a brewer buying pubs (forward)Secure supplies or outlets; cut out middlemen's profit; better quality controlLess flexibility; may lack expertise in the new stage; may reduce competition
ConglomerateFirms in unrelated industriesA food firm merging with a mobile phone firmSpreads risk (diversification); access to new markets; use of surplus profitsLack of expertise; no economies of scale; managing different businesses is hard

Why firms merge

  • To grow quickly and reach economies of scale.
  • To gain market power or remove rivals.
  • To spread risk or enter new markets.
  • To secure supply chains or outlets.

Mergers can reduce competition, so governments (competition authorities) may investigate or block large mergers.

Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Q1 Define 2 marks Define a horizontal merger.
ReminderSame stage, same industry.
A horizontal merger is when firms at the same stage of production in the same industry join together.
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain the difference between a vertical and a conglomerate merger.
ReminderLinked stages vs unrelated.
A vertical merger joins firms at different stages of the same production chain, for example a brewer buying pubs. A conglomerate merger joins firms in unrelated industries, for example a food firm buying a software company.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain two advantages of a horizontal merger for firms.
ReminderScale, market share, less competition.
The merged firm can gain economies of scale, so unit costs fall. It gains a larger market share and less competition, so it may raise prices or profit.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether a merger between two large supermarkets would benefit consumers.
ReminderLower costs, prices vs market power, choice. Judgement.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A horizontal merger could create economies of scale, lowering costs and possibly prices. Bigger firms may offer wider choice and invest more. However, less competition may let the merged firm raise prices and cut choice. Smaller suppliers and workers may lose out. Overall, consumers gain only if cost savings are passed on and competition remains strong, so regulators may need to approve it carefully.

Top tips

  • Three types: horizontal, vertical, conglomerate. Define each.
  • Use examples. Supermarkets, bakery and mill, food and phones.
  • Advantages and disadvantages. Scale vs market power.
  • Link to competition. Regulators may block.
