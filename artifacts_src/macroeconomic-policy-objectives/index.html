A Level Economics · 9708 · Syllabus 10.1.1

Government Macroeconomic Policy Objectives

10.1.1: the objectives governments set for inflation, unemployment, growth, the balance of payments, development, sustainability and redistribution, their indicators and targets, and why priorities differ. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

Governments set macroeconomic objectives for the whole economy: price stability (low, stable inflation), low unemployment, economic growth, a sustainable balance of payments, economic development, sustainability and a fairer distribution of income and wealth. Each objective has an indicator and a target, such as a CPI inflation rate near 2%. The objectives are linked, and some conflict, so no government achieves all of them at once. Which one comes first depends on the country and the time.

2 · Tools used to reach the objectives

  • Fiscal policy: government spending and taxes.
  • Monetary policy: interest rates, the money supply, quantitative easing.
  • Supply-side policy: education, training, infrastructure, competition, labour market reform.
  • Exchange rate policy: managing or targeting the currency's value.
  • International trade policy: tariffs, quotas, trade agreements.

No tool works on one objective alone. Interest rates, for example, affect inflation, growth, unemployment and the exchange rate together. That is why 10.2 (links) and 10.3 (conflicts) follow.

3 · Priorities differ

Developed economies

Often focus on stable inflation, low unemployment and steady growth, with an independent central bank targeting inflation. Sustainability and the distribution of income have become more prominent.

Developing economies

Often put growth, development and reducing poverty first, and need to manage the balance of payments and debt. Primary-product exporters may care about stable export earnings.

Strategy for exam answersName the objective, give its indicator, say why it matters, say which tool targets it, and mention that a tool aimed at one objective may help or harm another. Link to the stage of the economy: in a deep recession unemployment and growth usually come before inflation; when inflation is high it often comes first.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Inflation targeting

Many central banks, including the Bank of England and the European Central Bank, aim for inflation of about 2% a year and set interest rates to keep it near that level. The target makes price stability the clearest objective and the central bank is judged on it.

Exam use: an example of a numerical target for one objective. Evaluate by noting that a single target can pull interest rates away from jobs and growth.

The misery index

Adding the inflation rate and the unemployment rate gives a simple measure of how bad conditions feel. Stagflation in the 1970s pushed the index very high, because both numbers rose together.

Exam use: a way to combine two objectives and a reminder that they can move in different directions.

Development goals in low-income countries

Many governments in low-income countries aim for growth, poverty reduction and better health and education rather than only low inflation. Growth that leaves poverty untouched is judged a failure.

Exam use: development as an objective separate from growth. Say that GDP does not capture health, education or inequality.

Net zero targets

Governments have set targets to cut greenhouse gas emissions to zero by about 2050. This is a sustainability objective that sits beside growth and jobs, and policies such as carbon taxes affect prices, output and employment.

Exam use: sustainability as a macroeconomic objective, and the conflicts it can create with short-run growth.

Based on Oct/Nov 2020 · 9708/41 · Q1(b) 3 + 4 marks (i) State three macroeconomic aims a government might have. (ii) A report says a country has an unemployment rate of 3.9%, a current account surplus, inflation of 1.8% and growth of 1.5%. Is there evidence that the country is achieving any of the aims?
What the mark scheme wants
  • (i) Any three: price stability, low unemployment, economic growth, a stable balance of payments, redistribution of income, sustainability or development (1 each).
  • (ii) Use the figures: unemployment of 3.9% is low and near full employment; a current account surplus shows a healthy balance of payments; inflation of 1.8% is close to the usual 2% target.
  • Credit a comment on growth: 1.5% is positive but modest, so that aim is only partly achieved. Quote numbers and compare each with a target.
PlanList three aims. For each piece of data say which aim it measures and whether it is met.
Based on Oct/Nov 2021 · 9708/41 · Q5(b) 13 marks To what extent do you agree that controlling inflation should be a government's most important macroeconomic aim?
What the mark scheme wants
  • Case for: inflation erodes the value of money and savings, creates uncertainty, harms competitiveness and the current account, and can feed expectations that make it hard to reverse (the wage-price spiral).
  • Case against: high unemployment and recession waste resources and cause lasting harm (hysteresis); growth, development and poverty reduction may matter more, especially in low-income countries; controlling inflation can conflict with other aims.
  • A conclusion that relates the answer to the situation: priority depends on how high inflation is, the size of the output gap and the country's level of development.
PlanCase for. Case against. Link to the economic situation. Judgement.
Based on Feb/March 2021 · 9708/42 · Q7(a) 12 marks 'Economic growth should be the most important macroeconomic aim.' Discuss.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Define growth and list the other aims.
  • Benefits of growth: higher incomes, jobs, tax revenue, resources for public services. Costs: inflation, a current account deficit, inequality, pollution and resource depletion.
  • The aims are linked, so pursuing one affects the others. Level 4 needs a conclusion about the extent to which the statement holds.
PlanDefine. For and against. Links between aims. Conclude on when growth should come first.
Practice (not a past paper question) 8 marks Explain why sustainability might be treated as a macroeconomic objective, and how it could conflict with other objectives.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Explain sustainability: growth that meets present needs without reducing future productive potential, including environmental limits.
  • Why it is a macro objective: GDP growth can use up resources and damage the environment, and costs fall on future generations, so the whole economy's path matters.
  • Conflicts: tough environmental policy may raise costs and prices, reduce growth or jobs in some industries in the short run, and may hit poorer households.
  • Evaluation: green investment may create jobs and long-run growth, so the conflict may be temporary.
PlanDefine. Why macro. Two conflicts. One reason the conflict may be smaller than it looks.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Sustainability means economic growth that meets the needs of the present without reducing the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. It is a macroeconomic objective because GDP growth can use up non-renewable resources and cause pollution and climate change, and the costs of this fall on future generations and on the economy as a whole. A government that only targets growth may raise output today by reducing productive potential tomorrow.

Sustainability could conflict with other objectives. A carbon tax raises production costs and prices in energy-intensive industries, which could raise inflation and reduce output and jobs in the short run, so it may conflict with the aims of low unemployment and growth. The costs of higher energy prices also fall more heavily on poorer households, which conflicts with the aim of a fairer distribution of income. However, investment in renewable energy and efficiency could create jobs and raise productivity over time, so the conflict may be smaller in the long run than in the short run.

Top tips

  • List the objectives with their indicators. Inflation (CPI), unemployment rate, real GDP growth, current account, HDI, emissions, Gini.
  • Use numbers. Compare data to a target: 'inflation of 1.8% is close to the 2% target'.
  • Say objectives are linked. A tool aimed at one objective affects the others. This sets up a conflict or a benefit.
  • Priorities change. In a deep recession jobs and growth first. With high inflation, price stability first. Developing economies often put development first.
  • Name the tool. Fiscal, monetary, supply-side, exchange rate or trade policy, matched to the objective.
  • Include the newer objectives. Development, sustainability and distribution are on the syllabus list. Mention at least one when the question allows.
  • Separate growth from development. Growth is a rise in real GDP. Development covers health, education and living standards.
