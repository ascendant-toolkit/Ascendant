Indifference Curves and Budget Lines: the Building Blocks

What an indifference curve and an indifference map show, why the curves slope down and bend in, how a budget line is drawn and what shifts or pivots it. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

An indifference curve joins all the combinations of two goods that give a consumer the same satisfaction. It slopes down, is convex to the origin because the marginal rate of substitution diminishes, never crosses another curve, and a curve further from the origin means more satisfaction. A budget line shows all the combinations the consumer can afford with a given income and prices; its slope is Px ÷ Py. A price change pivots the line around one intercept, while an income change shifts it in parallel. Together the two tools set up the consumer's choice in parts 2 to 4.

1 · Indifference curves

What one curve shows

Every point on one curve gives the same level of satisfaction, so the consumer is indifferent between them. Utility does not have to be measured in numbers, only ranked (ordinal).

An indifference map

Many curves, one for each level of satisfaction. Further from the origin means more satisfaction, because the consumer prefers more to less.

PropertyWhy
Downward slopingTo keep satisfaction the same, getting more X means giving up some Y.
Convex to the originDiminishing marginal rate of substitution: the more X you have, the less Y you will give up for another unit of X.
Never crossIf they crossed, one point would be on two levels of satisfaction, which breaks consistency (transitivity).
Higher curve = more satisfactionMore of both goods is preferred.
Slope of the indifference curve = marginal rate of substitution (MRS) = MUx ÷ MUy

2 · Budget lines

With income M and prices Px and Py, the consumer can buy any combination where Px·x + Py·y ≤ M. The budget line is the limit.

ChangeEffect on the budget line
Income rises (or falls)Parallel shift outwards (or inwards). The slope is the same.
Price of X falls (or rises)Pivots outwards (or inwards) around the Y-axis intercept. The slope becomes flatter (or steeper).
Price of Y falls (or rises)Pivots outwards (or inwards) around the X-axis intercept.
Intercepts: M ÷ Px (on the X axis) and M ÷ Py (on the Y axis). Slope = Px ÷ Py

A sales tax on X acts like a rise in Px: the budget line pivots inwards. A subsidy pivots it outwards. Preferences do not shift the budget line (a 2025 Paper 3 question).

4 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. The numbers are simple on purpose, to show the idea.

A student's weekly budget

A student splits a fixed amount between food and travel. Each combination that uses up all the money is on the budget line. If bus fares fall, the line pivots outwards and the student may buy more of both.

Exam use: a clear everyday description of a budget line and a pivot caused by a price change.

Cinema or a meal out

A student with a fixed monthly entertainment budget can go to the cinema more and eat out less, or the reverse. Any pair of choices that feels equally good to the student lies on the same indifference curve, and the cost of the choices sets the budget line.

Exam use: a simple way to explain an indifference curve and a budget line with two goods.

Choosing a phone plan

Data and calls cost money from the same budget. A higher income allows more of both (a parallel shift out), while a price rise for data pivots the budget line inwards on the data axis.

Exam use: a change in price versus a change in income.

Feb/March 2022 · 9708/42 · Q3(a) 12 marks Explain what economists mean by indifference curves and budget lines and evaluate whether they might be used together to support rational consumer decision making.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Indifference curves: all combinations of two goods giving equal satisfaction; convex to the origin and downward sloping.
  • Budget lines: all combinations affordable given prices and income; a straight line sloping downwards.
  • Equilibrium: the furthest indifference curve from the origin that the consumer can reach while spending all income, at a tangent. Then evaluate how realistic it is to assume consumers behave this way.

B · Practice question with a model answer

Practice (not a past paper question) 8 marks Explain what is shown by an indifference curve and by a budget line.
PlanDefine each curve in a sentence, state two properties of an indifference curve, say what sets the position and slope of the budget line, and say what a price change does.
KANE

An indifference curve shows all the combinations of two goods that give a consumer the same level of satisfaction. Curves further from the origin show higher satisfaction. A budget line shows all the combinations of two goods that a consumer can afford given income and the prices of the goods.

An indifference curve slopes downward, because getting more of one good means giving up some of the other to stay equally satisfied, and is convex to the origin, because the marginal rate of substitution is likely to diminish as the consumer has more of one good. For example, a student may be equally happy with 10 cinema visits and 40 meals out, or 20 visits and 20 meals.

The budget line is straight, with intercepts at income divided by each price, and its slope is the ratio of the prices. A rise in income shifts it outwards in parallel, while a fall in the price of one good pivots it outwards around the other good's intercept. Together the two show what the consumer would like and what the consumer can afford, so the best choice is the highest curve that the budget line allows.

Top tips

  • Always draw the diagram. Several 20-mark questions cap the answer at Level 2 without one. Label axes (Good X, Good Y), IC1 and IC2, BL1 and BL2, and points A, B and C.
  • Say what the curves are. Indifference curve: same satisfaction. Budget line: affordable combinations. Equilibrium: tangency, highest curve reached.
  • Know the four properties of an indifference curve: downward sloping, convex (diminishing MRS), never crossing, higher curve means more satisfaction.
