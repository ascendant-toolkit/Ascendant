Unemployment: Measurement, Causes and Types

4.5.1 to 4.5.3: the meaning and measurement of unemployment, difficulties in measuring it, and the causes and types of unemployment. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

Unemployment means people who are able, available and actively seeking work but cannot find a job; the unemployment rate is the number unemployed as a percentage of the labour force (the employed plus the unemployed). It is measured by the labour force survey (a sample survey of who is seeking work) and the claimant count (people claiming unemployment benefit), and both have difficulties, such as discouraged workers, hidden unemployment, informal work and people who claim benefits without searching. The main types are frictional (between jobs), structural (mismatch of skills or location after industries decline), cyclical (lack of demand in a recession), seasonal (jobs that depend on the time of year) and technological (machines replace workers). Each has different causes and needs different policies.

1 · Meaning and measurement (4.5.1 to 4.5.2)

  • Unemployment rate = (number unemployed ÷ labour force) × 100. The labour force = employed + unemployed (not the whole population, and not those who have stopped looking).
  • Labour force survey (ILO method): a sample of households is asked whether people are without a job, have looked for work recently and are available to start. It is internationally comparable but costly, depends on the sample and on honest answers.
  • Claimant count: those registered and claiming unemployment benefit. It is cheap and quick but misses people not eligible or not claiming, and may include people who are working illegally or not really seeking work.
  • Difficulties: discouraged workers who have given up looking are not counted; underemployment (part-time workers wanting full-time work) and disguised unemployment (people in jobs with little real work) are hidden; the informal economy hides workers; regional and seasonal differences are averaged out; definitions differ between countries.

  • In a developing economy, measurement is harder: much work is informal, self-employment and subsistence farming are common, there is no unemployment benefit (so no claimant count) and surveys are difficult in rural areas.
  • Hidden unemployment includes discouraged workers, people on training schemes and those in jobs with little real work (disguised unemployment).

5 · The types of unemployment in more detail

  • Frictional: short-term and natural; it exists even when there are vacancies, because job search takes time. Better information reduces it.
  • Structural: the demand for one kind of labour falls permanently (coal mining, textiles) while workers lack the skills or live far from new jobs. It is usually long term and concentrated in certain regions.
  • Cyclical (demand-deficient): AD falls, so firms need less labour in every industry. It rises in recessions and falls in booms. It is the type that expansionary demand-side policy can reduce.
  • Seasonal: predictable, for example harvest workers and holiday resorts; usually short term.
  • Technological: automation and AI replace tasks. In the long run new industries may create jobs, but workers displaced in the short run may be unable to move into them.
  • Wage-related: a minimum wage above equilibrium, or strong unions, can reduce demand for labour (sometimes called real-wage unemployment).

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

A factory closes

A car plant shuts and 3,000 workers lose their jobs. Local firms do not need their specialised skills, and few other employers are nearby. This is structural unemployment, which can last a long time unless workers retrain or move.

Exam use: structural unemployment.

A recession

Spending falls across the economy, so firms cut output and staff in many industries at once. This is cyclical unemployment, and it tends to fall as demand recovers.

Exam use: cyclical (demand-deficient) unemployment.

Harvest and holiday jobs

Fruit pickers are needed in autumn but not in winter, and hotels in a ski resort hire in the season. When the season ends many lose work: seasonal unemployment.

Exam use: seasonal unemployment.

Discouraged workers

After months of searching, a worker stops looking. They are not counted as unemployed in the labour force survey, so the published rate understates the true level of joblessness.

Exam use: difficulties in measuring unemployment.

A town loses its main employer

When a steelworks closes, local workers have skills that no employer nearby needs. Structural unemployment persists unless workers retrain or move, or new industries are attracted.

Exam use: structural unemployment.

Discouraged workers

After a long search with no success, some jobless people stop looking. They no longer count as unemployed in the survey, so the unemployment rate can fall while real unemployment does not.

Exam use: difficulties in measuring unemployment.

Based on Oct/Nov 2024 · 9708/22 · Q4(a) 8 marks Explain two methods of measuring unemployment and consider the extent of the possible difficulties in obtaining an accurate measurement of unemployment.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 3): identification of two methods of measuring unemployment (1), plus a brief explanation of each (1 each). The two most used are the claimant count, based on those claiming benefits, and the labour force survey, based on a survey of people actively seeking a job. Answers on types of unemployment rather than methods are not rewarded.
  • Analysis (up to 3): the difficulties in obtaining an accurate measurement with both methods, for example the claimant count may omit people who are not eligible for or do not claim benefits, and the survey depends on sample size and honest answers; discouraged workers and the informal economy.
  • Evaluation (up to 2): a judgement on the extent of the difficulties and which method is more accurate, with a conclusion.
PlanTwo methods: claimant count, labour force survey. Difficulties of each: omissions, discouraged workers, informal work. Judge how serious.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

The claimant count measures unemployment by counting people who claim unemployment benefit. The labour force survey asks a sample of households whether people are without a job, looking for work and available to start, and then scales up the results.

The claimant count can understate unemployment because some people are not eligible for benefits or do not claim, while it can overstate it if people claim without looking for work. The survey relies on the sample being representative and on honest replies, and neither method counts discouraged workers who have given up looking, or people in the informal economy.

Both methods give a rough guide rather than an exact figure, and the survey is probably more accurate because it follows an international definition and does not depend on benefit rules. The problem of hidden unemployment means that any measure understates the true level of jobless people.

Based on May/June 2024 · 9708/21 · Q5(a) 8 marks Explain three causes of unemployment and consider which cause is likely to be most damaging to a high-income economy.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 3): a clear and accurate explanation of three causes from frictional, structural, cyclical (demand deficient), seasonal or technological (1 each).
  • Analysis (up to 3): each cause is analysed and judgements are made about how damaging it might be to a high-income economy: frictional depends on information provision; structural may be permanent and hard to solve; cyclical depends on the economic cycle; seasonal depends on alternative sources of employment; technological depends on occupational or geographical immobility.
  • Evaluation (up to 2): an assessment of how damaging each cause is in comparison with the others (1), leading to a justified conclusion as to which is most damaging (1).
PlanThree causes explained. How damaging for a high-income economy? Judge: structural is likely to be most damaging because it can be long term.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Frictional unemployment arises when workers are between jobs and searching, perhaps because they lack information about vacancies. Structural unemployment arises when industries decline and workers' skills no longer match available jobs. Cyclical unemployment arises when AD falls in a recession and firms cut output and jobs.

In a high-income economy frictional unemployment is short and mostly harmless, and cyclical unemployment falls when the economy recovers. Structural unemployment, such as the closure of heavy industry, can be long term because workers may need retraining or to move.

Structural unemployment is probably the most damaging, since it can persist for years, wastes skills and concentrates in particular regions, while the others are more temporary. A deep recession could still cause very high cyclical unemployment.

Based on Oct/Nov 2023 · 9708/23 · Q4(a) 8 marks With reference to a country that is known to you, explain the main causes of unemployment and consider which of these causes is most significant.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (up to 3): a definition of unemployment (1) and an understanding of at least two types of unemployment (1 each).
  • Analysis (up to 3): an analysis of the causes of those two types of unemployment and their significance, applied to a named country. If only one type is analysed, a maximum of 2; if no country is named, a maximum of 1.
  • Evaluation (up to 2): an evaluation of which cause is most significant in the named country, leading to a justified conclusion (1).
PlanDefine unemployment. Two types and their causes, applied to a named country. Judge which is most significant there.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Unemployment means people who are willing and able to work and are looking for work but cannot find a job. Two types are structural unemployment, caused by changes in the pattern of industry, and cyclical unemployment, caused by a fall in aggregate demand.

In the UK the decline of coal mining and heavy manufacturing in the north left workers with skills that were no longer needed, which created structural unemployment. In the financial crisis of 2008 a fall in AD led firms across the economy to cut jobs, creating cyclical unemployment.

In the UK, structural unemployment has probably been the most significant cause of long-term unemployment in particular regions, while cyclical unemployment rises and falls with the economy. Which is most significant depends on the period examined.

Based on May/June 2023 · 9708/21 · Q1(c) 4 marks Consider the extent to which difficulties may arise in the measurement of unemployment in a developing country with a large rural and informal sector.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Up to 2 marks for each valid difficulty explained, such as inactive workers who might look for work if their situation changed, discouraged workers who have had no success, disguised unemployment, unreported employment in the informal economy, the efficiency of record collection, the scale of the survey and the difficulty of measurement in rural areas.
  • Up to 3 marks overall for difficulties, with 1 mark for a valid judgement on the extent.
PlanTwo difficulties explained (discouraged workers, informal work, rural areas). Then judge how far they distort the figure.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Many unemployed people may have stopped looking, so they are not counted as unemployed, and others work in the informal economy and may be recorded as unemployed or missed entirely. Rural areas are hard to survey, so figures may be unreliable. These difficulties are likely to make the measured rate inaccurate, so figures should be treated as a rough guide, although trends over time may still be useful if the method stays the same.

Top tips

  • Unemployment rate = unemployed ÷ labour force. Not ÷ population.
  • Two methods: survey and claimant count. Explain each, then a difficulty.
  • Do not confuse methods with types. Types are frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal, technological.
  • Give a cause and an example for each type. Mine closures: structural. Recession: cyclical.
  • Mention hidden unemployment. Discouraged workers, underemployment, informal work.
  • Match the remedy to the type. Retraining for structural; demand for cyclical.
  • Judge which is most damaging. Structural can last years.
