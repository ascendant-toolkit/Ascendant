The Internet, the WWW and Bit Streaming

Coursebook: Chapter 2 · Communication · section 2.1 Networks including the internet

The difference between the internet and the World Wide Web, real-time and on-demand bit streaming with buffers and compression, bit rate and broadband speed, and modems, the PSTN and dedicated lines; animations, a buffering lab and the real Paper 1 questions from 2021 to 2025.

This page in five sentences

The internet is the worldwide network of networks: the physical infrastructure of cables, routers and servers that carries data. The World Wide Web (WWW) is one service that runs on the internet: a collection of web pages and resources found with URLs and viewed in a browser; email, file transfer and streaming also use the internet. Bit streaming sends audio or video as a continuous flow of bits that is played as it arrives, using a buffer on the receiving device and compression to cut the bandwidth needed; it is real-time when live (a video call) and on-demand when a stored file is played whenever you choose. The speed of the connection (the bit rate or broadband speed) decides whether it plays smoothly or keeps stopping to buffer. The internet is reached with hardware such as modems, the PSTN, dedicated lines, the cell phone network and routers.

Using webmail (an email account accessed through a website) uses both: the internet, because data is sent across the infrastructure, and the WWW, because you reach the account through a website stored on a web server.

2 · Bit streaming

Streaming sends a continuous series of bits. The receiver does not wait for the whole file: it starts playing while the rest is still arriving.

  1. The video or audio is compressed and uploaded to a media server.
  2. The server sends the data to the user as a stream of bits.
  3. On the user's device the data goes into a buffer, which stores data from the server until the device is ready to play it, and smooths out differences between the speed of arrival and the speed of playback.
  4. The player takes the bits from the buffer and displays them.
Real-time streamingOn-demand streaming
SourceLive events, captured and sent as they happen (video conferences, live concerts)Pre-recorded files stored on a media server
PlaybackImmediately, as it happens; the viewer cannot choose the time and usually cannot rewindWhen the user chooses; can pause, rewind and skip
NeedsEnough bandwidth all the time and heavy compression so people are not behind in the conversationA buffer that fills ahead of the viewing point

Why compress?

Video is data-intensive, so the file size has to be reduced to reduce the bandwidth used and the buffering. That lets people with lower bandwidth take part. For real-time streaming lossy compression is more appropriate: it shrinks the file much more than lossless, so less bandwidth and less buffering are needed, and the data removed (fine detail, or a reduced resolution or sample rate) is not noticed.

Bit rate and broadband speed

The bit rate is the number of bits per second the video needs. If the connection (broadband speed) delivers bits more slowly than the player uses them, the buffer empties and playback pauses to buffer. A higher quality video needs a higher bit rate, so it needs a faster connection.

3 · Hardware that supports the internet

  • Modem (modulator-demodulator): converts digital data into analogue signals for transmission over telephone lines, and analogue back into digital at the other end.
  • PSTN (Public Switched Telephone Network): the worldwide telephone network, made of many different types of communication line. Data may be converted into another form (analogue signals), can travel in both directions at the same time (duplex), and passes through different switching centres and ISPs.
  • Dedicated lines: a direct, private connection, such as a leased line, giving faster, more reliable transmission than shared links.
  • Cell phone network: wireless access through towers (see 2.1.2).
  • Routers: receive packets, read the destination IP address and forward them towards their destination using a routing table.

4 · Common ways to lose marks

  • Using "internet" and "web" as if they were the same. The web is a service that runs on the internet.
  • Describing streaming as "downloading a video". The data is played as it arrives, via a buffer.
  • Leaving out what the buffer does: it stores data from the server until the device can process it.
  • Saying lossy compression is used "because it is better". Say it reduces file size more, so less bandwidth and buffering.
  • Writing that a modem "connects to the internet". It converts digital to analogue and back.

6 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Watching a film on a streaming service

The film is stored on a media server. Your device buffers a few seconds ahead so it can keep playing, and if your Wi-Fi slows down the picture pauses to buffer.

Exam use: on-demand streaming with a buffer.

A live football match online

Cameras capture it, it is compressed, and sent to viewers as it happens. You cannot skip ahead of real time.

Exam use: real-time bit streaming.

Sending an email from a website

You open a web page (the WWW) to write the email, and the message crosses the internet to the other person.

Exam use: webmail uses both the web and the internet.

Old dial-up internet

A modem turned computer data into sounds that could travel over the telephone line, and back again at the other end.

Exam use: a modem converts digital to analogue and back.

A business with a leased line

A company pays for a direct private link to its ISP so its connection is faster and more reliable than a shared one.

Exam use: dedicated lines give direct, private connections.

Top tips

  • Internet = infrastructure. WWW = a service. Webmail uses both.
  • Streaming: continuous bits, a buffer. Played as it arrives.
  • Buffer: stores until the device can process it. It evens out speed differences.
  • Compression: less bandwidth, less buffering. Lossy suits real-time.
  • Modem: digital to analogue and back. PSTN: many line types, switching centres.
  • Dedicated line. A direct private connection, faster.
