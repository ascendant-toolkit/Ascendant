Equity, Equality and Measuring Inequality

Equity and equality, income and wealth, absolute and relative poverty, the Lorenz curve and the Gini coefficient. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

Economic equality means an equal distribution of income, wealth or opportunity, while equity means fairness, a value judgement about what distribution is just. Income is a flow received over a period and wealth is a stock of assets at a point in time, and wealth is usually distributed more unequally. Inequality is measured with the Lorenz curve, which plots cumulative % of income against cumulative % of the population, and the Gini coefficient, the area between the curve and the line of equality as a share of the area under that line. Absolute poverty is income below a survival level; relative poverty is low income compared with the average.

1 · Equity, equality, income and wealth

Equality

An equal distribution of income, wealth or opportunity. It is a positive idea that can be measured.

Equity

Fairness or justice, for example that people in the same situation are treated the same, or that those with greater ability to pay contribute more. It is normative: people disagree on what is fair.

Income

A flow over a period: wages, profit, rent, interest, dividends and benefits.

Wealth

A stock of assets at a point in time: property, savings, shares, pension funds. It is usually more unequally distributed than income.

Causes of inequality include differences in skills and education, ownership of assets and inheritance, wage differences between jobs, unemployment, discrimination, regional differences, and tax and benefit systems. Differences in opportunity (for example, access to education) can matter as much as differences in outcomes.

2 · Measuring inequality

The Lorenz curve plots the cumulative percentage of income against the cumulative percentage of the population, ranked from poorest to richest. The diagonal is the line of perfect equality. The further the curve bows away from it, the greater the inequality.

Gini coefficient = area A ÷ (area A + area B)  ·  0 = perfect equality  ·  1 = perfect inequality

A is the area between the line of equality and the Lorenz curve. B is the area under the Lorenz curve. A bigger bow means a higher Gini. Lorenz curves can be drawn before and after tax and benefits to show how far policy redistributes.

Strengths

  • Shows the whole distribution, not just an average
  • Lets countries and years be compared
  • Can show the effect of taxes and benefits

Limitations

  • Different Lorenz curves can give the same Gini
  • Does not show who is poor, or wealth (usually uses income)
  • A large informal economy and hidden income are missed
  • Does not show differences in opportunity or living costs
MeasureMeaning
Absolute povertyIncome too low to buy the basics needed to survive, often shown by a poverty line (such as an income per day)

Comparing countries' Gini coefficients

Countries in northern Europe tend to have low Gini coefficients (around 0.25 to 0.3), partly because of progressive taxes and generous benefits. Some countries in Latin America and southern Africa have much higher ones (above 0.5). The Gini can fall when policy changes, as in several Latin American countries in the 2008 to 2018 period.

Exam use: the May/June 2024 data response used Gini coefficients, literacy ratios and poverty rates. Say what the data show, then warn that a correlation is not a cause.

Income compared with wealth

A household may have a modest salary but own a valuable house. Its income is low and its wealth is high. Wealth is usually shared more unequally than income because assets are passed on and can grow.

Exam use: distinguishing income (a flow) from wealth (a stock).

May/June 2024 · 9708/41 and 43 · Q1(a) and (b) 3 + 3 marks (a) Describe what is meant by the Gini coefficient and its possible range of values. (b) Distinguish between absolute poverty and relative poverty.
What the mark scheme wants
  • (a) A measure of income inequality (or, less often, wealth) in a population (1). It ranges from 0 to 1, or 0% to 100% (1), with 0 perfect equality and 1 perfect inequality (1).
  • (b) Absolute poverty: not enough income to buy what is needed to sustain life, shown by a poverty line such as an income per day (World Bank definitions accepted). Relative poverty: income low compared with others in the same society.
KANE

The Gini coefficient is a measure of the inequality of income (or sometimes wealth) among a population. It has a value between 0 and 1, where 0 means perfect equality, with everyone receiving the same, and 1 means perfect inequality, with one person receiving everything.

Absolute poverty is where a person's income is too low to buy the goods needed to survive, often defined by an income below a poverty line, while relative poverty is where income is low compared with the incomes of others in the same society.

May/June 2024 · 9708/41 and 43 · Q1(c) 6 marks Consider whether Table 1.1 supports the conclusion that greater inequality of incomes is linked to poor literacy ratios of females to males and leads to greater poverty.
Data in the tableFive countries: Brazil (Gini 0.53, literacy ratio 1.01, 4.2% below the poverty line), Mauritius (0.36, 0.95, 10.3%), India (0.35, 0.90, 21.9%), Egypt (0.31, 0.80, 32.5%), Pakistan (0.30, 0.67, 24.3%).
What the mark scheme wants
  • No apparent link to poor literacy ratios: the better ratios are in the countries with the greater inequality (Brazil).
  • Higher Gini coefficients are also in countries with a lower percentage below the poverty line.
  • Cause and effect cannot be determined from five countries. No link to absolute poverty, and nothing is said about relative poverty.
  • Up to 2 marks for correct use of the data.
KANE

The table does not support the conclusion. Brazil has the highest Gini coefficient (0.53) but also the best female to male literacy ratio (1.01), while Pakistan has the lowest Gini (0.30) and the worst ratio (0.67). The countries with higher Gini coefficients, such as Brazil at 4.2% and Mauritius at 10.3%, also have lower percentages of the population below the poverty line than Egypt at 32.5% with a Gini of 0.31. So the data show no clear link, and with only five countries, and no information on relative poverty, it is not possible to say that inequality causes either poor literacy ratios or greater poverty.

Top tips

  • Define equality and equity separately. Equality: the same share or amount. Equity: fairness, a value judgement.
  • Gini: give the range and meaning. 0 is perfect equality, 1 perfect inequality. It is usually about income, so it can miss wealth.
  • Draw the Lorenz curve. Label the axes, the line of equality, areas A and B, and show the curve before and after redistribution.
  • Absolute versus relative poverty: survival (a poverty line) versus compared with others in the same society.
