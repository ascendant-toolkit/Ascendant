Trading Blocs, Trade Creation and Trade Diversion

11.6.2 to 11.6.3: free trade areas, customs unions, monetary and full economic union, and the gains and losses from trade creation and trade diversion. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

Countries link their economies in stages. In a free trade area members remove tariffs and quotas on trade between themselves but each sets its own tariffs on outsiders; a customs union adds a common external tariff; a monetary union adds a single currency and central bank; and a full economic union adds common economic policies, free movement of labour and capital and a unified market. Joining a customs union causes trade creation (higher-cost domestic output is replaced by cheaper imports from partners, a gain) and trade diversion (cheaper imports from the rest of the world are replaced by dearer imports from partners, a loss), so the net effect may be a gain or a loss.

2 · Trade creation and trade diversion

  • Starting point: a country imposes a tariff on all imports. The domestic price is the world price plus the tariff. It buys some goods from the cheapest world supplier and pays the tariff.
  • After joining a customs union the country removes tariffs on a partner's goods. The partner is cheaper than the tariff-inclusive price but may be dearer than the cheapest world supplier. The domestic price falls to the partner's price.
  • Trade creation (gain): the lower price means (1) domestic producers who are less efficient than the partner are replaced by imports (a production effect: resources are used more efficiently), and (2) consumers buy more at the lower price (a consumption effect: more consumer surplus). Both are the two triangles on the diagram.
  • Trade diversion (loss): goods that used to be imported from the cheapest world producer are now bought from the partner at a higher price (partner price minus world price). This rectangle is a loss, because resources are used less efficiently and the government also loses the tariff revenue. A common external tariff makes this likely.
  • Net welfare effect = trade creation − trade diversion. It is more likely to be positive if the partners are efficient, the external tariff is low, the partners have similar prices to the world price, and demand and supply are elastic.

3 · Evaluating membership

  • Gains: lower prices and greater variety, economies of scale from a larger market, more competition and efficiency, more investment, stronger bargaining power in world trade talks, and dynamic gains.
  • Costs: trade diversion, structural unemployment where industries cannot compete, loss of sovereignty and policy independence (for a customs or monetary union), and adjustment costs. Weaker members may lose from monetary union because they cannot devalue.
  • The effect depends on the elasticities of demand and supply, the size of the efficiency gap between partners and non-members, the initial tariff, and macroeconomic conditions.
  • Compared with global free trade, blocs are a second-best: they can liberalise trade among members while raising barriers to outsiders.
Evaluation pointAn FTA does not give "all the benefits and none of the costs" of a customs union: it avoids the common external tariff (and some trade diversion) and keeps freedom to make other trade deals, but it must police rules of origin, and it lacks the shared bargaining power and simpler customs of a union.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

The European Union

The EU started as a customs union and developed into a single market where goods, services, capital and labour move freely, with a subset of members using the euro. It shows several stages of integration within one project.

Exam use: the ladder from a free trade area to monetary union, and the gains and costs of each stage.

The euro area and weaker members

After 2010, countries such as Greece and Spain with high unemployment and falling competitiveness could not devalue because they shared the euro. Adjustment came through wage cuts, lower government spending and higher unemployment, while a strong economy such as Germany gained from a currency that was weaker than its own would have been.

Exam use: the cost of a monetary union to weaker members, as in a past Paper 4 data set.

USMCA and trade within a free trade area

The United States, Mexico and Canada trade with few tariffs among themselves but each has its own tariffs on outsiders. Rules of origin decide how much of a vehicle must be made in the region to qualify for tariff-free trade.

Exam use: a free trade area and the need for rules of origin.

Brexit and trade

When the UK left the EU's single market and customs union, new customs checks and rules appeared on trade with the EU, raising costs for many firms. The UK gained the freedom to set its own tariffs and trade deals.

Exam use: the benefits of membership of a union that are lost on leaving, and the trade-off with sovereignty.

Based on May/June 2025 · 9708/41 and 43 · Q5 20 marks A free trade area gains all the benefits of being a member of a customs union while avoiding all the costs of membership. Evaluate this statement.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Define a free trade area (removal of tariffs and quotas between members) and a customs union (no tariffs between members plus a common external tariff).
  • A country in an FTA can gain faster growth through specialisation and a global market; lower tariffs reduce firms' costs, raise incomes, increase competition and may raise productivity.
  • A customs union shares protection from outsiders while giving access to a large single market, and members may gain from trade creation.
  • Evaluation: a customs union may cost sovereignty; trade diversion can occur because members must adopt the common external tariff, causing a misallocation of resources.
PlanDefine both. Benefits shared by FTA and customs union. What a union adds: common external tariff and bargaining power. Costs: sovereignty, trade diversion. Judge: statement is only partly true.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

In a free trade area members remove tariffs and quotas on trade among themselves but keep their own tariffs on outsiders. A customs union does the same but also adopts a common external tariff. Both could give lower prices, more competition and economies of scale from specialisation, so many of the gains are the same. In a customs union, members also share protection against outside competitors, have more bargaining power in trade talks, and do not need rules of origin to stop goods entering through the lowest-tariff member.

On the other hand, a customs union has costs that an FTA avoids: a member must accept the common external tariff and gives up some sovereignty. When a member has to raise its tariff on a cheaper non-member, trade diversion may occur, which could reduce allocative efficiency. So the statement is partly true, since an FTA avoids those costs, but it does not gain all the benefits, such as the shared bargaining power. The net result may depend on the pattern of trade.

Based on Oct/Nov 2024 · 9708/41 · Q5 20 marks In recent years many countries have joined or set up a free trade area. Evaluate, with the help of a diagram, whether membership of an FTA is always beneficial to a country.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Define an FTA, give examples, define a tariff and non-tariff barriers and show the effect of removing them: lower prices, more consumer surplus, greater allocative efficiency, economies of scale. A diagram is required (maximum Level 2 without).
  • Diagram of the domestic market with a tariff, the fall in price when the tariff is removed, the change in domestic output and the gain in consumer surplus.
  • Evaluation: the effect depends on elasticities; structural unemployment where demand shifts to imports; polluting production may move to members; cheaper inputs may raise competitiveness; at the macro level, the effect depends on the marginal propensities to export and import and the effect on net exports, employment and the multiplier.
PlanDefine. Diagram: tariff removal lowers price, increases imports. Micro gains: consumer surplus, scale. Evaluation: elasticities, jobs, pollution, macro effect (X − M).
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

A free trade area removes tariffs and other barriers between members. A diagram of the domestic market shows that with a tariff the price is the world price plus the tariff. When the tariff is removed, the price falls, domestic output falls and consumption rises, so imports increase and consumer surplus increases. The lower prices raise allocative efficiency, and a larger market allows firms to benefit from economies of scale, which raises productive efficiency. Cheaper inputs may also lower firms' costs and make exports more competitive.

However, membership is not always beneficial. If demand and supply are inelastic the gains may be small. Domestic producers that cannot compete may close, causing structural unemployment, and polluting industries may relocate. At the macro level, the effect on aggregate demand depends on whether exports rise by more than imports. So membership is probably beneficial overall, but the effect depends on the country's industries and policies.

Based on Oct/Nov 2020 · 9708/41 · Q1(c) 8 marks Spain and Greece use the euro and so cannot devalue. They have had to adjust their domestic policies, reducing wages and government spending. Discuss the possible effects on the Spanish and Greek economies of these policy changes.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Identify the likely policy outcome (2 marks): wages and government spending were cut.
  • Consider the effect of wage decreases (3 marks) and of reduced government spending (3 marks).
PlanLink cuts to wages: lower costs but lower consumption. Link cuts in government spending: lower AD, higher unemployment, but lower deficit. Judge.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Because they share the euro and cannot devalue, Spain and Greece have to improve competitiveness by cutting labour costs and reducing government spending. Lower wages reduce firms' costs and could make exports cheaper, raising net exports over time. However, they also lower workers' incomes and consumption, which reduces aggregate demand and may raise unemployment in the short run. Cuts in government spending could narrow the budget deficit but also reduce aggregate demand, output and public services.

The result could be a slow adjustment with high unemployment, as in these countries, while a stronger economy such as Germany had no need to adjust. The gains in competitiveness may take years, so the policies could reduce living standards in the short run.

Practice question · modelled on the 8 mark style (no past paper) 8 marks Explain what is meant by trade creation and trade diversion when a country joins a customs union.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Trade creation: higher-cost domestic output replaced by lower-cost imports from a partner; gains from more efficient production and more consumption.
  • Trade diversion: imports switched from a lower-cost non-member to a higher-cost member because of the common external tariff; a loss of efficiency.
  • A short development, possibly with the net effect depending on the size of each.
PlanDefine trade creation with the gain. Define trade diversion with the loss. Net effect.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Trade creation occurs when the removal of tariffs on a partner's goods causes domestic output that is more expensive than the partner's to be replaced by imports from the partner. Resources are used more efficiently, and consumers pay a lower price and buy more.

Trade diversion occurs when a country that joins a customs union switches from importing from a low-cost world supplier outside the union to a higher-cost partner inside, because the common external tariff makes outsiders' goods dearer. This is a loss because resources are used less efficiently and the government loses tariff revenue. The net welfare effect of joining depends on whether the gains from trade creation outweigh the loss from trade diversion.

Top tips

  • Know the ladder. Free trade area, customs union, monetary union, full economic union: each adds something.
  • Say what a customs union adds. A common external tariff.
  • Draw the tariff diagram. Domestic supply and demand, the world price and the price with the tariff, with shaded areas.
  • Define trade creation and diversion with the effect. Creation: cheaper partner replaces dearer domestic output. Diversion: dearer partner replaces cheaper world supplier.
  • Say the net effect depends. Gains from creation against losses from diversion.
  • For monetary union, mention the lost tools. No exchange rate or independent interest rate for weaker members.
  • Evaluate FTA versus customs union. FTA keeps freedom; the union shares protection and bargaining power.
