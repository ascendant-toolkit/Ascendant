Causes and Consequences of Recession

What a recession is, why it happens, and how it affects consumers, workers, firms and the government. Syllabus 4.5.4.

The big idea

A recession is a period when real GDP falls, usually defined as a fall for two or more successive quarters. It may be caused by a fall in total demand, or by a fall in the quantity or quality of resources. A recession hurts consumers, workers, firms and the government, and the effects can build on each other.

Causes of a recession

CauseExamplesEffect
Fall in total demandLower consumer or business confidence, higher interest rates, cuts in government spending, falling exports (a world slowdown), a rise in importsAD shifts left
Fall in quantity of resourcesEmigration, a fall in population, war, natural disasters destroying factories and farmsAS shifts left
Fall in quality of resourcesCuts to education and healthcare, falling investment so capital becomes outdatedAS shifts left

Consequences of a recession

WhoPossible consequences
ConsumersLower incomes and living standards; lower confidence so they spend and borrow less; they may fall into debt
WorkersUnemployment rises; wages may be cut or frozen; fewer hours and job openings
Producers and firmsFalling sales and profits; less investment; some firms close, especially weaker ones; falling prices
The governmentTax revenue falls and benefit spending rises, so the budget deficit widens; borrowing and debt rise

A recession can feed on itself: unemployment lowers spending, which lowers sales, which leads firms to cut more jobs. Firms may also cut investment because of low confidence, which reduces productive capacity for the future.

Ending a recession

  • Demand-side policy: cut interest rates, cut taxes or raise government spending to raise AD.
  • Automatic stabilisers cushion the fall: benefits rise and taxes fall without new decisions.
  • Supply-side policy helps recovery in the long run, but is slow.
  • Limits: low confidence may stop people spending, deficits may already be large, and effects take time.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

The 2008 financial crisis

After banks in the US and Europe ran into trouble in 2008, lending dried up, confidence collapsed and many economies fell into recession. Unemployment rose and governments' deficits widened.

Exam use: A fall in demand, with consequences for workers, firms and government.

COVID-19 in 2020

Lockdowns caused real GDP to fall sharply in most countries in 2020. Governments paid wage support and cut interest rates, which raised their deficits.

Exam use: A sharp recession and policy response. A fall in quantity of resources (workers unable to work) and demand.

Albania and emigration

A past paper notes that after Albania's 2013 recession, emigration rose and many workers abroad did not wish to return.

Exam use: A fall in the quantity of resources as a cause and consequence.

Austerity in Greece

During its crisis Greece cut spending and raised taxes. Output fell sharply for several years and unemployment passed 20%.

Exam use: How the recession spiral deepens, and the limits of cutting spending.

Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Q1 Define 2 marks What is meant by a recession?
ReminderReal GDP falls, for two or more successive quarters.
A recession is a fall in real GDP for two or more successive quarters (six months or more).
Q2 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of a recession. (May/June 2023, P23)
ReminderTwo different causes, each developed. Fall in AD (confidence, rates, G, exports), fewer workers (emigration), loss of resources (war, disaster).
One cause is a fall in total demand. If consumer confidence falls, households may cut spending and borrowing, so aggregate demand shifts left and real GDP falls. A second cause is a fall in the quantity of resources, for example emigration. If many workers leave, the labour force and productive capacity could fall, so output may fall.
Q3 Explain 4 marks Explain two disadvantages of a recession. (May/June 2024, P21)
ReminderTwo different disadvantages: unemployment, lower incomes, firm closures, lower investment, higher deficit.
One disadvantage is higher unemployment. Firms cut output and lay off workers, so household incomes fall and the government has to pay more unemployment benefit. A second is lower profits and investment. With less demand, firms' profits fall and some may close, and low confidence could reduce investment in new machinery.
Q4 Identify 2 marks Identify two reasons why tax revenue is likely to fall during a recession. (May/June 2024, P22)
ReminderShort answers: lower incomes, lower spending, lower profits.
Lower incomes mean less income tax. Lower spending means less sales tax (and lower profits mean less corporation tax).
Q5 Analyse 6 marks Analyse how a rise in the interest rate could cause a recession. (May/June 2021, P22)
ReminderChain: rate up, borrowing costs, C and I fall, AD left, GDP falls. Say what happens to firms and households.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A rise in the interest rate raises the cost of borrowing and the reward for saving. Households with loans and mortgages have less to spend and saving becomes more attractive, so consumption could fall. Firms find fewer investment projects profitable, so investment may fall. Lower consumption and investment shift aggregate demand to the left, so real GDP falls. If it falls for two successive quarters, the economy is in recession. The effect could be larger if confidence is already low.
Q6 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether or not a reduction in income tax will end a recession. (Feb/Mar 2022, P22)
ReminderFor: raises disposable income and AD. Against: people may save it, confidence, time lag, deficit, and other policies may be better.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
A recession is a fall in real GDP for at least two quarters, and a cut in income tax is expansionary fiscal policy. Lower income tax raises disposable income, so consumption could rise and AD could shift right. Higher spending may encourage firms to produce more and hire workers, so unemployment may fall and real GDP could start to rise. The effect is likely to be stronger if households have low incomes and spend most of what they receive. However, in a recession people may be worried about their jobs and could save the tax cut, so spending might not rise by much. The cut also reduces government revenue when it is already falling, so the deficit may widen, and the effects take time. Overall, an income tax cut could help but is unlikely to end a recession on its own; it depends on how much is spent, the cause of the recession and whether it is combined with lower interest rates or government spending.

Top tips

  • Recession = two or more quarters of falling real GDP. Quote this.
  • Three causes: fall in demand, fewer resources, lower-quality resources.
  • Name the group affected. Consumers, workers, firms, government.
  • Recession widens the deficit by itself. Tax revenue falls, benefit spending rises.
  • Evaluate policies for ending recession. Low confidence, time lags, the deficit, size of the fall.
