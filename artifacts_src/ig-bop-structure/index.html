Structure of the Current Account

The four components of the current account, how to calculate balances and what deficits and surpluses mean. Syllabus 6.4.1.

The big idea

The current account of the balance of payments records a country's trade in goods, trade in services, primary income and secondary income with the rest of the world over a year. It shows money flowing in (receipts) and out (payments). If receipts are greater than payments there is a surplus; if payments are greater there is a deficit. Each component has its own balance, and the four balances add up to the current account balance.

The four components

ComponentWhat it recordsExamples
Trade in goods (visible trade)Exports of goods minus imports of goodsCars, oil, machinery, food
Trade in services (invisible trade)Exports of services minus imports of servicesTourism, banking, insurance, education, shipping
Primary incomeIncome earned from abroad minus income paid abroad: wages, profits, interest and dividends from owning assets or working abroadProfits sent home by a country's firms abroad; dividends paid to foreign shareholders; interest on foreign loans
Secondary incomeTransfers with no good or service in return: money sent or received by governments and individualsWorkers' remittances, foreign aid, government contributions to international bodies

Calculating balances

Trade in goods balance = exports of goods − imports of goods
Current account balance = goods balance + services balance + primary income balance + secondary income balance

A positive answer is a surplus; a negative answer is a deficit. A trade in goods deficit is when expenditure on imports of goods exceeds revenue from exports of goods.

Where the current account fits

The balance of payments also includes the capital and financial accounts (investment flows, loans and changes in reserves). If there is a deficit on the current account, it must be financed by a surplus on the financial account (borrowing from abroad, selling assets, or using reserves). The current account is the part you are asked about in IGCSE.

Services are a large share of exports for many rich countries (for example banking and tourism), so a country can have a deficit in goods but a surplus in services.

Q6 Explain 4 marks Explain the difference between primary income and secondary income.
ReminderPrimary: earnings from work and assets abroad. Secondary: transfers with nothing in return.
Primary income is income earned from, and paid to, other countries from work and assets, such as profits, interest and dividends. Secondary income is transfers of money with nothing received in return, such as workers' remittances and foreign aid.

Top tips

  • Four components: goods, services, primary income, secondary income.
  • Balance = receipts − payments. Positive is a surplus; negative is a deficit.
  • Calculate each balance separately. Then add them for the current account.
  • Services are invisible trade. Tourism, banking, insurance.
  • Show working and units. Even for a 1-mark calculation.
