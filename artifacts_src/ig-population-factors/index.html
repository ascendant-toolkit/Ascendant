Factors that Affect Population Growth

Birth rates, death rates and migration, why they vary between countries, and what changes the age and gender structure of a population. Syllabus 5.3.1.

The big idea

A country's population changes for two reasons: natural change (births minus deaths) and net migration (people moving in minus people moving out). Economists use rates per 1,000 people so countries of different sizes can be compared. Birth rates, death rates and migration differ between countries because of income, healthcare, education, culture, jobs and safety.

Key definitions

TermMeaning
Birth rateThe number of live births per 1,000 of the population in a year
Death rateThe number of deaths per 1,000 of the population in a year
Natural increaseBirth rate − death rate (per 1,000). Divide by 10 to get a percentage
ImmigrationPeople moving into a country to live
EmigrationPeople leaving a country to live in another country
Net migrationImmigration − emigration
Population growth rateNatural increase + net migration (as a percentage)
Population change = (births − deaths) + (immigrants − emigrants)

Why birth rates differ between countries

Higher birth rates

  • High infant mortality, so families have more children
  • Children help on farms and support parents in old age
  • Little access to contraception or family planning
  • Low education and employment for women
  • Cultural or religious preferences for large families
  • A young population has many people of child-bearing age

Lower birth rates

  • Higher cost of raising children (education, housing)
  • More women in education and work, who delay or limit childbirth
  • Access to contraception
  • State pensions and welfare reduce the need for children
  • Low infant mortality
  • Ageing population: fewer women of child-bearing age

Why death rates differ

  • Income and living standards: better food, housing and sanitation.
  • Healthcare and medical technology: vaccines, hospitals, clean water.
  • Education: health knowledge and nutrition.
  • Age structure: a country with many old people has a higher death rate even if healthcare is good.
  • War, disease and disasters (for example HIV, COVID-19, famine, earthquakes).
  • Lifestyle factors such as diet, smoking and accidents.

Migration: push and pull

Push factors (leaving)Pull factors (arriving)
Low wages and unemploymentHigher wages and more job opportunities
War, persecution, political instabilitySafety and stability
Poverty and poor servicesBetter healthcare and education, higher living standards
Natural disasters, droughtFamily and friends already there

People may also move within a country (internal migration), often from rural areas to cities. Young, skilled people are most likely to migrate, which can leave the home country with an older population and a loss of skilled workers (a brain drain).

Changes in the age and gender structure

The age structure shows the share of the population in each age group. A country can become an ageing population (a growing share aged 65 and over) because of:

  • A fall in the death rate and rising life expectancy (better healthcare, nutrition, income).
  • A fall in the birth rate, so fewer young people.
  • Emigration of young people.

Gender structure: a country may have more women than men because women tend to live longer, because men die in war or dangerous work, or because young men emigrate to work abroad.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Niger and Japan

Niger has one of the highest birth rates in the world, with an average of around six or seven children per woman, while Japan's birth rate is very low and its population is falling.

Exam use: Why birth rates differ: income, education, infant mortality and cost of children.

Romania's falling population

A past paper notes that Romania's population fell between 1990 and 2022, because of a low birth rate and emigration of workers to other EU countries.

Exam use: Natural change and net migration both negative.

The UAE's foreign workers

Most residents of the United Arab Emirates are foreign nationals who migrated for work. A past paper asks for the percentage of foreign nationals.

Exam use: Immigration pulled by jobs and higher wages.

Refugees from conflict

Millions of Syrians left their country after 2011 because of war, an example of push factors causing emigration.

Exam use: Push factors: war and persecution.

COVID-19 and death rates

The pandemic raised death rates in many countries in 2020 and 2021, particularly among the old.

Exam use: Disease affecting the death rate and age structure.

Q3 Identify 2 marks Identify two reasons why death rates may vary between countries. (May/June 2023, P21)
ReminderDifferences in income, healthcare, education, nutrition, average age, disease. Not population size.
Differences in healthcare. Differences in income or standard of living (or nutrition, education, average age).
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of a decrease in the death rate. (May/June 2020, P22)
ReminderIncome, education, healthcare, end of war, each with a link.
One cause is better healthcare. Advances in medical technology, vaccines and more hospitals mean people survive illnesses that were once fatal, so deaths per 1,000 fall. Another cause is a rise in income. People can afford better food, housing and sanitation, which improves their health and reduces the number of deaths.
Q5 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of a fall in the birth rate. (Oct/Nov 2020, P23)
ReminderCost of children, women's education and work, state pensions, contraception, lower infant mortality.
A rise in the education of women is one cause. Educated women may be more likely to enter employment and delay having children, so fewer babies are born. Another is greater access to contraception. This makes family planning more effective, so couples can choose to have fewer children.
Q6 Explain 4 marks Explain two causes of an ageing population. (May/June 2021, P21)
ReminderFall in the death rate or higher life expectancy; fall in the birth rate; emigration of young people.
A fall in the death rate and rise in life expectancy is one cause. Better healthcare, nutrition and incomes mean people live longer, so the share of older people rises. A fall in the birth rate is another. If fewer babies are born because of higher costs of raising children or more women working, the proportion of older people in the population rises.
Q7 Explain 4 marks Explain two reasons why emigration from a country may increase. (Oct/Nov 2020, P22)
ReminderPush factors: unemployment, low wages, conflict, disasters; pull factors abroad.
High unemployment and low wages at home are one reason. People may emigrate to countries where they can find jobs and earn higher incomes, so more leave. War or political instability is another. People may leave to find safety, increasing emigration.
Q8 Analyse 6 marks Analyse the causes of migration between countries. (Oct/Nov 2024, P23)
ReminderPull factors (wages, jobs, services, family); push factors; each with a link. Identifying alone is capped at 3 marks.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
People may migrate to countries with higher wages and more job opportunities. This raises their income and the goods and services they can consume, so their standard of living rises. They may also move to gain better healthcare or education, to improve life expectancy and the job prospects of their children. Push factors such as unemployment, low pay, war or natural disasters at home may force people to leave. Some move to join family members already abroad, which makes settling in easier. The importance of each depends on the migrant and on the country.

Top tips

  • Rates are per 1,000. Say 'per 1,000' in every definition.
  • Natural increase = births − deaths. Net migration = immigration − emigration. Add them for total change.
  • Give a reason and a link. 'Better healthcare, so fewer people die.'
  • Use push and pull for migration. Say what pushes people out and what pulls them in.
  • Ageing: lower death rate, lower birth rate, young people leaving. Three causes to remember.
