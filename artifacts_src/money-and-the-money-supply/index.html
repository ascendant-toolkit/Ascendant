Money and the Money Supply

9.4.1 to 9.4.2: the functions and characteristics of money, and how the money supply is defined and measured. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and exam practice.

This part in four sentences

Money is anything generally accepted in payment for goods and services and in settlement of debts. It has four functions: a medium of exchange, a unit of account, a store of value and a standard of deferred payment. To work, it needs certain characteristics: acceptability, durability, portability, divisibility, uniformity and limited supply. The money supply is the total amount of money in the economy, measured as narrow money (the most liquid forms) or broad money (which adds less liquid deposits), and which measure is used affects how monetary policy is judged.

1 · Why money exists (9.4.1)

Under barter a trade needs a double coincidence of wants: each person must have what the other wants, in the right amounts. Money removes this problem, and allows specialisation and trade at lower cost.

Functions of money

FunctionMeaningExample
Medium of exchangeAccepted in payment, so goods can be bought and sold without barterPaying for a coffee with a card
Unit of accountA common measure of value, so prices can be comparedA website comparing grocery prices
Store of valueKeeps purchasing power over time (inflation weakens this)Savings in a bank account
Standard of deferred paymentAllows debts and contracts to be settled in the futurePaying half a car's price now and half in six months, a mortgage

Characteristics of good money

  • Acceptable: trusted and widely accepted, which is what makes money work.
  • Durable: does not wear out quickly.
  • Portable: easy to carry and transfer (bank transfers and cards are very portable).
  • Divisible: can be broken into small units to pay for items of different values.
  • Uniform: every unit of the same value is identical, so they can be exchanged without checking.
  • Limited in supply (scarce): if supply were unlimited its value would fall.
  • Stable in value: a store of value needs low inflation.

Modern money is mostly fiat money: it has value because the government says it is legal tender and people trust it, not because it is made of something valuable. Most money is held as bank deposits, not as notes and coins.

2 · The money supply (9.4.2)

The money supply is the total stock of money in an economy at a point in time. Definitions differ by how liquid the assets are. Liquidity is the ease with which an asset can be converted into cash without losing value.

MeasureWhat it includesLiquidity
Narrow moneyNotes and coins in circulation, banks' deposits at the central bank, and (in wider definitions) current (checking) account depositsMost liquid: used for everyday spending
Broad moneyNarrow money plus savings deposits, time deposits and other less liquid bank depositsLess liquid: needs notice or may carry a penalty
Money or near money?Assets such as savings accounts, bonds and shares are near money or other assets. They can be turned into money but they are not generally accepted as a means of payment. Broad money counts the more liquid of these.
  • Money is more than cash: most of the money supply is created by commercial banks when they lend (see 9.4.4 to 9.4.5).
  • Why the measure matters: monetarists argue that the growth of the money supply drives inflation (9.4.3), so central banks need a reliable measure; but new payment technologies make the boundaries harder to draw.
  • The demand for money is the desire to hold liquid assets (9.4.7). The money supply is how much of it exists.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Cigarettes as money in prison camps

In some prisoner-of-war camps cigarettes became a medium of exchange and a unit of account: goods were priced in cigarettes, and cigarettes were durable, divisible and uniform. When supplies of cigarettes ran short, their value as a store of value was disrupted.

Exam use: a vivid example of the functions and characteristics of money, and why acceptability matters.

Mobile money in Kenya

M-Pesa lets people hold and send money using a mobile phone. Balances are stored by a mobile operator and backed by bank deposits. It acts as a medium of exchange for people without bank accounts.

Exam use: money is more than notes and coins, and new payment forms blur the boundary between narrow and broad money.

Hyperinflation in Zimbabwe

Prices doubled every day at the peak in 2008. People stopped holding Zimbabwean dollars as a store of value and began using the US dollar and barter. The currency lost its functions as a unit of account and store of value.

Exam use: what happens when money loses its characteristic of stable value.

Cryptocurrencies

Bitcoin is accepted by some firms as a medium of exchange, but its value changes sharply from day to day. It scores poorly as a store of value and unit of account, and is not legal tender in most countries.

Exam use: evaluating whether something is money: test it against the functions and characteristics.

Practice (not a past paper question) 8 marks Explain the functions of money and why money is better than barter.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Barter needs a double coincidence of wants; money solves this as a medium of exchange.
  • Four functions explained: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value and standard of deferred payment, each with a short example.
  • Link to benefits: lower transaction costs, specialisation, trade, saving and borrowing.
  • Evaluation: inflation weakens the store of value and the standard of deferred payment.
PlanBarter problem. Four functions with an example each. A limit linked to inflation.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Money is anything that is generally accepted in payment for goods and services. Under barter a trade needs a double coincidence of wants, so that each person has what the other wants, which makes trade slow and costly. Money works as a medium of exchange, which removes this problem. It is also a unit of account, so that a shop can price a coffee and a laptop in the same units and customers can compare them.

As a store of value it lets people save purchasing power to spend later, and as a standard of deferred payment it allows loans and contracts to be agreed in money terms, for example a mortgage. By making exchange easy, money lets people specialise in what they do best, which raises output. However, if inflation is high money loses value quickly, which weakens its role as a store of value and a standard of deferred payment, and in hyperinflation people may return to barter or use a foreign currency.

Top tips

  • Learn the four functions with examples. Medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, standard of deferred payment.
  • Do not confuse functions and characteristics. Divisible, portable and durable are characteristics. The four functions are what money does.
  • Say 'double coincidence of wants'. It is the key problem of barter and the reason money exists.
  • Narrow versus broad is about liquidity. Narrow is the most liquid. Broad adds less liquid deposits.
  • Most money is bank deposits. Notes and coins are a small part of the money supply.
  • Say what inflation does. It weakens money as a store of value and standard of deferred payment.
  • Test new forms of money. Check acceptance, stability and functions before calling something money.
