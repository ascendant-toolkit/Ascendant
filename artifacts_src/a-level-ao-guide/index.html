AO1, AO2 and AO3: What Each Mark Rewards

Every mark on Paper 3 and Paper 4 belongs to one of three assessment objectives. Knowing which one a sentence is earning tells you what to write more of, and what to stop writing.

Three skills, three jobs

AO1 Knowledge and understanding is saying what is true: definitions, formulae, facts, and using them on the information you are given. AO2 Analysis is saying why: a chain of cause and effect, built from the data and the right model. AO3 Evaluation is saying how far: weighing the argument, recognising its limits, and reaching a judgement.

Most lost marks come from writing a lot of one objective and none of the next. Typical examples are a perfect definition and no chain, or a good chain with no judgement.

Reading the tableOn Paper 4, AO1 and AO2 together are about 70% and AO3 is 30%. In a 20-mark essay that works out as 14 marks for knowledge, application and analysis, marked together on one scale, and 6 marks for evaluation, marked on a separate scale. Six marks is nearly the same as one whole analytical chain, so an essay with no real evaluation is capped well below the top.

AO1: show what you know, then use it on the question

The syllabus has three strands: recall facts, formulae and definitions; explain with appropriate examples; and apply knowledge to economic information in written, numerical and diagrammatic form. The third strand is the one students forget. Application is AO1, so it is worth marks even on the shortest part.

Do

  • Define a term only when the question turns on it. One precise sentence, with the feature that matters ("an externality is a cost or benefit to a third party, not reflected in the market price").
  • Quote the data with number, unit and year, and tie it to a concept.
  • Name the model the question asks for and draw it: labelled axes, curves, original and new equilibrium, a shift arrow, and the context in the labels.
  • Use a real example or the extract in each paragraph. Level 2 examples are "limited or over-generalised"; Level 3 are used to support the explanation.

Avoid

  • Defining every term in the stem. After the first couple of lines, extra definitions earn very little.
  • Pre-written essays. The principal examiner reports repeatedly say that a relevant, concise answer is rewarded in full and a learned one that misses the question is not.
  • Confusing near neighbours: real and nominal, equity and equality, production and productivity, a rise and a fall in the interest rate.
  • Leaving out part of the stem. A question on both perfect and imperfect markets needs both.
Quick testDelete everything from a paragraph except the facts. If a stranger could not tell which question it answers, the application is missing.

AO2: build a chain, not a list

Analysis means examining an issue in detail: select and organise the information, then show causes, effects, relationships and consequences. In practice that is a chain of linked steps where each one is followed by because or so. Level 3 on the 14-mark scale asks for "developed, detailed" analysis using accurate theory and diagrams that are fully explained.

Trigger → mechanism → effect → consequence for the objective named in the question

A chain that gets stuck, and one that does not

Stuck at one step"A higher interest rate reduces investment." That is a statement. It is true, but nothing is explained, so it is AO1 at best.
Developed"A higher interest rate raises the cost of borrowing, so fewer projects have an expected return above that cost and firms cut investment. Investment is a component of AD, so AD shifts left, and real GDP and employment are likely to fall."

Habits that raise AO2

  • Aim for three developed chains in a 20-mark essay, each applied to the context, rather than five thin ones.
  • Draw the right diagram and then write about it. A diagram nobody refers to, and an explanation with no diagram where one was asked for, both lose marks. A missing required diagram caps the 14-mark scale at Level 2.
  • Use data to infer, not to repeat. "Exports grew 12% while imports grew 4%, so the trade deficit is narrowing" is analysis. "Exports grew 12%" is not.
  • Link back to the question's words at the end of each paragraph. Analysis that does not reach the objective being asked about (growth, equity, efficiency) is analysis of the wrong thing.
  • Stay in the model. A micro question answered with macro effects, or an indifference curve question answered with marginal utility, loses the marks even if the analysis is sound.

AO3: weigh it and decide

Evaluation means recognising the assumptions and limits of models and data, assessing the strength of an argument, recognising that some decisions rest on priorities and value judgements, and reaching a reasoned judgement. The mark scheme's top band asks for a justified conclusion that answers the question, and developed, reasoned comments.

The six levers

LeverThe question to askA sentence that uses it
MagnitudeIs the effect big enough to matter?"A 2% fall in petrol demand is small next to the rise in tax revenue, so the policy may serve revenue better than the environment."
TimeShort run versus long run?"In the short run few households can switch car, so demand is likely to be inelastic; over ten years, electric cars could make it far more elastic."
ElasticityHow responsive are the key variables?"If PES is low, a subsidy may mainly raise price rather than output."
ContextDoes it depend on the type of economy or market?"In a country with a large informal sector, a higher income tax may be harder to collect."
Trade-off / stakeholdersWho gains, who loses, which objective suffers?"Lower inflation may be bought with higher unemployment, and the cost falls mainly on lower-income households."
Assumptions and dataWhat does the model assume, and what can the data not show?"The Laffer curve assumes people respond to the rate, yet the data here cover only four years and cannot show cause."

Evaluation that earns the marks

Lever → because → so what for the judgement
Named, not explained"However, there could be time lags." Examiners call this a list. It scores at the bottom of the scale.
Developed"However, time lags could weaken this policy: if interest-rate cuts take 18 months to work, the economy may already be recovering, so the cut could add to inflation rather than ease a recession. That makes the policy less reliable than the fiscal alternative here."

The judgement

  • Answer the scope word. Always, only, alone, best, to what extent: each one demands a position, not a summary.
  • Give a verdict, the strongest reason, and what it depends on. "On balance, a carbon tax is likely to be effective only where substitutes exist, because without them demand barely falls."
  • Evaluate inside each paragraph and again at the end. A single evaluative paragraph on the last page gets little credit if it is one-sided.
  • Do not confuse analysis with evaluation. A third advantage is more AO2. Evaluation questions the strength of the first two.
