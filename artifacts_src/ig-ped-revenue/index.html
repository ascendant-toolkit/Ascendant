PED, Consumer Expenditure and Firms' Revenue

How price changes affect consumer spending and firms' revenue, in diagrams and calculations. Syllabus 2.6.4.

The big idea

When the price changes, the amount spent by consumers (consumer expenditure) and the revenue earned by firms change by an amount that depends on PED. Revenue (= consumer expenditure) = price × quantity. On a diagram it is the area of the rectangle under the price and quantity.

The rule

PEDIf price risesIf price falls
Inelastic (< 1)Revenue rises (quantity falls by less than the price rises)Revenue falls
Unitary (= 1)Revenue unchangedRevenue unchanged
Elastic (> 1)Revenue falls (quantity falls by more than the price rises)Revenue rises

Calculation

Revenue = price × quantity sold

Example, elastic: price $10, sells 100 (revenue $1,000). Price rises to $12 and sells 60 (revenue $720): revenue falls.

Example, inelastic: price $10, sells 100 ($1,000). Price rises to $12 and sells 90 ($1,080): revenue rises.

On a diagram

  • Revenue before: rectangle P₀ × Q₀. Revenue after: rectangle P₁ × Q₁.
  • Compare the areas: the larger rectangle is greater revenue.

The rule links PED, price change and revenue. In exams, show the before and after revenue and state the relationship with PED. Consumer expenditure is the same as the firm's revenue.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Fuel prices

Petrol retailers can raise prices and still keep revenue because demand is inelastic.

Exam use: Inelastic demand.

Airline sales

Discounted fares fill planes: demand for holidays is elastic, so revenue rises.

Exam use: Elastic demand.

Cinema tickets

Cheap-ticket days raise attendance and total takings.

Exam use: Elastic demand.

Tobacco taxes

Governments tax cigarettes knowing consumer spending will rise as quantities fall little.

Exam use: Inelastic demand.

Q1 Explain 4 marks Explain how an increase in the price of milk may affect the revenue earned by milk producers. (Oct/Nov 2020, P22 style)
ReminderDepends on PED: inelastic revenue rises; elastic falls.
Revenue is price multiplied by quantity sold. If demand for milk is inelastic, a rise in price causes a smaller percentage fall in quantity, so revenue rises. If demand is elastic, quantity falls by a bigger percentage, so revenue falls.
Q2 Calculate 3 marks Price rises from $10 to $12 and quantity falls from 100 to 90. Calculate the change in revenue and say what it shows about PED.
ReminderBefore 1,000; after 1,080; revenue rises: inelastic.
Old revenue 10 × 100 = $1,000; new revenue 12 × 90 = $1,080. Revenue rises by $80. Revenue rises when price rises, so demand is inelastic.
Q3 Analyse 6 marks Analyse the relationship between PED and the revenue earned by a firm when it changes its price.
ReminderRule for inelastic, elastic, unitary.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Revenue is price multiplied by quantity. If demand is inelastic, a price rise causes a smaller % fall in quantity, so revenue rises, and a price fall reduces revenue. If demand is elastic, a price rise causes a bigger % fall in quantity, so revenue falls, and a price fall raises revenue. If demand is unitary revenue does not change. The actual effect depends on how accurately the firm knows its PED.
Q4 Discuss 8 marks Discuss whether a firm should lower its price to increase revenue.
ReminderDepends on PED, costs, rival reactions.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Lowering price raises revenue only if demand is elastic, as quantity rises by a bigger percentage than the price falls. If demand is inelastic, revenue falls. The firm should also consider costs: extra output may raise total costs and lower profit. Rival firms may cut prices too, cancelling the advantage. Overall, cutting price is likely to raise revenue only for elastic goods with spare capacity, so the firm needs to know its PED and its costs first.

Top tips

  • Revenue = P × Q. Compare before and after.
  • Inelastic: price and revenue same direction. Elastic: opposite.
  • Always calculate both revenues. Then state the change.
  • Link to PED. Say whether elastic or inelastic.
  • Evaluate with costs and rivals. Revenue is not profit.
