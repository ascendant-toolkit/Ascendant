Definitions of Terms Associated with Market Failure

Public goods, merit goods, demerit goods, private, external and social costs and benefits, and monopoly. Syllabus 2.9.2.

The big idea

Market failure uses a set of key terms: public goods, merit goods, demerit goods, private, external and social costs and benefits, and monopoly. Learn each definition precisely, because exam questions ask for them directly.

Costs and benefits

TermDefinitionExample
Private benefitsBenefits to the individual consumer or producer of an activityA graduate's higher pay
External benefitsBenefits to third parties, not the buyer or sellerNeighbours protected when others are vaccinated
Social benefitsPrivate benefits + external benefitsTotal benefit of education to the graduate and society
Private costsCosts paid by the individual consumer or producerA firm's wage and materials bill
External costsCosts to third parties, not paid by the buyer or sellerPollution harming nearby residents
Social costsPrivate costs + external costsTotal cost of a factory including pollution

Types of goods

TermDefinitionExamples
Public goodA good that is non-excludable (cannot stop people using it) and non-rival (one person's use does not reduce another's): the market does not supply itNational defence, street lighting, lighthouses
Merit goodA good that gives more benefit to society than consumers realise, so it is under-consumed by the marketEducation, healthcare, vaccinations
Demerit goodA good that causes more harm than consumers realise, so it is over-consumedCigarettes, alcohol, drugs, junk food
MonopolyA market with a single dominant seller (or one with a very large share), which can restrict output and raise priceA single water supplier in an area

Public goods have the free-rider problem: people can enjoy them without paying, so firms cannot earn profit and do not provide them.

Examples are described in general terms so they stay accurate. Check current details before quoting figures.

Lighthouses

Ships cannot be excluded from using the light and one ship's use does not reduce another's.

Exam use: A public good.

Education

Schooling gives individuals higher pay and society a more productive workforce.

Exam use: A merit good.

Tobacco

Smoking harms health and others via passive smoking.

Exam use: A demerit good.

Traffic congestion

One driver's trip raises delay for others.

Exam use: An external cost.

Exam-style questions

Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)
Q1 Define 2 marks Define a public good. (Oct/Nov 2021, P22 style)
ReminderNon-excludable and non-rival.
A public good is non-excludable, so people cannot be stopped from using it, and non-rival, so one person's use does not reduce another's.
Q2 Define 2 marks Define a merit good.
ReminderMore benefit than realised; under-consumed.
A merit good is a good that gives more benefit to society than consumers realise, so it is under-consumed by the market.
Q3 Define 2 marks Define social costs.
ReminderPrivate + external.
Social costs are the private costs plus the external costs of an activity.
Q4 Explain 4 marks Explain the difference between a merit good and a demerit good.
ReminderUnder- vs over-consumed.
A merit good gives more benefit than consumers realise, so it is under-consumed, such as education. A demerit good causes more harm than consumers realise, so it is over-consumed, such as cigarettes.

Top tips

  • Learn the definitions exactly. Short and precise.
  • Public goods: non-excludable, non-rival. Free riders.
  • Social = private + external. For costs and benefits.
  • Use examples. Education, cigarettes, street lighting.
