Factors of Production and Their Rewards

1.3.1 to 1.3.3: land, labour, capital and enterprise, the difference between human and physical capital, and the rewards of rent, wages, interest and profit. Animated diagrams, labs, drills and the past paper questions.

This part in four sentences

The factors of production are the resources used to make goods and services: land (natural resources), labour (human effort), capital (human-made goods used in production) and enterprise (organising the other factors and bearing risk). Capital can be physical (machines, buildings) or human (the knowledge and skills of workers). Each factor earns a reward: rent for land, wages for labour, interest for capital and profit for enterprise, and profit is what is left after the other rewards are paid, so it can be negative.

1 · The four factors of production (1.3.1)

FactorDefinitionExamplesNot included
LandAll natural resources, "gifts of nature", used in productionFarmland, forests, fish stocks, oil, minerals (including undiscovered ones), waterBuildings or machinery on the land
LabourThe physical and mental effort of people in productionFactory workers, teachers, doctors, software developersUnpaid leisure activities
CapitalHuman-made resources used to produce other goods and servicesMachines, ovens, factories, tools, aeroplanes, roads and computersMoney, shares and loans (this is financial capital, which pays for factors but is not itself a factor)
EnterpriseThe entrepreneur's role in organising the other factors and taking the risk of productionThe founder of a start-up, the owner-manager of a restaurantHired managers who bear no risk of loss
  • All factors are scarce, which is the source of the economic problem. Their quantity and quality determine how much an economy can produce.
  • Enterprise is needed first to set up a new business: someone must decide to combine the other factors and accept the risk.
  • A business expansion usually involves all four: for example a bakery needs a site (land), staff (labour), ovens (capital) and an owner who takes the risk (enterprise). Raising money from shareholders is not a factor of production; it is how the capital is financed.

2 · Human capital and physical capital (1.3.2)

Physical capitalHuman capital
MeaningMan-made goods used in productionThe qualities, knowledge and skills that make a worker more productive
ExamplesMachinery, factories, vehicles, computers, infrastructureEducation, training, apprenticeships, work experience, health
How it is builtInvestment in equipment and buildingsSpending on schools, training and health care
EffectRaises output per worker; wears out (depreciates)Raises productivity and adaptability; it is embodied in people and moves with them
  • Both raise productivity (output per worker) and the productive potential of the economy. They often work together: a skilled worker gets more from a machine.
  • A country that spends more on education but taxes imports of machinery may increase human capital while physical capital grows more slowly.

3 · Rewards to the factors of production (1.3.3)

FactorRewardWhat it is
LandRentPayment for the use of land and natural resources
LabourWages (and salaries)Payment for work done
CapitalInterestPayment for the use of capital (or the funds that buy it)
EnterpriseProfitThe surplus after all other costs are paid: the residual, and the reward for taking risk
  • Profit is different from the other rewards: rent, wages and interest are contractual (agreed in advance), whereas profit is uncertain and may be zero or negative (a loss). This is why the share of income from profit varies more over time than the shares of rent, wages and interest.
  • In a modern economy ownership and control are often separate (shareholders own, managers control), but profit still rewards the risk of those who own the business.
  • Rewards depend on the supply and demand for each factor: scarce, productive factors earn more.
Exam tipDo not list money or shares as capital. Capital in economics means real things used to produce, such as machines, and its reward is interest. Profit belongs to enterprise.

5 · Multiple choice

Examples are explained in general terms. Check current details before quoting them in an exam answer.

Apprenticeships and human capital

Many countries run apprenticeship schemes that combine work and study. The trainee earns a lower wage while learning, and later earns more because the skills raise productivity. The employer, the worker and the economy gain from the extra human capital.

Exam use: human capital as an investment that raises productivity and later wages.

Robots in a car factory

A car plant that replaces assembly workers with robots uses more physical capital and less labour per car. Output per worker rises, but workers need new skills to program and maintain the machines, so human capital matters too.

Exam use: physical capital raising productivity and its link with human capital.

A start-up and its founder

A founder who starts an online shop organises the website, suppliers and staff and puts in savings. If sales are poor she bears the loss, and if sales boom she keeps the profit. Wages, rent and software licences must be paid either way.

Exam use: enterprise as risk-taking and profit as the residual reward.

Oil-rich land

A country with large oil reserves has valuable land in the economist's sense. Companies pay rent or royalties to the government or owners to extract it. The reserves are natural resources, whereas the rigs and pipelines are capital.

Exam use: the difference between land (natural resources) and capital (man-made equipment).

Based on Oct/Nov 2020 · 9708/22 · Q3(a) 8 marks Explain how the factor of production enterprise contributes to aggregate supply in a modern economy and how it differs from the other factors of production in terms of the reward for its services.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (2): enterprise organises the other factors of production (1) and bears the risk of production (1).
  • Application (up to 6): profit is the reward for enterprise (1); to produce, enterprise hires labour (wages, 1), land (rent, 1) and capital (interest, 1); enterprise uses the factors to produce goods and services that raise aggregate supply (1), through an increase in productive potential or a fall in unused resources (1).
  • Note: profit is the reward for risk and in loss-making firms there is no profit, which contrasts with the contractual rewards to the other factors; in a modern economy ownership (shareholders) is often separate from control (managers).
PlanWhat enterprise does (organise, risk). How it raises output. Its reward is profit (residual and uncertain) versus rent, wages and interest.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Enterprise is the factor of production that organises the other factors, bringing together land, labour and capital, and that bears the risk of production, since the entrepreneur cannot be sure that revenue will cover costs. In a modern economy enterprise contributes to aggregate supply because the entrepreneur hires workers, land and capital to produce goods and services. If new firms are set up or existing firms expand, productive potential rises, and if enterprise puts unused resources to work, output rises.

The reward differs from the other factors. Labour receives wages, land receives rent and capital receives interest, which are agreed in advance. Enterprise receives profit, which is what is left after those payments, so it is uncertain and can be negative if the firm makes a loss. Profit is therefore the reward for taking risk.

What the mark scheme wants
  • Human capital: the knowledge, skills and qualities of workers that make them more productive (1); example such as training or education (1).
  • Physical capital: human-made resources such as machinery used in production (1); example such as a delivery van or an oven (1).
PlanTwo definitions and two examples.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Human capital is the knowledge, skills and health of workers, built up through education and training, for example a nurse's qualification. Physical capital is man-made equipment and buildings used to produce goods and services, for example a bakery's ovens. Both raise productivity, but human capital is part of the worker, whereas physical capital is an asset that wears out.

Practice question · modelled on the 8 mark style (no past paper) 8 marks Explain the rewards to the factors of production and consider why profit is different from the other rewards.
What the mark scheme wants
  • Knowledge (3): land earns rent, labour wages, capital interest and enterprise profit; definitions of each factor.
  • Analysis (3): rent, wages and interest are agreed in advance as costs; profit is what remains after paying them; profit rewards risk.
  • Evaluation (2): profit can be negative, and varies more over time; judgement that it is uncertain unlike the other rewards.
PlanFour factors with their rewards. Profit as residual. Judge: uncertain and can be a loss.
Knowledge (K)Application (A)Analysis (N)Evaluation (E)

Land, which is natural resources, earns rent. Labour, the human effort in production, earns wages. Capital, the human-made equipment used to produce, earns interest, and enterprise, which organises production and bears the risk, earns profit. Rent, wages and interest are costs of production that the firm agrees to pay in advance. Profit is different: it is the surplus after those costs have been paid, so it depends on revenue.

Because profit is the residual, it is uncertain and varies more than the other rewards. If revenue is lower than costs the entrepreneur makes a loss, so profit can be negative, which can never happen to a contracted wage or rent. This is why profit is described as the reward for taking risk.

Top tips

  • Capital is not money. Say machines, equipment and buildings. Shares and loans are financial.
  • Match every factor to its reward. Land: rent. Labour: wages. Capital: interest. Enterprise: profit.
  • Call profit the residual. It is what is left, so it can be negative.
  • Define human capital with skills. Knowledge, skills and health that raise productivity.
  • Give an example each time. Ovens, apprenticeships, farmland, a start-up founder.
  • Enterprise comes first. Someone must organise and take the risk before the other factors are hired.
  • Link to productivity. Both types of capital raise output per worker.
