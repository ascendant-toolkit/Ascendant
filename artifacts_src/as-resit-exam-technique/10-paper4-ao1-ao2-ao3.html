Paper 4 Technique: AO1, AO2 and AO3

9708 A Level Paper 4 — the same three skills as Paper 2, with higher demands

What changes from Paper 2
The assessment objectives are weighted the same on Paper 2 and Paper 4 (AO1 33%, AO2 37%, AO3 30%), so everything in this pack still applies. What changes is how much is asked of each skill. Essays are one 20-mark question, not an 8 plus a 12. The evaluation scale is worth 6, not 4. Chains are expected to be longer, diagrams are more often required, and AS content is assumed.

How the 20 marks are awarded

MarksObjectivesLevels
14 marksAO1 + AO2 together: knowledge, application and analysisLevel 3: 11–14. Level 2: 6–10. Level 1: 1–5.
6 marksAO3: evaluation and a justified conclusionE2: 4–6, developed and reasoned. E1: 1–3, undeveloped comments, vague conclusion.

Compared with the 12-mark question: 14 instead of 8 means three developed chains instead of two, and a required diagram is more likely. Six instead of four means evaluation cannot sit in the last two lines.

AO1: knowledge and application

DoAvoid
Define a term only if the question turns on it, in one precise sentence.Defining everything in the stem. It earns little after the first two lines.
Draw the exact diagram the stem names, labelled with the context.A generic diagram with curves labelled D and S when the question is on AD and AS.
Put the question's context nouns (the country, the good, the group) in every paragraph.An answer that would fit any question on the topic. Apply the swap test: change the country and see if the paragraph still works.
Cover every element of a long stem (both markets, both income groups, the named policy).A perfect answer on one half. Mark schemes cap it.

AO2: analysis

Build each chain as: trigger → mechanism → effect → consequence for the objective in the question. For 14 marks, aim for three chains, each applied to the context.

One step "A cut in interest rates increases consumption."
Developed "A cut in interest rates lowers the cost of borrowing and the return on saving, so households are likely to borrow and spend more, particularly on durable goods such as cars. Consumption is a component of AD, so AD may shift right, raising real GDP and employment; but in a country with high household debt, much of the extra income may be used to repay loans rather than to spend."
  • Use the model the question names and stay inside it.
  • Refer to your diagram in the writing: "the shift from AD1 to AD2 raises the price level from P1 to P2".
  • Finish each chain by naming the objective asked about, using the question's words.

AO3: evaluation

Use the CIT framework (page 3), and use it twice: inside the analysis and again in the conclusion. With 6 marks, E2 needs developed comments, so one lever with a because and a so-what beats three named levers.

LeverPaper 4 flavour
TimeShort run versus long run, and policy time lags.
ElasticityPED, PES, income elasticity, interest-rate sensitivity.
ContextDeveloped versus developing, open versus closed, size of the informal sector.
MagnitudeSize of the shift, size of the multiplier, size of the output gap.
Trade-off / stakeholdersConflicts between macro objectives; who gains and who loses; equity versus efficiency.
AssumptionsWhat the model assumes (perfect competition, rational consumers) and where it may fail.
Justified conclusion
Answer the scope word (always, only, alone, best, to what extent) with a verdict, the strongest reason, and what it depends on: "On balance, fiscal policy alone is unlikely to be sufficient, because the time lag in spending decisions may mean the stimulus arrives after the recession has ended. Its success depends mainly on how quickly projects can start."

Section A (data response) on Paper 4

PartMainly earnsHow
Early parts (2 to 4 marks)AO1Precise, short: identify, define, describe the data with numbers. One mark per point. No essay.
Middle part (about 6)AO1 + AO2Quote the data, say what it shows, say what it cannot show.
Last part (7 or 8)AO1 + AO2 + AO3Use the extract, develop two points, give a counter-point and a reasoned judgement.

Timing for a 20-mark essay

About 40 minutes. Plan for 5, write the chains and diagram in about 25, keep about 10 for evaluation and the conclusion. If time runs out, finish with evaluation, not a fourth chain.

Practice

Question: "Assess whether a government should use a subsidy to increase the consumption of a good with positive externalities." Write one AO2 chain applied to a named good, then one developed AO3 comment on it.
